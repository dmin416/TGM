# Part II — Broader historical reference (≈1914)

**Historically weighty place names** for **1914**: imperial capitals, strategic chokepoints, industrial regions, colonial hubs, and theatres that became (or almost became) synonymous with the Great War. Spelling follows common English usage of the period. *In-story geography and AU differences: **Part I**.*

## European capitals and seats of power

- **London** — British Empire finance, Admiralty, and coalition diplomacy.
- **Berlin** — German General Staff, industrial cartels, and Weltpolitik decisions.
- **Paris** — French Third Republic government; Seine industrial belt.
- **Vienna** — Habsburg dual monarchy administration; Ballhausplatz foreign ministry.
- **Budapest** — Hungarian half of the monarchy; Magyar political veto power.
- **Saint Petersburg** — Imperial Russian capital (renamed Petrograd Aug 1914).
- **Constantinople** — Ottoman capital and Straits control (Bosporus, Dardanelles).
- **Rome** — Italian kingdom; later intervention against former Triple Alliance partner.
- **Madrid** — Spanish neutrality and colonial rear base.
- **Brussels** — Belgian government; invasion route and international law symbol.
- **The Hague** — Permanent Court of Arbitration; neutrality (Netherlands).
- **Copenhagen**, **Oslo** (Kristiania), **Stockholm** — Nordic neutrals; Baltic trade.
- **Bucharest** — Romanian kingdom; oil fields and neutrality until 1916.
- **Sofia** — Bulgarian tsardom; Balkan alignment.
- **Belgrade** — Serbian capital; July ultimatum target.
- **Athens** — Greek kingdom; neutrality and “Megali Idea” pressure.
- **Cetinje** — Montenegrin capital; Balkan front.
- **Dublin** — Irish Home Rule crisis; British rear area.
- **Edinburgh**, **Cardiff** — British industrial and naval support regions.

## Western Front and Franco-Belgian war geography (1914 battles)

- **Liège**, **Namur** — Belgian fortress rings; first German shocks.
- **Mons** — BEF first major engagement (Aug 1914).
- **Le Cateau** — British rearguard.
- **Marne River** — “Miracle of the Marne”; Paris saved.
- **Aisne River** — Race to the sea; trench line hardens.
- **Ypres** (Yper) — First Ypres; salient and channel ports stake.
- **Lorraine**, **Alsace** — Franco-German border provinces; Plan XVII vs Schlieffen–Moltke.
- **Ardennes** — Forest transit; cavalry and infantry collisions.
- **Verdun** — Fortress city; later synonymous with attrition (battle 1916).
- **Reims** — Cathedral city; shelling symbol.

## Eastern Front and Central Europe

- **East Prussia** — German province; Tannenberg myth.
- **Tannenberg** (Stębark vicinity) — 1914 German victory field.
- **Masurian Lakes** — Second German blow vs Russians.
- **Galicia** — Habsburg crownland; Lemberg/Lwów fighting; Russian advances.
- **Carpathian Mountains** — Winter war passes; Russian vs Austro-Hungarian lines.
- **Warsaw** — Russian Poland administrative hub; later German occupation.
- **Lodz** — Textile industry; central Polish corridor.
- **Kraków** — Austro-Hungarian Galician fortress.
- **Przemyśl** — Siege epic (Austro-Hungarian fortress).
- **Riga** — Baltic German–Russian economic hub; later front.
- **Wilno** (Vilnius) — Northwestern Russian military logistics.

## Balkans and Adriatic

- **Sarajevo** — June 1914 assassination site (if extending slightly past “January” lists, still the defining 1914 place name).
- **Skopje**, **Bitola** — Macedonian front logistics (later).
- **Salonika** — Greek port; Entente base after 1915.
- **Durrës** (Durazzo), **Vlorë** — Albanian provisional politics.
- **Kotor** (Cattaro) — Austro-Hungarian Adriatic base.
- **Pola** (Pula) — Austro-Hungarian naval arsenal.
- **Corfu** — Serbian government-in-exile (1915–16).

## Ottoman Middle East and Caucasus

- **Basra**, **Baghdad** — Ottoman Mesopotamia; later British Indian Army advance.
- **Mosul** — Oil-bearing vilayet; post-war stakes.
- **Aleppo**, **Damascus** — Syrian interior; Hejaz railway.
- **Jerusalem**, **Gaza**, **Beersheba** — Sinai–Palestine corridor.
- **Çanakkale** — Dardanelles Asian shore fortresses (Gallipoli prelude).
- **Gallipoli Peninsula** — 1915–16 campaign (listed for 1914 Ottoman defensive geography).
- **Erzurum** — Anatolian fortress; Russian Caucasus offensives.
- **Batumi**, **Baku** — Caspian oil; Russian–Ottoman–Persian margins.
- **Suez Canal** — British imperial lifeline; Ottoman raid threat.

## Africa (colonial theatres and logistics)

- **Cairo**, **Alexandria** — British Egypt and Suez base.
- **Khartoum**, **Omdurman** — Anglo-Egyptian Sudan.
- **Dakar** — French West Africa naval relay.
- **Duala** — Kamerun colonial capital; Anglo-French conquest 1914–16.
- **Windhoek** — German South-West Africa.
- **Dar es Salaam** — German East Africa coast.
- **Lake Tanganyika** — Naval skirmishes; Lettow-Vorbeck theatre.
- **Addis Ababa** — Ethiopian Empire independence.
- **Monrovia** — Liberian capital; U.S. tie.

## Asia and Pacific

- **Singapore** — Imperial fortress; Australian and Indian troop staging.
- **Hong Kong** — British entrepôt; South China Sea.
- **Shanghai** — International concessions; Chinese civil strife backdrop.
- **Peking** (Beijing) — Republican China political centre.
- **Tsingtao** (Qingdao) — German Kiautschou Bay lease; Anglo-Japanese siege 1914.
- **Weihaiwei** — British leased territory on Shandong peninsula.
- **Tokyo**, **Yokohama** — Imperial Japan; Twenty-One Demands (1915) prelude.
- **Port Arthur** / **Dalian** — Kwantung Japanese sphere.
- **Seoul** — Japanese-ruled Korea (Chōsen).
- **Manila** — U.S. Philippines; transpacific cable node.
- **Hanoi**, **Saigon** — French Indochina.
- **Batavia** (Jakarta) — Dutch East Indies; oil and rubber.
- **Calcutta**, **Bombay**, **Karachi**, **Delhi** — British India mobilization and logistics.
- **Kabul** — Afghan buffer state.
- **Tehran** — Qajar Persia; Anglo-Russian spheres.

## Americas and Atlantic

- **Washington, D.C.** — U.S. neutrality and finance.
- **New York** — Loans and arms trade (later).
- **Ottawa**, **Wellington**, **Canberra** (Melbourne capital 1914), **Pretoria** — Dominion capitals.
- **Mexico City** — Mexican Revolution; Tampico and Veracruz incidents (1914).
- **Panama Canal Zone** — U.S. strategic transit (opened 1914).
- **Havana**, **Kingston** — Caribbean neutral trade.

## Oceans, seas, and straits

- **English Channel** — BEF lifeline; U-boat hunting grounds later.
- **North Sea** — Grand Fleet vs High Seas Fleet; Heligoland Bight.
- **Baltic Sea** — Russian and German coastal flotillas.
- **Adriatic Sea** — Otranto barrage (later); Austro-Hungarian littoral.
- **Mediterranean Sea** — French fleet zones; Dardanelles closure effect.
- **South Atlantic** — German commerce raiders (*Emden*, etc.).
- **Pacific Ocean** — German colonial islands; Australian–New Zealand seizures 1914.
- **Gibraltar** — *(OTL)* British fortress; *TGM: French-controlled — see **Part I**.*

## Industrial and resource regions (strategic, not only urban)

- **Ruhr** — Coal, steel, and Krupp complexes.
- **Silesia** — Coal and chemicals (German vs Polish claims later).
- **Lorraine iron ore** — Briey–Longwy basin.
- **Donbas** — Russian coal and metallurgy.
- **Baku oil fields** — Fuel for Russian navy and industry.
- **Ploiești** — Romanian oil (critical from 1916).
- **Witwatersrand** — South African gold; imperial finance.
