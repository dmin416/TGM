**When:** **Future** relative to the edited story through `Story/19`. None of these decorations are on Max’s person or record in current chapters.

**Trigger:** All listed awards are granted **only after Madrid is taken** in the **final** Spain campaign victory: the German backed side and International Brigades capture the capital from Royalist hold and end the peninsular war (~chapter 20 onward, unwritten). ## Battle for the City, 1913

### What Max Did

- Held a position after the line was assessed as lost, with the city considered strategically critical
- Improvised shaped-charge (Monroe Effect) munitions under fire by reworking existing ordnance with a copper liner, applying knowledge no one else on the field possessed
- Used the improvised rounds to defeat Britain's first deployment of tanks, a weapon system Germany had no prior knowledge of or doctrine against
- Turned the battle from a total loss into a defensive victory single-handedly

### Decorations Awarded

- **Iron Cross** (1st Class, with 2nd Class preceding it): empire-wide award, stacks freely with state honors
- **Military Order of St. Henry** (Saxony): Knight's Cross at minimum, Grand Cross plausible given the magnitude of the action
- **Pour le Mérite**: status depends on whether Prussia retains independent court honors under the Wettin throne; if so, awarded for the singular, empire-level significance of the act
- **Military Karl-Friedrich Merit Order** (Baden): Knight's Cross, contingent on a Baden affiliation (birth, regiment, or command structure at the battle)

### Notes

- Grand Cross of the Karl-Friedrich Order is reserved for senior generals/statesmen; not applicable to Max's rank
- Historical precedent for simultaneous holding of St. Henry + Karl-Friedrich + Iron Cross exists (Hermann Frommherz)
- Promotion (Oberleutnant or Hauptmann) and possible ennoblement run in parallel with the decorations above

## Madrid (final Spain campaign victory)

### Citation Summary

Second Lieutenant Maximilian Friedrich von Zähringen, youngest son of the House of Baden, was decorated **after Madrid fell to the German backed side** at the end of the Spain campaign. He received the Iron Cross Second Class and First Class for his conduct in combat, the Military Karl Friedrich Merit Order in recognition of his courage under fire, the Order of the Zähringer Lion for the invention of the heat rounds that broke the previously undefeated enemy line at Madrid, and, upon the personal recommendation of his commanding officer, the Pour le Mérite, making him one of the youngest and lowest ranking recipients of the Blue Max in the history of the Imperial German military.
