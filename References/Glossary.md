## Premise

### Prehistory

At some point in deep prehistory, a meteor struck the earth. The impact was not catastrophic in the conventional sense. Its effect was chemical. Wherever petroleum exists underground it remains stable, but the moment it surfaces it crystallizes into Luminite. No liquid oil has ever been refined, burned, or traded in human history.

Britain and France rose to power as the magitech makers of the world. Through their colonial empires they control the largest Aetherium reserves globally. Britain holds the most, France the second most. Their dominance in magitech development and magical infrastructure makes them the superpowers of this era.

France (Paris) is powerful through its Aetherium reserves and does not prioritize reclaiming Alsace-Lorraine's Luminite deposits. Their strategic strength comes from magic, not industrial fuel.

Germany's Aetherium reserves are negligible. Their power comes from Luminite deposits in the German homeland and Alsace-Lorraine, giving them superior industrial capacity and the most advanced combustion engineering in Europe.

### The Spanish question

Spain retains its colonial empire including Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines. The Spanish-American War is scheduled for the late 1910s (1917–1920 window) but has not yet occurred as of 1914.

Survey work revealed a significant Aetherium belt in the southern Spanish mountains. Britain and Germany both moved to influence Spain's internal politics before the other could secure access to the deposits. This triggered the civil war.

### Spanish civil war timeline

- Spanish Civil War begins in 1912
- Germany aids the Republican forces in exchange for Aetherium access
- Republicans + Germany + International Brigades fight Royalists + British forces
- By early 1914, Republicans control Andalusia and approach Madrid
- France mediates the conflict
- Germany's technological superiority defeats British forces on Spanish soil
- Settlement establishes Alfonso XIV as constitutional monarch under Republican government

### Historical baseline relationships — 1914

- **Germany–Britain:** Naval and imperial rivals.
- **Germany–France:** Enemies since 1870. France wants Alsace-Lorraine back.
- **Britain–France:** Entente Cordiale allies since 1904.
- **Triple Alliance:** Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy (since 1882).
- **Triple Entente:** Britain, France, and the **Russian fragments** (treaty habit still says “Russia” since 1907; no Romanov unitary state since 1913).
- **Germany–Russia:** The Great Russian Empire (Romanov dynasty) collapsed in 1913. Russia is now fragmented into warring factions: Kolchak's royalist forces (British-backed), Denikin's warlord faction, Kerensky's Western Provisional National Government (German-aligned), ethnic independence movements, and various warlords. Germany supports Kerensky's faction as a counter to British influence. Britain and France use their backed fragments as a **proxy stick** in the east and Balkans where OTL used a single empire.
- **Austria-Hungary–Serbia:** Austria views Serbia as Balkan threat. Anglo-French-backed Russian factions and Serbian nationalism pressure Vienna (no single tsar, same strategic lever for the Entente).
- **Britain–Belgium:** Britain guarantees Belgian neutrality (since 1839).
- **United States:** Isolationist. No European entanglements.
- **Britain–Japan:** Naval alliance since 1902.
- **Germany–Korea:** Korea retains independence due to Aetherium deposit and German military backing against Japanese expansion.

## Resources

**Aetherium Crystals**  
Extraterrestrial meteorite ore; powers all magitech. Deep mine exposure is tied to the **Spell Plague** outbreak (~240+ years ago). Full rules: [Magic.md](Magic.md) (Spell Plague).

**Spell Plague** (folk: magic plague)  
Overload of raw magic on an unbalanced soul: delirium like hearing voices (feeling the world, seeing universal energy), physical warping, soul corruption; worst on high ring mages who mixed schools without proper engraving balance. Post plague doctrine: one school past the third ring; balanced multiclass is theorized but never attempted in the towers.

**Luminite Ore**  
Refined into diesel fuel; powers German armored knights.

## January 1914 inventories (other powers)

Equipment **in service or inventory** as of **January 1914** for Britain, Belgium, France, and Spain. Germany conventional artillery: [German-Artillery-1914.md](German-Artillery-1914.md); organization and small arms: [Military.md](Military.md). Representative notes are for fiction consistency.

### United Kingdom (British Army / Royal Navy)

#### Infantry weapons

- **SMLE Mk III**
  - Caliber: .303 British
  - Capacity: 10 rounds
  - Technology: cock-on-closing bolt; rapid aimed-fire doctrine

#### Sidearms

- **Webley service revolver (typical)**
  - Caliber: .455 Webley
  - Capacity: 6 rounds
  - Technology: top-break; simultaneous extraction / ejection

#### Machine guns

- **Vickers machine gun**
  - Caliber: .303 British
  - Feed: 250-round belt (typical)
  - Technology: recoil-operated, water-cooled
  - Note: early-war stocks still mixed with older Maxims in some formations

#### Artillery (representative inventory)

- **Ordnance QF 18-pounder** (standard field gun)
- **QF 13-pounder** (horse artillery field gun)
- **QF 4.5-inch howitzer** (divisional howitzer class)

#### Vehicles, air, and sea

- **Motor:** staff cars, trucks, motorcycles (limited pre-war)
- **Air:** RFC / RNAS reconnaissance aircraft; balloons / airships in roles depending on theater
- **Sea:** Royal Navy dreadnought-era supremacy; destroyers, submarines, mine warfare, minesweeping

### Belgium

#### Infantry weapons

- **Mauser Model 1889 (“Belgian Mauser”)**
  - Caliber: 7.65×53mm Mauser
  - Capacity: 5 rounds (box magazine; charger / stripper loaded)
  - Technology: bolt-action repeating rifle
- **Carbine variants** (cavalry / engineer / fortress patterns of the 1889 family)

#### Sidearms (typical)

- **Service revolvers** in inventory (common European patterns; officer purchase common)
- **FN Browning pistols** (commercial / service sidearms in circulation pre-war)

#### Machine guns

- **Maxim-type heavy machine guns** (water-cooled, tripod-mounted)
- **Hotchkiss light / portable patterns (M1909 family)** (mounted / fast units in the period)
- **Lewis guns (BSA / Armes Automatiques)** purchased in **1913** (in inventory by Jan 1914)

#### Artillery and fortifications

- **Field artillery:** pre-war European field gun / howitzer park (imported patterns)
- **Fortresses:** Brialmont ring forts (Liège / Namur / Antwerp) with fixed guns, searchlights, protected positions

#### Vehicles and air

- **Motor:** staff cars, trucks, motorcycles (limited)
- **Armored cars:** **Minerva** pre-war automobile base (armored-car conversion program begins with the 1914 mobilization period; relevant immediately in 1914 operations)
- **Air:** **Aviation Militaire** exists by 1913; **Henri Farman**-type reconnaissance aircraft in Belgian squadrons pre-war

### France (Armée française / Marine nationale)

#### Infantry weapons

- **Lebel M1886/93** (8mm Lebel; tube magazine; standard rifle)
- **Berthier carbines** (cavalry / colonial patterns; limited rifle issue pre-war)

#### Sidearms

- **Service revolvers** (8mm Lebel patterns; common issue)

#### Machine guns

- **St. Étienne Mle 1907**
  - Caliber: 8mm Lebel
  - Technology: gas-operated, air-cooled (complex mechanism; heavily present at war start)
- **Hotchkiss patterns** (Mle 1900 / Mle 1909 family) in some units and inventories pre-war

#### Artillery

- **Canon de 75 modèle 1897** (“French 75”; standard field gun)
- Heavy guns / howitzers exist in smaller numbers than the 75mm park (pre-war doctrine)

#### Vehicles, air, and sea

- **Motor:** staff cars / trucks (limited); armored-car concepts exist
- **Air:** large pre-war aviation industry (recon / scouting focus)
- **Sea:** modern battleships / cruisers / destroyers / submarines; mine warfare

### Spain

#### Sidearms (notable)

- **Campo Giro**
  - Caliber: 9mm Largo
  - Capacity: 8 rounds
  - Technology: delayed blowback + buffer system

#### Inventory (representative)

- **Rifles:** Mauser-family bolt-action service rifles common in Spanish service
- **Machine guns:** Maxim / Hotchkiss-class automatic weapons present in limited quantities depending on unit
- **Artillery:** imported European field guns / howitzers in common pre-war patterns
- **Motor / air / sea:** limited motorization; early aviation; navy operating in the dreadnought era

### Common cross-cutting kit (Jan 1914)

#### Communications and observation

- **Field telephones**, **field telegraph**, signal flags / lamps / heliographs
- **Binoculars**, **rangefinders**, maps / compasses
- **Searchlights** (fortress / coastal defense; some field use)

#### Mobility

- **Horses** (primary tactical mobility)
- **Bicycles** (military cyclist units in many European forces)
- **Rail** (strategic mobility), **motor** (limited staff / logistics use)
