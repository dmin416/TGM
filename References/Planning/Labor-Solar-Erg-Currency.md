# Socialist Currency Concept: The Labor-Solar Erg (LSE)

Rough draft. Energy anchored socialist currency design for alternate or postwar civic economy planning. Not story canon until cited. Cross links [Alternate-History-Baseline.md](Alternate-History-Baseline.md), [Imperial-Judicial-System.md](Imperial-Judicial-System.md), [Market-Crash-Wealth-Reference.md](Market-Crash-Wealth-Reference.md), [Notes-for-the-Future.md](Notes-for-the-Future.md).

## Foundational Unit

The base unit is defined by anchoring two universal, objective phenomena:

**1 LSE = the energy delivered by 1 hour of average solar irradiance on 1 m² of temperate land**

At ~500W average (accounting for day/night, cloud cover, and seasons at ~50° latitude), that equals **1,800 kilojoules (1.8 MJ)**.

This is deliberately close to the metabolic output of **one person hour of moderate physical labor** (~300–500W sustained), creating a natural equivalence between solar energy and human work.

---

## The Three Anchors

| Anchor | Physical Basis | LSE Value |
|---|---|---|
| Solar hour | 1.8 MJ on 1 m² | 1.0 LSE |
| Labor hour | Adult human moderate work | ~0.8–1.2 LSE |
| Caloric day | 2,000 kcal ≈ 8.4 MJ | ~4.7 LSE |

A person consuming one day's food has spent roughly **5 LSE** of energy, and must contribute roughly **5 LSE** of work or equivalent to balance the ledger.

## Denominations

| Name | LSE Value | Human Analog |
|---|---|---|
| Micro erg | 0.001 LSE | ~3.6 seconds of labor |
| Centi erg | 0.01 LSE | ~36 seconds of labor |
| Erg | 1.0 LSE | 1 solar hour / ~1 labor hour |
| Kilo erg | 1,000 LSE | ~1 year of full time work |
| Mega erg | 1,000,000 LSE | community/industrial scale |

## Skill Multipliers

Raw physical labor = **1.0 LSE/hour** baseline.

Skilled labor earns a multiplier based on **training investment**, not market scarcity:

| Category | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Unskilled physical labor | 1.0× |
| Trained craft (mason, machinist) | 1.5–2.0× |
| Long trained specialist (surgeon, engineer) | 2.5–4.0× |

The multiplier is set by **certified training hours invested**, not by social prestige. This prevents the currency from drifting back into wage hierarchy while still rewarding genuine skill development.

## Weather and the Labor Premium

Solar yield is geographically unequal. A community in cloudy northern latitudes harvests far fewer LSE from the sun per year than one in a sunny region. This is a **real material inequality** that the currency reflects honestly.

However, poor weather imposes additional costs on labor that must be compensated:

### Cold and Inclement Weather Surcharges

Work performed under adverse weather conditions requires greater caloric input, heavier equipment, slower pace, and higher injury risk. These are **real energy costs**, not social preferences.

| Condition | Labor Cost Basis | Surcharge |
|---|---|---|
| Below 0°C sustained outdoor work | Increased caloric burn (~20–40% above baseline), insulation gear overhead | +0.3–0.5 LSE/hour |
| Rain/wind exposure above threshold | Slower pace, protective equipment, elevated fatigue | +0.2–0.3 LSE/hour |
| Snow/ice terrain (transport, construction) | Mechanical resistance, safety overhead, reduced output | +0.3–0.6 LSE/hour |
| Arctic/subarctic conditions | Extreme caloric demand, constant frostbite risk, gear degradation | +0.8–1.5 LSE/hour |

These surcharges are not arbitrary. They are calculated from **measured caloric expenditure data** and **documented injury rate differentials** for each condition class.

### The Inverse Relationship

This creates a natural corrective:

> **Where the sun pays less, human labor pays more.**

A community in northern Finland harvests perhaps 30% of the annual solar LSE of a community in southern Spain. But the Finnish laborer working outdoors in winter earns significantly more LSE per hour, reflecting the genuine biological cost of that labor.

This is not a subsidy. It is an accurate measurement of real energy expenditure.

### Regional Solar Adjustment Index (RSAI)

Each region is assigned an RSAI based on mean annual solar irradiance:

| Zone | Example Region | Mean Annual LSE/m² | RSAI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 (highest) | Sahara, Arabian Peninsula | ~2,200 | 1.0 |
| 4 | Mediterranean, Central Asia | ~1,700 | 0.77 |
| 3 | Central Europe, Pacific Northwest | ~1,100 | 0.50 |
| 2 | Northern Europe, Canada | ~700 | 0.32 |
| 1 (lowest) | Arctic regions, Iceland | ~350 | 0.16 |

Lower RSAI zones receive proportionally higher **labor premiums** to compensate for the reduced solar contribution to their community's total energy budget.

## Anti Inflation Mechanisms

Since LSE is energy anchored, inflation is physically bounded:

1. **Energy audit:** total LSE in circulation must be ≤ total energy harvested and worked in the period
2. **Decay rate:** unspent LSE decays at ~1% per month; energy not used is energy lost, discouraging hoarding
3. **No fractional reserve:** LSE cannot be lent into existence; only labor and solar harvest create new units

---

## Key Properties

- **Non accumulative:** decay prevents dynastic wealth
- **Universal baseline:** every person generates ~1 LSE/hour regardless of geography or politics
- **Honest about geography:** communities with more sun are genuinely richer in solar terms; this is visible, not hidden
- **Compensates real hardship:** cold weather labor premium reflects actual biological cost, not political favoritism
- **Grounded:** tied to physical reality, not debt, gold, or state fiat

---

## Summary Principle

> The sun does not negotiate. One m² hour is one m² hour.  
> But a person working in a blizzard spends more of themselves than a person working in mild sun.  
> The currency measures both truths simultaneously.
