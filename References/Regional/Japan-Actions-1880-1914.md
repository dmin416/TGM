# Japan's Actions: 1880–1914

## Overview

Between 1880 and 1914, Japan transformed from a feudal island nation into one of the world's recognized imperial powers. Through military modernization, aggressive expansion, and calculated diplomacy, Japan carved out a colonial empire across East Asia and the Pacific.

---

## 1880s: Modernization and Early Expansion

### 1882
- Japan sends military advisors to Korea, increasing political influence over the **Joseon Dynasty**
- The **Imo Incident**: a mutiny by Korean soldiers leads Japan to pressure Korea into closer ties; Japanese military presence in Seoul expands

### 1884
- **Gapsin Coup**: Japanese-backed Korean reformers attempt to seize power in Seoul; Chinese intervention suppresses the coup and reasserts Qing influence
- Japan and China sign the **Convention of Tientsin**, agreeing to mutual withdrawal from Korea and equal right to send troops if unrest erupts again. Japan would later exploit that clause

### 1885–1889
- Japan accelerates military buildup under the **Meiji government**, modernizing the army along German lines and the navy along British lines
- Compulsory military service, modern artillery, and a professional officer corps are established
- The **Meiji Constitution** is enacted in 1889, formalizing state power and placing the military directly under the Emperor

---

## 1890s: First Major Imperial Moves

### 1894–1895: First Sino-Japanese War
- Sparked by competing influence over Korea and the **Donghak Peasant Rebellion**
- Japanese forces decisively defeat the Chinese Qing military on land and sea
- Key battles: **Battle of the Yalu River** (naval), **Battle of Port Arthur**, **Battle of Weihaiwei**
- **Atrocities**: Japanese troops massacre thousands of Chinese civilians and soldiers at **Port Arthur (Lüshun)** in November 1894

### 1895: Treaty of Shimonoseki
- China cedes **Taiwan**, the **Pescadores Islands**, and the **Liaodong Peninsula** to Japan
- Korea is recognized as independent from China, opening it fully to Japanese influence
- China pays a massive war indemnity

### 1895: Triple Intervention
- Russia, France, and Germany pressure Japan into returning the Liaodong Peninsula to China
- Japan is humiliated but accepts, vowing to build a stronger military for future confrontation with Russia

### 1895–1902: Taiwan Pacification Campaigns
- Indigenous Taiwanese and Han Chinese resisters fight Japanese rule
- Japan wages a prolonged military campaign with significant civilian casualties
- Taiwan is formally incorporated as Japan's first overseas colony

---

## 1900s: Rising Imperial Power

### 1900: Boxer Rebellion
- Japan contributes the **largest foreign military contingent** to the Eight-Nation Alliance that suppresses the Boxer Rebellion in China
- Demonstrates Japan's military credibility to Western powers
- Japan loots, burns, and executes Chinese civilians alongside other foreign forces in Beijing and surrounding areas

### 1902: Anglo-Japanese Alliance
- Japan signs a formal military alliance with **Britain**, recognizing Japan as a legitimate great power
- Guarantees British neutrality if Japan fights one power, and British support if Japan fights two
- Frees Japan to confront Russia without fear of a multi-front European war

### 1904–1905: Russo-Japanese War
- Japan launches a surprise naval attack on the Russian fleet at **Port Arthur** in February 1904
- Major land battles in **Manchuria**: Battle of Liaoyang, Battle of Shaho, Battle of Mukden (one of the largest land battles in history to that date)
- **Battle of Tsushima (May 1905)**: Japan annihilates the Russian Baltic Fleet in a decisive naval engagement
- Japan becomes the **first Asian nation to defeat a European great power** in modern warfare

### 1905: Treaty of Portsmouth
- Russia cedes the southern half of **Sakhalin Island** and the Liaodong leasehold (including Port Arthur) to Japan
- Russia acknowledges Japan's paramount interest in **Korea**
- Japan gains control of the **South Manchuria Railway**, the economic spine of the region
- Mediated by U.S. President Theodore Roosevelt; Japan wins international recognition as a major power

### 1905: Japan–Korea Protectorate Treaty (Eulsa Treaty)
- Korea is stripped of diplomatic independence and placed under Japanese control
- **Itō Hirobumi** becomes the first Japanese Resident-General of Korea
- Korean Emperor Gojong refuses to sign; the treaty is imposed by force
- Korean resistance movements are crushed militarily

### 1907–1910: Suppression of Korean Resistance
- Japan disbands the Korean army in 1907, triggering widespread armed resistance
- The **Righteous Army** (Uibyeong) insurgency is fought across the Korean countryside
- Japanese forces conduct large-scale counter-insurgency operations, killing thousands of Korean fighters and civilians
- Over 14,000 Korean guerrillas killed in recorded engagements alone; civilian death toll is higher

---

## 1910s (Pre-WWI): Consolidation of Empire

### 1910: Annexation of Korea
- The **Japan–Korea Annexation Treaty** formally absorbs Korea into the Japanese Empire
- Korean sovereignty is extinguished; the peninsula becomes **Chōsen** under Japanese colonial administration
- Land surveys dispossess thousands of Korean farmers
- Korean language, culture, and political organization are systematically suppressed

### 1910–1914: Colonial Consolidation in Korea and Taiwan
- Forced labor policies established for infrastructure construction
- Japanese settlers receive preferential land rights
- Korean and Taiwanese schools mandated to teach in Japanese
- Political organizations and independent press are banned

### 1911: Response to Chinese Revolution
- Japan watches closely as the **Qing Dynasty collapses** and the Republic of China is established
- Japan begins cultivating relationships with regional Chinese warlords to expand influence in Manchuria

### 1914: Entry into World War I
- Japan declares war on Germany in August 1914, honoring the Anglo-Japanese Alliance
- Japanese forces **seize Germany's Pacific island colonies**: the Marshall Islands, Caroline Islands, and Mariana Islands
- Japan also attacks and captures **Tsingtao (Qingdao)**, Germany's fortified naval base in China
- Japan positions itself to demand major concessions from a weakened China, setting the stage for the **Twenty-One Demands** in 1915

*This document covers the period 1880–1914. Japan's actions after 1914, including the Twenty-One Demands, the interwar period, and the Second Sino-Japanese War, represent a continuation and escalation of the imperial patterns established in this era.*
