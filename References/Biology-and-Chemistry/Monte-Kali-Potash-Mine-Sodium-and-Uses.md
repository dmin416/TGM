## Location of the Mine

**Monte Kali** (also called "Kalimanjaro") is located near the town of **Heringen, Hesse**, in central Germany, in the **Werra-Fulda region**. The broader mining district spans parts of Hesse and Thuringia, close to the former inner German border.

The region is sometimes called **"Land der weißen Berge"** (Land of the White Mountains) due to the multiple salt dumps in the area.

The salt mountain itself:
- Rises over 250 metres above the surrounding land
- Reaches 520 metres above sea level at its summit
- Covers more than 98 hectares (about 114 football fields)
- Contains approximately 200+ million tonnes of salt
- Grows by around 900 tonnes every hour via a 1.5 km conveyor belt

## History of Mining at the Site

- **1903:** Potash mining begins in the region, originally by the company Wintershall
- **1976:** The Monte Kali salt dump begins accumulating as a byproduct of intensified potash extraction
- **2020:** K+S (Kali und Salz), the current operator, receives approval to expand Monte Kali by 25 hectares
- **2060:** K+S operating licence runs until this year

By 1913, the Werra potash district was already a decade into operation and was a strategically vital industry. Germany was the dominant global supplier of potash before and during World War I.

## Sodium Available from the Site

The mining process extracts **potash (potassium salts)** from underground deposits. The byproduct is enormous quantities of **sodium chloride (NaCl)**, common table salt, which is dumped to form Monte Kali.

From sodium chloride, **sodium metal** can be extracted via the **Downs Process**:
- NaCl is heated to molten state (~800°C)
- Electrical current passes through it (electrolysis)
- Sodium metal collects at the cathode
- Chlorine gas is released at the anode

This requires industrial-scale equipment and is carried out in specialist chemical facilities.

## Uses of Sodium

### Industrial Uses
- Street lighting: sodium vapour lamps produce the characteristic orange glow
- Heat exchange fluid in some nuclear reactors
- Manufacturing of titanium and other metals
- Production of sodium compounds (sodium peroxide, sodium hydride)

### Medical & Biological Uses
- Essential electrolyte: the human body cannot function without it
- IV saline solutions (one of the most widely used treatments in medicine)
- Sodium bicarbonate treats acid buildup in blood and digestive conditions
- Sodium hydroxide used in manufacturing many pharmaceuticals

---

## Uses of Chloride / Chlorine

### Industrial
- Water purification: saves millions of lives globally
- PVC and medical-grade plastic tubing and equipment
- Antiseptics and disinfectants

### Pharmaceutical
Chlorine chemistry is involved in the production of roughly **85% of all pharmaceuticals** at some stage. Medicines that rely on chlorine compounds include:

- Ibuprofen
- Amoxicillin
- Antihistamines
- Certain antidepressants
- Some chemotherapy drugs
- Chloroquine (malaria treatment)

---

## Uses of Sodium Chloride Itself (NaCl)

- Wound irrigation
- Rehydration therapy
- Preserving donor organs for transplant
- Nasal rinses
- Food preservation
- Road de-icing
- Industrial chemical feedstock

---

## Environmental Notes

The growing salt dump has caused the nearby **Werra River** to become saline, and groundwater in the area has been affected. Of the 60 to 100 invertebrate species once native to the area around Heringen, only 3 remain. The site remains controversial but economically vital, supporting several thousand jobs in the region.
