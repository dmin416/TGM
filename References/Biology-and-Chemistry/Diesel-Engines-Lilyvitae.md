# Diesel Engines: History, Limits and Lilyvitae Improvements

Rough draft. OTL diesel development, conventional efficiency ceilings, and projected gains from Lilyvitae material forms. Planning reference; not story canon until cited. Material properties: [Lilyvitae.md](Lilyvitae.md).

## Conventional Efficiency Ceiling

- Carnot limit at 2,500 K combustion / 300 K rejection: **88%**
- Best large slow speed diesel without waste heat recovery: **50 to 52%**
- Best with full waste heat recovery systems: **55 to 57%**
- High speed automotive best efficiency point: **46 to 48%**

## Conventional Loss Categories

| Loss Source | Approximate Share of Fuel Energy Lost |
|---|---|
| Cylinder wall heat transfer | 25 to 30% |
| Exhaust enthalpy | 30 to 35% |
| Friction (rings, bearings, valves) | 5 to 10% |
| Pumping losses | 2 to 5% |
| Incomplete combustion | 1 to 3% |

---

## Cleanliness vs Efficiency Conflict

- High temperature improves efficiency and increases NOx simultaneously
- Retarded injection timing cuts NOx but reduces efficiency
- EGR lowers combustion temperature but raises particulate and cuts efficiency
- SCR (2004 trucks, 2008 cars) breaks the NOx/efficiency tradeoff via exhaust treatment
- Euro 6d limits: 0.002 g/km particulate, 0.06 g/km NOx
- Every emissions strategy trades efficiency against cleanliness or vice versa without aftertreatment

## Lilyvitae Material Improvements

### Compression Ratio: Bark Form

- Steel limits practical compression to ~23:1 before structural failure
- Bark compressive strength: 85,000 MPa vs steel's 250 MPa
- Compression ratio becomes design-limited, not materials-limited
- At 50:1 compression ratio alone reaches ~72% efficiency
- At 100:1 approaches ~77%

### Cylinder Wall Heat Loss: Moss Form

- Conventional wall losses: 25 to 30% of fuel energy
- Moss low density: 0.010 W/m·K, better than aerogel
- Approaches true adiabatic operation
- Eliminating wall loss alone pushes efficiency from 52% toward 65 to 68%

### Friction: Vine and Bark Forms

- Bark bearing surfaces at 8,500 HV eliminate wear at any contact pressure
- Vine internal resin provides permanent self-lubrication
- Friction losses approach zero across the full engine

### Turbocharger and Intercooler: Grass and Moss Forms

- Steel turbine inlets limited to 600 to 750°C by alloy limits
- Grass and bark turbine components face no meaningful temperature ceiling
- Moss high density at 480 W/m·K outperforms copper by 25%
- Perfect intercooling approaches isothermal compression

### Waste Heat Recovery: Scroll Form

- Conventional systems capture 30 to 40% of exhaust energy
- Scroll superconducting generators convert recovered heat with zero resistance loss
- Moss exchangers extract exhaust and jacket water at near-perfect efficiency
- Combined recovery captures 85 to 90% of exhaust energy

### Sealing: Bark and Sap Forms

- Bark ring faces at 8,500 HV never wear against any bore
- Sap form seals strengthen under pressure differential
