## 1913 Technology Baseline

The world's technology in 1913 is equivalent in development level to real-world 1913, built on different foundations.

**Transport and Power**

- Steam dominant for ships, railways, and heavy industry
- Luminite slurry IC engines at equivalent stage to real-world 1913 gasoline engines: rough, unreliable, improving
- Early aviation (biplane equivalent, limited range and payload)
- Horse transport still common in civilian and military use
- Castor oil is the primary lubricant: known industry pain point, inadequate for sustained high-heat use

**Communications**

- Telegraph well established
- Telephone in major cities
- Radio at Marconi-equivalent stage: spark gap transmitters; no continuous wave; no AM broadcasting; no superheterodyne receivers
- Early vacuum tubes (triode equivalent exists)
- No radar
- No practical induction or wireless power charging
- Tesla coil exists (1891); rotating magnetic field and AC motor established; Wardenclyffe-type wireless power transmission theorized but not achieved
- Tesla bladeless turbine patented 1913: exists but not yet industrially adopted

**Military**

- Bolt-action rifles (Mauser equivalent)
- Maxim-equivalent machine guns
- Field artillery and howitzers at 1913 real-world level
- Early submarines
- No steel helmets: cloth and leather only
- No tanks
- Smokeless powder (coal-tar derived) replacing black powder

**Materials and Chemistry**

- Bessemer and open hearth steel
- No stainless steel
- Coal-tar chemistry exists but limited: no byproduct coking recovery
- Early Bakelite equivalents from coal-tar phenolics (limited feedstock)
- Haber-Bosch nitrogen fixation operational
- Natural rubber only: no synthetic equivalent yet
- Early ester chemistry exists but not optimized for industrial application
- Borosilicate glass exists in early laboratory form (Schott 1887) but not industrially optimized
- No tempered glass industrial process
- No superfest glass
- Byproduct coking ovens do not exist: beehive coke ovens burn off all volatile fractions as waste
- Anaerobic digestion of organic waste for biogas does not exist at industrial scale
- Wood distillation producing methanol, acetone, and wood tar exists as a small industrial sector at 1913 scale
- Turpentine from pine resin processing is an established solvent trade at 1913 scale

**Medicine**

- No antibiotics
- Early vaccines (smallpox, typhoid)
- Aspirin equivalents, morphine, chloroform anesthesia
- Germ theory established and accepted
- Blood types known since 1901 but transfusion practice still primitive and dangerous
- Water chlorination exists in very limited municipal applications but is primitive and produces chemical byproducts: Max bypasses it entirely
- No UV or ozone water purification
- No multi-stage activated carbon filtration systems
- Dental hygiene is poor: toothbrushes exist but technique, flossing, and effective formulations are not standardized or widely practiced

**Manufacturing**

- Machine tools at 1913 real-world level
- Arc welding exists but primitive electrode chemistry
- No standardized unified screw thread system
- No assembly line manufacturing
- Precision measurement tools at 1913 real-world level

## Luminite

- Petroleum solidifies into a stable solid hydrocarbon on contact with air
- Known as luminite; mined from surface and near-surface deposits like coal
- Burns hotter and cleaner than coal; combustion byproducts are CO₂, water vapor, and carbon soot
- Used as a finely ground slurry suspended in a carrier fluid for engines and furnaces
- Carrier fluid development and synthetic lubricant development are the same problem
- Castor oil is the current best carrier; ester synthesis is the known next step but not yet achieved at industrial scale
- Luminite deposits follow real-world petroleum geography
- Austria-Hungary holds significant luminite deposits in Galicia
- Fine particle milling below a critical threshold produces near-complete combustion with negligible soot: not yet achieved industrially
- Luminite fractionation (partial re-dissolution under pressure and heat) separates it into lighter and heavier combustion fractions: theoretically understood but not yet operational
- Luminite engines at 1913 level require periodic carbon scrubbing and are prone to injector fouling

## Energy Context

- Coal: naturally occurring sedimentary rock; contains sulfur, nitrogen, mineral ash, and volatile compounds depending on grade; lignite through bituminous to anthracite
- Charcoal: man-made processed carbon; wood heated in low-oxygen environment drives off water and volatiles; burns hotter and cleaner than coal; used in precision smelting; distinct from coal in origin, impurity profile, and combustion behavior
- Luminite slurry is the primary mobile fuel
- No petroleum-derived products in any form
