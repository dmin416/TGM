## Ancient China

- Silk cultivation and weaving: legendary origin approximately 2700 BCE, verified approximately 3000 BCE
- Bronze casting at scale: Shang dynasty, approximately 1600 BCE
- Cast iron: approximately 500 BCE, nearly two thousand years before Europe
- Kite: approximately 500 BCE
- Crossbow with trigger mechanism: approximately 400 BCE
- Seismograph: Zhang Heng, 132 CE
- Paper: Cai Lun, 105 CE
- Porcelain: Han dynasty onward, refined Tang and Song dynasties
- Mechanical clock escapement: Yi Xing, 725 CE
- Woodblock printing: Tang dynasty, approximately 600 CE
- Gunpowder: Tang dynasty, approximately 850 CE
- Paper money: Song dynasty, approximately 1000 CE
- Navigational compass: Song dynasty, approximately 1000 CE
- Movable type: Bi Sheng, 1040 CE
- Mechanical spinning machine: Song dynasty, approximately 1000 CE
- Suspension bridge: ancient, refined Song dynasty
- Grand Canal engineering: Sui dynasty, 605 CE

## Ancient India

- Indus Valley urban sewage and plumbing: approximately 2500 BCE
- Wootz crucible steel, predecessor to Damascus steel: approximately 300 BCE
- Zero as positional mathematical concept: Brahmagupta, 628 CE
- Decimal positional number system: Indian mathematicians, approximately 500 CE
- Algebra and quadratic equation solutions: Brahmagupta, 628 CE
- Chess: approximately 600 CE
- Yoga systematic practice: codified Patanjali, approximately 400 CE
- Ayurvedic medicine system: approximately 600 BCE onward
- Cotton textile weaving at industrial scale: Indus Valley, approximately 3000 BCE
- Rhinoplasty reconstructive surgery: Sushruta, approximately 600 BCE

## Notes

Korea's metal movable type predates Gutenberg by over two centuries and is almost entirely absent from Western historical awareness. The Jikji printed 1377 using metal type is the oldest surviving metal type printed book and sits in the Bibliothèque nationale de France.

China's cast iron achievement represents roughly nineteen hundred years of metallurgical advantage over Europe. Cast iron appears in China approximately 500 BCE. Europe achieves it approximately 1380 CE. The implications for construction, agriculture, weapons and tools during that gap are incalculable.

India's zero and decimal system is the foundation of all modern mathematics and computing. Every calculation performed on every computer on earth uses the Indian positional decimal system. The compensation paid to India for this contribution is zero.
