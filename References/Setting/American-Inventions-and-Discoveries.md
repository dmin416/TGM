Indigenous Mesoamerica, Andes, and North America; modern Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Caribbean. ## Ancient Mesoamerica

- Maize domestication from teosinte grass: Mexico, approximately 7000 BCE
- Cacao cultivation and chocolate preparation: Olmec and Maya, approximately 1900 BCE
- Zero as mathematical concept independent of India: Maya, approximately 350 CE
- Maya Long Count calendar and astronomical precision: approximately 200 BCE onward
- Maya corbelled arch construction: approximately 300 CE
- Rubber processing from latex: Olmec, approximately 1600 BCE
- Vulcanization like rubber treatment: Mesoamerican peoples, approximately 1600 BCE
- Ball game with rubber ball: Olmec, approximately 1600 BCE
- Obsidian blade surgical edge: Mesoamerican cultures, ancient
- Popcorn: Mexico, approximately 3600 BCE
- Tomato cultivation: Mexico, approximately 500 BCE
- Vanilla cultivation: Mexico, approximately 1400 CE
- Chili pepper cultivation: Mexico, approximately 6000 BCE

## Contributions to Global Food Supply

The Americas contributed more to global caloric intake than any other region after contact. The following crops now feed the majority of humanity and originated entirely in the Americas:

- Maize: now third largest caloric crop globally
- Potato: now fourth largest caloric crop globally
- Cassava: major caloric staple across Africa and Asia
- Tomato: foundational to European and Asian cuisines
- Chili pepper: foundational to Asian, African and European cuisines
- Cacao: global confectionery industry
- Vanilla: global flavoring industry
- Peanut: major protein source across Africa and Asia
- Sunflower: major oil crop globally
- Squash and pumpkin: global cultivation
- Sweet potato: major crop across Africa and Asia
- Turkey: poultry industry contribution
- Rubber: entire industrial revolution depended on it

## Notes

Quinine from Andean cinchona bark enabled European colonialism in tropical Africa and Asia by making malaria survivable. Without Andean indigenous pharmacological knowledge identifying this plant, British and French colonial expansion into malaria zones would have been far more costly and potentially impossible at scale. The populations whose knowledge made this possible received nothing.

The potato alone transformed European demographic history. Irish, German and Russian population explosions in the 1700s and 1800s were substantially driven by caloric density and cold climate adaptability of the Andean potato. Every European alive today descends partly from people who survived on Andean agricultural knowledge.

Argentina's fingerprint identification system became the global standard for criminal identification and is the direct ancestor of modern biometric security used in every phone, border crossing and forensic laboratory on earth.
