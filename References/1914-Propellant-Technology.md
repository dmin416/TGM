# 1914 Propellant Technology: Capabilities and Ceiling

Reference for *Trenches, Guns and Magic*. What German industry and a well funded research program can realistically reach in 1914, and what remains out of reach until later manufacturing and instrumentation exist.

---

## What 1914 Technology Can Produce

### Achievable

- Single base nitrocellulose: fully understood and in production
- Double base (nitrocellulose + nitroglycerine): available; Ballistite already existed
- Centralite stabilizer: synthesizable with 1914 organic chemistry; Germany capable
- Basic deterrent coatings: functional versions achievable, not modern precision
- Diethylene glycol dinitrate: Germany already experimenting successfully
- Nitroguanidine (triple base): first synthesized 1877, chemistry known, but practical propellant application requires deliberate concentrated research effort to achieve

### Not Achievable

- Precision granule geometry at scale: 1914 manufacturing tolerances insufficient for modern ball powder consistency
- Modern temperature stabilizing packages: key stabilizers not yet synthesized
- Lot to lot consistency at modern levels: quality control instrumentation does not exist

---

## Realistic Best Case for a Well Funded 1914 German Program

| Achievement | Feasibility |
|---|---|
| Centralite stabilized double base powder | Fully achievable |
| Meaningful deterrent coating | Achievable with effort |
| Reduced barrel erosion approaching WWII levels | Achievable |
| Basic nitroguanidine triple base | Theoretically possible, practically very difficult |
| Modern ball powder geometry | Not achievable (manufacturing limits) |
| Modern temperature stability | Partially achievable |

| German Gudol, late WWI | ~60 to 65% |

---

## Hard Ceiling

The practical ceiling for 1914 is approximately **70 to 75% of modern powder performance**: roughly late WWII German propellant quality achieved early through concentrated effort. The barrier is manufacturing precision and analytical instrumentation. Granule level engineering is not possible without measurement tools to detect and control what is being made, and those tools do not exist in 1914.
