# German Military Structure (1914)

Reference: Imperial German Army (*Deutsches Heer*) at **late January 1914** — peacetime footing, pre–World War I offensive doctrine still dominant. Bavarian, Saxon, and Württemberg contingents followed the same general pattern with state-specific nuances.

## Supreme command

- **Kaiser Wilhelm II** — Supreme War Lord (*Oberster Kriegsherr*).
- **Great General Staff** (*Großer Generalstab*) — Chief: **Helmuth von Moltke (the Younger)**; operational planning, mobilization timetables, deployment plans.
- **Prussian War Ministry** (*Preußisches Kriegsministerium*) — Administration, supply, personnel, budgets; in this period distinct from the General Staff’s operational role.

## Unit hierarchy

| Unit | Commander | Strength | Composition |
|------|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Army** | General der Infanterie+ | Variable | 2+ corps + army troops |
| **Corps** | General der Infanterie | ~44,000 | 2 divisions + corps troops |
| **Division** | Generalleutnant | ~17,500 | 2 brigades (4 regiments) + 1 cavalry regt + 1 artillery brigade |
| **Regiment** | Oberst | ~3,000 | 3 battalions + 1 MG company |
| **Battalion** | Major | ~1,000 | 4 companies |
| **Company** | Hauptmann | ~250 | 4 platoons |
| **Platoon** | Leutnant / Oberleutnant | ~60 | 4 squads |
| **Squad** | Unteroffizier | ~15 | 2 half-squads (sections) |

---

## Division organization (standard)

- 4 infantry regiments (2 brigades × 2 regiments).
- 1 cavalry regiment (4 squadrons, ~600 men).
- 1 field artillery brigade (2 regiments, 72 × 77 mm FK 96 n/A guns).
- 1 pioneer company.
- Communications, medical, supply, veterinary units.

---

## Regiment structure

- **Staff:** Colonel, adjutant, doctor, quartermaster, paymaster.
- **Combat:** 3 battalions (I, II, III).
- **Support:** 1 MG company (6 × MG 08, ~150 men).
- **Trains:** Supply, kitchens, ammunition, medical.

---

## Firepower

- **Squad:** 15 × Gewehr 98 rifles, bayonets, 120–150 rounds per man.
- **Platoon:** ~56 rifles.
- **Company:** ~240 rifles.
- **Battalion:** ~800 rifles, 0–2 MG 08.
- **Regiment:** ~2,400 rifles, 6 MG 08.

| Grade | Equivalent | Role |
|-------|------------|------|
| Gefreiter | Lance corporal | Section leader assistant |
| Unteroffizier | Corporal | Squad leader |
| Unterfeldwebel | Sergeant | Junior platoon sergeant |
| Feldwebel | Staff sergeant | Senior platoon / company sergeant |
| Vizefeldwebel | Sergeant major | Officer-candidate, platoon command |
| Feldwebelleutnant | Warrant officer | Senior NCO, near-officer status |

**Note:** NCOs were career professionals, trained in *Unteroffizierschulen*, and could command independently when officers fell.

## Officer ranks and commands

| Rank | German | Command | Years to reach (typical) |
|------|--------|---------|--------------------------|
| 2nd lieutenant | Leutnant | Platoon (~60) | Entry |
| 1st lieutenant | Oberleutnant | Platoon / deputy company (~60) | 4–6 |
| Captain | Hauptmann | Company (~250) | 6–10 |
| Major | Major | Battalion (~1,000) | 10–15+ |
| Lt. colonel | Oberstleutnant | Battalion / deputy regiment | 15–20+ |
| Colonel | Oberst | Regiment (~3,000) | 20–25+ |

**General Staff:** ~160 officers per year from the *Kriegsakademie* (three-year War Academy); crimson-striped trousers (*Kragenspiegel* / parade distinction); rotation between staff and line command.

## Support services

- **Pioneer battalion:** 4 companies, ~1,000 men — fortifications, bridges, demolitions.
- **Communications:** Telegraph, telephone, signals, pigeons, motorcycle dispatch.
- **Medical:** Field hospitals, ambulances, veterinary services.
- **Supply:** Ammunition, food, bakeries, butcheries, remounts.

---

## Reserve system

| Category | Age | Status | Equipment |
|----------|-----|--------|-----------|
| **Reserve** | <32 | Completed active service | Same as active |
| **Landwehr** | 32–39 | Territorial force | Older equipment |
| **Landsturm** | 17–45 | Home guard | Mixed |

**Mirror system:** Active regiment 33 → reserve regiment 33 (same number, parallel organization).

---

## Mobilization strength

### Peacetime (January 1914)

- 25 active corps.
- 50 infantry divisions.
- 11 cavalry divisions.
- ~800,000 men.

### Mobilized (August 1914)

- 25 active corps + 14 reserve corps + 11 *Landwehr* corps (partial).
- ~87 divisions.
- ~3,800,000 men.
- **Western Front:** 7 armies, ~1,500,000 initially.
- **Eastern Front:** 8th Army, ~200,000 initially.

---

## Peacetime distribution

- **I–XXI Corps:** Prussian.
- **I–III Bavarian Corps.**
- **XII–XIII Corps:** Saxon.
- **XIV–XV Corps:** Baden and Württemberg.

---

## Combat doctrine (January 1914)

- **Offensive:** “Attack with the bayonet,” fire and movement, skirmish lines.
- **Defensive:** Immediate counterattack, forward defense, limited entrenchment.
- ***Auftragstaktik*:** Mission tactics — objective assigned, execution left to subordinates.
- **Fire discipline:** Volley fire at company / platoon level; aimed shots emphasized.
- **Attack formation:** Skirmish lines (3–5 paces) → support lines (100–200 m) → reserve.

---

## Key characteristics

- NCO corps = institutional backbone; independent tactical capability.
- Officer corps = Junker and bourgeois mix; *Kriegsakademie* elite; technically educated.
- Company = smallest independent command (*Hauptmann* first true autonomy).
- Battalion = smallest combined-arms element; can operate on its own axis.
- Major = first level of true operational independence.
- Machine guns present but not yet fully appreciated as decisive (1914 perspective).
