## Detailed summaries

### 1. `1914-Propellant-Technology.md`

Technical ceiling reference for German propellant chemistry and manufacturing in 1914. Separates what a well funded program can achieve (centralite stabilized double base, deterrent coatings, partial nitroguanidine triple base, erosion reduction) from what is impossible without modern instrumentation (precision ball powder geometry, full temperature stability packages, lot consistency). Includes a proximity table from black powder through postwar powders; states the realistic 1914 best case at roughly 70–75% of modern performance, comparable to late WWII German quality reached early through concentrated effort.

### 2. `Alliances-outline-with-source-coverage.md`

Editorial map of alliance blocs (Spanish Civil War Jan–Mar 1914, August 1914 world war, later phases) tied to upstream `Source/*.md` chapter indices. Uses stand in nation names from the source novel (Saxon block for Germany, Britannia for Britain, etc.). Rules: notes only where source already mentions a beat, no plot spoilers, ripgrep hints for exhaustive coverage. Anchors pervasive threads (e.g. Saxon block) to sample chapters instead of listing hundreds of paths. Primary use: find where upstream material supports a faction beat before adapting it in `Story/`.

### 3. `Alternate-History-Baseline.md`

Rough draft, not strict canon until tied to story beats. Max's omniscient tech knowledge, patent and reinvestment rules, world divergence (January 1914 start, Central Powers victory), Germany's bloc (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Spain), 1913 technology floor by domain, Luminite and energy context, magic split (Britain/France magitech vs German conventional focus; Max patents conventional only), financial baseline (signing bonus, three-nation GDP, treaty royalties, strategic premium). Cross links [Market-Crash-Wealth-Reference.md](Market-Crash-Wealth-Reference.md), [Notes-for-the-Future.md](Notes-for-the-Future.md).

### 4. `Alternate-History-Story-Ideas.md`

Rough long arc brainstorming, not strict canon. Frames a reformed Germany allied with Republican Spain and Korea against British, French, Italian, and Russian power blocs, with a corrupt Catholic Church as ideological antagonist and Protestant plus Teutonic Order response. Develops moral gradients (deceived peasant to cynical cardinal), three “demonic” frameworks (literal, functional, symbolic), alliance tensions inside the Protestant side, antagonist factions, character and structural hooks (excommunication as weapon, forged prophecy, child pope), and central themes (institutional corruption, cost of victory). Cross links [Notes-for-the-Future.md](Notes-for-the-Future.md), [Glossary.md](Glossary.md), [Market-Crash-Wealth-Reference.md](Market-Crash-Wealth-Reference.md).

### 5. `Countries.md`

January 1914 ranked list of sovereign states and British dominions by combined global importance, state power, and population. **TGM overrides OTL** where noted: Romanov collapse 1913 and fragmented Russia; independent Korean Empire with German alignment; Spain retaining Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam, Philippines; peninsular Spanish Civil War context. Explains scope (listed vs colonies), de facto states, and Entente habit of saying “Russia” without a unitary empire. Top entries: UK, Germany, USA, fragmented former Russian Empire, France, Austria-Hungary, Japan, Korea, Italy, Ottoman Empire, then descending through world states.

### 6. `Christian-Denominations-and-Religions.md`

Rough draft. 1913 faith landscape: Lutheran Protestantism as TGM true faith (sola scriptura, faith, grace), Roman Catholicism, Anglicanism, Eastern Orthodoxy; world religions (Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism, Taoism, Confucianism, Shinto, Zoroastrianism, Jainism); cults and fringe movements active in period (Mormonism, Bible Students, Christian Science, Adventism, Theosophy, Golden Dawn, Thelema, Spiritualism, Bahá'í, Völkisch, Russian sectarians). Cross links [Imperial-Judicial-System.md](Imperial-Judicial-System.md), [Notes-for-the-Future.md](Notes-for-the-Future.md), [Alternate-History-Story-Ideas.md](Alternate-History-Story-Ideas.md).

### 7. `German-Armored-Airship-Armament.md`

Setting canon for Imperial German **L class** armored airships (L28–L32, including **L30** in Seville). Main battery: four twin turrets, eight **210 mm** barrels (Krupp family, deck adapted). Distinguishes this from Heer divisional artillery in [German-Artillery-1914.md](German-Artillery-1914.md). Doctrine: bombardment, fortress breach, magitech spotting rounds; secondaries MG08, bombs, loudspeakers. Story anchor: `Story/16.md`. Upstream source is not edited manuscript; references and story use 210 mm only.

### 8. `German-Artillery-1914.md`

August 1914 **Heer** conventional artillery inventory: divisional 77 mm FK 96 n.A. and 10.5 cm leFH 98/09; corps 150 mm and 130 mm; foot 21 cm Mörser siege lines; super heavy 28 cm, 30.5 cm Austrian, 42 cm Gamma and M-Gerät for Belgian forts. Notes scale (~5,000 field guns, ~6,000 total mobilized), tactical advantage of German divisional howitzers in 1914, and that 21 cm did most siege work while Big Bertha was rare.

### 9. `German-Military-Calibers.md`

Ballistic planning table: 1913 historical cartridges (9 mm, 7.92 mm, 13.2 mm TuF, 20 mm) vs **Max driven 1914 program** (6 mm rifle, 7 mm DMR/MG, 9×87 mm sniper, 13 mm HMG, improved 20 mm) with approximate muzzle energies and effective ranges. Links propellant and armor references for consistency when Max introduces new small arms.

### 22. `Prose-Mechanics.md`

Mandatory style rules for edited prose. Pre flight: verify weapons, names, places against references; correct source historical errors. Punctuation: no hyphens or em dashes, no Oxford comma, no comma before and/but/or in parallel lists, no colons in dialogue. Minimum four words per sentence; paragraph openers must name subject; consecutive sentences use pronoun on second. No new similes. Layout: dialogue and thoughts on own lines, sounds as bold onomatopoeia, system messages in code blocks, no blank lines inside narrative paragraphs. Preservation: do not shorten source sentences; tight third on Max with occasional other POV.
