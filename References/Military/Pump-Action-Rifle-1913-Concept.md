## The Core Concept

A pump action rifle chambered in 7.92×57 Mauser, with a rotating bolt and detachable box magazine, built in 1913 with future knowledge. Mechanically achievable with existing technology.

---

## Why Pump Action Over Bolt Action

- Pump is faster for follow-up shots: two hand movements vs four for bolt
- Support hand never leaves the gun
- Simpler under stress than a lever action
- Works better from prone than lever action
- Reliable in dirt and adverse conditions: no gas system to foul
- Faster than bolt, more reliable than semi-auto

---

## Rotating Bolt

- Same fundamental design as the AK-47 and M1 Garand
- Bolt rotates ~35 degrees, locking lugs engage recesses in the trunnion
- The pump stroke cams the bolt open and closed, replacing the gas piston
- Strong, secure lockup: handles high pressure cartridges well
- Symmetrical lockup promotes accuracy
- German manufacturing in 1913 was world-class: Mauser Werke already producing tight-tolerance bolt actions
- Conceptually ahead of its time but physically achievable

---

## The 7.92×57 Mauser Cartridge

- Rimless bottleneck: ideal for box magazine feeding, no rim-stacking issues
- Base diameter: 11.94mm
- Overall length: 82mm
- Max pressure: ~390 MPa (56,500 PSI): rotating bolt handles this fine
- Spitzer bullet tip makes tubular magazines dangerous: box magazine solves this entirely
- Already proven in 20-round double-stack machine gun magazines (ZB-26, MG-34)

---

## Magazine

- **Single stack:** 10 rounds, simple and reliable
- **Double stack:** 20 rounds realistic: staggered feed, ~26mm wide
- Detachable box = fast swap vs slow tube top-off
- Germans already used 20-round 7.92×57 box mags in WWI/WWII machine guns: proven design
- Rimless case stacks cleanly with no interference

---

## Recoil Management

- Butt planted on ground at ~60 degrees: Earth absorbs recoil, not the shooter
- Recoil vector travels down the stock into the ground
- Shooter holds position, feels minimal kick
- Practical for firing upward at elevated targets: trenches, parapets, elevated positions
- Slam fire equivalent: rapid pump cycling without shouldering = sustained fire with low fatigue

---

## Comparison to What Existed in 1913

| Feature | Gewehr 98 | This Concept |
|---|---|---|
| Action | Bolt (4 movements) | Pump (2 movements) |
| Magazine | 5-round internal stripper | 20-round detachable box |
| Reload | Stripper clip, slow | Magazine swap, fast |
| Bolt type | Rotating (manual handle) | Rotating (pump-driven) |
| Rate of fire | ~15 rpm trained | ~60 to 80 rpm trained |

---

## Why Germany Didn't Do This

- Doctrine locked into bolt action precision at range
- Machine gun (MG08) was the German answer to volume of fire
- Conservative military bureaucracy
- No conceptual framework connecting pump stroke to rotating bolt cycling
- Manufacturing complexity = cost
- Proof it was achievable: Americans showed up with the Model 1897 shotgun and devastated German troops in trenches. Germans petitioned to ban it as a war crime
