# German Leutnant, 1913: Battle Kit

Typical **second lieutenant** field and campaign load for the Imperial German Army on the eve of the Great War. Officers purchased almost all of this privately; regulation listed items, the officer paid. See also [German-Heer-Privileges-Pageantry-and-Leutnant-1914.md](German-Heer-Privileges-Pageantry-and-Leutnant-1914.md) for mess culture, *Bursche* practice, and social rank. Enlisted baseline: [Military.md](Military.md) (Late January 1914 baseline kit).

**Related:** [Glossary.md](../Setting/Glossary.md) (Luger P08) · [Weapons-vs-Armor-Reference.md](Weapons-vs-Armor-Reference.md)

---

## Uniform and personal equipment

- **Feldgrau tunic (*Waffenrock* / *Feldrock*)** — wool, branch-color collar patches and shoulder straps showing rank
- **Schirmmütze** — peaked field cap with branch-color band and cockade
- **Pickelhaube** — leather spiked helmet with brass fittings (field cover added in wartime)
- **Breeches** — field grey, worn with leather knee boots or ankle boots with puttees
- **Leather belt** — officer's pattern, brown leather with brass or steel buckle
- **Braces / suspenders** — supporting the belt and equipment load
- **Wool greatcoat** — double-breasted, field grey
- **Gloves** — leather, brown

---

## Personal weapons

- **Pistole 08 (Luger P08)** — standard officer sidearm from 1908 onward; 9mm Parabellum, 8-round magazine
- **Pistol holster** — brown leather, right hip, with magazine pouch attached
- **Degen or Säbel** — officers still carried a sword on parade; increasingly left behind in actual field service by 1913–14
- **Feldmesser** — utility knife / fighting knife, privately purchased by many officers
- Some officers privately purchased a **second pistol** (Mauser C96 was popular)

---

## Field equipment

**Mapcase (*Kartentasche*)** — brown leather, shoulder strap; typically contained:

- Topographic maps of the operational area
- Compass (usually prismatic or lensatic type)
- Pencils and grease pencils for map marking
- Notebook / field journal

**Other:**

- **Binoculars (*Fernglas*)** — 6× or 8×, leather case on neck strap; essential for a lieutenant commanding a platoon
- **Whistle** — signaling troop movement and assault
- **Watch** — pocket or early wristwatch; wristwatches spreading rapidly among officers by 1913

---

## Sustenance and survival

- **Feldflasche** — canteen, aluminum or enameled steel, felt cover and leather strap
- **Essgeschirr** — nested two-piece mess tin for field cooking
- **Brotbeutel** — canvas bread bag, right hip; often held:
  - Iron ration (*Eiserne Portion*): hardtack, tinned meat, salt, coffee concentrate
  - Personal food supplements, tobacco, personal items
- **Kochgeschirr** — smaller personal cookpot, often shared with orderly
- **Schnapsflasche** — small spirits flask; common private purchase

---

## Medical and personal

- **Verbandpäckchen** — individual field dressing, tunic pocket
- **Gamaschen / puttees** — leg wrapping if not wearing tall boots
- **Spare socks and underlinen** — in the pack or sent ahead with baggage
- **Razor, soap, toothbrush** — small leather toiletry roll

---

## Command and communication tools

- **Signal whistle** — platoon-level commands
- **Leuchtpistole (flare pistol)** — not universal in 1913 but increasingly issued for signaling
- **Written orders** — carried in the mapcase, often memorized then destroyed
- **Notebook** — orders, casualty lists, supply requests

---

## What the orderly (*Bursche*) carried for him

Officers rated a personal orderly who managed:

- The officer's pack and spare kit
- Cleaning supplies for weapons and boots
- Additional rations

Official term *Ordonnanz*; colloquially *Bursche* or *Putzer*. See [German-Heer-Privileges-Pageantry-and-Leutnant-1914.md](German-Heer-Privileges-Pageantry-and-Leutnant-1914.md).
