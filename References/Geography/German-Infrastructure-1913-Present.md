# German Infrastructure — 1913 vs Present

OTL civil engineering reference for Germany: materials, networks, and standards at the 1913 baseline and at modern (2026) capability, with a chronological road development section between those endpoints. Use for scene dressing, Max's future knowledge pacing, and contrast with in setting Luminite industry. ## Rail

### 1913

- **Network length:** ~63,000 km; densest rail network on the continent
- **Rail material:** Steel rail, ~30–45 kg per meter weight, laid in sections bolted at fishplated joints
- **Sleepers (ties):** Untreated or creosote impregnated hardwood, primarily oak and beech
- **Ballast:** Crushed granite or gravel, hand tamped
- **Track gauge:** Standard 1,435 mm throughout the main network
- **Welding:** None; rail sections joined mechanically, producing the characteristic rhythmic clack
- **Signaling:** Mechanical semaphore arms, controlled by telegraph and block section telephone, line of sight operation
- **Traction:** Steam locomotives burning coal; no electrification on main lines
- **Bridges:** Wrought iron and steel plate girder on stone or brick masonry piers
- **Tunnels:** Brick lined with lime mortar, hand excavated

### 2026

- **Network length:** ~33,000 km; reduced but far heavier in usage and speed capability
- **Rail material:** UIC60 steel rail at 60 kg per meter, thermite welded into continuous lengths of several kilometers; no joints, no clack
- **Sleepers:** Pre stressed concrete monoblock sleepers as standard; wood retained only on heritage and branch lines
- **Ballast:** Machine tamped crushed granite to millimeter tolerances; slab track (concrete bed, no ballast) on high speed lines
- **High speed network:** ICE lines rated 250–300 km/h; dedicated alignment with long curves and minimal grade
- **Signaling:** ETCS digital cab signaling; no lineside signals on high speed routes; trains receive movement authority by radio
- **Traction:** 15 kV 16.7 Hz AC overhead catenary throughout the main network; electric locomotives and multiple units
- **Bridges:** Pre stressed and post tensioned concrete; structural steel box girder on major spans

## Road

### 1913 baseline

- **Urban streets:** Granite sett (cobblestone) paving; hand cut stone blocks bedded in sand on a rubble or broken brick base; durable under iron shod horse hooves; loud and difficult for pneumatic tyres
- **Secondary urban:** Macadam; layers of compacted broken stone, graded smallest on top; dust prone in dry weather, mud in wet
- **Early tarmacadam:** Tar bound macadam appearing on premium urban routes from around 1900 onward; bound the surface and controlled dust
- **Rural roads:** Compacted earthen surface, gravel where available; impassable to motor vehicles in wet seasons
- **Design standard:** Horse drawn vehicle at walking pace; road crowns pitched to shed water to open ditches
- **Bridges:** Stone masonry arch on rural routes; iron and steel plate girder on heavier crossings
- **Motor vehicles nationally:** Approximately 60,000–70,000 registered automobiles in the entire country
- **Drainage:** Open stone gutters at curb; no subsurface storm collection on most routes; sheet flow across crowned surface into ditches

### Road surface evolution (1913–2026)

Chronological development of German road engineering from dirt tracks to low noise, drained modern pavements. Dates are approximate national trends; eastern Länder often lagged western standards until reunification.

#### 1910s–1920s: motor age begins on inherited surfaces

- **1913–1918:** War traffic destroys macadam and gravel; military priority on rail; civilian roads deteriorate
- **Post 1918:** Patch repair with tar spray on macadam; first systematic road numbering (1920s Reichsstraßen concept)
- **1920s:** Bitumen imports and domestic coal tar supply improve binders; **Asphaltic concrete** (hot mixed bitumen and graded aggregate) on urban arterials in Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, Cologne
- **1920s drainage:** Crown and ditch unchanged; occasional **curb and gutter** cast iron insets in rebuilt city blocks; no separate storm pipe network except in largest sewer combined systems
- **1927–1932:** HaFraBa (association for preparation of autobahn) studies; no national motorway yet, but design standards drafted (dual carriageway, grade separated crossings, 8 m lanes)

#### 1930s–1940s: autobahn prototype and wartime damage

- **1934 onward:** Reichsautobahn construction; **28 cm cement stabilized base** under **5–6 cm asphalt wearing course** on early sections (e.g. Frankfurt–Darmstadt, Cologne bypass)
- **1930s mix design:** Mechanically stabilized base course; bitumen penetration grades 80/100 common; hand spread and early finishers
- **1930s drainage:** **Side ditches** and **concrete U drains** along autobahn; **longitudinal slope** 2.5% typical; **transverse slope** 2% on carriageway; **no porous pavement**
- **1939–1945:** Strategic bombing and retreat traffic destroy bridges and urban pavements; rural roads return to gravel and mud in many districts

#### 1950s–1960s: Wirtschaftswunder reconstruction and Bundesautobahn expansion

- **1950s:** **Bundesautobahn** network formalized; **Asphalt concrete (AC)** wearing course standard on new build; **cement concrete (PCC)** still used on high stress bus stops and industrial yards
- **1950s binders:** **Penetration bitumen 180/200** then move toward **viscosity graded** material as refineries modernize
- **1950s–1960s base:** **Crushed stone base** and **cement treated base** over **compacted subgrade**; **proof rolled** with static rollers then vibratory
- **1950s drainage:** **Subsurface pipe drains** (French drains) under autobahn embankments; **gully pots** and **storm connections** in cities tied to **combined or separate** sewer programs
- **1960s urban:** Widespread **asphalt resurfacing** over wartime patched sett; sett retained in historic cores only

#### 1970s–1980s: standardization, concrete revival, environmental pressure

- **1970s:** **ZTV Asphalt-StB** (standard specifications for asphalt road construction) unify Länder practice; **Marshall stability** and **gyratory compactor** testing replace purely empirical mixes
- **1970s concrete pavements:** **Jointed plain concrete pavement (PCC)** on heavy duty sections (climbing lanes, toll plaza approaches, some federal highways); **doweled joints** every 4–5 m; **expansion joints** at bridges
- **1970s drainage:** **Linear drainage channels** (polymer concrete or steel slot drains) at autobahn median; **oil separators** at service areas mandatory
- **1980s:** **Thin overlay systems** (2–3 cm) for maintenance; **SMA (stone mastic asphalt)** introduced from Austria/Germany research for rut resistance on bus routes
- **1980s noise:** First **low noise asphalt** trials (open graded friction course, OGFC); 2–3 dB reduction vs dense asphalt; durability problems on early OGFC (ravelling)

#### 1990s–2000s: reunification catch up, quiet pavements, drainage engineering

- **1990–2000:** Massive **Ost-West** bridge and **Bundesstraße** rebuild; eastern network brought from concrete slabs and patched AC to western spec
- **1990s SMA widespread:** **Stone mastic asphalt** wearing course on autobahn; high stone content, fiber modified binder, rut resistant
- **1990s–2000s quiet surfaces:**
  - **OGL (open graded asphalt)** and **PA (porous asphalt)** on urban motorways near housing
  - **Twin layer systems:** porous top 2 cm over dense binder
  - **Up to 4–5 dB** tyre noise reduction vs dense AC at 80 km/h
- **1990s drainage:** **Side tolerant mixes** discouraged; **positive drainage** philosophy (water off surface in minutes); **geotextile wrapped edge drains**
- **2000s:** **RSP (Rhineland Palatinate porous asphalt)** and Länder variants; mandatory noise mapping under EU Environmental Noise Directive (2002); roads above threshold get quiet surface at repaving

#### 2010s–2026: sustainability, smart drainage, ultra quiet systems

- **2010s maintenance:** **Cold in place recycling** of asphalt; **warm mix asphalt (WMA)** lowers production temperature 20–30 °C, cuts fumes and energy
- **2010s concrete:** **Continuously reinforced concrete pavement (CRCP)** on selected heavy freight corridors; **whisper concrete** (exposed aggregate, positive macro texture) for noise; **diamond grinding** to restore texture and drainability
- **Quiet pavement standard (2020s):**
  - **LIG (low noise gap graded)** and **BBT (thin layer)** systems on autobahn near cities
  - **5–8 dB** reduction possible vs 1970s dense asphalt (frequency dependent)
  - **Acoustic optimization** paired with **noise barriers** and **low noise tyres** (EU tyre label)
- **Draining / permeable roadways (2020s):**
  - **Full depth porous asphalt** on parking, service roads, some urban collectors: water infiltrates vertical drain to **attenuation tank** or **swale**
  - **Permeable interlocking concrete pavers** in new subdivisions (stormwater code)
  - **Autobahn:** still **impermeable** wearing course for structural and freeze thaw reasons; drainage via **crossfall**, **edge drains**, **gully**, **retention basin**; **no sheet flow** off mainline
  - **Urban SUDS:** **Bioswales** beside cycle tracks; **permeable shoulder** on some Bundesstraßen rebuilds
- **2026 federal and state roads:** ~230,000 km paved asphalt surface; **Autobahn** 13,600 km
- **2026 typical new build section (autobahn):** **Asphalt base + SMA or low noise wearing course** on **asphalt base + hydraulically bound base** on **processed subbase**; **subsurface drainage blanket**; **geogrid** on weak subgrade; design life 30+ years with mill and overlay
- **2026 typical urban arterial:** **Asphalt** or **concrete bus lane**; **slot drains**; **tactile paving** at crossings; **porous cycle path** adjacent in new schemes

### 2026 summary (roads)

- **Autobahn:** 13,600 km; asphalt concrete surface on reinforced concrete base slab or full depth asphalt; design speed 130 km/h advisory or unrestricted; median barrier, lighting on interchange zones, emergency lanes throughout
- **Federal and state roads:** ~230,000 km of paved asphalt surface
- **Asphalt composition:** Bitumen bound aggregate in multiple layers (surface course, binder course, base course) over a granular sub base and prepared subgrade
- **Concrete roads:** Portland cement concrete used on heavy freight routes and bus corridors for longevity
- **Bridges:** Prestressed concrete box girder standard; cable stayed on long spans; expansion joints engineered to millimeter tolerances for thermal movement
- **Noise reduction:** Porous and gap graded asphalt on urban motorways; whisper concrete where applicable; combined with barriers for EU limit compliance
- **Drainage:** Positive crossfall, edge drain networks, gully and pipe to treatment or infiltration; green infrastructure on new developments (see Stormwater section)

## Water Supply

### 1913

- **Source:** River extraction and groundwater wells; major cities had gravity fed reservoir systems built in the 1870s–1890s
- **Treatment:** Slow sand filtration as primary method; water passed through deep sand beds over hours; removed particles and much bacterial load but not reliably
- **Chlorination:** Introduced in Germany around 1910–1914; not yet universal; many systems still unchlorinated
- **Mains material:** Spun cast iron, lead caulked bell and spigot joints; mains typically 100–300 mm diameter in cities
- **Service pipes (household connections):** Lead pipe universally; this was not considered a hazard in 1913
- **Pressure:** Gravity fed or steam pump maintained; inconsistent in upper floors of tall buildings
- **Rural coverage:** Minimal; most rural households drew from wells or surface sources with no treatment

### 2026

- **Source:** Groundwater (primary in most regions), surface water, and riverbank filtration
- **Treatment stages:** Coagulation and flocculation, sedimentation, rapid sand filtration, activated carbon adsorption, UV disinfection, residual chlorination; six or more stages standard
- **Mains material:** Ductile iron with cement mortar lining; high density polyethylene (HDPE) for new installations and replacements; no lead anywhere in the distribution network
- **Service pipes:** Copper or cross linked polyethylene (PEX) to individual properties; all lead service pipes replaced by law
- **Pressure:** Computer monitored pressure zones; district metering areas detect leaks within hours
- **Rural coverage:** Universal; every German household has treated mains water by law

## Sewage and Wastewater

### 1913

- **Urban systems:** Combined sewers carrying both stormwater and sewage in the same pipe; Berlin's Hobrecht system from the 1870s was the model; brick built egg shaped sewers discharging to sewage farms on the city outskirts
- **Sewer material:** Brick with lime mortar lining; vitrified clay pipe on smaller branches; no concrete pipe yet
- **Treatment:** None at point of discharge; raw sewage applied to sewage farms (land irrigation) or discharged directly to rivers in many cities
- **Rural:** Cesspits beneath outhouses; emptied by night soil collectors; no connection to any sewer network
- **Smell:** A permanent feature of urban life at street level near sewer openings

### 2026

- **System type:** Separate systems; foul water sewers carry sewage only; stormwater sewers carry surface runoff only; the two never mix in modern construction
- **Sewer material:** Vitrified clay, concrete, HDPE and GRP (glass reinforced plastic) pipe; older brick sewers retained where structurally sound
- **Treatment:** Multi stage wastewater treatment plants; mechanical screening, primary sedimentation, biological treatment (activated sludge), secondary clarification, phosphorus and nitrogen removal, UV or ozone disinfection before discharge
- **Sludge:** Digested anaerobically to produce biogas; stabilised sludge applied to agricultural land or incinerated
- **EU standard:** German discharge standards meet or exceed EU Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive

## Stormwater and Drainage

### 1913

- **Urban:** Combined with sewage as above; excess combined sewer overflow discharged untreated to waterways during heavy rain; accepted practice
- **Street drainage:** Stone dressed gutters running to iron grated inlets; manually cleared of debris
- **Rural:** Open roadside ditches, hand dug and hand maintained; no subsurface drainage on agricultural land beyond field ditches
- **Flooding:** Regular occurrence in low lying city districts after heavy rain; accepted as seasonal reality

### 2026

- **Urban:** Separate stormwater networks discharging to retention basins, infiltration swales and rivers after settling
- **Green infrastructure:** Mandatory in new developments; green roofs, permeable paving, rain gardens attenuate peak flow before it reaches pipes
- **Pipe material:** Reinforced concrete on large culverts; HDPE on smaller runs; pre cast concrete box culverts on major buried watercourses
- **Flood protection:** Engineered retention basins, flood bypass tunnels in major cities, digital flood early warning systems on all major rivers
- **Road linked drainage:** Highway edge drains, gully, oil separators at yards; motorway runoff sometimes treated before discharge

## Energy Distribution

### 1913

- **Gas:** Coal gas mains in cast iron serving city centre street lighting and some domestic cookers; pressure maintained by gas holders visible on every city skyline
- **Electricity:** Low voltage DC networks in central districts of large cities only; no standardisation of voltage or frequency between cities; Werner von Siemens systems operated at various DC voltages
- **Rural:** No gas, no electricity; oil lamps and candles universal

### 2026

- **Electricity:** 380 kV and 220 kV high voltage AC transmission grid covering the entire country; 110 kV and 20 kV regional distribution; 230 V 50 Hz single phase to every household
- **Gas:** High pressure natural gas steel mains at national level; medium pressure polyethylene distribution mains at regional level; copper or corrugated stainless steel to appliances indoors; coal gas is entirely gone
- **Hydrogen:** Early hydrogen pipeline network under construction as of 2026

## Communications Infrastructure

### 1913

- **Telegraph:** Iron wire on wooden poles, insulated on ceramic pin insulators; Morse code; operated by trained operators
- **Telephone:** Copper wire on pole routes between major cities; exchange switching entirely manual via operators with plug boards
- **Rural telephone:** Rare to nonexistent outside market towns
- **Radio:** Military experimental only; Marconi type spark gap transmitters

### 2026

- **Fibre optic:** Single mode glass fibre as national backbone; FTTH (fibre to the home) rollout ongoing; multi terabit capacity per cable
- **Copper:** Legacy VDSL2 and ADSL on existing telephone copper pairs in areas awaiting fibre; being retired
- **Mobile:** 5G NR on 700 MHz, 3.5 GHz and 26 GHz bands; 4G LTE as fallback; full national voice coverage
- **Poles:** Concrete and steel replacing wooden; underground ducting in urban areas
