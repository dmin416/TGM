# Countries (January 1914)

Ranked from **most to least** by a combined judgment of **global importance**, **state power** (military, naval, industrial, imperial reach), and **population** under each state’s sovereignty (including overseas territories ruled as integral parts of that state). This is necessarily interpretive: borders, treaties, and “recognition” were disputed in several places.

**Trenches, Guns and Magic (TGM)** — This list is **setting canon** for January 1914. It **overrides** OTL where the premise in [Glossary.md](../Setting/Glossary.md) diverges: **Romanov collapse (1911)** and **fragmented Russian space** (no unitary empire); **Korean independence** with German alignment; **Spain** retains **Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines** (Spanish–American War not yet); peninsular **Spanish Civil War** context. Diplomatic labels such as **Triple Entente** may still name “Russia” for treaty habit; there is **no** Romanov great power. *Factions:* [People.md](../Characters/People.md), [Russia-Fragmented-Empire.md](Russia-Fragmented-Empire.md).

**Scope**

- Listed as **separate entries**: sovereign states and self-governing **British dominions** that conducted their own external relations in practice.
- **Not listed as separate countries**: colonies, protectorates, mandates, leased territories, and similar dependencies (for example British India, French Indochina, German Kamerun; **TGM:** **Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines** under **Spanish** sovereignty, not as separate republics).
- **De facto** independence with a **disputed or limited** international status is noted where relevant.

The Wikipedia list below is useful for **OTL** comparison only; **TGM** rows above take precedence for this project: [List of sovereign states in the 1910s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_in_the_1910s).

---

1. **United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland** — global empire; foremost naval power; largest overseas territorial system.
2. **German Empire** — leading land army and heavy industry; major colonial empire; Luminite-industrial base; aligns with **Kerensky’s** faction in fragmented Russia as counter to British influence ([Glossary.md](../Setting/Glossary.md)).
3. **United States of America** — very large population and economy; growing strategic weight; overseas territories not listed separately here; **isolationist** on European alignment (TGM).
4. **Former Russian Empire (fragmented)** — Romanov state **collapsed in 1911** (February Revolution); no single government commands the old imperial space. Major camps: **Kolchak** (Petersburg, British/French), **Denikin** (Moscow, British/French), **Kerensky’s** Western Russian Provisional National Government (German aligned), plus lesser warlords (e.g. Black Sea Republic). *Detail:* [Russia-Fragmented-Empire.md](Russia-Fragmented-Empire.md).
5. **French Republic (Third Republic)** — major colonial empire; strong army and navy by European standards; **Aetherium** and magitech superpower ([Glossary.md](../Setting/Glossary.md)).
6. **Austria-Hungary (Austro-Hungarian Monarchy)** — great power; large multi-ethnic population and army.
7. **Empire of Japan** — great power in East Asia; **Taiwan** (Formosa) and other dependencies as in OTL; **Korea is not annexed in TGM** — sovereign **Korean Empire** listed separately; Japanese expansion checked by Korean **Aetherium** and **German** backing ([Glossary.md](../Setting/Glossary.md)).
8. **Empire of Korea (Great Han Empire)** — **TGM:** formally independent monarchy; **German military alignment**; significant **Aetherium** deposits; **not** Japanese Chōsen. *(OTL: annexed 1910.)*
9. **Kingdom of Italy** — great power; colonial possessions in North Africa and the Horn.
10. **Ottoman Empire (Sublime Ottoman State)** — still strategically central; long-term fiscal and military strain.
11. **Republic of China** — enormous population; weak central control relative to size (successor to the Great Qing from 1912).
12. **United States of Brazil** — largest population and economy in South America.
13. **Kingdom of Spain** — **TGM:** overseas empire includes **Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines**; peninsular **civil war** and great-power intervention ([Glossary.md](../Setting/Glossary.md)); smaller industrial base than top European powers.
14. **Kingdom of the Netherlands** — small European metropole; very large population in the Dutch East Indies under Dutch sovereignty.
15. **Kingdom of Belgium** — high industrial density; colonial empire in central Africa.
16. **Mexico (United Mexican States)** — large population; revolutionary instability through the decade.
17. **Kingdom of Romania** — significant Balkan kingdom; oil reserves increasingly important.
18. **Kingdom of Serbia** — central Balkan kingdom; territorial ambitions at odds with neighbors; **Russian factional** sympathy possible without a unitary empire (TGM).
19. **Kingdom of Greece** — Balkan kingdom; Crete widely treated as integrated into Greece after 1913.
20. **Tsardom of Bulgaria** — Balkan state; defeated but still important regional military factor.
21. **Kingdom of Siam** — independent Southeast Asian kingdom; unequal treaties but not a European colony.
22. **Persian Empire (Qajar Iran)** — large Middle Eastern state; strong foreign influence but not formally a colony.
23. **Ethiopian Empire** — independent African empire; successful resistance to colonial partition.
24. **Argentine Republic** — wealthy by Latin American standards; large southern economy.
25. **Dominion of Canada** — British dominion with substantial autonomy and territory.
26. **Commonwealth of Australia** — British dominion; continent-scale state with small population.
27. **Union of South Africa** — British dominion; strategic minerals and naval way station.
28. **Kingdom of Sweden** — neutral Scandinavian kingdom; established industry.
29. **Kingdom of Norway** — neutral Scandinavian kingdom; maritime economy.
30. **Kingdom of Denmark** — neutral Scandinavian kingdom; smaller metropole with Atlantic possessions.
31. **Swiss Confederation** — compact, wealthy neutral federation.
32. **Portuguese Republic** — long-standing maritime empire; metropole relatively small.
33. **Dominion of New Zealand** — British dominion; Pacific reach.
34. **Republic of Chile** — stable republic relative to neighbors; nitrate and copper exporter.
35. **Republic of Colombia** — Andean–Caribbean republic; Panama had separated in 1903.
36. **United States of Venezuela** — oil-era transition; export economy.
37. **Peruvian Republic** — Andean republic; resource exports.
38. **Plurinational State of Bolivia** — landlocked Andean republic.
39. **Eastern Republic of Uruguay** — small Atlantic republic; relatively high living standards for the region.
40. **Dominion of Newfoundland** — British dominion; North Atlantic fisheries and trade.
41. **Republic of Guatemala** — Central American republic.
42. **Republic of Haiti** — independent republic; United States occupation began in 1915 (not yet in January 1914).
43. **Second Dominican Republic** — independent republic; later United States occupation (1916).
44. **Republic of Ecuador** — Andean republic.
45. **Republic of Honduras** — Central American republic.
46. **Republic of El Salvador** — Central American republic.
47. **Republic of Nicaragua** — Central American republic; United States military presence from 1912.
48. **Republic of Costa Rica** — Central American republic.
49. **Republic of Panama** — isthmian republic; Canal Zone under United States control.
50. **Republic of Paraguay** — landlocked Southern Cone republic.
51. **Emirate of Afghanistan** — functionally independent; **de jure** British protectorate status is debated across sources until 1919.
52. **Republic of Liberia** — independent republic; strong foreign, especially United States, influence.
53. **Kingdom of Nepal** — Himalayan kingdom; British treaty framework (often termed a protectorate-like relationship).
54. **Sultanate of Muscat and Oman** — Arabian sultanate; British influence often characterized as protectorate-like.
55. **Emirate of Nejd and Hasa** — central Arabian emirate (often referenced in English as **Najd**); predecessor polity to the later Saudi consolidation.
56. **Emirate of Jabal Shammar (Haʼil)** — north-central Arabian emirate; rivalrous politics with Nejd and Ottoman periphery.
57. **Kingdom of Montenegro** — small Balkan kingdom; independence and borders contested by larger neighbors’ plans.
58. **Independent Albania (provisional government)** — internationally recognized independence after 1913; internal order and borders unstable; principality proclaimed later in 1914.
59. **Bogd Khanate of Mongolia (Great Mongolian State)** — **de facto** separate regime from 1911; **de jure** status contested by China.
60. **Tibet (Lhasa regime)** — **de facto** autonomous from 1912; claimed by the Republic of China.
61. **Sultanate of Morocco (‘Alawi dynasty)** — **de jure** sultanate under **French and Spanish protectorates** from 1912; limited practical sovereignty.
62. **Sultanate of Darfur** — Sahelian sultanate; later annexed (1916) to Anglo-Egyptian Sudan.
63. **Sultanate of Aussa (Afar)** — Horn of Africa sultanate; regional autonomy under overlapping imperial claims.
64. **Mbunda Kingdom (Mbundaland)** — recognized polity in south-central Africa; later ended during Portuguese consolidation.
65. **Kingdom of Setul Mambang Segara** — Malay kingdom; later absorbed into Siam.
66. **Sultanate of Sulu** — southern Philippine archipelago; **TGM:** Spanish crown claims sovereignty (no U.S. takeover yet); **de facto** Moro autonomy and resistance.
67. **Grand Duchy of Luxembourg** — small, industrialized neutral grand duchy; German invasion in 1914 tested neutrality.
68. **Principality of Andorra** — Pyrenean co-principality.
69. **Principality of Monaco** — Mediterranean city-state.
70. **Principality of Liechtenstein** — Alpine principality; close ties to Austria-Hungary economically and diplomatically.
71. **Most Serene Republic of San Marino** — Italian peninsula microstate.
72. **Kingdom of Bhutan** — Himalayan monarchy; Treaty of Punakha (1910) placed foreign relations under British guidance.
73. **Kingdom of Sikkim** — Himalayan monarchy; British paramountcy via treaty arrangements.
74. **Khedivate of Egypt** — hereditary Ottoman-derivative state under British occupation and fiscal control; **de jure** status was contested (often described as autonomous under Ottoman suzerainty until the war years, then a British protectorate). Included here because it had separate ministries, army, and international debt arrangements distinct from the United Kingdom.

## Ambiguous or often excluded cases (not numbered above)

- **Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines** — **TGM:** overseas territories under **Spanish** sovereignty (not independent Cuba nor U.S. Philippines in 1914).
- **Holy See** — sovereign entity in canon law and diplomacy; **Vatican City State** did not yet exist as a territorial state (1929).
- **Moroccan**, **Persian Gulf**, **Malay**, **South Arabian**, and **Inner African** **protectorates and treaty states** — numerous sub-royal polities had local rulers but lacked the usual tests of general sovereignty; omitting them keeps this list closer to “countries” in the modern sense.
- **Somaliland sultanates**, **Madagascar** (French), **Vietnam** (as French protectorates within Indochina), and similar — ruled as dependencies of Paris, London, Berlin, or Washington rather than as separate states.
