# Alternate History Story Ideas

Rough draft. Long arc ideology, holy war, and alliance beats. See [Notes-for-the-Future.md](Notes-for-the-Future.md) (faith, Teutonic Knights, purge regions), [Glossary.md](Glossary.md) (Spain, Korea, Germany), [Market-Crash-Wealth-Reference.md](Market-Crash-Wealth-Reference.md).

## Core Premise

A better ideological Germany (WWI) allies with Republican Spain and Korea, pushing back oppressive British, French, Italian, and Russian powers. The backdrop includes a corrupt Catholic Church sparking holy war, beaten back by Protestants and the Teutonic Order.

---

## 1. The Holy War: Corrupt Church vs. Protestant-Teutonic Alliance

**The Church as Antagonist:**
- Papal authority treated as demonic allegiance: rooted in real Protestant theology (Luther called the Pope the Antichrist)
- Indulgences = spiritually inverted false salvation; buying grace is serving the opposite of God
- The Pope claiming to be Christ's vicar on Earth = ultimate blasphemy
- Rome's "Whore of Babylon" interpretation widely held by Reformers

**The Corruption Ladder (moral gradients, not cartoonish evil):**
- Peasant paying tithes out of fear: deceived, not evil
- Priest enforcing indulgences knowingly: complicit
- Bishop using Church power for land: actively corrupt
- Cardinals engineering wars: demonically aligned
- The Pope: knowingly serving a spiritual inversion

**The Demonic Framework (three options):**
- Literally demonic: supernatural forces working through Rome
- Functionally demonic: no supernatural element, but the system reliably produces evil
- Symbolically demonic: characters interpret it as such; story neither confirms nor denies

**Salvation as battleground:**
- Rome: salvation earned through works, sacraments, payments, obedience
- Protagonists: salvation freely given by God, cannot be bought; any system selling it serves the enemy
- Every indulgence, crusade promise, or excommunication threat is spiritually fraudulent

---

## 2. The Protestant-Teutonic Alliance

**Why it's interesting:**
- The Teutonic Order was Catholic by charter; something must drive them to the Protestant side (e.g., the Pope disbands or attacks them)
- They bring military discipline, territorial control, and legitimacy; Protestants bring theological fire
- An uneasy alliance: they agree on the enemy, not much else

**Internal alliance tensions:**
- Faith-driven reformers paired with survival-driven warrior-monks
- Disagreement on whether to destroy Rome or reform it
- Whether violence is ever spiritually clean in a just cause
- What to do with deceived Catholic populations after victory

**Antagonist factions:**
- Inner Circle: cynical cardinals who believe nothing, want everything
- True Believers: devout Catholics weaponized and ultimately betrayed (tragic)
- Enforcers: inquisitors, crusade commanders, mercenary armies
- Compromised Kings: secular rulers who need papal legitimacy, useful idiots of the system

## 3. Additional Story Hooks

### Character Hooks
- A **papal assassin** who genuinely believes he's doing God's work: killing Protestant leaders to save their souls from leading others astray
- A **Teutonic knight who is secretly atheist**: fighting for political order, not God, while surrounded by men dying for faith
- A **former cardinal turned Protestant** who knows exactly how Rome operates from the inside and becomes the alliance's most dangerous strategist
- A **young Korean soldier** fighting in a European holy war he barely understands theologically but believes in politically

### Structural Hooks
- The war begins not with armies but with the **Church excommunicating an entire nation**: cutting millions off from salvation overnight as a political weapon
- A **forged prophecy** circulated by Rome that the Protestant alliance is literally the army of hell, and some Protestants start to wonder if their own darkness proves it true
- The **Teutonic Order discovers ancient documentation** proving the medieval papacy fabricated key theological justifications for their own power centuries earlier

### Thematic Hooks
- **A town that switches sides three times**: showing how ordinary people survive under each regime and what actually changes versus what stays the same
- The Protestant alliance wins a major battle and immediately **splinters over what to do with Rome**: destroy it, occupy it, or let a reform pope take over
- A **child pope** installed by corrupt cardinals as a puppet, and the moral horror of fighting a war against someone who is technically innocent

### The Darkest Hook

The Protestant leadership secretly **makes a deal with a pagan Baltic faction** to flank Rome's armies, and must decide whether alliance with non-Christians is pragmatic or proof that Rome was right about them all along.

---

## 4. Central Moral Themes

- Institutions corrupt ideals; fighting corruption can corrupt the fighters
- The Protestant-Teutonic side is not clean: princes seize Church property for themselves, the Order's brutal Baltic history haunts them, reformed communities become self-righteous
- **The haunting beat:** A Protestant leader realizes his war machine demands the same blind obedience Rome did
- Victory creates a power vacuum worse actors fill
- "At what cost?" is the story's real question, not just "what if?"
