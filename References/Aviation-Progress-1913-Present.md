# Aviation Progress: 1913 to Present

## Every Major Improvement Increment

Reference for *Trenches, Guns and Magic*. OTL aviation timeline from the story's 1914 baseline through present day: mechanical, structural, and avionics increments. Use for Max tech pipeline, industrial buyouts, and what fixed wing aviation can plausibly reach in each era. Does not override in setting magitech airships ([German-Armored-Airship-Armament.md](German-Armored-Airship-Armament.md), [Military.md](Military.md)).

---

# PART ONE: MECHANICAL & STRUCTURAL & TECHNICAL

---

## 1913 to 1918 | World War I Era

- Wire-braced wood-and-fabric construction standard on all aircraft
- Rotary engines (Gnome, Le Rhône) dominant; whole engine spins with propeller
- Synchronized gun gear (Fokker Synchronizer, 1915); fires through propeller arc
- Monocoque fuselage construction begins replacing wire-braced frames (Deperdussin, 1912 to 13)
- Streamlined cowlings start appearing on inline-engine aircraft
- Ailerons replacing wing-warping for roll control becomes standard
- Multi-row rotary engines attempted (limited success)
- Water-cooled inline engines (Mercedes, Hispano-Suiza) offer slimmer profile for fighters
- First purpose-built bomber aircraft (Caproni, Handley Page, Gotha)
- First large multi-engine aircraft (Sikorsky Ilya Muromets, 1913)
- Steel tube fuselage framing begins replacing wood in some designs

---

## 1919 to 1928 | Post-War Barnstorming & Early Airmail

- Surplus war engines and airframes widely available; drives civil aviation boom
- Radial engines mature and displace rotaries entirely (Wright Whirlwind, Pratt & Whitney Wasp)
- All-metal corrugated aluminum construction introduced (Junkers J1, then F13)
- Monoplane low-wing designs begin challenging biplane dominance
- First enclosed cockpits on commercial transport aircraft
- Semi-monocoque aluminum fuselage construction refined
- Variable-pitch propeller concepts explored
- First transatlantic flight (Alcock & Brown, 1919); demonstrates long-range capability
- First solo transatlantic flight (Lindbergh, 1927); Spirit of St. Louis uses Wright Whirlwind
- Cantilevered wing designs eliminating external bracing wires (Fokker, Junkers)
- Flying boats and seaplanes expand to routes without landing fields
- Supercharged engines begin appearing for high-altitude performance

---

## 1929 to 1938 | Golden Age of Aviation

- All-metal smooth-skin semi-monocoque becomes standard construction
- Retractable landing gear widely adopted; major drag reduction
- Controllable-pitch / constant-speed propellers introduced (game-changing efficiency)
- Enclosed cockpits become universal
- Wing flaps developed for slower landing speeds
- NACA cowling ring dramatically reduces radial engine drag
- Stressed-skin construction matures; skin carries structural load
- Multi-row radial engines achieve 800 to 1,000+ hp (Pratt & Whitney Twin Wasp)
- Liquid-cooled V-12 engines refined (Rolls-Royce Merlin, Allison V-1710)
- Pressurized aircraft cabins first tested (Lockheed XC-35, 1937)
- Tricycle landing gear experimented with; improves ground handling
- Cantilever monoplane fully displaces biplane for high-performance roles
- Turbo-superchargers allow high-altitude operations
- Stressed-skin monocoque fuselage universal on modern designs

---

## 1939 to 1945 | World War II

- Turbosuperchargers mature and become standard on heavy bombers
- Laminar-flow wing profiles reduce skin friction drag (P-51 Mustang)
- Two-stage superchargers dramatically extend high-altitude engine performance
- Water-methanol injection boosts emergency power output
- Fuel injection replaces carburetors on many engines; no cutout in negative-G flight
- Armored cockpits and self-sealing fuel tanks become standard on combat aircraft
- Pressurized cabins enter service (Boeing B-29)
- Jet propulsion enters service (Me 262, Gloster Meteor, 1944)
- Rocket-powered aircraft tested (Me 163 Komet)
- Ejection seats first introduced (Heinkel He 280, 1942)
- Tricycle landing gear becomes dominant on new designs
- Bubble canopies improve pilot visibility
- Large radial engines reach 2,000 to 3,500 hp (Wright R-3350, Pratt & Whitney R-4360)
- Contra-rotating propellers manage torque at extreme power levels

---

## 1946 to 1957 | Early Jet Age & Post-War

- Jet engines displace piston engines entirely for high-performance roles
- Centrifugal-flow turbojets refined (de Havilland, Rolls-Royce Derwent)
- Axial-flow turbojets offer better efficiency and higher thrust (J47, Avon)
- Swept wings adopted to delay compressibility effects near Mach 1
- Sound barrier broken (Chuck Yeager, Bell X-1, 1947)
- Afterburners (reheat) add thrust for supersonic bursts
- Area rule (Coke bottle fuselage shaping) reduces transonic drag (1952)
- Delta wing configurations explored for supersonic flight
- Turboprop engines bridge gap for medium-speed transport and military patrol
- Ejection seats refined with rocket propulsion
- Pressurization becomes universal on airliners
- Swept-wing commercial jet transport enters service (de Havilland Comet, 1952)
- All-moving tailplane (stabilator) replaces conventional elevator at high speeds
- Titanium begins replacing aluminum in hot sections of airframes

---

## 1958 to 1969 | Commercial Jet Age & Supersonic Push

- High-bypass turbofan concept developed; far more fuel-efficient than pure jets
- Turbofan engines enter service on airliners (JT3D, Conway)
- Mach 2+ fighters become operational (F-104, MiG-21)
- Variable-sweep wings enter service (F-111, 1964) for multi-regime performance
- Thrust reversers standard on jet airliners
- Anti-skid brake systems (early form of ABS) on landing gear
- Honeycomb composite sandwich structures introduced in secondary structures
- First supersonic airliner flights (Concorde prototype, Tu-144; late 1960s)
- Wide-body airliners begin development (Boeing 747 first flight 1969)
- Turbine blade cooling technology enables higher turbine inlet temperatures
- Blown flaps and boundary-layer control on some military aircraft
- Zero-zero ejection seats (safe at ground level, zero speed)
- Ceramic thermal barrier coatings begin development for turbine blades

---

## 1970 to 1979 | Wide-Body & High-Bypass Era

- High-bypass turbofans enter wide service (JT9D, CF6, RB211); transform fuel economy
- Wide-body airliners operational (747, DC-10, L-1011)
- Concorde enters commercial service (1976); sustained Mach 2 passenger flight
- Carbon fiber reinforced polymer (CFRP) composites begin entering secondary structures
- Supercritical wing profiles reduce drag at cruise speeds
- Full-scale wind tunnel and computational methods improve aerodynamic design
- Engine nacelles designed as separate aerodynamic bodies
- Multi-spool turbofan architecture matures (fan, LP compressor, HP compressor separate shafts)
- FADEC (Full Authority Digital Engine Control) concepts developed
- Winglets explored for induced drag reduction (Whitcomb, 1976)

---

## 1980 to 1989 | Composites & Digital Controls

- Fly-by-wire flight controls enter service on commercial aircraft (Airbus A310/A320)
- Composite materials move into primary structures on some aircraft
- FADEC enters service on jet engines; computerized engine management
- Winglets adopted on business jets and begin appearing on airliners
- Glass cockpit (CRT-based EFIS) replaces round-dial instruments
- Active flutter suppression through flight control computers
- Stealth shaping and radar-absorbent materials operational (F-117, 1983)
- Three-dimensional CFD (computational fluid dynamics) transforms aerodynamic design
- STOVL (Short Take-Off Vertical Landing) matures (Harrier refined)
- Thrust vectoring explored on experimental aircraft

---

## 1990 to 1999 | Maturation of Digital Aircraft

- Composite primary structures on production aircraft expand significantly
- Blended winglets become standard on new airliner designs
- High-lift systems (slats, multi-element flaps) fully optimized with CFD
- Titanium aluminide and single-crystal turbine blades enable higher temperatures
- Three-shaft turbofan architecture (Rolls-Royce Trent) enters wide service
- Advanced turboprop engines for regional aircraft (PW100, CT7)
- Geared turbofan concepts revisited for next generation
- Reduced vertical separation minimum (RVSM) enables denser high-altitude routing
- Active noise and vibration control systems on turboprop aircraft

---

## 2000 to 2009 | Composite Airframes

- Boeing 787 and Airbus A350 designed with 50%+ composite primary structure
- Friction stir welding replaces riveting in some aluminum structures
- Additive manufacturing (3D printing) begins entering tooling and small parts
- Geared turbofan (PW1000G) achieves certification; major fuel efficiency gain
- Chevron nozzles reduce jet noise on turbofans
- Open-rotor (unducted fan) concepts revisited
- Electric and hybrid-electric propulsion serious research begins
- UAVs / drones scale up dramatically (Predator, Global Hawk)
- Blended wing body concepts under active research

---

## 2010 to 2019 | Efficiency Revolution

- Geared turbofan enters commercial service (A320neo, 2016); 15 to 20% fuel saving
- LEAP engine ceramic matrix composite (CMC) turbine components enter service
- Additive manufactured fuel nozzles in production (GE LEAP engine, 2016)
- Composite fan blades standard on new high-bypass engines
- Active load alleviation via fly-by-wire reduces structural weight
- Morphing wing surfaces under research
- Electric taxiing systems (wheel motors) reduce fuel burn on ground
- Urban air mobility and eVTOL aircraft serious development begins
- Supersonic business jet development restarts (Boom Overture, Aerion)
- Next-generation single-crystal turbine blades with advanced cooling channels

---

## 2020 to Present | Electrification & New Propulsion

- Hybrid-electric regional aircraft prototypes flying
- Hydrogen propulsion ground and flight tests underway (ZEROe, 2020)
- Sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) approved for blend use on commercial flights
- eVTOL aircraft (Joby, Lilium, Wisk) reach advanced certification stages
- Distributed electric propulsion (many small motors on wing) demonstrated
- Open fan (unducted) engine architecture re-entering development (CFM RISE program)
- CMC components now standard in production turbofan hot sections
- 3D-printed structural titanium components in production aircraft
- Morphing winglet concepts under flight test
- Supersonic passenger aircraft targeting re-entry to service (Boom XB-1 supersonic demonstrator flown 2023)

# PART TWO: ELECTRONICS, AVIONICS & COMMUNICATIONS

---

## 1913 to 1918 | World War I Era

- No onboard electronics; zero
- Ground-based radio telegraphy used for coordination, not yet airborne
- Experimental airborne spark-gap radio sets attempted late in the war (heavy, unreliable)
- First practical airborne wireless sets tested by RFC (1916 to 17); one-way only
- No instruments beyond basic compass, altimeter, and tachometer

---

## 1919 to 1928 | Early Instruments

- Magnetic compass universal but affected by engine vibration and metal airframe
- Barometric altimeter standard equipment
- Engine RPM gauge and oil pressure gauge common
- Airspeed indicator (pitot-static system) becomes standard
- Turn-and-bank indicator introduced
- First ground-based radio navigation beacons installed
- Vacuum-driven gyroscopic instruments begin development
- First voice radio communication from aircraft (experimental, mid-1920s)

---

## 1929 to 1938 | Instrument Flying Begins

- Artificial horizon (gyroscopic attitude indicator) enters service; enables blind flying
- Directional gyro (heading indicator) supplements unreliable magnetic compass
- Rate-of-climb (variometer) indicator standard
- Automatic direction finder (ADF) using NDB ground beacons; first reliable radio navigation
- Instrument Landing System (ILS) concept developed and first tested (1929 to 38)
- Sperry autopilot; first practical aircraft autopilot enters service (1930)
- Two-way VHF voice radio becomes standard on airliners
- First instrument-only flight demonstrated (Jimmy Doolittle, 1929)
- Engine manifold pressure gauges standard
- Carburettor temperature and cylinder head temperature gauges added

---

## 1939 to 1945 | World War II Electronics Explosion

- Radar developed and operational (Chain Home, 1940)
- Airborne radar enters service; AI (airborne interception) radar for night fighters
- H2S ground-mapping radar for blind bombing (1943)
- IFF (Identify Friend or Foe) transponders standard on military aircraft
- VHF radio universal on Allied aircraft; reliable two-way communication
- GEE hyperbolic navigation system (1942); radio fixes over Europe
- LORAN (Long Range Navigation) developed (1942)
- Rebecca/Eureka radar transponder navigation system
- Gyro gunsight; lead-computing optical sight
- Radio altimeter (true radar altitude) enters service
- Autopilots integrated with bombing systems (Norden bombsight)
- Early electronic countermeasures (Window/chaff jamming)
- First airborne computers (analog, mechanical-electronic) in bombsights

---

## 1946 to 1957 | Post-War Navigation & Radar

- ILS (Instrument Landing System) becomes standard at major airports
- VOR (VHF Omnidirectional Range) navigation system deployed; replaces older LF ranges
- DME (Distance Measuring Equipment) paired with VOR
- Weather radar appears on airliners (late 1950s)
- Doppler navigation radar for over-ocean position keeping
- Inertial navigation systems (INS) under development for military use
- Mach meters added as jets approach transonic speeds
- Improved autopilots capable of maintaining altitude and heading precisely
- Early air traffic control radar deployed at major airports
- TACAN (military UHF navigation) enters service
- HF radio for long-range transoceanic communication

---

## 1958 to 1969 | Jet Age Avionics

- Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) enter service; position without radio (1960s)
- Flight data recorders (black boxes) mandated following Comet accidents
- Cockpit voice recorders mandated
- Traffic alert concepts developed (early TCAS research)
- Doppler radar standard for over-ocean navigation
- Weather radar standard on all commercial jets
- Mach/airspeed warning systems standard
- Ground proximity warning concepts developed
- Stall warning systems standard
- Autopilots capable of coupled ILS approaches
- Stability augmentation systems on high-performance military jets
- Early fly-by-wire research (F-8 Crusader testbed, 1972; just outside this window)
- VLF/Omega global navigation system developed
- First satellite communications experiments

---

## 1970 to 1979 | Early Digital Era

- First digital flight management concepts
- Area navigation (RNAV) computers; fly off airways using VOR/DME
- GPWS (Ground Proximity Warning System) mandated after accidents; warns of terrain
- TCAS (Traffic Collision Avoidance System) development accelerates
- Digital engine monitoring systems early development
- HUD (Heads-Up Display) enters military service, explored for civil use
- Inertial navigation systems become lighter and standard on long-haul jets
- Digital autopilots and flight directors improve precision approaches
- SELCAL (selective calling) for HF oceanic communication
- Early digital air data computers replace pneumatic/mechanical systems

---

## 1980 to 1989 | Glass Cockpit Revolution

- EFIS (Electronic Flight Instrument System); CRT glass cockpit replaces round dials
- EICAS/ECAM engine and systems monitoring on CRT displays
- Flight Management Systems (FMS) with CDU; computerized route and performance management
- Digital fly-by-wire flight control computers (Airbus A320, 1988)
- ACARS (Aircraft Communications Addressing and Reporting System); digital datalink
- TCAS II operational and mandated; active collision avoidance with resolution advisories
- Enhanced GPWS with forward-looking terrain awareness in development
- Digital weather radar with color display standard
- SATCOM voice and data communication on long-haul aircraft
- Mode S transponder; enhanced secondary radar capability
- Digital autopilots capable of full autoland in zero visibility (Cat III ILS)
- FADEC digital engine control universal on new designs
- GPS satellites begin launching (Block I, 1978 to 1985)

---

## 1990 to 1999 | GPS & Digital Maturation

- GPS declared fully operational (1995); transforms navigation
- GPS navigation certified for aviation use; VOR routes begin to be supplemented
- EGPWS (Enhanced GPWS with terrain database) enters service; eliminates CFIT accidents
- TCAS fully mandated internationally
- Required Navigation Performance (RNP) procedures using GPS
- LCD displays begin replacing CRTs in cockpits
- Digital map displays in cockpit
- ACARS datalink expands; weather, clearances, performance data
- RVSM (Reduced Vertical Separation Minimum) enabled by accurate digital altimetry
- ADS-B (Automatic Dependent Surveillance Broadcast) concept developed
- Synthetic vision concept prototypes
- Flat-panel MFDs (multi-function displays) on business jets
- Enhanced weather radar with predictive windshear detection
- Digital radio management systems

---

## 2000 to 2009 | Connectivity & Surveillance

- ADS-B begins deployment; aircraft broadcast GPS position to all listeners
- Required Navigation Performance (RNP AR) precision curved approaches certified
- Synthetic vision systems certified and entering service
- Enhanced vision systems (infrared cameras for runway detection in fog)
- NextGen (US) and SESAR (Europe) air traffic modernization programs launched
- EFB (Electronic Flight Bag) tablets replace paper charts and manuals in cockpit
- FANS (Future Air Navigation System); satellite datalink for oceanic ATC
- Improved onboard weather radar with turbulence detection
- Full LCD cockpit displays universal on new aircraft
- CPDLC (Controller-Pilot Datalink Communications); text-based ATC instructions
- WiFi passenger connectivity begins on airliners
- Integrated modular avionics (IMA); shared computing platforms replace individual boxes
- UAV/drone control systems mature into sophisticated digital architectures

---

## 2010 to 2019 | Integration & Automation

- ADS-B mandate set and progressively enforced (2020 US deadline)
- NextGen GPS approaches proliferate; thousands of new RNP procedures worldwide
- Synthetic vision standard on new business jets and increasingly on airliners
- Enhanced vision (IR camera) combined with synthetic vision into SVS-EVS
- Electronic flight bags fully replacing paper; iPads standard in most cockpits
- Real-time aircraft health monitoring via satellite datalink (Boeing 787, A350)
- Onboard data networks (ARINC 664/AFDX) replace individual wire bundles
- Passenger satellite WiFi fast enough for streaming
- Touchscreen cockpit interfaces introduced (Garmin G3000, Dassault FalseX)
- Terrain and traffic fully integrated into single display
- Automatic Emergency Descent Mode on some aircraft
- Runway overrun protection systems (autobrake enhancements)
- Voice recognition for cockpit checklist assistance (research stage)

---

## 2020 to Present | AI, Autonomy & Advanced Connectivity

- ADS-B fully mandated in US and EU; surveillance without secondary radar
- Advanced AI-assisted flight envelope protection systems
- Autonomous UAV/drone operations at scale; FAA Part 135 certification granted
- eVTOL aircraft use fully digital fly-by-wire with no mechanical backup
- Urban air traffic management (UTM) digital frameworks operational
- Real-time global aircraft tracking via low-Earth orbit satellite ADS-B receivers (Aireon)
- Single-pilot operations (SiPO) research and regulatory groundwork underway
- AI copilot assistance and decision support systems under development
- High-speed low-latency LEO satellite internet (Starlink) on commercial aircraft
- Quantum inertial navigation research (no GPS dependency)
- Digital twin aircraft models for real-time maintenance prediction
- Fully electric aircraft with digital power management systems flying
- Autonomous cargo aircraft entering commercial operation (Natilus, Xwing)

---

*Compiled 2026. From fabric-and-wire biplanes to autonomous electric aircraft in roughly 113 years.*
