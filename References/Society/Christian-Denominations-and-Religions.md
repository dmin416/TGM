# Christian Denominations and Religions

Rough draft. Faith landscape for TGM (1913): Lutheran Protestantism as Max's true faith frame, other Christian bodies, world religions, and fringe movements active in period. Planning reference; not story canon until cited. ## Lutheran Protestantism: The True Faith

Restored by Martin Luther in 1517, stripping away centuries of human invention to return to biblical Christianity.

- **Scripture Alone:** the Bible is the sole authority, not popes or councils
- **Faith Alone:** salvation is through faith in Christ, not works or rituals
- **Grace Alone:** salvation is God's free gift, not earned
- Two sacraments: Baptism and Communion; Christ truly present in Communion
- No purgatory, no papal authority, clergy may marry

---

## Roman Catholicism

Claims the Pope holds supreme authority over Scripture. Adds doctrines absent from the Bible: purgatory, indulgences, mandatory clerical celibacy. Teaches salvation requires faith plus works plus church sacraments. Split from the Eastern church in 1054; provoked the Reformation in 1517.

---

## Anglicanism

Created in 1534 when Henry VIII broke from Rome for political reasons. Structured like Catholicism with Protestant doctrine, deliberately ambiguous on most theological disputes. Authority rests with the Crown rather than the Pope or Scripture alone.

---

## Eastern Orthodoxy

Split from Rome in 1054 over papal authority and the *Filioque* clause. Governed by councils of patriarchs rather than a single head. Emphasizes *theosis* (gradual union with God) as the path to salvation. Retains seven sacraments and ancient liturgical tradition. No purgatory, but bishops must be celibate monks.

# Other World Religions

## Islam

Founded by Muhammad in 7th century Arabia. Monotheistic; the Quran is the final and perfect word of God, superseding all prior scripture. Five Pillars: declaration of faith, prayer five times daily, almsgiving, fasting during Ramadan, pilgrimage to Mecca. No clergy, no sacraments. Splits into Sunni (majority, authority through community consensus) and Shia (authority through Ali's bloodline). Rejects the Trinity and divinity of Christ.

## Judaism

The original Abrahamic faith. The Torah (first five books of Moses) is the foundation; the Talmud provides rabbinic interpretation. Awaits the Messiah, who has not yet come. Does not proselytize. Splits into Orthodox (strict observance), Conservative (moderate), and Reform (liberal). Rejects Christ as Messiah.

## Hinduism

World's oldest living religion; no single founder. Polytheistic in practice, though philosophically many traditions are monist (all is one divine reality, *Brahman*). Sacred texts include the Vedas and Upanishads. Karma and reincarnation are central; the soul cycles through lives until liberation (*moksha*). No single authority or creed.

## Buddhism

Founded by Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha) in 5th century BCE India. Not theistic in origin; focuses on ending suffering through the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path. Goal is *nirvana*, the cessation of the cycle of rebirth. Major splits: Theravada (strict original teachings), Mahayana (broader path, includes Zen and Pure Land), Vajrayana (Tibetan, ritual heavy).

## Sikhism

Founded by Guru Nanak in 15th century Punjab. Monotheistic; believes in one formless God. Rejects caste, priesthood, and idol worship. Ten human Gurus; the eternal living Guru is the sacred scripture, the *Guru Granth Sahib*. Emphasizes equality, service, and honest living.

## Taoism

Chinese philosophical and religious tradition rooted in Laozi's *Tao Te Ching*. The *Tao* (the Way) is the fundamental principle underlying all reality: formless, eternal, beyond description. Emphasizes living in harmony with nature, simplicity, and non action (*wu wei*). Later developed a rich pantheon of deities and ritual practice.

## Confucianism

Chinese ethical and philosophical system based on the teachings of Confucius (551 to 479 BCE). Not a religion in the Western sense: no deity, no afterlife doctrine. Focuses on social harmony, filial piety, ritual propriety, and moral self cultivation. Profoundly shaped Chinese, Korean, Japanese, and Vietnamese civilization.

## Shinto

Indigenous religion of Japan. Polytheistic; venerates *kami* (spirits) present in natural phenomena, ancestors, and sacred places. No founder, no fixed scripture. Closely tied to Japanese national identity. Ritual purity and harmony with nature are central.

## Zoroastrianism

One of the oldest monotheistic religions, founded by the prophet Zoroaster in ancient Persia. Worships Ahura Mazda (Wise Lord) against the evil spirit Angra Mainyu. Emphasizes truth, righteousness, and free will. Heavily influenced later Judaism, Christianity, and Islam (concepts of heaven, hell, judgment, resurrection). Now a small community, mostly Parsis in India.

## Jainism

Ancient Indian religion emphasizing non violence (*ahimsa*) above all else. No creator God; the universe is eternal and self sustaining. The soul can achieve liberation (*moksha*) through strict ethical living and asceticism. Jain monks and nuns practice extreme non harm, including filtering water and sweeping the ground before stepping.

# Cults and Fringe Movements Active in 1913

## Mormonism

Founded 1830 by Joseph Smith in New York. New scripture (Book of Mormon), new prophets, and additional theology beyond the Bible. Plural marriage officially abandoned in 1890 but still practiced by splinter groups in 1913. Denies the Trinity in orthodox form.

## Jehovah's Witnesses (Bible Students)

Founded 1870s by Charles Taze Russell. Denied the Trinity, rejected blood transfusion doctrine, and predicted the world would end in 1914. No clergy hierarchy; door to door evangelism central to practice.

## Christian Science

Founded 1879 by Mary Baker Eddy. Teaches that physical illness and death are illusions; healing comes through prayer and correct spiritual understanding alone. Rejects conventional medicine entirely.

## Seventh-day Adventism

Founded 1863. Worships on Saturday, observes dietary laws from Leviticus, and holds imminent Second Coming as central doctrine.

## New Thought

Loose American movement teaching that the mind directly shapes physical reality. Positive thinking as spiritual force. No single founder or creed; spawned numerous offshoots.

## Theosophy

Founded 1875 by Helena Blavatsky. Blended Hinduism, Buddhism, and Western occultism. Claimed hidden masters guided humanity secretly from Tibet. Hugely influential on early 20th century intellectuals and artists.

## Anthroposophy

Founded 1912 by Rudolf Steiner, who broke from Theosophy. Spiritual science combining Western philosophy, mysticism, and esoteric Christianity. Later influenced Waldorf education and biodynamic farming.

## Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

Founded 1888 in London. Ritual magic, Kabbalah, tarot, and astrology. Produced Aleister Crowley, W.B. Yeats, and Arthur Machen before internal schisms destroyed it.

## Thelema

Founded 1904 by Aleister Crowley. Central law: "Do what thou wilt." Ceremonial magic, deeply anti Christian, drew from Egyptian religion and Western occultism. Crowley was calling himself the Great Beast 666 by 1913.

## Rosicrucianism

Ancient claimed origins, revived repeatedly through the 1800s. Secret brotherhood claiming alchemical, mystical, and healing knowledge passed down through hidden initiates.

## Spiritualism

Massive popular movement by 1913 across Britain and America. Claimed the dead communicate through mediums via séances. Attracted serious figures including Arthur Conan Doyle. No central organization; millions of adherents.

## Bahá'í Faith

Founded 1844 in Persia by the Báb, developed by Bahá'u'lláh. Teaches all major religions lead to one God; universal peace and unity of mankind. By 1913 actively spreading into Europe and North America.

## Völkisch Movements (Germany/Austria)

Blended Germanic paganism, racial mysticism, and anti Christian ideology. Drew on Norse mythology and pseudo occultism. Deeply nationalist. Would later feed directly into National Socialist ideology.

## Russian Sectarians

Radical dissenter sects from Russian Orthodoxy still active in 1913: Doukhobors (rejected church and state authority), Khlysts (ecstatic ritual, flagellation), and Skoptsy (practiced castration as spiritual purification). Persecuted by the Tsarist government.
