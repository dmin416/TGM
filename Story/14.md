# Chapter 14

Arc: Seville Counterattack

Date: 7 February 1914

---

*"All Highland mages, follow me!"*

Eldridge's cold voice carried across the camp.

Two other Highland mages and two mage apprentices followed at once without delay.

Eldridge looked at the two young apprentices and thought again of the apprentice killed by an enemy trap in the small building.

All three apprentices were seedlings he had chosen himself and trained himself.

He believed that with their talent they could in time reach his own level of achievement and widen his faction within the mage corps.

This deployment to Spain was also meant to temper his apprentices and add overseas service to their records.

That helped Highland mage apprentices win promotion.

Before this they had not even seen the enemy when one apprentice died in a trap.

For Eldridge, who had invested so much in them, that loss was severe.

He turned his head slightly toward his two remaining apprentices and spoke a warning.

*"Stay alert and stay careful. Do not grow careless like Brad. In an emergency you may withdraw from the fight. Do you understand?"*

*"Yes, Instructor."*

*"Yes, Instructor."*

As the five Highland mages prepared to move out the newly replenished Northumberland Fusiliers battalion also went forward on Colonel Henry's order.

The British expedition meant to crush the resistance inside Seville with absolute numbers.

Max was studying the distribution of both sides on his system map and preparing to chew off a British platoon through tight control when the map for the first time flashed a warning.

```
Warning! High threat unit approaching! Count: 5!
```

He shrank the map at once and saw five special markers for spellcasters appear among the red enemy placards and close in.

One icon carried a purple high risk mark.

*Damn it. Highland mages! Why have they come to the front line?*

Max had been concerned about the armored knights but the arrival of the Highland mages officially made the battle a nightmare.

The British had clearly been driven to desperation by the string of losses and had sent their casters forward.

Before he could react the five icons reached Seville's urban districts at an unreasonable speed.

The high risk mark for Highland Mage Corps senior instructor Eldridge drove in a near straight line into the interior of the city.

Max guessed at once that Eldridge was using the same flight spell Max had seen outside the walls before.

*What does he mean to do?*

Max stared at the map and tried to read the man's next move.

A second later Eldridge's icon halted near the tallest church in the city center.

Then a squad sized friendly placard on the church side turned grey.

A violent explosion and a tremor through the ground followed from far away.

**Boom rumble!**

A lookout soon reached Max with terror on his face.

*"Platoon leader! The church bell tower! A huge fireball fell from the sky and leveled it!"*

Max had expected as much.

That bell tower was the most important height and observation post in the city. It overlooked most of the districts and gave the garrison precious battlefield information.

Now it was gone.

The senior mage had meant to tear out the defenders' eyes and ears first.

*His next step is likely a decapitation strike.*

Max understood the intent at once.

Without the high ground the command post would lose much of its sense of what was happening inside the city.

A flying senior mage could cross line after line of defense with ease and deliver a devastating blow against brigade headquarters.

*"Run to brigade headquarters! Find a way to warn them that enemy senior mages are attempting a decapitation strike! Tell them to relocate at once!"*

Max shouted to the messenger at his side.

*"Yes, sir!"*

The messenger turned and ran without delay.

On the map the other two Highland mages and the two apprentices split apart and dropped onto separate lines of advance.

They clearly meant to use their magic to support the ground advance.

That did not match the usual practice of the Highland Mage Corps but it was happening all the same.

Fighting inside Seville turned fiercer than it had been at any point so far.

On a street held by the International Brigades a Highland mage descended from the sky into the midst of Northumberland Fusiliers soldiers.

He did not even seek cover. He stood in the center of the street with open arrogance.

*"Open fire! Kill the robed one!"*

International Brigade fighters in the buildings on both sides already knew British Highland mages were not invincible. They opened fire on him at once.

Every bullet stopped half a meter from his body against a translucent blue shield and threw ripples across its surface.

*"Damn it! That shield again!"*

Andrei saw the scene through binoculars and struck the window frame in fury.

The mage ignored the incoming fire. He only lifted his hand toward the building pouring the heaviest fire and chanted a few syllables.

A fireball the size of a human head formed before his palm and screamed away.

**Boom!**

The windows of a three story building burst open at once. Blast and flame swallowed the room.

A squad of International Brigade soldiers inside had no time to cry out before the spreading fire took them.

The same scene played out on another street held by German soldiers.

Two mage apprentices became the core of the attacking force.

Defenders kept firing from inside the buildings and even threw hand grenades and firebombs to concentrate fire on the two casters.

Though the infantry around them kept falling the two apprentices remained unharmed.

Rifle bullets, grenade fragments and the flames of firebombs all broke against the same seemingly fragile blue shield.

In street fighting it was hard to reach the density of hundreds of rifles firing together so Mage Armor was harder to break than it had been before.

One Highland mage apprentice cast *Life Sense* on a building whose windows still flashed with fire. In a flat voice he reported the defenders' positions to the British officer beside him.

*"Six on the second floor of the building on the left. Five on the third."*

That most basic spell, first developed in ancient times for exploration, had become a deadly advantage in the streets.

*"Riflemen suppress! Heavy armor, prepare to assault!"*

The British officer gave the order at once with precise intelligence in hand.

Northumberland Fusiliers soldiers began to pour fire into the windows of that building.

These elite men shot far better than the Royal Army soldiers who had come before them.

Though they all carried bolt action rifles their rate of fire was very fast. Dense bullet rain pinned the defenders behind the windows and left them no chance to answer.

**"Aaaaaah!"**

*"I want to go back."*

Bullets came through the windows and threw plaster dust into the air. The crackle of impacts filled the room.

German soldiers caught under that fire had never faced anything so terrible. Several dropped to the floor in fear and tried to crawl toward the door.

Heavy soldiers in full plate armor took the chance. They strode forward through scattered fire with greatswords in hand.

Their two handed blades smashed through sealed doors and windows. Infantry behind them poured into the building.

Soon fierce gunfire and screams sounded inside.

Other heavy soldiers advanced straight into fire from other directions and used their tools to tear down barbed wire and street obstacles.

At that sight a 77 millimeter field gun hidden in rubble at a street corner was pushed out by the artillery team posted there.

The crew sheltered behind the gun shield from incoming bullets. The men swiftly loaded a high explosive shell and fired by direct lay at once. The burst covered the whole area.

Several heavy soldiers working the obstacle line fell at once and did not rise again.

Their deaths did not frighten the rest.

More heavy soldiers with cold eyes came up from behind and continued the work.

They moved like war machines without feeling and cleared the path ahead with their own flesh and blood.

For British soldiers who had once broken other imperial armies on the Thin Red Line skill and will both stood far above the Royal Army troops who had fought here earlier.

Pressure on the defenders rose at once.

On his map Max saw several friendly positions near 3rd Platoon forced to give ground.

The line was tearing open bit by bit.

*"Platoon leader! The International Brigades on the left cannot hold! They request support!"*

*"2nd Platoon on the right reports a Highland mage on their front. Casualties are heavy!"*

Messengers kept bringing bad news and Max's face grew darker.

He looked at the map and saw red enemy arrows closing on 3rd Platoon's sector from both sides in a pincer.

If he did not act soon 3rd Platoon would be cut off and encircled.

*"We cannot wait any longer!"*

Max decided at once.

He seized his rifle and shouted to the two squads of reserves still with him. *"Follow me! We are going to put out the fire!"*

He led fewer than twenty men out of the temporary hideout at once. They moved through the buildings toward the hardest pressed ground on the left.

Corporal Baumann and the remnants of his two squads met them there. Twenty nine rifles stood ready between the rubble and the building shadows.

Max kept the party moving at speed.

His plan was clear. The combat power the British had shown made a frontal fight suicide.

To ease pressure on the flank the attack had to be interrupted.

In urban fighting the surest way to break pace was to kill their commanders.

This era's habit of officers pushing to the forward line gave Max that opening.

Max reached the left flank in short order. Fighting there had already turned white hot.

International Brigade fighters used broken buildings and street barricades against waves of Northumberland Fusiliers. The struggle was desperate.

The outlook on the left was grim.

Even with street fighting theory behind them the International Brigades, the National Army and German soldiers had never trained for this kind of war.

Without the defender's natural edge they would not have fought much better than the enemy in the streets.

The British had already seized the first row of buildings. Both sides fought room by room.

Max did not rush into the open fight. He took his men up a side three story building in silence.

From that height he had a clear view of the British mass in the street below.

He set his rifle on the sill and searched the crowd with care.

He found his mark quickly.

A British officer with a necktie was waving a small revolver and shouting orders.

In this era an officer was easy to spot.

They wore sharper uniform, carried sword or pistol and usually gave orders from the rear of the cluster.

Max drew a breath and squeezed the trigger with a steady hand.

**Bang!**

The sound vanished in the uproar of the battle.

The British officer who had been directing the fight jerked once. Blood burst from his chest. He stared down in disbelief then collapsed straight and still.

The soldiers around him went rigid.

Before they recovered several marksmen beside Max each picked a target and fired in turn.

**Bang! Bang! Bang!**

More men who looked like officers or senior NCOs dropped where they stood.

The rest fired at whatever targets they could see.

At once that slice of British line fell into chaos.

British tradition at the lower levels was rigid. Initiative among riflemen ran far lower than on the German side. Most fights still waited on an officer's direct order.

With several officers down the soldiers did not know whether to press or pull back. They stalled in place.

International Brigade fighters saw the opening and struck back. They drove the Northumberland men down the street.

*"Good work. Keep at it. Shoot the ones with neckties."*

Max spoke in a low voice.

Max could feel his body and mind growing stronger. He could feel the land and the air and the world moving around him more acutely than before. Something still felt held back in him yet he did not mind it. He kept his wits about him as he shifted along the wall and avoided getting shot.

His party hunted from cover and killed enemy leaders with cold precision.

Their fire soon gave the enemy a rough idea where they hid.

Riflemen sent a storm of bullets at the building. Plaster showered from the walls.

After several rounds punched the window frame Max and the others kept their heads down.

Worse still the International Brigade soldiers on the left flank began to fall back under losses.

Max could not blame them. Volunteers had come from across the world. Most had never seen military training before they reached Spain.

They had received less than three months of recruit drill after issue of arms before they were thrown into combat.

Set aside morale and grit and their soldier skills were hardly above the Royal Army's pressed men.

As they withdrew Max saw on the map that 3rd Platoon's sector was tightening into ground that would cut the platoon off and leave it encircled from three sides.

That was the shape Max hated most.

If the enemy moved even a little faster the platoon could be sealed inside a ring.

*We have to pull back.*

Max decided without pause.

He sent the last messenger still with him.

*"Find Sergeant Klaus. Collect every section you can, including the heavy machine gun team and the field guns. Abandon these positions at once and fall back to the third defensive line inside the city. I will hold the enemy here. Move."*

The messenger left at a run.

Max made a harder choice on his own.

He scanned the men with him. They included Corporal Baumann and the two battered squads he still commanded. Twenty nine rifles remained in the group.

*"Corporal Baumann."*

*"Sir."*

*"Do you dare try something big with me?"*

Max looked at him. A reckless light burned in Max's eyes.

Corporal Baumann showed his teeth in a grin.

*"Whatever you say, sir. We will do it."*

*"Good man."*

Max clapped his shoulder hard then pulled a rough map of Seville from his orderly's satchel.

*"We are not falling back with the rest. We go inward. Pierce behind this enemy column and hit them hard. Then we circle back to the line."*

*"Pierce behind them?"*

Baumann and his men did not yet understand the word Max used for the maneuver but excitement spread through them.

They had learned that marching after Max meant a lesson the British would not forget.

Max led the twenty nine men into the maze of ruins. He used the clean sight from his system map to choose each gap between wall and rubble.

Meanwhile Sergeant Klaus received the runner's order.

The steady platoon sergeant frowned when he heard Max's plan.

He knew how reckless the choice was. Failure would mean the small party never returned.

He also knew it was the only way to buy the main body time to disengage.

He felt that only Max could carry a fight this dangerous.

*"Understood. Execute the order."*

Klaus did not hesitate. He began to move the platoon at once.

He passed word to every section he could reach. Each fell back under covering fire in turn.

The heavy machine gun crew broke the gun and tripod apart and withdrew under infantry cover.

The two precious guns went with the draft teams the gunners harnessed again. Wheels turned toward the inner city.

The retreat stayed tight and nervous but Klaus kept it orderly.

While Klaus pulled the platoon back Max drove his twenty nine men deeper through the ruins and the building shadows.

On the map the red and blue placards overlapped in a jagged tangle. The violence of the fight showed at a glance.

British soldiers and Royal Army soldiers had never fought a war in the streets this tangled.

They lacked the experience and never thought to hold every building they had cleared.

In their minds victory meant pushing down the street and killing every enemy in sight.

That blunt method might work in open country. In the streets it left a fatal gap.

They emptied one building and ran for the next without leaving anyone to hold what they had taken.

That gap gave Max his chance.

He led the party through zones the enemy treated as safe and slid toward their rear without being seen.

Max soon found a set of targets on the map that suited him.

Fewer than a hundred British infantry were advancing down one street. They looked like the rear guard of a whole company.

*"Those are the ones."*

Max checked the distance between that block and the next British placard and chose to cut the isolated party first.

He brought the squad to a point ahead and to the side of their route and found a cluster of buildings still mostly intact.

*"Corporal Baumann."*

*"Sir."*

*"Take two squads and set ambush on the second floor on both sides of the street. Open fire when they close. Do not stay in the buildings after the first bursts. Fall back behind this house and wait for me to link up with you."*

Max pointed to a rally mark on his paper map and gave the orders fast.

*"I will take the rest into this row on their right and build long flank fire."*

The plan was a classic L shaped ambush.

The short leg pinned the column with the main burst of fire.

The long leg struck from the flank and killed in the open.

*"Is that clear?"*

*"Clear, sir."*

Corporal Baumann nodded hard and vanished into the rubble with his men.

In the smashed city wreckage and street barricades let Baumann's party move without being seen.

Max took the remaining soldiers into a broken building on the enemy flank.

German riflemen crept to the first and second floor windows with their weapons ready.

They held their breath. Boots grinding stone in the street and loud talk from British soldiers came through the walls.

Every man waited on a signal.

Gunfire opened from the buildings where Corporal Baumann lay in wait.

**Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang!**

Bullets from Baumann's ambush cut down the leading British soldiers at once.

The sudden fire threw the British party into disorder.

*"Ambush! Ahead!"*

*"Cover! Get to cover!"*

Every eye turned toward the fire in front.

They raised rifles toward the sound and pressed against the walls on both sides of the street for shelter.

They did not see that the instinct to hug the walls drove them into the killing ground.

Max raised his rifle.

He aimed at an officer in the column who was waving his arms and shouting.

**Bang!**

His first shot was the signal. The round punched through the officer's chest.

The other soldiers in the building fired through broken windows at every target they could see.

The crossfire locked in at once.

British soldiers who were still sliding toward the walls for cover died before they found the ambush ahead.

The column went numb with shock.

Fire seemed to come from every direction. They tried to shoot back and could not place a muzzle in the dark.

Dark rooms and bright street gave Max's men the advantage of seeing without being seen.

British soldiers fired at muzzle flash in black window frames and wasted their rounds.

German riflemen in the rooms finished three or four volleys each.

Max did not cling to the kill. The map showed part of the main body ahead peeling off to reinforce the rear guard.

*"Enough. Pull out."*

If he and his men had carried MP18 submachine guns or denser automatic fire he would have pressed for more.

Infantry kit in this period was still a bolt action rifle and their hand grenades were nearly spent.

Max did not need a massacre. Wounded men in a town full of frightened troops dragged an advance worse than corpses.

A corpse could lie where it fell. A wounded man became another corpse if no one stopped to help.

Hit and run. Never stay for a second fight. That was the core of city guerrilla war.

Max led the party out through the rear doors of the buildings and into the rubble again.

The reinforcement column that had rushed back found an empty street.

They saw only bodies and wounded men. No attacker remained in sight.

Before they could catch their breath Max found them again.

He linked with Corporal Baumann and slipped to the flank of the relief column without a sound.

Then he used the same trick once more.

Those British soldiers had run to help friends under fire. Their column was tight and no one watched the sides.

Max's party fired from the flank and dropped a large swath of them.

Two strikes from the rear left the British commander for this sector unable to read the map in his head.

He roared at the officers beside him. *"What is happening?! How are there enemies behind us?! Did we not clear this street?! Did these Germans crawl out of the ground?!"*

He could not understand how beaten defenders kept reappearing behind his line.

Max's raids with full map sight did not break the enemy column but they slowed several hundred British soldiers step by step.

The British never knew how many Germans hid around them.

They had to stop, detach men to move the wounded and send parties to comb the blocks behind them for the rat column Max led.

That bought the retreating main body of 3rd Platoon precious minutes.

More British units fed into the sector and Max's party began to feel a net close around them.

At that point Max knew the infiltration fight had run its course.

Sergeant Klaus had brought the main platoon to the third defensive line and called the roll.

A full 3rd Platoon with two extra rifle squads attached now counted only thirty six men.

Klaus did not know how many men Max had taken but the number could not be large and the platoon leader had not returned.

The sergeant could not sit still.

*"I have to bring the platoon leader in."*

After a short struggle with the choice he left the line under other rifles and took one squad toward the sector where Max had been fighting.

Max had the survivors in ambush among the corpses and wounded from the last strike.

He meant to use the bodies as bait and spring one more trap on any party that came to help them.

After that last strike he could break out through the gap the fighting had torn in the British net.

The system map showed a search party of serious size moving toward his mark.

*"Stand to. Hold fire until they close."*

Max kept his voice low and checked the chamber on his rifle with the bolt half open.

Every man gripped his weapon and felt his pulse climb.

As the enemy neared the kill zone Max saw something wrong in the column.

Between ordinary British riflemen walked two robed casters and two plate armored heavy soldiers of abnormal height.

The map tags on the two robed figures read Highland Mage apprentice.

*They are mixing elite men into the search teams already?*

The apprentices changed the risk of the ambush.

Max did not know what spells they carried but instinct and experience said not to fight without a clear edge.

He was about to order a pull back when the two apprentices reached the wounded and the dead and stopped.

Each raised a hand toward the buildings on either side of the street and spoke a short spell.

The apprentice facing Max's hideout went pale.

*Life Sense* had shown more than twenty clear signatures in a ruin that had looked empty.

*"Ambush! Many men in the building!"*

He screamed the warning.

Max had been watching from concealment. The change on the apprentice's face and the shout told him the trap was blown.

He rolled up and fired without another thought.

**Bang!**

German rifles in the building answered his shot and poured every round they had into the street.

British soldiers in the open dropped in a wide swath before they could find cover.

The two apprentices and the two heavy soldiers survived behind *Mage Armor* and thick plate.

*"They are in that building! Go in and kill them!"*

A surviving apprentice shrieked the order and the remaining infantry rushed Max's position.

Northumberland Fusiliers showed their training in that moment.

The column had just taken heavy loss but officers and the apprentices pulled them into order. Two heavy soldiers led the charge into the building.

Another squad crept toward the rear of the British party at the sound of the guns.

Sergeant Klaus led that party.

*"The British are assaulting that building. Our men must be inside."*

A squad leader beside him spoke in a low voice.

*"Most friendlies are already on the third line. Whoever is still fighting here must be the platoon leader."*

Klaus watched muzzle flash in the windows and the enemy rush at the doors and read the field at once.

*"Prepare to attack. Hit them from behind and hit them hard."*

Inside the building Max saw the enemy close and roared.

*"Grenades!"*

The grenadiers pulled pins and threw the last three grenades they still carried from the second floor windows.

**Boom! Boom! Boom!**

Three bursts tore through the British cluster in the street.

Heat and steel cut down more men.

The two heavy soldiers at the front staggered in the blast and felt their limbs go numb.

They still drove forward on will and enchanted plate and reached the door.

**Bang!**

One heavy soldier kicked the sagging door open and infantry poured in behind him.

They regretted the rush as soon as they crossed the threshold.

They regretted obeying the two apprentices.

The heavy men did not carry sword and shield. They carried two handed greatswords.

The narrow rooms would not let those blades swing.

They reversed the weapons at once and gripped the blunt spine and the pommel to strike with guard and counterweight at close range.

The best marksmen among the Germans fired from corners and furniture at the helmets of the heavy soldiers until sparks jumped and their heads rang.

The rest poured fire into the ordinary infantry behind them.

At that range there was no plan left on either side.

Both sides killed by reflex.

Shouts, gunfire and wild screams filled the tight space.

*"Two more grenades!"*

Corporal Baumann shouted from the stairwell while he reloaded and two more grenades flew into the fight.

The blasts stunned even the German soldiers on the first floor.

When the smoke cleared no British rifleman in the room could still stand.

The two heavy soldiers still lived but they swayed when they walked and their plate was dented and scarred by bullets.

*"Kill them!"*

Several German soldiers left cover with empty rifles and fixed bayonets because they had no time to load again.

Men who had survived the earlier blade work in the city had told the platoon how Max had killed plate with the bayonet.

They drove the points at armpit, throat and every gap the armor did not cover.

The heavy soldiers knocked down a few Germans with pommel strikes before their strength failed and they fell.

*"They are down!"*

Before the survivors could breathe two head sized fireballs screamed in through the windows.

*"Down!"*

Max had time for only that warning.

The fireballs burst in the center of the floor.

Heat and blast swallowed the first story and tore away part of the second floor.

On the map the two squad icons on the first floor turned grey at once.

Twelve men died in that room.

Corporal Baumann had been on the stairwell and lived.

Max had eighteen men left counting himself.

Outside the two apprentices began another spell to finish the building.

Dense rifle fire cracked behind them.

**Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang!**

Klaus and his squad had come up on the British rear and opened fire.

British soldiers screening the apprentices dropped where they stood.

The two apprentices spun toward the new fire and saw German rifles rise from the rubble behind them.

They thought the trap had closed around them from both sides.

The two apprentices tried to activate *Mage Armor* but felt nothing happen. They had already run out of mana.

They had used up everything they had in the earlier fights and had not kept track. They could barely force a cantrip through in their current state.

Fear took them and their instructor's warning before battle returned in their ears.

*"Withdraw! Withdraw at once!"*

They turned to run with the last few British soldiers at their backs.

Max read panic in how the two mages clung to the withdrawing British and ran without a backward glance toward the ambush. On the system map they were no longer marked as dangerous enemies.

After hours of fighting in the streets he knew this might be the only chance to end them.

If those two walking bombs escaped they would kill more Germans later.

*"Do not let them run!"*

Max's eyes were red. He slid down the broken slope from the second floor and burst into the street.

He shouted toward Klaus as he ran. *"Mind friendly fire! Mind friendly fire!"*

Klaus heard the platoon leader and understood.

*"Hold your fire on the front! Cover the platoon leader!"*

Both squads chased the fleeing party.

A grenadier threw the last grenade on the run.

**Boom!**

The burst knocked the rear of the group off their feet.

Before they could rise Max and the soldiers were on them with bayonets fixed.

*"Kill!"*

The soldiers kept Max's advice in mind.

At the last step before blade contact they fired the last round in the chamber instead of thrusting first.

**Bang! Bang!**

At that range they did not need to aim.

Two British soldiers screening the apprentices fell at once.

Then the bayonets went in.

Two more British soldiers tried to block Max at the front.

He caught the first in the throat with a quick thrust up into his neck.

Blood from the dying man sprayed across Max's face.

The last soldier screamed and rushed to trade his life for a kill.

Max stopped, raised his rifle and fired.

**Bang!**

The round took him between the eyes. He died still wondering why a blood covered officer had paused to shoot.

Max ejected the case and worked the bolt with a blank face. He looked at the two apprentices as if they were already dead while other Germans passed him at speed.

The apprentices glanced back in time to see his eyes and their fear sharpened.

These Highland apprentices had never imagined enemy infantry would reach them.

In their training a mage stood behind an army, spoke a spell and erased whole ranks with grace.

Now they rolled in mud and blood with steel at their throats.

*Mage Armor* was gone. They used *Mage Hand* at the two fastest Germans and shoved them aside.

Max and Corporal Baumann slipped past the staggering Germans the shove had thrown wide and closed on the robed pair.

Max and Corporal Baumann drove S98/05 bayonets into them from left and right.

Cold steel went through fine robes and soft flesh.

One apprentice stared at the blade in his belly and coughed blood.

Max twisted the rifle and ground the bayonet in the wound.

Corporal Baumann copied the motion and both apprentices went limp.

The last British soldiers fell to follow up shots.

Max leaned on his rifle and fought for breath over the wreckage in the room.

He knew they could not stay.

He dropped to one knee, searched the two apprentice bodies with one hand on the weapon and pulled out two heavy books that looked like spell notes and several bottles and packets of powder and liquid.

```
Spell tab unlocked. Review at earliest opportunity.
Personal training manual (basic) x2 acquired.
```

The tone sounded in his head but he had no time to read the screens. He shoved the loot into his satchel.

*"Clear the field! Take any weapon worth carrying and move. We link with 3rd Platoon now!"*

Max stood and gave the last order to the survivors.

Before long the party that had walked the edge of death vanished again into the broken streets of Seville.
