# Chapter 17

Arc: Seville Counterattack

Date: Late January 1914

---

A flood of information flowed into Max the moment he chose to use the two basic manuals from the apprentice bodies.

Knowledge of meditation, spell structure and how magic worked in this world burned into Max's memory without him having to read the manuals himself.

Max felt his body draw in something from the air around him.

Tiny traces he could not see settled on his skin and his mind felt clearer and stronger than it had ever been in this life.

```
Use successful.
Caster Level: 7
Highest ring available: 3rd
Schools available: Abjuration, Conjuration, Divination, Enchantment, Illusion, Transmutation
```

Max opened his eyes and felt the changes in his body and mind.

Max could feel all around him what had been hidden from his senses, the energy that infused his body and empowered him since the first time he leveled up.

He saw motes of light for every element and every thread of existence, the energy of the universe and the laws that sustained it, the breath of creation and something he could not name.

Max thanked God for this glorious gift. Though many suffered, it was their own greed that drove them. Fortunately he did not need to worry about all of that anymore.

The new prompts pulled him straight back to the Spell tab.

The system treated cantrips as unlimited once learned.

The rules on the tab followed rings and stars the way the briefing books described.

A mage carved patterns onto the soul's surface and could cast spells for each specialization they had developed.

The two basic manuals had instilled much magic up to the third ring of magic in him.

Max could inscribe on the core for every ring of magic now open to him.

Max looked inward and observed his soul. He could feel the presence of the system guiding his attention, like a tutorial making it easier to see where to place spells.

The tab indicated that it would record what spells he had engraved and what spells were in the repository.

Max knew that beyond his own recovery the system would reset his spell usage every morning for him.

Max wondered whether enemy mage spell refreshment followed how long they had slept or a fixed reset at a certain hour each day or if it was based on mana.

A night raid or constant shelling might keep them from refilling their mana.

He would have to check that later.

Max looked through the spells the apprentices knew.

Max saw a few familiar spells and others he had never seen them use in the fight. He selected a few right away. *Blade Ward*, *Mage Hand*, *Life Detect* and *Mending* went into four of his seven cantrip slots.

In Seville you could shoot at one enemy and then wrestle with another in melee.

*Blade Ward* was for stopping blades and little else in the books. It was not a dead thing in his hands. Used right it was fluid and flexible. He could redirect a bullet or add speed that carried it past him instead of into him. At his level it was already strong enough to stop a bayonet or an arrow. As he grew stronger the uses would only multiply.

*Mage Hand* could stop an opponent's grenade in the air, jam a gun from a short distance, or cheat in melee when he needed a trick. It could lift a grenade from his belt or work the bolt while both hands stayed on the Gewehr.

*Life Detect* could sense energy in his vicinity, a man behind plaster, mana on a charm, anything that burned brighter than cold stone.

*Mending* could fix tools and weapons in the field.

It could even service a rifle.

He was tired of cleaning the Gewehr by hand.

Max considered the spell *Light*, but unlike *Blade Ward*, which was surprisingly simple in design and open in its applications, *Light* was shockingly complex. It drew ambient mana from the surroundings to last for an hour while providing light. It was not a spell he could release all at once as a flashbang.

After that Max looked deeper inside himself. He had felt the influx of energy ever since he leveled up and it did not feel like it came from the system. He mobilized the mana that had accumulated in his body, circulated it through his limbs and explored what secrets it held.

Max saw the traces of a soul that had merged into his own, Celtic and druidic heritage and access to the nature spells of this world.

He injected soul power into those shards and empowered the ancestral memories buried deep in them.

He saw the secrets of druidcraft, how to command the elements and turn plants into weapons.

Max activated *Druidcraft*, *Poison Spray* and *Plant Manipulation* from what he saw, filling the last three cantrip slots. He would save manipulating the elements for when he had more slots to fill.

Max saw that each aspect of nature had its own laws governing it.

On the back of his neck Max felt the druid circle that gave him control over his own body. Around his soul he could see another pair of circles orbiting there, deepening his connection to everything around him.

On his forehead he found the circle that showed him the world in a sphere around him, everything within about a hundred meters at once.

When his attention reached his feet he could see Celtic markings at his heels drawing energy into his body, a circle of the land that continually replenished him.

On the balls of his feet he found more circles like the ones at his heels but pushing energy into his surroundings and changing it to his comfort. The stones on the ground felt less sharp and easier to be on.

Max flexed his muscles and felt them draw nutrients and reinforce themselves, his bones strengthening to match. He channeled mana into the Circle of the Moon on his neck and felt it strengthen the cells of his body even further.

Max pulled out his bayonet and played with it lightly. It was hard to push the point through his skin without real force.

He grew hungrier by the second as his body grew stronger and stronger. He quickly stopped improving his body lest he break down his brain and become a simpleton.












Max did not need to bind a school of magic. He closed that prompt and went to the spell list instead.

He wanted to stay alive before he figured out how to kill people more efficiently.










He marked eight 1st ring spells and two at 2nd ring in the book for inscription.

1st ring: *Shield*, *Mage Armor*, *Feather Fall*, *Longstrider*, *Disguise Self*, *Sleep*, *Life Sense*, *Find Familiar*.

2nd ring: *Invisibility*, *Misty Step*.

If a fight turned bad he would run.

Rifles and machine guns would do the killing.

Daily stars were too scarce for a line soldier who fought every day.

Most enemies in front of him were still riflemen.

A pistol or a Gewehr or a machine gun would outlast any burst of magic he could afford.

Max was about to commit the list to his spellbook when a new prompt appeared.

```
About to begin learning the first spell. Completion will add it to your spellbook.
Ensure enough uninterrupted time. Outside disturbance will break progress.
```

Max stared at the text.

He had assumed the system would fill the book the way it prepared his stars for the day.

The system intended to teach him for real.

*"So I have to learn it?"*

The company was in rest.

Sentries were set.

Nothing should interrupt him.

Max started with *Shield*.

A three dimensional spell model hung in front of him with layers of notation wrapped around it.

He regretted the choice at once.

The diagram moved like a lecture animation while a flat voice explained structure, stress points and failure modes.

It felt like advanced mathematics on a battlefield he could still smell on his coat.

The progress bar in his vision had not moved after a quarter hour of listening.

The system wanted him to understand the model, not tap a button and move on.

*"It really is a class."*

*"I am not doing this."*

Max tried to back out.

There was no exit control.

He tried again and again.

The learning state held him.

Max lay back on his bunk and forced himself to listen.

At least he was horizontal.

Sergeant Klaus finished the last walk of the night with Corporal Baumann and thought he should look in on the platoon leader.

Klaus had questions from the fight that Max could answer.

He straightened his tunic at the door and leaned his rifle against the wall.

The door was not latched and lamplight showed through the crack.

Klaus guessed the platoon leader was still awake.

Klaus pushed the door open and saw Max on his bunk with his hands folded on his stomach.

Max's eyes were closed and his breathing was even.

*"Platoon leader?"*

Klaus kept his voice low.

Max did not answer.

Klaus was not surprised.

The man had barely slept in two days.

Klaus decided the questions could wait until morning.

He crossed the room and blew out the lamp.

He pulled the door shut with care.

*"Rest well, platoon leader."*

*Do not go, Klaus. Shake me awake.*

Klaus could not hear him.

Max had felt a flash of hope when the door opened.

The lamp went out and the latch clicked and the lesson pulled him under again.

Sometimes a thoughtful sergeant was the worst luck in the world.

Max stayed inside the study trance until sunrise ended the session by time limit.

Max sat up on his bunk looking hollowed out, as if he had been shut in a room all night with no way out.

The system had at least let his body sleep while his mind worked.

The progress bar for *Shield* stood at fifty two percent.

He understood how the model was built even though he had not finished the course.

Max sat until his head cleared.

He took Captain Hauser's watch from his coat and saw it was still before the morning meal call.

Max stood, worked his shoulders and drank water for his dry throat.

He found a quiet spot outside and opened the Intel tab.

New red marks had appeared after he became a caster.

He skimmed entries on magic in this world.

The piece he wanted was titled Holy Britannian Empire mage corps composition.

```
Holy Britannian Empire active mages: 642
Rings 3 and below: 72%
Rings 4 through 6: 25%
Rings 7 and above: 3%
Evocation: 83%
Transmutation: 11%
All other schools: 6%
```

*"So the Highland Mage Corps is almost all evocation."*

Max had not expected so many evokers to dominate the roster.

The intel summaries explained the habit.

Holy Britannia had used mages as fragile artillery for centuries.

After the Spell Plague forced high ring work into a single school the corps trained evokers to drop area fire from behind the line.

The usual pattern was lift, bomb the formation and let infantry finish a broken enemy.

Britain's pool of talent was the largest in Europe so the method kept winning in the age of musket and rifle alike.

That was why Eldridge and his apprentices had fought like men reading one script.

Sergeant Klaus came up the street early with worry on his face.

He bent close to Max and spoke barely above a breath.

*"Platoon leader, battalion has a rumor and nobody knows if it is true."*

*"What rumor?"*

*"Madrid may have fallen."*

Max straightened on the stone where he sat.

*"How reliable is it? Where did you hear it?"*

He hoped it was only a rumor.

*"Battalion headquarters is spreading it. Half the company is whispering already."*

Klaus did not look any calmer.

*"The officers are not saying anything. Their faces look like it is true."*

Max went quiet.

Bad news always ran faster than good news in an army and it was usually right.

Madrid was the capital of the Kingdom of Aragon and the most important city on the National Army side.

The best National Army units and the International Brigades had held it until now.

If the capital was gone the Saxon detachment in Spain was in mortal danger.

Max looked at the men around him.

Most had only just woken into a short peace after the counterattack.

They sat in small groups cleaning weapons or staring at the cook fire while they waited for breakfast.

They did not know a larger storm was forming.

*"Go back to 3rd Company. Watch the men. If anyone asks tell them nothing is confirmed until orders arrive. No more private talk. Anyone who disobeys faces field punishment."*

Max needed his own house steady first.

*"Yes, platoon leader."*

Klaus saluted and left at a quick pace.

Max opened the map alone.

The view still stopped at the edge of Seville.

The rest of Aragon and Madrid lay in fog.

Max decided to walk to battalion headquarters and learn what he could.

He found the command post in a house that still had four walls.

Guards stood at the door and the mood was tighter than in the resting lines.

Max did not go inside.

He paced nearby and glanced through the doorway when it opened.

Officers in the battalion knew his face by now.

He hoped to catch someone he could speak with.

Major Thomas came out after a few minutes.

His arm was still in a sling and bandages wrapped his head.

His eyes were more tired than they had been in the fighting.

The battalion commander had come out for air and caught Max hovering at the edge of the yard.

*"Second Lieutenant Max."*

Major Thomas waved him over.

*"Sir."*

Max ran up and stood at attention.

*"What are you skulking around for?"*

Major Thomas looked him over.

The tone was hard but the look was not angry.

*"I nearly had the guard take you until I saw who it was."*

Max scratched his head and tried a sheepish smile.

*"Sir, I came to hear the latest."*

Max asked outright.

*"Sir, there is talk about Madrid. Is any of it true?"*

Major Thomas's face turned graver.

He did not answer at once.

Then he met Max's eyes and nodded once.

Max felt his stomach drop.

*"The situation is bad."*

Major Thomas kept his voice low.

*"It may be worse than Madrid alone."*

He clapped Max on the shoulder.

*"Go back and do your duty. Whatever comes next we are soldiers of the German Empire. We obey orders."*

*"Yes, sir."*

Max saluted and turned away.

The moment Max was out of the major's sight a string of tones rang in his head.

```
Aragon theater intelligence updated.
Battle of Madrid concluded. National Army and International Brigade main forces destroyed.
Royal Army follow on plan extrapolated.
```

Max found a corner away from the company and opened the panel.

A red mark burned on the Intel tab.

The map had changed.

Before this only Seville and the ground around it had been clear.

Now most of the Kingdom of Aragon showed on the board.

The icon for Madrid sat entirely under enemy red.

The few friendly counters near the capital were fleeing south and east and most were marked routed or heavily cut up.

The Intel tab laid out the battle in short lines.

The Royal Army and the Holy Britannian expedition had massed overwhelming force with Highland mages and armored knights in front.

Under Lieutenant General Maximilian von Prittwitz the Saxon expedition had pulled its line back to preserve strength.

Saxon staff pulled back but the National Army and the International Brigades stayed to defend the city.

Max could not tell whether that was bad staff work or a refusal to retreat.

They fought hard.

Weight of numbers broke them anyway.

Max went cold when he saw the arrows the system drew next.

The enemy did not pause after taking the capital.

Main columns split toward the provinces of Cuenca and Valencia.

If both fell the whole Saxon expedition deep in Aragon would be a lone force with no land route home.

Strategic goals would not matter.

Survival would.

Max closed the panel and looked up at a gray sky.

He could only hope the units that had pulled out of Madrid early would slow the enemy long enough to keep the army from being surrounded.

War did not care what one lieutenant prayed for.

Two days later new word ran through the garrison in Seville.

Cuenca had fallen.

Valencia had fallen.

The land line back to the German Empire was cut.
