# Chapter 17

Arc: Seville Counterattack

Date: Late January 1914

---

A flood of information flowed into Max the moment he chose to use the two basic manuals from the apprentice bodies.

Knowledge of meditation, spell structure and how magic worked in this world burned into Max's memory without him having to read the manuals himself.

Max felt his body draw in something from the air around him.

Tiny traces he could not see settled on his skin and his mind felt clearer and stronger than it had ever been in this life.

```
Use successful.
Caster Level: 7
Highest ring available: 3rd
Schools available: Abjuration, Conjuration, Divination, Enchantment, Illusion, Transmutation
```

Max opened his eyes and felt the changes in his body and mind.

Max could feel all around him what had been hidden from his senses, the energy that infused his body and empowered him since the first time he leveled up.

He saw motes of light for every element and every thread of existence, the energy of the universe and the laws that sustained it, the breath of creation and something he could not name.

Max thanked God for this glorious gift. Though many suffered, it was their own greed that drove them. Fortunately he did not need to worry about all of that anymore.

The new prompts pulled him straight back to the Spell tab.

The system treated cantrips as unlimited once learned.

The rules on the tab followed rings and stars the way the briefing books described.

A mage carved patterns onto the soul's surface and could cast spells for each specialization they had developed.

The two basic manuals had instilled much magic up to the third ring of magic in him.

Max could inscribe on the core for every ring of magic now open to him.

Max looked inward and observed his soul. He could feel the presence of the system guiding his attention, like a tutorial making it easier to see where to place spells.

The tab indicated that it would record what spells he had engraved and what spells were in the repository.

Max knew that beyond his own recovery the system would reset his spell usage every morning for him.

Max wondered whether enemy mage spell refreshment followed how long they had slept or a fixed reset at a certain hour each day or if it was based on mana.

A night raid or constant shelling might keep them from refilling their mana.

He would have to check that later.

Max looked through the spells the apprentices knew.

Max saw a few familiar spells and others he had never seen them use in the fight. He selected a few right away. *Blade Ward*, *Mage Hand*, *Life Detect* and *Mending* went into four of his seven cantrip slots.

In Seville you could shoot at one enemy and then wrestle with another in melee.

*Blade Ward* was for stopping blades and little else in the books. It was not a dead thing in his hands. Used right it was fluid and flexible. He could redirect a bullet or add speed that carried it past him instead of into him. At his level it was already strong enough to stop a bayonet or an arrow. As he grew stronger the uses would only multiply.

*Mage Hand* could stop an opponent's grenade in the air, jam a gun from a short distance, or cheat in melee when he needed a trick. It could lift a grenade from his belt or work the bolt while both hands stayed on the Gewehr.

*Life Detect* could sense energy in his vicinity, a man behind plaster, mana on a charm, anything that burned brighter than cold stone.

*Mending* could fix tools and weapons in the field.

It could even service a rifle.

He was tired of cleaning the Gewehr by hand.

Max considered the spell *Light*, but unlike *Blade Ward*, which was surprisingly simple in design and open in its applications, *Light* was shockingly complex. It drew ambient mana from the surroundings to last for an hour while providing light. It was not a spell he could release all at once as a flashbang.

After that Max looked deeper inside himself. He had felt the influx of energy ever since he leveled up and it did not feel like it came from the system. He mobilized the mana that had accumulated in his body, circulated it through his limbs and explored what secrets it held.

Max saw the traces of a soul that had merged into his own, Celtic and druidic heritage and access to the nature spells of this world.

He injected soul power into those shards and empowered the ancestral memories buried deep in them.

He saw the secrets of druidcraft, how to command the elements and turn plants into weapons.

Max activated *Druidcraft*, *Poison Spray* and *Plant Manipulation* from what he saw, filling the last three cantrip slots. He would save manipulating the elements for when he had more slots to fill.

Max saw that each aspect of nature had its own laws governing it.

On the back of his neck Max felt the druid circle that gave him control over his own body. Around his soul he could see another pair of circles orbiting there, deepening his connection to everything around him.

On his forehead he found the circle that showed him the world in a sphere around him, everything within about a hundred meters at once.

When his attention reached his feet he could see Celtic markings at his heels drawing energy into his body, a circle of the land that continually replenished him.

On the balls of his feet he found more circles like the ones at his heels but pushing energy into his surroundings and changing it to his comfort. The stones on the ground felt less sharp and easier to be on.

Max flexed his muscles and felt them draw nutrients and reinforce themselves, his bones strengthening to match. He channeled mana into the Circle of the Moon on his neck and felt it strengthen the cells of his body even further.

Max pulled out his bayonet and played with it lightly. It was hard to push the point through his skin without real force.

He grew hungrier by the second as his body grew stronger and stronger. He quickly stopped improving his body lest he break down his brain and become a simpleton.

Max went back to the menu.

He saw an option to bind a school of magic as a specialization.

He considered evocation for power. He considered abjuration for defense.

He felt he would have enough of both in his spells already.

He scrolled lower and selected another school, a pathway he had always enjoyed playing with, the school of luck and fate, his 777, Divination.

```
Specialization bound: Divination
Portent: unlocked
The Third Eye: unlocked
```

Max smiled, feeling the power falling upon him, the world looking slightly richer and more detailed somehow.

He then opened the spell list.

He wanted to stay alive before he figured out how to kill people more efficiently or get killed messing around and getting distracted.

He looked through the many first ring spells available to him and wondered if Eldridge had anything special to add.

He activated the advanced manual and felt a wealth of knowledge wash over him.

```
Use successful.
Caster Level: 7
Spell knowledge through 7th ring added to repository
Spells through 3rd ring available
```

Before Max could select specific spells he felt the system engrave them neatly onto his soul, making them easy to access and use.

Unlike cantrips, which were limited in how they drew on ambient mana, spells were limited only by knowledge and the robustness of the soul.

His soul was massive, though it did not hold much mana compared to its capacity. Max did not mind.

Even though the system was helpful for the most part, it was a little too thoughtful. It did its very best to replenish Max's mana through the land and air but it only knew him as a normal man at the third ring and tried to maintain his mana safely at that level, ignoring the fact that Max could utilize much more.

But getting everything from the beginning was no fun unless the world was peaceful enough to wander and do whatever he pleased. With the level of conflict here he would have plenty of opportunities to level up, use his skills and enjoy the grind.

Max now had spells for all sorts of occasions ready at hand. The panel still capped his reserves at what it called safe for third ring. 

Even though Max had his soul, his knowledge and the system, it didn't make him omnipotent. He still had to be careful of danger.

Fortunately most enemies in front of Max were still human. They would die to a bullet and bullets were cheaper and more plentiful than the mana he could channel.

Max felt the power settle in his body and soul.

He stilled for a moment to luxuriate in it.

Max felt his stomach growl.

At this moment Sergeant Klaus and Corporal Baumann walked in from the last walk of the night.

Klaus and Baumann both heard Max's stomach growl before anyone spoke.

Klaus kept his voice low at the door.

*"Platoon leader, we had questions from the fight but we can talk about them over some food."*

Max sat up on his bunk at the offer and reached for his coat.

Max followed Klaus and Baumann out of the side room the clerks had cleared for him.

Klaus and Baumann led him into the street where Max had reported to Major Thomas that evening and walked toward the field kitchen.

Max kept between them over the same broken stone the remnant had marched. Shell fragments glittered in the filth and the dead still lay where the column had stepped around them on the way in.

Max smelled gunpowder and burned flesh still hanging over the municipal hall from the airship.

**Crackle Pop.**

Wood popped ahead where Captain Hauser's cooks had kept the kettle all day.

Max found the kitchen set in the lee of a grocer's shop whose upper storey the barrage had torn open. The cellar door still held and the cooks had dragged up olive oil, dried chickpeas, chorizo and morcilla the owners hadn't taken with them.

Max told Klaus to make sure they left money or goods behind.

A cook mashed the chickpeas and sausage into a thick stew over the banked coals and passed Max a heel of army bread and a crust scorched at one edge from sitting too close to the fire.

Klaus poured wine from a dusty bottle found in the grocer's cellar into tin cups.

Baumann laid out jamon and manchego from a charcutier's cold room in a shop the company had cleared that afternoon, the oil paper still white with plaster from the collapsed ceiling.

Max tore into the meal.

The stew was thick with paprika and pork fat, the chorizo sharp with smoke and the morcilla dark with blood and spice.

He broke the army bread through the scorched crust and soaked up the dregs until the mess tin shone.

The jamon sat salty on his tongue and the manchego left a blunt sheep tang behind it.

The wine was thin and tart and cut the grease.

Warmth spread through his belly while the power still sat settled in his soul.

Max practiced using his magic during the meal.

He sensed the energy of those around him and saw who was healthy and who was weak.

Max saw the world flow with colors and energies that he could see with his eyes and feel with his eyes closed.

When Max closed his eyes he could still feel the world around him up to a hundred meters, in clearer detail than his tired eyes gave him.

He felt the beauty with every part of his body and soul and nearly missed his mouth with a bite of food.

He felt his body storing energy and quickly use up everything it could to grow even stronger and more robust.

Klaus and Baumann asked poignant questions about tactics and observations on the battlefield, what to do in certain situations and why some things had worked better than others.

Max answered between bites until the chickpeas were gone and the bread was crumbs and only oil and salt lingered in the mess tin.

Klaus told him to sleep while the company still could and walked him back to the office door.

Max lay on his bunk again after they left.

Max straightened a makeshift curtain with *Mage Hand*.

Max lit a candle with a druidic snap.

When Max closed his eyes his mind flowed with the actions of his surroundings.

Everyone within a hundred meters was clear in his mind.

Max continued to explore the applications of magic, casting spell after spell and feeling the energy of earth and sky gather in his body.

Max let the land and air feed him until he was comfortable and sharp again.

Max called on *Life Detect* and watched life, stray warmth and the faint signatures of magic bloom in the dark around the office.

Max shaped a mouthful of clean water with *Create or Destroy Water* and swallowed it.

Max cast *Fortune's Favor* on himself and felt luck settle on him, slight and sure.

Max bent his attention to the weeds pushing through a crack in the stone outside the door.

Max used *Speak with Plants* and heard them in impressions more than words, thirst, grit and the stubborn will to live.

Max helped them grow, drawing a thread of his own mana into their roots until leaves unfurled greener than they had any right to be in January.

One patch gave back a tone that felt almost smug, too pleased with its own cleverness.

Max withered that lippy clump with a pinch of reversed growth and sent the stolen vigor to a quieter neighbor that had only asked for rain.

The polite weed straightened and flitted as if in thanks.

Max put out the candle with another druidic snap.

Max cast *Detect Evil and Good* and saw none.

Max cast *Sleep* on himself and let the enchantment take him.

---

Max woke in the early morning before dawn with the power settled in his body and soul.

Max cast *Mending* on his teeth and felt them freshen.

Max conjured water with *Create or Destroy Water*, drank it, sat up and stretched and felt refreshed.

Max took Captain Hauser's watch from his coat and saw it was still before the morning meal call.

Max went outside and found a random spot to sit.

Max felt the ground smooth beneath him and opened the Intel tab.

Max found new notifications on the Intel tab.

He skimmed entries on magic in this world.

The piece he wanted was titled Holy Britannian Empire mage corps composition.

```
Holy Britannian Empire active mages: 642
Expendables (rings 1-2): 48%
Treasured apprentices (rings 3-4): 32%
Full mages (rings 5-6): 17%
High instructors and above (ring 7+): 3%
Evocation: 83%
Transmutation: 11%
All other schools: 6%
```

*"So the Highland Mage Corps is almost all evocation."*

Max had not expected so many evokers to dominate the roster.

Nearly half the roster were expendables at the first two rings and another third were treasured apprentices the corps still treated as glass cannon.

The intel summaries explained the habit.

Holy Britannia had used mages as fragile artillery for centuries.

After the Spell Plague forced high ring work into a single school the corps trained evokers to drop area fire from behind the line.

The usual pattern was lift, bomb the formation and let infantry finish a broken enemy.

Britain's pool of talent was the largest in Europe so the method kept winning in the age of musket and rifle alike.

That was why Eldridge and his apprentices had fought like men cut from the same cloth.

Sergeant Klaus came up the street early with worry on his face.

He bent close to Max and spoke barely above a breath.

*"Platoon leader, battalion has a rumor and nobody knows if it is true."*

*"What rumor?"*

*"Madrid may have fallen."*

Max straightened on the stone where he sat.

*"How reliable is it? Where did you hear it?"*

He hoped it was only a rumor.

*"Battalion headquarters is spreading it. Half the company is whispering already."*

Klaus did not look any calmer.

*"The officers are not saying anything. Their faces look like it is true."*

Max went quiet.

Bad news always ran faster than good news in an army and it was usually right.

Madrid was the capital of Spain and the most important city on the National Army side.

The best National Army units and the International Brigades had held it until now.

If the capital was gone the German detachment in Spain was in mortal danger.

Max looked at the men around him.

Most had only just woken into a short peace after the counterattack.

They sat in small groups cleaning weapons or staring at the cook fire while they waited for breakfast.

They did not know a larger storm was forming.

*"Go back to 3rd Company. Watch the men and keep discipline tight. Keep gossip to a minimum. If anyone asks tell them nothing is confirmed until orders arrive. No private talk. Anyone who disobeys faces field punishment."*

Max needed his own house steady first. There could be no unrest in the platoon.

*"Yes, platoon leader."*

Klaus saluted and left at a quick pace.

Max opened the map.

The view still stopped at the edge of Seville.

The rest of Aragon and Madrid lay in fog.

Max decided to walk to battalion headquarters and learn what he could.

He found the command post in a house that still had four walls.

Guards stood at the door and the mood was tighter than in the resting lines.

Max did not go inside.

He paced nearby and glanced through the doorway when it opened.

Officers in the battalion knew his face by now.

He hoped to catch someone he could speak with.

Major Thomas came out after a few minutes.

His arm was still in a sling and bandages wrapped his head.

His eyes were more tired than they had been in the fighting.

The battalion commander had come out for air and caught Max hovering at the edge of the yard.

*"Second Lieutenant Max."*

Major Thomas waved him over.

*"Sir."*

Max ran up and stood at attention.

*"What are you skulking around for?"*

Major Thomas looked him over.

The tone was hard but the look was not angry.

*"I nearly had the guard take you until I saw who it was."*

Max scratched his head and tried a sheepish smile.

*"Sir, I came to hear the latest."*

Max asked outright.

*"Sir, there is talk about Madrid. Is any of it true?"*

Major Thomas's face turned graver.

He did not answer at once.

Then he met Max's eyes and nodded once.

Max felt his stomach drop.

*"The situation is bad."*

Major Thomas kept his voice low.

*"It may be worse than Madrid alone."*

He clapped Max on the shoulder.

*"Go back and do your duty. Whatever comes next we are soldiers of the German Empire. We obey orders."*

*"Yes, sir."*

Max saluted and turned away.

The moment Max was out of the major's sight a string of tones rang in his head.

```
Spain theater intelligence updated.
Battle of Madrid concluded. National Army and International Brigade main forces destroyed.
Royal Army and Holy Britannian columns toward Cuenca and Valencia predicted.
```

Max found a corner away from the company and opened the panel.

A red mark burned on the Intel tab.

The map had changed.

Before this only Seville and the ground around it had been clear.

Now most of the Kingdom of Aragon showed on the board.

The icon for Madrid sat entirely under enemy red.

The few friendly units near the capital were fleeing south and east and most were marked as routed or as having taken heavy casualties.

The Intel tab laid out the battle in short lines.

The Royal Army and the Holy Britannian expedition had massed overwhelming force with Highland mages and armored knights in front.

Under Lieutenant General Maximilian von Prittwitz the German expedition had pulled its line back to preserve strength.

German staff pulled back but the National Army and the International Brigades stayed to defend the city.

Max could not tell whether that was bad staff work or a refusal to retreat.

They fought hard.

Sheer numbers broke them anyway.

Max felt oppressed when he saw the enemy arrows on the map.

The Royal Army and the Holy Britannian expedition did not pause after taking the capital.

Main columns split toward Cuenca and Valencia.

If both provinces fell the German expedition still deep in Spain would have no land route home.

Strategic goals would not matter anymore.

Only survival would matter.

Max closed the panel and looked up at a gray sky.

Max could only hope the units that had pulled out of Madrid early would slow the enemy long enough to keep the army from being surrounded.

Max did not worry about what he could not control. He continued to replenish nutrients, storing more and reinforcing his body.

Max repaired his gear gradually. Mild applications of *Mending* on marginally defective pieces brought them closer to optimal. His cartridges and weapons stayed roughly polished and slightly dirty on purpose but their tolerances and accuracy had been tuned.

Max's teeth and bones grew harder and more robust without growing heavier. His tissues and organs followed the same pattern. Because of the system's influence he could not push his raw strength far but he could make himself harder to kill.

When he was not keeping the platoon in line Max walked the outskirts of the city and took in the sights. He picked sweet and sour oranges from the groves that ringed the walls. A little *Druidcraft* made the fruit a true delight in that time of turmoil and added splashes of brightness to his life and to the lives of the soldiers with whom he shared them.

One afternoon Max found a particularly fine patch of blackthorn beyond the walls and cut a stout stem. He shaped it with *Druidcraft* and fitted new grips to his *Feldmesser*, his S98/05 bayonet and his **Degen**, the straight infantry officer's sword he had bought for parade and still carried in Spain.

The wood felt almost warm in his hands as it replaced the old beech and leather. With a grin Max drew his Degen and felt blackthorn spikes glide between his fingers as he guided them to grow.

Max did not know how useful that trick would prove but it was still fun to have on hand. He did not have an indestructible body that healed in an instant so he would have to be more careful about charging into close combat against men with .303 and other rounds.

Max looked up at the sky and beyond. Several wisps of magic left his body. They quickly returned and let him know a storm was coming, here in Seville and across the world.

Max went to the Teutonic knight park and found Lieutenant Colonel Ludwig among the machines.

Work crews had Ludwig's Siegfried open on trestles while armorers replaced battered panels and checked the Luminite lines that fed its engines.

A second gang worked the white Saint George wrecks dragged in after the counterattack.

Max watched them strip those hulls for salvage.

Teutonic command meant to ship the magically enhanced Aetherium plate home for study.

Ludwig stood with his arms folded and watched the same work with a bandaged rib under his coat.

Max asked for one of those panels to carry on the battlefield.

Ludwig raised an eyebrow at the request.

Ludwig told a sergeant of the salvage gang to let Max take what he wanted from the salvage pile.

Max took a few pieces small enough to use.

Most of the plate he lifted stood close to a foot thick and was far too heavy for one man to wear.

The Saint George cockpits had taken damage from the heat rounds but still held thinner panels that had reinforced them.

Max kept one piece the size of his fist and placed it over his heart.

Max strapped another panel just large enough to cover what he wanted to his left shoulder.

Two days later new rumors ran through the garrison in Seville.

Cuenca had fallen.

Valencia had fallen.

The path back to the German Empire was cut.

---

At the border of the German Empire, Kaliban railway station.

A military train marked with the black eagle of the German Empire rolled into the platform.

The locomotive let out one last puff of white steam and gave a long whistle.

Doors opened on the middle coaches and General August von Mackensen stepped down, surrounded by staff officers.

Officers who had waited on the platform came forward at once.

*"Welcome back, General."*

*"Good to see you home, General."*

General Mackensen nodded to them without expression.

*"How is Prittwitz's counterattack going?"*

When Madrid fell the old general had wanted to stay and command the fighting that followed.

Prittwitz insisted and other officers pleaded until Mackensen obeyed the Emperor and General Staff and came home.

His special train carried no wireless telegraph carriage, so Mackensen had heard nothing reliable from Spain all the way back.

A staff officer who had waited on the platform stepped close with a grave face and held out a fresh urgent telegram.

*"General, urgent telegram from the Spanish front."*

General Mackensen took the sheet in a firm grip.

His face went still.

Cuenca and Valencia lost, the expedition encircled, the path to retreat cut off: the words struck the veteran and he swayed.

*"General!"*

His aide caught him by the arm.

*"I am all right."*

General Mackensen waved them off and drew several breaths before his composure returned.

His jaw set hard and the paper shook in his fist.

When Mackensen left the expedition headquarters he had handed command to Lieutenant General Prittwitz with repeated warnings.

The next phase must not turn timid.

He must not plan only for a safe defensive counterattack.

National Army and International Brigade troops could not hold forever under pressure while the Germans waited for the perfect counterattack.

Prittwitz had ignored every word of it.

When the situation on the front turned he ordered the expedition to pull back, concentrate strength and wait for the enemy to spend itself before his textbook counterattack.

The order was idiotic.

The German withdrawal exposed friendly flanks and the whole defense formation broke apart.

The retreat broke apart the cohesion that had held them together. Allied units scattered and the Royal Army with the Holy Britannian expedition found gaps and destroyed separated units one by one.

*"Fool! Prittwitz is a fool through and through!"*

General Mackensen could not contain himself and cursed aloud in front of every officer and official on the platform.

His roar carried across the empty stone and everyone fell silent.

*"And the General Staff! Who approved this appointment? Who suggested handing command to a man with no foresight who only reads manuals?"*

No one answered.

General Mackensen paced the platform with his chest heaving.

Rage would not fix the situation.

The urgent task was to retake Cuenca and Valencia and restore contact with the main expedition force.

General Mackensen stopped and spoke to the officer who had brought the telegram.

*"Get a telegraph desk ready at once. I will address the General Staff."*

*"Yes, General!"*

Minutes later in a temporary signal room at the station General Mackensen dictated while clerks keyed at full speed.

The message was short and every word showed the old soldier's urgency and will to fight.

He asked the General Staff and the Emperor to authorize his personal command and the immediate use of twelve infantry divisions already assembled and ready on the border.

They would cross into Spain and break the encirclement at any cost.
