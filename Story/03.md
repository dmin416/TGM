# Chapter 3

Arc: Battle 1

Date: 6 February 1914, pre-dawn

The first shell cut through the air before landing at the edge of San Isidro. The detonation threw up a pillar of black smoke.

The intel map pulled up in his field of vision. The fog of war retreated and the town became clear.

The map faded from his field of vision. He watched a second round fly toward the town and confirm the range.

Soon the sky was filled with shells howling in the wind.

The field artillery battalion was composed of twelve 7.7 cm FK 96 n.A. guns. They were not the biggest pieces on the continent but when they fired together they were devastatingly effective.

**BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM**

The explosions merged into a continuous roar. San Isidro vanished into smoke and dust and the grey morning air filled with earth and stone and roof tiles hurled upward and crashing back down.

A brief silence fell over the battlefield.

Around Max the soldiers were breathing harder and many wore excited expressions. In their eyes that artillery preparation was enough to crush the morale of any Spanish farmer-soldier still standing inside the village.

Then a sharp whistle cut through the settling dust.

The signal to attack.

Beyond the brush 1st Company's three skirmish lines began to advance. The soldiers marched in steady uniform steps with Gewehr 98s held level across their chests and muzzles pointed at the smoke covering the village. Each step was measured and solid and the formation held with almost no disorder.

After 1st Company advanced roughly a hundred meters 2nd Company began to move. Still in dense column they followed at the same cadence under their officers' commands. They did not deploy. They held tight and prepared to drive forward the moment 1st Company opened a breach.

The rising sun poured gold across the fields and lit the spikes on their helmets and the long line of bayonets at the ends of their rifles.

Three hundred and sixty men formed the grey assault wave and moved toward San Isidro. Boots brushed the earth. Occasional low commands carried down the line.

Max felt his heart tighten. The intel map pulled up in his field of vision. The blue icons of 1st and 2nd Companies moved steadily toward the grey diamond marking the unknown enemy position.

Everything matched Hauser's prediction. 1st and 2nd Companies crossed more than half the distance without resistance. Some soldiers in 3rd Company began to relax and murmur to each other. Hauser took out his pipe and tapped it against his boot heel.

When 1st Company's leading skirmish line came within two hundred meters of the village outskirts rifle fire erupted from the ruins. Several soldiers dropped and lay writhing.

The assault line wavered. The NCOs were already moving.

*"Halt! Present arms!"*

1st Company halted in unison and dropped to one knee and raised their rifles.

*"Take aim!"*

*"Fire!"*

**BANG**

The volley landed and the fire from the village edge weakened.

*"Advance!"*

They rose and pressed forward. Halt, aim, fire, advance. The cycle repeated across the next few dozen meters. Men kept falling but the momentum barely slowed. Synchronized rifle fire suppressed the defenders and it looked as if the assault would carry.

Max kept his eyes on the map. A red icon appeared without warning on San Isidro's outer positions.

```
Spanish Royal Army — Maxim machine-gun position.
```

**RAT-TAT-TAT-TAT-TAT-TAT-TAT-TAT**

1st Company's leading skirmish line came apart from left to right. Men collapsed in rapid succession. An entire platoon was gone from the battlefield in under ten seconds.

The grey assault wave shattered. The survivors threw themselves flat and pressed their faces into the dirt. Officers shouted themselves hoarse to no effect. The Maxim did not pause. The gunner swept across 1st Company's second and third skirmish lines and then onto 2nd Company still moving in dense column. In a tight formation every burst found bodies.

Hauser's smile was gone. He stood with his mouth open and his face drained of color.

The British had equipped the Royal Army with weapons and they had set a machine gun on the edge of San Isidro. Though the German army had been experimenting with using machine guns in battle they still had not grasped how deadly they were. That was why the 33rd Regiment only had eight machine guns used by a single company. They hadn't even been brought to the front line.

1st Company was finished. The survivors lay with their arms over their helmets. Anyone who tried to rise was cut down before they could straighten. 2nd Company dissolved into a knot of men who could neither advance nor withdraw. The Maxim swept through them without pause.

Max pulled his eyes back to the map. A fan of red extended ahead of the Maxim icon and covered the entire assault frontage. The markers for 1st and 2nd Companies sat inside it. He pulled out his binoculars and found the position. A second story window in an unremarkable farmhouse with muzzle flash visible between bursts.

Across the brush Major Thomas was speaking rapidly to messengers. One mounted and rode hard to the rear.

*Artillery. Ten minutes at minimum.*

*In ten minutes the machine gun would finish what it had started.*

Another messenger was already running toward 3rd Company.

*Don't tell me they're committing the reserve.*

The messenger reached Hauser panting.

*"Captain! Major Thomas's order! 3rd Company is to enter the battle immediately and press from the front!"*

Every officer's face changed.

Hauser grabbed the messenger by the collar.

*"Are you sure that's the order? Has the major gone mad? Can't he see what's happening up there?"*

*"Y-yes sir. Those were the major's exact words."*

Hauser let go. His face had gone dark red. Orders were orders and refusing under fire meant a court martial and a firing squad. He took a breath and faced his men.

*"You all heard it. Prepare to fight. 3rd Platoon left, 1st Platoon center, 2nd Platoon right. Spread into skirmish order. Advance by bounds and covering fire."*

He had at least seen enough not to send them in a packed column. But without taking out the Maxim any advance was a death sentence and there was no telling how many other defenders were dug into the village.

Just as Hauser raised the whistle Max stepped forward.

*"Sir!"*

Hauser turned with a deep frown.

*"What is it, Lieutenant? Are you planning to disobey orders?"*

*"No sir."* Max kept his voice level. *"I request permission to take 3rd Platoon and flank from the left."*

The officers and NCOs nearby went still.

*"Flank."* Hauser's eyes were hard. *"What exactly are you proposing."*

*"Sir, I've found the machine gun's position. It's in a two story farmhouse and its field of fire is fixed."* Max pointed left. *"There is a dried riverbed and brush in that direction. Natural cover. My platoon can use it to reach the village flank and attack the gun from the side. Even if we cannot take it out immediately the attention we draw gives the main force a chance to advance."*

Hauser stared at him.

The Maxim answered for both of them.

**RAT-TAT-TAT-TAT-TAT-TAT-TAT-TAT**

Another sweep across the open ground. Another row of men who would not be getting up.

When another platoon leader from 3rd Company heard that Max seemed to have other options besides a direct assault he also spoke up.

*"Sir, a frontal assault would result in too many casualties. Perhaps we could try Lieutenant Maximilian's method!"*

Time ticked by and the screams of the survivors from 1st and 2nd Companies ahead along with the intermittent bursts from the Maxim lashed at everyone's nerves.

A few seconds later Hauser waved his hand sharply.

*"Alright! Max! Take your men and attack from the left flank! If your plan fails I don't care how well you fight, you must immediately move toward the main force! Understand?"*

*"Yes, sir!"*

Without the slightest hesitation he turned to face his subordinates.

*"All squads! Follow me!"*

Without another word he took off running along the edge of the brush toward the more complex terrain on the left.

Sergeant Klaus was the first to react.

*"Follow the platoon leader! Quick! Get moving!"*

The four corporals and the squad leaders immediately urged their soldiers to break away from the main force and following Max's figure plunged into the complex terrain on the left flank.

Upon entering the forest Max issued his first truly meaningful combat order in this world.

*"All squads, spread out and then advance! Maintain proper spacing and do not crowd together!"*

Max's voice was clear and decisive and with Sergeant Klaus and four other corporals relaying the order repeatedly the soldiers followed.

*"First squad, stop at that earthen slope fifty meters ahead! If any enemy approaches the main force from other directions open fire to cover us!"*

Max pointed to a spot and called out to a corporal.

*"Yes, sir!"*

The corporal quickly led two squads of soldiers from the first platoon to the earthen slope where they set up their rifles using the trees and the slope as cover.

*"The rest of you, follow me! Cross the riverbed!"*

Max took the lead and slid down the dry riverbed which was more than a meter high.

The other soldiers followed closely behind moving quickly forward under the cover of the riverbed's depressions.

The roar of the Maxim continued to rage on the right but its attention was completely drawn to the frontal battlefield and the unit did not notice the group quietly approaching from its blind spot.

After running nearly 150 meters across the riverbed Max pulled up the intel map in his field of vision. They were almost at the village flank.

Gunfire cracked from the earthen slope where the first squad had posted.

He raised his fist and the entire group stopped and dropped low in the riverbed.

Sergeant Klaus was already moving without being told. He climbed to the rim of the riverbed and peered over.

*"Lieutenant, a small number of enemies are approaching from the village. The first squad has them suppressed."*

Max nodded and turned to face his platoon.

*"Sergeant Klaus, you and the second squad establish a foothold here. If we are pushed back you cover our retreat to the riverbed."*

*"Lieutenant, stay here with us. Attacking from the front is too dangerous."*

*"Stop talking. That is an order."*

Sergeant Klaus stopped hesitating and immediately led the second squad to quietly climb the gentle slope by the riverbed.

Max led the remaining two squads of more than forty soldiers keeping their bodies low and continued forward along the riverbed. The first squad's cover held and nothing interfered with the advance. Max brought everyone to the point where the riverbed met the village.

The two story farmhouse serving as the machine gun emplacement was exposed to their side and front. The map put it at less than a hundred meters.

*"Corporal Jonah!"*

Max beckoned and Jonah came over at once with his rifle in hand.

*"See that two story farmhouse?"*

*"I see it, Lieutenant!"*

*"I'll lead the third squad in the assault. You take the fourth squad and watch the doors and windows facing our direction. Any movement, open fire immediately and suppress them. Can you do that?"*

*"Don't worry, Lieutenant!"*

Max nodded and led the last corporal and two squads up out of the riverbed toward the farmhouse.

The enemy quickly discovered the commotion.

Corporal Jonah caught a figure moving in a second floor window of the farmhouse. He raised his Gewehr 98 and pulled the trigger before the figure could act.

*"Second floor window! Free fire! Open fire!"*

**CRACK**

**CRACK CRACK CRACK**

A dozen rounds hit the second floor window together. The Gewehr 98 was renowned for its accuracy and German soldiers trained to demanding standards every day. All twelve bullets found the window frame. Splinters burst outward and the figure dropped from sight.

Corporal Jonah's face lit up and he immediately shouted.

*"Keep firing! Do not let up! Watch those other windows and doors!"*

He pulled the bolt. The hot cartridge case ejected and another round chambered.

On the gentle slope of the riverbed Sergeant Klaus lay watching and could not quite work out why the new platoon leader had led the troops into the assault himself while sending his most experienced man to guard a rear position.

Before he could settle the question he saw soldiers in black Royalist Army uniforms pour out of the buildings to the right of the farmhouse and run toward Max and the others.

**CRACK CRACK CRACK CRACK CRACK CRACK CRACK CRACK CRACK CRACK**

The soldiers Max had positioned on the slope opened fire and routed the charging Royalist soldiers with precise sustained rifle fire.

Sergeant Klaus finally understood.

*Crossfire.*

The gentle slope of the riverbed brought the two story farmhouse and the surrounding buildings into a clean field of fire. Any enemy moving to attack Max's group had to pass directly in front of Klaus's guns. As long as the second squad held that position the village defenders could not threaten the assault.

On the other side Max led two squads unimpeded to the edge of the farmhouse.

Max rushed to the farmhouse gate and pressed his back against the wall.

The soldiers of the fourth squad along with two messengers who had stayed close throughout followed and posted themselves on both sides of the gate.

Max pressed his back against the wall and leaned close to the nearest soldier.

*"Throw a grenade through the second floor window. And someone get one through the machine gun firing port."*

Two soldiers from the fourth squad pulled the fuses and hurled them through the second floor window.

One of the soldiers peered around the corner to check the ground was clear then slipped along the wall to the base of the machine gun firing port. He pulled the pin and threw the grenade through the opening.

**BOOM**

**BOOM**

**BOOM**

The second floor shook with the explosions. The red icon marking the Maxim position on the intel map blinked once and went dark.

The chilling roar of machine guns on the battlefield finally ceased.

The first to react was Captain Hauser. He had been pinned down the moment he led his men forward and had been able to do nothing but press himself into the dirt. He was on his feet before the echo faded. He drew his pistol and turned to the soldiers lying behind him.

*"Get up! The machine gun is down! Charge with me!"*

The officers of 1st and 2nd Platoons blew their whistles. The soldiers who had been flat on the ground rose one by one and took up their rifles with bayonets fixed and charged forward through gritted teeth.

The survivors of 1st and 2nd Companies who had lain through the machine gun swept the field with their eyes when they saw the reserve pushing forward in another charge. When they confirmed the Maxim had gone quiet a surge of elation and a long suppressed fury swept through every man still breathing.

*"For the Empire! Charge!"*

No one could say which officer shouted first. It did not matter. Every able-bodied soldier from 1st and 2nd Companies came up off the ground and surged forward alongside the soldiers of 3rd Company already advancing on their flank. They moved like men who had been waiting for exactly this moment.

Minutes before they had been cut down like wheat on that open field. They had watched their comrades torn apart and been powerless to answer it. That anger had nowhere to go.

Now it did.

Without the Maxim holding the field and with Max's assault from the left flank pulling part of the Royalist force out of position the rifle fire from the village edge meant nothing against the grey wave pouring toward it. Hundreds of German soldiers crossed the last stretch of open ground and the defenders could not stop them.

After suffering so heavily the officers and sergeants of 1st Battalion including Hauser had learned. They ran along the line shouting at the men to spread out and stop bunching together. Loose skirmish lines formed again. The soldiers stopped chasing orderly ranks and pushed forward with rifles and bayonets as fast as their legs would carry them.

Max noticed the main force had resumed the offensive and turned his attention back to the farmhouse.

*The village still had defenders. Clearing the farmhouse was not the end of it.*

He patted the messenger beside him and pointed toward the riverbed.

*"Go back along the river and tell Sergeant Klaus and the other squads to advance under cover and rendezvous with the main force here."*

The messenger took the order and moved to leave.

Max called after him.

*"Stay low and keep your head down. Bullets do not care about you."*

*"Yes, sir!"*

Max watched the messenger drop into the riverbed and crouch walk away then turned back to the squad beside him.

After the machine gun fire subsided the soldiers were already itching to rush the farmhouse but Max raised his hand and stopped them.

*"Don't rush off to your death."*

His voice was not loud. The hot-headed soldiers settled immediately.

*"Grenadier, come here!"*

The two soldiers who had just silenced the machine gun with grenades stepped forward at once.

*"Listen carefully. Wait until I open the door a little then throw the grenade through the crack in the door."*

The order puzzled both men. They had never heard anything like it before. But the trust that had just been built held and neither soldier hesitated. They took their grenades from their belt clips and held them ready to throw at any moment.

Max took a deep breath. After confirming everyone else was ready he pressed the barrel of his rifle against the farmhouse door and eased it open a crack.

Almost simultaneously the two grenadiers pulled the pins on their grenades and rolled them one after the other through the crack. Max pulled the door shut and stepped back quickly.

**BOOM**

A muffled explosion came from inside the farmhouse. Planks shattered and something heavy hit the floor.

*"Big guy, kick the door down!"*

The tall soldier standing opposite Max did not wait. He drove his boot into the already weakened door and it flew open.

*"Fire!"*

**CRACK**

The soldiers behind Max poured a volley through the dark doorway.

The gunfire echoed against the farmhouse walls and tore through the wooden farm tools scattered outside, kicking up clouds of dust. The group pulled back behind the wall quickly. Bolts worked and hot shell casings bounced and clattered across the ground.

When they looked again the first floor had gone still.

Max led the group inside with his rifle up. On the floor and at the base of the stairwell leading to the second floor three soldiers in Royalist black lay dying. Their bodies were riddled with bullet holes and shrapnel wounds.

*"Give them a quick death."*

Max sighed almost imperceptibly and ordered three soldiers forward. They finished the dying men with bayonets.

*"Be careful, go up to the second floor!"*

The soldiers behind Max raised their rifles toward the ceiling and followed him to the stairwell. They went up one at a time on the creaking steps.

The first soldier to peek out from the stairwell discovered that the scene on the second floor was even more horrific. In the confined space the grenades had reached their fullest potential.

The grenade thrown through the machine gun firing port had landed at the shooter's feet. The explosion marks on the floor made that plain. The machine gunner and his assistant and the two riflemen on guard duty had all fallen into pools of blood and were no longer breathing.

The Maxim itself had come through largely intact. The two gunners had absorbed most of the grenade damage and shielded the gun behind them.

Max stepped around the bodies and went to the side of the window to look the Maxim over. The gun was covered in dust and fragments of wood and plaster but the water cooling jacket and the receiver and the feed mechanism had not suffered any damage that mattered.

This relieved him. Barring any other problems this Maxim machine gun would be the 3rd Platoon's first trophy.

Although the available ammunition might not be sufficient there could never be too many machine guns in a platoon's hands.

*"Move the machine gun and ammunition boxes to the first floor. Go through the window from the side with your heads down. Do not let the friendly forces outside mistake us for the enemy."*

Max left three soldiers behind with their instructions and led the rest of his men back downstairs.

Outside the farmhouse gate the three squads that had been scattered along the river had been called back by the messenger and were rejoining the soldiers who had remained behind.

Dozens of soldiers crowded around the farmhouse with their faces beaming. The men from Sergeant Klaus's squad were already boasting to their comrades about what they had just done.

Max looked at the scene and felt his temples throb.

*If the enemy counterattacks now, or if a shell comes down, the casualty count is going to skyrocket.*
