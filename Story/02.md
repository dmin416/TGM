# Chapter 2

Arc: Deployment

Date: Late January 1914, night

When the assembly whistles tore through the night, Max's mind went blank for a moment.

The alarm brought the same feeling of helplessness as pulling an all nighter only to learn you were needed early at work for an emergency.

There was nothing to do but accept it, though.

Max rubbed his face and looked at Klaus standing in the entryway to his tent.

*"Is the whole company mobilizing?"*

*"Yes, sir."*

Max let any notion of rest completely leave him and sat up from his platoon leader's cot and let his muscle memory guide him.

*"Klaus, rouse the platoon, take down the tents. Anything the men are not carrying, get it to the train section."*

The company's two draft wagons' main job on the march was to haul tents and equipment for over 200 soldiers.

*"Yes, sir."*

Klaus vanished into the dark.

Max's orderly brought the last platoon leader's kit with a polished rifle, a heavy pistol in leather and a German leather spike helmet.

Being captured by the enemy had stripped him of basically everything except his uniform and his new revolver.

Even his ID would need to be reissued.

For now he took on his predecessor's gear.

The heavy rifle was a Gewehr 98 and the pistol was a classic P08 Luger.

Even though history was different in this world, the equipment remained similar and that made it easier to adapt.

But he had no time to appreciate the weapons.

He attached the pistol case to his belt and clipped on the ammo pouches. He put on his helmet and tightened the chin strap.

Cold leather and metal on skin brought a strange kind of familiarity.

He lifted the Gewehr 98 and worked the bolt. The crisp clacks of metal spread in the tent and cleared the last of his drowsiness.

When he stepped out of his tent he had the orderly call for some men who quickly broke down his tent and folded his cot before taking it to the draft wagon.

By this time the camp had transformed so much that it was unrecognizable.

All the lines of tents were gone and only the marks on the ground showed where they used to be.

Soldiers moved towards the back of the company with canvas and supplies for the wagons. The rest stood with bags on and rifles slung.

Nearby Third Company's other platoons formed tight blocks in the dark. Orders crossed in low voices with rough discipline.

Max looked past them. The whole first battalion camp stirred.

Oil lamps swayed in clusters and the whole battalion seemed to breathe in one slow rhythm.

The cold efficiency told Max he had slid another step toward the stage named war.

Far behind that camp the sixteenth infantry brigade HQ burned bright in the central tent.

Lieutenant General Mackensen stood over an open map though he still wore bandages. His stance stayed straight as a pike.

Staff officers stood around him. Every eye focused on Seville.

Mackensen still tasted blood when he looked at the city on the map.

After his rescue, his first thought was immediate retaliation.

*Hit the enemy before the British could respond and hit hard.*

At his staff's urging, he restrained himself and sent a telegram to the General Staff at home first.

Along with a detailed account of the military attaché delegation being attacked, the most important thing was a request for authorization to launch a retaliatory offensive.

Even with the telegram line that ran along the German Spanish railway, the fastest turnaround for that telegram would still be three or four hours.

Without authorization in hand yet, Mackensen and his staff ran through every possible offensive plan.

They could only wait anxiously for the final decision from Potsdam.

*"Seville had no permanent fortifications before,"*

The major traced the map with a fingertip as he spoke.

*"The Royal Army has taken the initiative. They have advanced and built fortifications across the forward line. The 24th Infantry Division holds the ground with no fewer than two Northumberland Fusilier battalions in direct support and cavalry on the flanks. They outnumber us in the field and they are prepared. An assault under these conditions will cost us badly."*

Mackensen shook his head and tapped the markings on both flanks of the 16th Brigade.

*"We are not without allies. The National Army and the International Brigade each have a brigade on our wings. Add our 16th Infantry Brigade and on paper we are not at a disadvantage."*

He looked at the symbols on the map representing the Royal Army and let out a cold laugh.

*"And whether it is equipment or will to fight, the Royal Army simply is not strong enough. In a head-on fight with our troops, hell, even against the National Army or the International Brigade, they have no chance of winning."*

*"But we need to pay special attention to the British,"* another staff officer cut in.

*"Besides the two Northumberland Fusilier battalions, a task force from the Highland Mage Corps and the Order of Garde Knights may have already arrived in Seville."*

The moment he said it the tent fell silent.

Even Mackensen's blood cooled at the names.

He had always been confident in the Imperial German Army. High-quality junior officers and NCOs, years of strict training, the foundation that let the army compete on a continent ringed by enemies. Germany had been among the first to industrialize and its military technology had advanced by leaps and bounds. They had standardized mass-producible heavy artillery and reworked the armored knight designs that had once belonged exclusively to Britain and France into versions that drastically reduced the need for true magitek.

Even so, the British mage corps and knightly orders were not to be underestimated.

In overseas colonial fighting the Empire had suffered repeated setbacks because of enemy archmages. Those were people who could change the course of a battle with only a handful of individuals.

And the Order of Garde Knights, elite armored knights, were the sort of force that could be worth a hundred men, even a thousand, on the battlefield.

Mackensen's thoughts drifted, unwillingly, to the scorching sands of North Africa years ago.

It had been a colonial conflict.

At first the war had gone smoothly beyond anyone's expectations. The North Africa Corps under his command, together with German colonial troops and loyal local auxiliary forces, had advanced in triumph, uprooting British outposts and strongpoints one after another.

Victory reports had flown home like snowflakes. Everyone believed that rich land was about to change hands.

Until British reinforcements arrived.

A small detachment of the Order of Garde Knights and only three archmages from the Highland Mage Corps.

Mackensen would never forget what he saw that day.

He had led his troops into a frontal encounter with the main body of the British expeditionary force. Everyone knew it would be the decisive battle that determined the course of the North African campaign.

When the fighting began it was not with the roar of artillery.

That archmage, hailed by the British as a legendary evoker, stood behind their lines, staff raised high to the sky.

The color of the heavens changed in an instant. The clouds tore open and countless burning meteors, each trailing a long tail of flame, screamed down into the positions of the German North Africa Corps.

Where they fell a firestorm rose that swallowed everything.

A giant wall of flame rolled forward, consuming soldiers' flesh and warping steel barrels.

A battle-hardened, full-strength infantry regiment vanished from the world in just a few minutes. No intact bodies left to find.

Then the armored knights of the Order of Garde launched their charge. With a single assault they shattered the formations of two more infantry regiments.

That scene became a nightmare that never left Mackensen.

If the Teutonic Knights attached to the force hadn't thrown themselves forward and by sheer numbers temporarily pinned down those armored knights of pure magitek...

If Duke Ernst August of Brunswick hadn't personally led the Brunswick Death Hussars in three suicidal charges against the archmages' position, disregarding casualties, using the hussars' flesh and blood to force the three archmages to break their casting, defend, and withdraw...

Then that battle might have ended with the entire North Africa Corps buried there.

*"General?"*

A staff officer's voice pulled Mackensen out of his memories.

He drew a slow breath. The smell of kerosene and tobacco in the tent made the moment feel real again.

After a long silence, Mackensen asked, *"Where are the Teutonic Knights' armored knights?"*

*"Still on the military train, sir. At the latest they will reach the station behind us before dawn and finish assembling."*

*"Good. Once they arrive send them straight to Seville and commit them to battle. All elements of the 16th Brigade and the attached cavalry regiment will proceed to the attack assembly area as planned. The attached artillery regiment can move into its bombardment positions as well."*

The staff officers exchanged glances. The one closest to him finally looked up.

*"But General, the General Staff's order still hasn't come down."*

*"That is only a matter of time."*

Mackensen waved a hand.

*"Whether it is His Majesty, the General Staff, or the Imperial Diet, none of them can tolerate British hands reaching right to our doorstep. This battle will be fought."*

He looked around at the tense staff officers. Most of these relatively young men had no experience fighting in overseas colonies. For them the coming battle would be their first.

Mackensen let out a slow sigh.

As the old comrades who had truly experienced war rose to higher positions or retired to second-line roles, youthening had become an unstoppable trend in the Imperial German forces. The new generation of young staff officers surpassed their predecessors in many respects. But actual combat experience was not something military schools or daily training could teach.

*May this limited war help the Empire cultivate a new generation of experienced officers.*

He looked to the commander of the 16th Brigade beside him.

*"The detailed combat deployment is in your hands. After the General Staff's order arrives from home I will return to the expeditionary command."*

*"Yes, General!"*

Mackensen sat down in a corner of the tent and waited quietly for the final order from home.

As commander of the expeditionary forces within the Kingdom of Spain he ought to be at the expeditionary headquarters closer to Germany, overseeing the whole situation. In his mind the map of Spain slowly unfolded, dispositions of the German expeditionary army, the National Army, and the International Brigade appearing across it. He closed his eyes and thought through troop movements in other directions. Once the battle for Seville began, fighting would soon erupt along the entire front.

The other officers began making preparations and plans for the combat that might break out at any moment. Runners came and went through the tent flap and orders flowed with them to every unit. Words on paper became soldiers' marching steps, became the ruts pressed into earth by towed artillery.

Nearly three hours passed before an officer lifted the flap and strode to Mackensen, who was smoking a pipe.

*"General! A reply telegram from the General Staff!"*

Mackensen snatched the telegram and tore it open. In the tent the only sound was the dry rasp of paper.

A moment later an odd expression spread across his face, anger and confusion tangled together, before collapsing into a cold sneer. He walked over to the staff and slapped the sheet onto the table.

*"Read it. This is what our General Staff has decided."*

The commander of the 16th Brigade picked it up as if it might bite him. After only one glance his face turned just as colorful as Mackensen's.

*"Authorization for the expeditionary forces inside the Kingdom of Spain to immediately carry out necessary military action... but it orders you to return home at once to recuperate?"*

Everyone froze.

To remove a humiliated general burning for revenge right before the fighting started. Changing commanders on the eve of battle.

*"The order says General von Prittwitz will assume your duties,"* the chief of staff read, voice strained.

*"Prittwitz."*

Mackensen tasted the name with contempt.

*"That coward who only dares fight defensive battles with superior numbers? And he is supposed to command an offensive?"*

He looked around the tent. His gaze lingered on the assault plan. Unwillingness flickered in his eyes and then it was gone. He gave a short harsh snort.

*"Authorization has been granted. Then we attack exactly as you just planned. Drive the Royal Army and the British out of Seville."*

His voice was not loud but it carried a decisive weight that tolerated no argument.

*"Until that idiot Prittwitz arrives I am still the expeditionary commander. Necessary military action, is it? Fine. I will show them what necessary really means."*

---

While Mackensen and his staff waited in that brigade tent, the 1st Battalion of the 33rd Infantry Regiment had already completed its departure.

Night was the best cover for a large infantry march.

Three rifle companies plus the battalion's attached units and the baggage train moved down country dirt roads, slipping along with the fields on either side.

In the Imperial German Army the battalion was the basic tactical unit and the march column was built around it, orderly and controlled.

Aside from boots grinding over gravel and the occasional hoofbeat of a messenger galloping past, nearly a thousand men made almost no unnecessary noise. Discipline was not something they performed. It lived in their bones.

Max and his platoon marched within 3rd Company's column.

Max could feel this body tensing up as the march began, apprehension trying to tighten his chest and shoulders as inexperience pushed up through the muscles like a reflex. He pressed it down and breathed through it, refusing to let the body be green when he was not.

As it loosened he could look around and actually take in the column. His mind was sharp where the body was not, and the knowledge from officer school came up naturally to fill the gaps, letting him read the march the way a trained officer should, catching when the pace drifted or the spacing between men opened too wide. As the body and the memories began to settle into each other he could feel his understanding of the world around him sharpening.

At least physically he was now a real German second lieutenant.

Moonlight filtered through branches and scattered pale flecks across the spiked helmets. The air was cool, smelling of damp earth and grass, with a faint hint of cordite from the direction of Seville.

After a little more than two hours the column reached the designated assembly area, a sparse patch of woods near the main road with gently uneven terrain that offered concealment.

As Major Thomas's orders rippled down the chain the battalion shifted instantly from march mode to combat preparation. Companies and platoons fanned out, finding cover with practiced ease, and low murmurs replaced the earlier silence as tension and anticipation threaded through the trees.

As Max finished settling his men Klaus came up beside him and spoke quietly.

*"Sir, a messenger just said the company wants you over there."*

*"Understood."*

Max handed his rifle to his orderly, straightened his uniform and webbing and walked toward a few oil lamps flickering deeper in the woods.

That was 3rd Company's temporary command post, where Captain Hauser and the other two platoon leaders were hunched over a map spread on the ground.

*"Max, you're here, good timing,"* Hauser said, gesturing him in and pointing to the map. *"Bad news and good news. Which do you want first?"*

*"Sir, when you ask it like that it usually means neither is great."*

Hauser let out a brief laugh then sobered.

*"Bad news: the attack order is down. Our 1st Battalion is the regiment's first echelon. We are assaulting that village up front, San Isidro, head-on."*

He took a pencil and drew a thick arrow straight into the village symbol.

A frontal assault in the first echelon, two phrases that together were basically synonymous with heavy casualties.

*"And the good news?"*

*"3rd Company is the battalion reserve. 1st and 2nd Companies go in first and we will be committed as needed or used to plug a gap."*

Max nodded and quietly exhaled. Being reserve to the first assault wave was not perfectly safe but at least he would not be the first body thrown forward to probe the enemy's fire.

*This will be fun,* he thought.

But as his eyes returned to the map more memories surfaced, more doctrine and more common sense of this world's ground warfare.

And Max realized something was very wrong.

On the map the attack front assigned to 1st and 2nd Companies was extremely narrow.

1st Company would deploy its three platoons as three staggered skirmish lines across a 150-meter frontage, men spaced one to two meters apart, advancing slowly while firing volleys to suppress the enemy's forward positions.

Roughly a hundred meters behind them 2nd Company would follow in extended order, waiting to push through and launch a bayonet charge as soon as 1st Company's skirmish line opened a breach.

No flanking, no infiltration, none of the later squad-based tactics of alternating bounds and dispersed movement and slipping forward under covering fire.

Unpleasant memories began to flash in Max's mind, the tactical lectures this body's original owner had swallowed whole in officer school, teachers foaming with pride about German shock tactics and the courage of the skirmish line and the destructive power of regimental attack groups.

He could practically hear that white-haired tactics instructor praising the magnificent sight of soldiers in skirmish order, a gray tide stepping to the beat, walking straight into shellfire with unwavering resolve.

Back then, this body's original owner, like all his classmates, had been fired up, believing it was the purest expression of German military glory.

But now Max felt genuinely sick at the sight of it.

Even with magitek and armored knights and other extraordinary forces in this world, conventional infantry tactics here were still stuck at the level of early World War I Europe, faith in courage and discipline and massed formations and their impact.

He had arrived just in time for the swan song of dense-formation assaults.

---

Max had learned about the tactics used in World War 1. Scholars often emphasized the infantry assaults that defined this era.

It was an awkward brutal era for infantry.

Armies still clung to the tradition of dense formations. Compared to the old line-infantry age men now stood one to two meters apart, but to Max it was still in a sense lining up to be shot.

That did not truly change until machine guns stepped onto the stage of history and harvested soldiers' lives with cold efficiency.

And the infantry in this world was obviously stuck in that same awkward phase.

Worse, this world also had magic and magitek. For infantry who had to walk onto the battlefield in their own flesh that was just as deadly a threat.

Max was genuinely rattled. He understood perfectly that machine-gun bullets were woke enough not to differentiate between men and women, and officer or private, get caught in the sweep and you would be cut down.

Forcing his face under control Max asked cautiously, *"Captain, do we know roughly what the enemy's force disposition looks like in that village?"*

At his question Hauser's expression turned into a clear *Good. I knew you'd ask that.*

*"Battalion sent scouts around the outskirts,"* Hauser said, using his pencil to circle the village on the map. *"What we can confirm is that the Royal Army is already inside. But to avoid alerting them the scouts didn't get too close, so we don't know their exact numbers."*

One of the other platoon leaders, a taciturn lieutenant, added, *"Sir, will there be artillery preparation?"*

*"Of course."* Hauser nodded, his tone confident. *"A battalion from the brigade artillery regiment will fire three rapid volleys on the village before the assault. That will be enough to scare the guts out of those Royal Army boys."*

As he spoke Hauser pulled out a pocket watch, checked it and told the three platoon leaders, *"It's 04:37. The attack will begin at 05:07 sharp. Go make your preparations."*

The brief conference ended and the other two platoon leaders turned to leave.

Before Max could go Hauser stepped closer and gave him a firm clap on the shoulder.

*"I know you came straight from officer school to the front. First real combat, being nervous is normal,"* Hauser said more quietly, with the tone of an older hand looking out for a junior. *"I noticed you don't have a pocket watch. That won't do. An officer has to have a watch to keep time."*

Only then did Max realize what had been feeling missing all this time. The original owner of this body had lost his pocket watch when he was captured. After being rescued there had been no time to recover it and aside from the system interface Max had no way to tell the time.

Hauser called over his orderly, had him fetch a cloth-wrapped bundle from the luggage and handed it to Max.

*"This was my old pocket watch. Before we left my wife gave me a new one. This one's been sitting unused, take it for now."*

*"Captain, that's too valuable,"* Max blurted. In this era a pocket watch was still a luxury.

*"Take it."* Hauser shoved the bundle into his hand. *"I don't want you missing the timing and screwing things up."*

Then he continued, half reassurance and half contemptuous overconfidence. *"Relax, Max. Those Royal Army troops in the village are mostly conscripted farmers. Their will to fight and their quality are nowhere near ours. Most likely 1st and 2nd Companies will finish it with a single charge and the reserve won't even see action."*

*"Then I'll take that as a blessing, sir."*

*"Go back and get your men ready."*

*"Yes, sir."*

Max saluted and headed back to his platoon's rest area.

He knew Hauser's last words were half reassurance and half underestimating the enemy. That attitude seemed widespread in the German Army, a near-blind confidence in their own combat power and a deep disdain for whoever stood across from them. When things went well that mindset became high morale. When they didn't it could turn into a catastrophic rout.

Max returned to his area and found Sergeant Klaus checking the men's equipment.

*"Sergeant Klaus, the attack order is in. We assault in about half an hour."*

Klaus's stern face didn't change though his hands paused for a beat.

*"Yes, sir,"* he replied in a low voice. *"I'll have the squad leaders do final checks and make sure everyone is in the best condition."*

The seasoned veteran understood exactly what was coming.

*"Please rest assured, sir. I'll help you command the platoon."*

That steadied Max a little.

After speaking with Klaus, Max found a relatively dry slope and sat down with his back against a thick tree trunk.

Around him the soldiers had stopped talking. The woods held only small sounds, the faint clink of gear and the restrained rhythm of breathing.

Max closed his eyes. The darkness was immediately replaced by the familiar UI and a huge map unfolded again.

He experimented with zooming, quickly finding the blue unit markers for the 33rd Infantry Regiment's three battalions. 1st Battalion's marker sat at the very front of the regiment.

With a thought the map zoomed further. The battalion marker resolved into smaller icons, the three rifle companies, battalion headquarters and the battalion baggage elements. He could even see that the two small wagon icons belonging to 1st Company's baggage train were sitting farther back with the battalion trains.

That god's-eye view of the whole situation calmed his scattered thoughts.

Then he shifted the view to their target, San Isidro.

The village area was covered by a translucent shadow, presumably the fog of war. Unlike friendly markers that updated in real time, enemy markers seemed to appear only after being scouted.

At the center of the village there was only a lonely gray diamond labeled:

```
Spanish Royal Army unit (size unknown) (armament unknown)
```

That matched Hauser's claim. The scouts had only looked from afar and gained no real intelligence.

Max tried to focus on the diamond with his mind. A prompt appeared:

```
Insufficient information. Unable to parse.
```

Another detail on the map caught his eye. About three kilometers behind their assembly area an artillery position icon was already lit up. He zoomed in and saw it was a battalion from the brigade artillery regiment. When he focused on the icon a list appeared:

```
28th Field Artillery Regiment, 2nd Field Artillery Battalion
77mm field guns x12
```

When Max saw the maximum range of 5,500 meters he understood why the battery was positioned so far forward. This was not a high-angle howitzer. It was a close-support field gun, shorter-ranged and less powerful but more mobile and flexible.

Faint ballistic arcs extended from the icon, their impact zone covering most of the southeast side of San Isidro. But the coverage was too broad. This was area suppression, not precision fire. Its main purpose was probably exactly what Hauser had said, to pin the enemy down.

*Fine,* Max thought. *Let's hope it really does go the way Hauser claims.*

---

Fifteen minutes later, with Klaus's help, Max assembled the entire platoon.

After checking their weapons and ammunition the soldiers slung their packs and stood in three files by squad, silent and waiting for the next order.

*"Platoon leader, the company's moving,"* Klaus said.

*"Move out."*

Max led 3rd Platoon toward the other two platoons and company headquarters.

The sun still hadn't risen. There was only the faintest glow below the horizon and the woods remained dim. Boots crunched softly through dead leaves and brittle twigs, mixing with the faint metallic clink of gear.

Before long they reached 3rd Company's forming-up area. Captain Hauser stood at the head of the column and the other two platoons had already dressed their ranks behind him. Max guided his men into place at the rear.

A tall severe-looking company sergeant major strode up to Hauser, snapped to attention and reported, *"All present, sir!"*

*"Thank you, Sergeant Major."* Hauser stepped to the very front of the company and called out, *"Company! Dress on center! Quick march!"*

The sergeant major took position behind the captain and other company officers moved immediately to the flanks, repeating orders and maintaining formation.

*"Left, right, left! Left, right, left!"*

Excluding the baggage elements nearly two hundred men flowed forward again, moving toward the designated attack preparation position.

After about ten minutes the column halted behind a patch of low scrub. This was 3rd Company's waiting area, its attack preparation position as 1st Battalion reserve.

Through gaps in the brush Max could clearly see the open ground ahead.

1st Company had already deployed into three skirmish lines roughly 150 meters wide, men spaced one to two meters apart. Behind them 2nd Company stood in extended order. As the unit meant to push through the moment a gap opened 2nd Company had fixed bayonets in advance and in the growing dawn cold points flashed along the line of steel.

The whole scene was terrifyingly quiet, only the wind's low moan across the fields.

Max glanced at the pocket watch Hauser had lent him. The silver hands marched steadily toward 05:07.

The instant the time arrived the oppressive silence was ripped apart by a sharp tearing whistle.
