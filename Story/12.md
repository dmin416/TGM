# Chapter 12

Arc: Seville Counterattack

Date: Late January 1914

---

Daniel was a farmer from the hill country of northern Aragon.

Unmarried but with a girl he liked.

Three months ago the Royal Army's draft squad had dragged him from his fields, stuffed him into an ill fitting uniform and issued him an old rifle whose rifling was almost worn smooth.

He did not want to fight. He only wanted to go home, work the land and save enough money to marry Maria from the next village over.

Yet now he lay pressed to Seville's cold flagstones. Beside him lay the cooling corpse of a comrade. Bullets flew from every direction, striking earth and walls, kicking up clouds of dust and sparks.

Only minutes before their officers had still been telling them those Germans and the rebels had run. This would be an easy armed parade.

When the first shot rang out everyone froze.

Then gunfire popped like firecrackers from every window along the street, every rooftop and every corner they could not see.

Daniel could not even tell where the bullets came from. He only watched men beside him drop one after another, screaming in pain.

Instinct drove him to drop to the ground with the crowd, head buried low, body shaking like a sieve.

*"Take cover! Find cover now!"*

An officer roared himself hoarse.

But where was there cover?

The broad street offered no shelter. They were live targets exposed to countless muzzles.

*"Into the houses! Get into those buildings!"*

Survival instinct hurled the soldiers forward. They scrambled and crawled toward the buildings on both sides of the street.

Daniel crawled up too. He fixed on the nearest two story house and threw his whole strength into a dash.

When he reached the door despair waited. Every ground floor window and the door on this building had been bricked and boarded shut from within. There was no way in.

He rammed the door, smashed the glass with his rifle butt. The damned door did not budge.

Behind him the street had already become a killing ground.

The MG08 heavy machine gun emplaced at the far end of the street finally roared.

The equipment Max had treated like treasure now showed its true character as a reaper of the battlefield.

The gunner did not need careful aim. Royal Army soldiers in black uniforms were packed along both sides of the street. The gunner only had to open fire on the densest mass of men and hold the trigger down. Hot bullets tore through bodies with ease and blood mist rose in thick clouds over the crowd.

Royal Army soldiers fell like cut wheat in rows.

Daniel was scared out of his wits. He gave up on the door and spun about, looking for another way in.

Then he saw a building farther off whose door stood open.

Daniel charged toward the open door without regard for anything else.

Many others had the same idea. A dozen or more Royal Army soldiers surged like madmen into that structure.

The cramped space filled with bodies shoving one another, everyone fighting to press deeper inside.

No one noticed that the chimney base had been hollowed out, even the metal grate meant to block it removed.

Daniel was squeezed in the middle, barely able to breathe.

Then he heard a strange noise overhead, as if something were falling down the flue.

*"What is that?"*

Someone looked up and asked.

No one could answer. Only a few soldiers near the chimney saw something dark roll from the base of the flue.

The next instant a violent explosion tore the room apart.

A grenade dropped by a German soldier waiting on the roof had gone down the chimney into the packed interior.

Then a second grenade fell down the chimney and a third fell after it.

The roar nearly shattered Daniel's eardrums. Heat and flying fragments swallowed the room in an instant.

Daniel felt a huge force throw him down. Countless bodies piled on top of him.

The stink of powder and scorched meat filled his nose. His ears filled with shrieks and moans.

He did not know whether he was wounded. He only knew he had to get out.

Daniel clawed free of the heap of humanity and at last crawled from that hell on earth.

He fled with other blood soaked Royal Army soldiers, howling for his mother and father, bursting back out onto the street.

What awaited him was bullets coming in from every side.

This farmer from the hills of northern Aragon had just cleared the doorway when his thigh went numb and he pitched forward.

Daniel looked down at his trousers. A new bullet hole had opened there and blood poured from it.

Agony rolled through him in a wave.

He tried to crawl, to leave this terrible place but his body no longer obeyed.

Daniel's sight blurred. He saw the golden wheat of home. Maria stood in the field smiling at him.

*"Maria."*

He whispered her name. His head lolled to one side. Consciousness left him.

The same scene played out on every street of Seville at once.

The first wave of the Royal Army and the British could not even advance a hundred meters before it smashed to pieces against the net of interlocking fire, barricades and indoor death traps.

They abandoned corpses strewn across the ground and groaning wounded and fell back in disorder.

In the rear provisional command post Jos√© Sanjurjo, commander of the Royal Army's 24th Infantry Division, listened to the courier's report from the front with a face like iron.

*"What did you say? The first wave was beaten back?"*

*"Yes, General."*

The courier kept his head down. He did not dare meet the man's eyes.

*"Our men could not force a way in. The casualties are very heavy."*

*"Worthless! All of them worthless!"*

Sanjurjo kicked the chair beside him over. The atmosphere in the tent turned oppressive at once.

He could not understand it. He held the numerical advantage and had British reinforcements. How could the first attack look like this?

*"What sorcery did those Germans use? Have they started sending mages with their columns?"*

He clawed at his hair like a maddened beast.

*"General, the enemy tactics are very strange. They gave up the streets and put all their strength inside the buildings. The moment our men entered the city they came under fire from every direction. We could not organize an effective counterattack."*

*"Inside buildings?"*

Sanjurjo blinked.

Sanjurjo was no genius of generalship but he had spent half his life in his uniform in Aragon and fought his share of battles. He had never heard of fighting like this.

Sanjurjo believed war meant ordered lines on open ground. Artillery, cavalry and infantry were supposed to fight together. The aim was to find the enemy's main force and destroy it in a field battle.

Only a brilliant victory in the style of Austerlitz was truly civilized war, war that did not reach civilians.

What were the Germans doing hiding in houses?

Was that even war?

*"Where are the British? Their Northumberland Fusiliers were supposed to follow behind. What did they report?"*

Now Sanjurjo remembered his allies.

The officer's face grew uglier still.

*"The British saw our troops stall and pulled straight back. They never committed to the fight."*

*"What?"*

Sanjurjo felt his blood pressure spike.

He had always known the proud British looked down on Royal Army troops and treated them as cannon fodder. He had not thought they would be this cold.

Humiliation and rage surged together.

Without the British were Royal Army soldiers good for nothing?

*"Pass my orders!"*

Sanjurjo's eyes were bloodshot. He stabbed a finger at Seville on the map and roared.

*"Another attack! Press every unit forward!"*

*"General, but"*

*"No buts!"*

Sanjurjo cut the officer off brusquely.

*"Have the surviving magic crystal battery open fire! Pound the city! Level those fortifications!"*

A staff officer stepped forward in alarm.

*"General, think again! If we fire now we reveal the position of our last magic crystal battery! German cavalry are still prowling outside. That is far too dangerous!"*

*"I cannot worry about that now!"*

Sanjurjo had lost his head. He only wanted the heaviest barrage possible to wash away the shame.

*"Execute the order! Now! Immediately!"*

Seeing the general's frenzy everyone fell silent.

They knew the second wave would be bloodier and crueler than the first.

Orders were orders.

Soon the dull thunder of guns rolled again. This time the shells were aimed inside the city of Seville.

**Boom!**

**Boom rumble!**

Dull heavy explosions began to sound one after another inside the city of Seville.

The earth shook through the city and houses swayed on every street. Doomsday seemed to have come to the city.

The last surviving magic crystal gun position opened fire on General Sanjurjo's reckless order. The crews poured shells into the northern districts of the city where enemy troops had appeared.

The gunners did not need precision. The mage apprentices did not even take part in guidance. The crews only set a rough direction and angle from the map and the coordinates and sent the shells on their way.

This time they were not using the shrapnel magic crystal shells meant to kill men. They were using high explosive magic crystal shells with far greater blast and shock wave.

Those shells outmatched ordinary artillery by a wide margin.

Most buildings in Seville were ordinary brick and tile. Under the high explosive magic crystal shells they went down like paper. One block after another collapsed into ruin.

Most civilians had already left the city. If they had not, the casualties would have been enormous.

Smoke and dust rose into the sky and hid it from view.

General Sanjurjo watched from the rear as smoke billowed from the city, his face twisted with vicious delight.

*"Blast them! Blast them all!"*

He murmured to himself.

*"Let us see where those cowards can hide now!"*

He believed that under such earth shaking fire every hard defense would break and every stubborn will would crumble.

In the siege battles he had known before, blasting the defenses with artillery was a common method.

He did not know that this grand bombardment had done very little real harm to the garrison inside the city.

Max's first set of street fighting notes had stated one rule above the rest.

*"Before enemy infantry close in, the main force should stay deep inside the city or in solid cellars to wait out enemy artillery."*

Only a few bold careful men were posted on high points such as church bell towers to serve as observation posts.

Two German soldiers stood in the bell tower of the tallest church in Seville. They watched the ground beyond the walls through binoculars, their nerves tight.

Heavy explosions kept tearing through the districts around them. Even the bell tower swayed.

*"Did you see the enemy artillery position?"*

One soldier spoke in a low voice.

*"I see it! Eleven o'clock, behind that patch of woods!"*

The other soldier answered.

*"Damn it, they hid it deep!"*

Their task was to watch enemy artillery fire. The moment the guns fell silent and enemy infantry began to move forward they had to warn the units to take their fighting positions.

The two men kept watching through the blasts. Anxiety and tension wore on them both.

The fire from beyond the walls stopped suddenly.

*"What happened? They stopped?"*

*"Look! The enemy is coming up!"*

Through the binoculars they saw a black mass of Royal Army soldiers pour from the positions outside the city like a tide. The second assault on Seville had begun.

*"Quick! Warn the other units!"*

*"Right away!"*

One soldier slid down the bell tower stairs. He put the whistle on his neck between his teeth at once.

**Tweeee!**

A sharp whistle cut the air. More whistles answered in turn and carried the signal from the church out across the other districts.

The garrison inside Seville had been quiet. Men who heard the whistles moved at once through secret passages punched through the interiors of buildings. They reached the second line of positions in structures that still stood.

Royal Army soldiers crossed the ruins the artillery had torn apart with fear in every step. No new fire struck them. For a moment many men breathed easier.

*"Looks like the Germans and the rebels are all dead from the artillery!"*

An officer forced a light tone into his voice. He raised his saber forward at an angle and sent the column deeper into the city.

On high ground outside Seville Colonel Braun of the 52nd Cavalry Regiment held his binoculars to his eyes. He studied the distant city.

When he saw enemy soldiers packed around the walls like ants on a mound his gaze turned sharp.

The enemy had attacked again. This time they had brought magic crystal artillery into play. If the cavalry could not destroy the last gun position the pressure on the defenders would grow much worse.

*"Colonel! We found it!"*

An excited shout came from his side. The cavalry colonel lowered his binoculars. A thin smile crossed his face.

The enemy gun position had given itself away in the bombardment.

*"Messenger!"*

Colonel Braun roared.

*"Here!"*

*"Send the coordinates to the horse artillery battalion! Have them move to the preset positions! They must finish deployment within fifteen minutes!"*

*"Order the 1st and 2nd Cavalry Battalions to prepare to charge! The moment the horse artillery finish firing they charge with me!"*

*"Yes, Colonel!"*

The messenger dug his heels into his mount and rode. The orders spread through the regiment at speed.

The horse artillery battalion of the cavalry regiment fielded twelve 77 millimeter field guns.

The caliber was modest. The guns made up for it in mobility. Six strong draft horses pulled each piece. The teams could move fast, even over rough ground.

The gun crews were veterans. At the positions the crews unhitched the horses from the pieces. The guns came up from march order into firing order. The crews set the muzzles and loaded the shells. The work ran without a pause.

In less than fifteen minutes twelve 77 millimeter field guns had raised their muzzles toward the magic crystal gun position that had been raging a moment before.

Gun number one finished two ranging rounds in quick order. The other eleven crews set their firing angles with the same brisk skill.

*"Fire!"*

The mobile artillery commander gave the word. Rolling gunfire broke out across the open field.

Twelve field guns fired together. Shells screamed through the air. Most of them fell inside the magic crystal gun position.

Explosions rose and fell without end.

The poor mobility of the magic crystal guns showed itself at once.

The crews had already taken ranging fire. When the German salvo landed the heavy pieces were still not packed for the road. They could not move in time.

Gunners and horses were thrown down in the blasts. Men screamed for their mothers and fathers.

The whole position turned into fire and disorder in an instant.

*"Charge!"*

Colonel Braun was the first to raise his lance and point it forward.

*"For the Emperor and the Empire!"*

**Wooo!**

The charge call rang loud.

The two cavalry battalions had waited ready. The first rank lowered their lances. They drove toward the position still under shellfire like a burst dam.

A forest of lance points in cold light ended the enemy gun crews that had caused so much trouble in an instant.

The gun position garrison had already taken artillery fire. When cavalry hit them in a wall of horse and steel they could not mount a useful defense.

The horsemen were clearing the last men who still fought on the position when the situation changed.

Three figures wrapped in strong magic power flew toward the field from far off at great speed.

Eldridge, the high ranking mage, led them.

When he saw the magic crystal gun position torn nearly to nothing his face turned black with rage.

Colonel Braun saw the three shapes racing through the air from the back of his charging horse.

A bad feeling rose in his chest.

*"Mages! Watch out!"*

He shouted the warning with everything he had.

The warning came too late.

Eldridge only lifted his hand and spoke a few syllables.

The next moment more than a hundred horsemen in the lead rank rose into the air with their mounts. They hung there as if a hand they could not see had seized them.

They screamed in terror. They fought the air. Nothing they did broke the hold.

Colonel Braun's last sight was his own body and the bodies of his comrades driven into the ground by that unseen hand.

Flesh and bone flew apart.

The world went dark.
