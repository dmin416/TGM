# Summary 1.5 (Chapter 1.5)

Arc: Emperor side view 1. Late January 1914, night, Potsdam, Sanssouci Palace.

* A coded telegram from the Spain front reaches Emperor Albert II in his study, with Chief of the Great General Staff Helmuth von Moltke the Younger and Chancellor Theobald von Bethmann Hollweg.
* The message reports Mackensen's capture and rescue; Albert is first alarmed, then relieved that the worst diplomatic and press disaster was avoided.
* Moltke urges retaliation: the Britannic ambush insults the army and the emperor, and Spain must not fall to Holy Britannia if the Royal Army wins.
* Bethmann adds political stakes: Crown Prince Alfonso favors Germany, and new surveys suggest southern Spain may hold the continent's largest Aetherium Crystal belt, so the civil war is no longer minor.
* Albert weighs magic crystal scarcity, rival magitech powers France and Britannia, depleted skyfall crystal stocks, and why both giants moved on Spain now.
* He asks whether a strong strike is wise and whether it equals declaring war on Holy Britannia; Moltke says Britannia already chose war and promises coordinated action with the National Army and International Brigades in Spain.
* Both men are uneasy about seven International Brigades of ideological volunteers the empire arms and funds, and possible future enemies at home.
* Bethmann warns rising Social Democrat marches and press hero worship of the brigades; Albert agrees to tolerate the left for now until Spain is settled.
* Albert authorizes necessary military action in Spain with allies to show Britannia force.
* He also orders Mackensen recalled home for rest before battle, calling him too precious for a limited war and wanting a steadier commander.
* Moltke is shocked at changing command on the eve of fighting but does not argue aloud.
* Albert asks who should replace Mackensen; Moltke names Maximilian von Prittwitz as calm, defensive and safe enough for Spain.
* The emperor approves and orders Prittwitz sent to the front at once to relieve Mackensen.
