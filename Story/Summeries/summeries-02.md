# Summary 2 (Chapter 2)

Arc: Deployment. Late January 1914, night, forward camp and march toward San Isidro.

* Assembly whistles mobilize the company; Max orders orderly Klaus to rouse the platoon, strike tents and send non carried gear to the train section.
* Max arms himself with his predecessor's Gewehr 98, P08 Luger and spike helmet; capture left him only uniform and his looted revolver, and his ID must be reissued.
* The camp packs down fast; Max's 3rd Company belongs to the 1st Battalion, 33rd Infantry Regiment, within the 16th Infantry Brigade.
* At brigade HQ Lieutenant General Mackensen, still bandaged, studies Seville; he wanted immediate retaliation after rescue but sent a telegram to the General Staff for offensive authorization (hours round trip on the railway).
* Staff warn Seville is fortified with the Royal Army 24th Division, Northumberland Fusiliers and cavalry, outnumbering and outpreparing the Germans for a costly assault.
* Mackensen counts National Army and International Brigade brigades on the flanks so paper strength is balanced and dismisses Royal Army quality in a straight fight.
* Officers name the greater threat: Highland Mage Corps and Order of Garde Knights possibly in the city.
* Mackensen recalls North Africa: a British archmage's meteor barrage wiped a regiment; Garde Knights broke two more until Teutonic Knights and the Brunswick Death Hussars barely saved the corps.
* Teutonic Knight armored units are still on a train and should arrive before dawn; Mackensen orders the brigade and attached cavalry and artillery into attack positions before formal approval returns.
* He broods on young staff lacking colonial combat experience and on Max as a once useless Dresden salon heir who stayed silent under torture, while the telegram from a connected woman still angers him.
* After nearly three hours the General Staff authorizes necessary military action in Spain but orders Mackensen home to recuperate and replaces him with General von Prittwitz, whom he despises as overly defensive.
* Mackensen still commands until Prittwitz arrives and tells the brigade to attack as planned and drive Royal and British forces from Seville.
* Meanwhile the 1st Battalion marches by night toward the assembly area; Max leads a platoon in 3rd Company, fighting his body's green nerves with officer training and memory.
* At the woods near San Isidro, Captain Hauser briefs platoon leaders: 1st Battalion is first echelon for a head on assault on San Isidro village, but 3rd Company is battalion reserve behind 1st and 2nd Companies.
* Max is sickened by the planned tactics: narrow front, three skirmish lines 150 meters wide, 2nd Company bayonet follow up, essentially early World War I massed assault doctrine in a world with magic and magitek.
* Scouts confirm Royal Army in the village but not strength; brigade artillery will fire three rapid volleys before a 05:07 assault.
* Hauser lends Max a pocket watch after his was lost in capture.
* Hauser predicts weak Royal conscripts and an easy win where reserve may not fight; Max notes widespread German overconfidence.
* Sergeant Klaus promises to help command the platoon through the coming fight.
* Max opens the system map, sees friendly unit markers and fog of war over San Isidro with only an unknown Royal Army symbol, plus a forward 77mm artillery battalion set for area suppression.
* The company moves to its reserve line behind scrub; Max watches 1st and 2nd Companies form skirmish and assault lines as the watch nears 05:07 and the chapter ends on the opening whistle of the bombardment.
