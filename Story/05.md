# Chapter 5

Arc: San Isidro

Date: Late January 1914, afternoon

Max and his six-man team pedaled hard and soon left the area around San Isidro.

The aftertaste of the *Muckefuck* still circled in his mouth, a mild scorched bitterness.

The country road was rough and bumpy. Wheels rattled over stones and clods of dirt with a constant creak as Max rode, keeping part of his attention on the minimap that had appeared in the upper-left of his vision.

Perhaps because he had just studied the battalion's paper map his own system map had updated and a minimap overlay had activated, condensing the tactical picture into a constant small display at the corner of his field of view.

The area ahead all the way to Seville now showed real detail, though it was still covered by a translucent layer of fog of war.

As they advanced that fog slowly pulled back in a circle centered on them, revealing sharper terrain features.

The feeling was strangely familiar, like playing a strategy game and personally steering the scout unit to uncover the map.

*"Don't just lower your heads and ride,"* Max reminded the men behind him in a low voice. *"Watch the woods and high ground on both sides. If anything looks off tell me immediately."*

*"Yes, sir,"* Corporal Baumann answered at once.

The others raised their heads too, scanning their surroundings with care.

They thought it was just a normal caution from their leader. They did not realize that every direction their eyes covered was also feeding Max's map with valuable updates.

During the earlier fight Max had already more or less figured out how the system map refreshed.

Anything that entered a friendly unit's field of view would appear on the map, whether that friendly unit noticed it or recognized it or not.

In terms of advantages the system map truly earned its keep.

Their first objective was the next village marked on the map: Alcolea.

Max planned to recon the outskirts first. If it looked safe they would enter to get a read on the situation and also let this makeshift recon team get a bit of practice working together.

Then depending on what they found they would scout farther toward Seville.

The whole ride was calm and they did not spot a single shadow.

When they were a little over two hundred meters from Alcolea Max raised a hand signal and everyone slowed to a stop.

*"Dismount and push the bikes. Find a place to hide them."*

They wheeled the bicycles into a dense patch of woods by the road, laid them down carefully behind brush and camouflaged them with branches and fallen leaves.

*"Remember this spot,"* Max said, pointing to a dead tree snapped partway up its trunk. *"We'll fall back here later."*

Max unscrewed his canteen, drank some water and motioned for the others to drink as well.

After the brief pause the six men shouldered rifles, formed a loose shape and began to creep toward the village.

Max led with his eyes moving constantly between the village entrance and the nearby houses while his finger rested by habit on the trigger guard.

Max glanced back and saw Baumann and the others walking with their index fingers on their triggers, ready to fire at the slightest twitch.

Max's heart tightened and he signaled them to stop.

*"Fingers off the triggers and onto the trigger guards. Keep them there,"* he hissed.

*"I don't want some idiot slipping and firing a round into my back or exposing all of us!"*

The soldiers were not used to it but they complied immediately.

Hugging the shadow of a dirt wall they slipped cautiously into the village.

The village was peaceful, which surprised Max.

Children chased each other along the dirt road. Near a well women washed clothes and chatted. A few old men even sat lazily in doorways, sunning themselves.

There was no hint of war's effects here. It felt like they had stumbled into an isolated paradise.

The abnormal calm pushed Max's caution to its highest point.

Max decided to ask a villager what was going on, part of what reconnaissance called civil information gathering.

Max gave Baumann a look. Baumann understood and stepped forward, stopping an old farmer carrying a hoe.

When the farmer saw armed soldiers he seemed only surprised and not frightened.

Baumann pointed toward Seville, made a few gestures and spoke several words that were clearly not German.

The farmer stared at the tall foreign soldier, face full of confusion. The farmer muttered in another language and waved both hands.

*"Sir,"* Baumann said helplessly over his shoulder, *"he doesn't understand the Spanish I'm speaking."*

*"Are you sure you're speaking Spanish?"* Max asked flatly.

*"…"*

Max frowned and looked to the others.

They all shook their heads. Aside from a few simple phrases some officers knew none of them could speak Spanish.

Max sighed and walked over to the old man himself. Max listened more closely. Spanish was not a language he had spoken in any of his previous lives.

The old farmer spoke again at length, and something strange happened.

The rapid unfamiliar syllables entering Max's ears translated themselves automatically. Max understood the meaning perfectly.

*"If you're looking for the soldiers in black uniforms, they left a while ago…"*

Max paused, somewhat surprised.

*That's pretty nifty.*

Max opened his mouth and responded, listening to the fluent Spanish that came out.

*"Friend, do you mean there were soldiers in black uniforms here earlier?"*

The moment the words left him the farmer stared in shock. Max himself went rigid. Even Baumann and the others behind him widened their eyes.

*"Sir… you can speak Spanish?"* Baumann blurted out.

Then a flood of fragmented memories surged into Max's mind.

In the Imperial capital of Dresden, inside a brightly lit banquet hall, the original owner of this body held a wine glass and flirted in smooth Spanish with a Spanish noblewoman studying abroad in Germany.

In an art salon Max discussed the latest paintings with a painter's wife in French.

There were even memories of a vacation in Vienna where Max spoke the local language well enough to charm a maid.

So that was it.

The original owner, the man General Mackensen had described as softened by alcohol, had actually worked hard to learn multiple foreign languages, all so he could have someone to talk to.

For that pampered young gentleman language was not knowledge.

It was a key that opened different doors.

*Damn. What a talent.*

The motives were questionable but the skill itself, right now, was practically divine.

*"You speak our language?"* the farmer asked, clearly surprised by the fluent Spanish.

*"A little,"* Max said, steadying himself. Max continued in Spanish. *"Do you know how many of those soldiers there were and which way they went?"*

*"About an hour ago,"* the farmer said, *"a few hundred passed through the east end of the village. Loud as can be. They even stole several chickens!"*

The farmer raised a wrinkled hand and pointed toward Seville. *"They went that way."*

With that key information Max asked a few more questions in a friendly tone about the village and the surrounding terrain.

The farmer answered them one by one.

Finally Max had Baumann hand over a strip of black bread and pressed it into the farmer's hands.

*"Sorry to trouble you,"* Max said.

Then Max led the men out of the village at once.

Back at the hidden bicycles in the woods Baumann could not hold it in any longer.

Baumann leaned in, face full of admiration.

*"Sir, you really can speak Spanish? How did you learn it?"*

Max cleared his throat and put on an inscrutable expression.

*"Just a little,"* he said. *"As a hobby."*

Max made up the answer casually, not wanting to say that the original owner had been lonely, then immediately shifted topics with his face turning serious.

*"Situation's changed. A Royal Army force of several hundred passed through here an hour ago heading toward Seville."*

Max unfolded a simple sketch map and tapped a point on it.

*"But we don't know if they went straight back to Seville or intend to set an ambush on the outskirts. We need to push forward and recon a bit farther."*

Max and the others swung back onto their bicycles and rushed along the direction the farmer said the Royal Army column had gone.

After hundreds of marching men and wagons had ground over the country dirt road they had left behind an unmistakable trail, so obvious it did not take any effort to follow.

*"Sir, look!"* Corporal Baumann pointed at a cap lying in the roadside grass.

The cap was worn and from the Spanish Royal Army, the brim still smeared with mud, clearly dropped by some soldier during the march and not even worth bending down to retrieve.

Max only spared it a glance and kept pedaling.

Not far ahead they spotted an abandoned ammunition box, empty and tossed brazenly right in the middle of the road.

Farther on, uncollected piles of horse dung were scattered across the track.

Max almost wanted to laugh.

This was not a regular army on the move. It was a rabble out for a picnic.

Their level of military discipline was worse than college students on a forced march back in Max's old world, and at least students knew to carry their trash away.

*"Looks like they really did head straight back to Seville,"* Max called back to Baumann.

From the traces they had left the column's direction was clear. There were no signs of splitting up or setting an ambush.

*Where did they get the confidence to believe the Germans wouldn't catch up this quickly?*

But that confidence, or ignorance, ended up giving Max's team perfect cover.

Following the trail the Royal Army had left behind them the six men pushed south without interruption.

As Seville drew nearer the land rose gradually.

Finally when they reached the crest of a low hill the sight ahead made everyone stop.

Roughly two kilometers in front of them Seville's old outer buildings were faintly visible.

On a piece of high ground outside the city a force was resting in place. A Spanish Royal Army flag snapped in the wind at the top of the rise.

*"Found them,"* Max muttered, taking his binoculars from around his neck and adjusting the focus.

Through the lenses Max could clearly see Royal Army soldiers in black uniforms.

Their numbers matched what the old farmer had said and it should be the same unit that passed through Alcolea.

If their organization was roughly comparable to the German side it looked like around half a battalion.

On the high ground scattered tents and hastily built cover positions had already taken shape.

Royal Army soldiers moved about in small clusters and some had even lit fires at the forward edge of the position, showing virtually no sense of security.

Max scanned the rise carefully. Soon something strange caught his eye.

Farther back on the high ground several pieces of artillery had been emplaced but they looked odd.

They did not resemble any gun the German Army fielded and they differed greatly from any conventional tube artillery Max knew.

The barrels were exceptionally thick and short. On the carriage and the stubby barrel there were inlaid crystals that faintly glowed.

As Max's confusion rose the system interface in his mind automatically popped a translucent window.

As his gaze focused on those strange guns new information surfaced.

```
Special unit detected. Parsing…
Spanish Royal Army — Mana-crystal artillery position
Weapon model: British "Comet" Type I mana-crystal cannon
Effective range: 4,500 meters
Ammunition: high-power mana-crystal shells; fragmentation mana-crystal shells
```

*"Mana-crystal cannon…?"* Max repeated silently, a bad feeling crawling up his spine.

Just from the name it was obvious this was not something friendly and it was likely a product of the world's unique magitek.

Max shifted his focus back to the system map.

Around the city icon representing Seville the hill they had just discovered now displayed two separate red unit markers.

One for the half-battalion of Royal Army infantry and one for the mana-crystal cannon position behind them.

What made Max's scalp prickle was this.

As Max continued scanning the surroundings with binoculars more red diamond markers representing sizable formations began appearing one after another to Seville's southeast on the map.

It suggested that the Royal Army and the British had concluded that the outer villages alone could not stop the German advance, nor the National Army and the International Brigade pushing on other axes.

So they had pulled the defense line back into the city's near suburbs, relying on these unknown mana-crystal cannons to build a complete defensive ring.

And Max's tiny six-man reconnaissance team right now had already slipped past every outer village and plunged straight into the belly of that ring.

On the map the small gold star representing their team sat alone inside a broad red enemy zone, isolated and unsupported.

*"Hmm…"*

Max lowered his binoculars and was thoughtful for a moment.

Max had overplayed it. They had rushed in too deep in a single push.

Because the Royal Army had not set up any checkpoints on the road toward Seville, Max's team had ridden unimpeded and their pace was much faster than even Max had expected.

The other soldiers still had not grasped how serious the danger was. They were only marveling at the scale of the enemy positions and the unfamiliar weapons.

Only Max, thanks to the god's-eye map, could clearly see their situation.

The good news was that this reconnaissance run had already produced valuable intelligence that would help the later assault.

The bad news was that their current position was dangerous.

*"Sir, we…"* Baumann had noticed Max's face change and edged closer, tense.

Max drew a deep breath.

As the core of this small team, panicking would do nothing but get everyone killed.

Max pushed his bicycle down behind the crest into cover, laid it flat and gave the others a low hide signal.

*"We may have gone a little too deep."*

*"Then what do we do, sir? Withdraw?"* Baumann and the others gathered around him, fear written plainly across their faces.

Max did not answer immediately.

Lying prone on the slope Max raised his binoculars again and studied the enemy position in the distance, mind racing.

*Withdraw?*

*No. If they'd come this far, how could they turn back after seeing only this much?*

The Royal Army's defensive deployment looked tight on paper but the soldiers' lax behavior was practically a list of openings.

*"Since we're already here…"* Max muttered.

Having already entered the tiger's den leaving right now felt like a waste.

Great reward lived behind great risk.

If Max could get truly valuable intelligence, making the coming assault smoother and reducing casualties, then Max's own odds of surviving would rise accordingly.

In that case the risk would be worth it.

Max swept his eyes across the system map. In his view the small moving icons representing enemy patrols were crystal clear.

That one-way transparency, information visible to him but not to them, was their greatest advantage.

*"No,"* Max said, making a decision that shocked everyone. *"We keep moving forward."*

*"Sir?!"* Baumann's voice jumped an octave.

*"Listen to my orders."*

Max's voice was not loud but it carried an unquestionable decisiveness.

*"We're not taking the main road. We'll thread through these country lanes and woods, get closer to the outer edge of the city and take another look."*

Max pointed at the gaps between patrol icons on the map, sketching a new route in his head.

*"Everyone hide the bicycles. We go on foot. Stay quiet and remember it."*

The soldiers looked at each other but in the end they chose obedience.

Led by Max the six-man team moved through Seville's outskirts.

The closer they got to the city the heavier the tension in the air became.

Max poured nearly all of his focus into the minimap in his vision.

Then at the edge of the map several moving red diamonds suddenly flickered into view.

```
Royal Army patrol (number: 12)
```

*"Stop and hide."* Max slashed a hand down, voice pressed to the lowest whisper.

Everyone immediately flattened into a waist-high irrigation ditch nearby. The ditch looked abandoned and overgrown with weeds, perfect for hiding their bodies.

Half a minute later a lazy line of Royal Army soldiers with cigarettes in their mouths strolled along the berm not far from the ditch.

They complained as they walked and never noticed the danger right beside them.

Only when the red dots on the map had moved away did Max let out a breath and motion for the team to continue.

Baumann looked at Max with even more admiration.

To Baumann it was as though the platoon leader had a third eye, always sensing danger before it arrived.

Baumann and the others did not know that what looked like supernatural prediction was for Max nothing more than having the full map open.

Enemy movements were almost one-way transparent to him.

Relying on that advantage they slipped past several patrols without incident and successfully infiltrated Seville's suburban belt.

When they quietly reached the outermost residential area of Seville what they saw in front of them gave Max an entirely new understanding of how low this army's floor could be.

In the street clusters of Royal Army soldiers leaned against walls, sunning themselves.

Some swaggered into roadside cafés and took coffee and pastries from terrified shopkeepers without paying a single coin.

Not far away in a corner several soldiers were playing cards and making a lot of noise.

This did not look like an army about to face a major battle. It looked like bandits who had occupied a city.

Then something even more outrageous happened.

Four men in smart military uniforms laughed loudly and forcibly dragged three young girls away from the street.

Ignoring the girls' screams and resistance the soldiers dragged them directly into a two-story building that appeared to have been requisitioned.

The surrounding soldiers were used to it and some even whistled and laughed.

When the girls' family and some angry citizens rushed forward to argue the soldiers immediately raised their rifles.

Dark muzzles pointed at these unarmed people, keeping them firmly outside.

Max's body tensed and something cold moved through him, but the corners of his mouth tickled with a smile that he could not quite stop. It was hard to reconcile what he was watching with the ideals of a so-called army but it was true that no matter what world or what dimension there was never a shortage of scumbags.

Max may have once been a saint but those days were long behind him. He had been given the privilege of making others learn that there were consequences to their actions.

Max rubbed his chest in a small cross pattern and got his emotions under control.

In his anger a bold idea also arose in his mind.

These overconfident officers were practically an open door.

Max noticed that although the soldiers had stopped the civilians no one dared approach the two-story building.

Clearly they did not want to disturb their superiors' pleasures.

That also meant the building had become a relatively isolated and unguarded target.

A cold glint flashed in Max's eyes.

Max gestured to Corporal Baumann and the others behind him, pointing to the back of the building.

Everyone understood immediately.

They quietly bypassed the street and using the buildings as cover quickly arrived at the backyard of the small building.

Just as Max had predicted there were no guards around.

The courtyard wall was not high and they easily climbed over it.

In the backyard of the small building only some hens huddled against the wall, showing no reaction to Max and his uninvited guests.

It was as if they had tacitly approved of something.

The cries of the girls and the maniacal laughter coming from upstairs fueled the anger of Corporal Baumann and his men and provided them with perfect cover for their actions.

Max gestured for everyone to sling their rifles behind their backs.

Max pulled the sling of his Gewehr 98 to its shortest length so that the rifle was pressed tightly against his back, then held it loosely with his hand to reduce bumps and noise during movement.

The nearly 1.3-meter-long rifle was an accurate weapon for killing enemies in open terrain but it became a burden in a narrow indoor environment.

The others immediately followed suit.

*"Bayonet,"* Max ordered in a whisper.

**Shhk. Shhk. Shhk.**

Six men including Max drew their S98/05 bayonets from their scabbards.

The standard bayonet of the German Army was over half a meter long, less like a knife and more like a short sword, gleaming with a cold sharp light in the dim backyard.

Max led the way to the back door of the building and found it ajar.

Max gently pushed the door open a crack, listened intently for a moment and after confirming no one was downstairs slipped inside.

A scent of alcohol and cologne drifted out to meet them.

They tiptoed to the foot of the stairs leading to the second floor. The noise from above grew louder.

*"What are you struggling for? Country bumpkins like you wouldn't even be fit for my bed in the north!"*

*"It is an honor for you to serve us and you should be grateful!"*

*"We're helping you fight the rebels and the Germans, we're risking our lives, so you'd better serve us well!"*

*Is this even human language?*

Max's gaze grew even colder. This had little to do with country or identity.

Max simply wanted to kill these scumbags who abused their status and bullied the weak.

Max peeked out from the stairs and saw that the three abducted girls seemed to have been taken to different rooms.

Those depraved officers had not even closed the doors.

It was practically written all over them.

After going upstairs Max quickly glanced around and instantly understood the layout of the entire second-floor corridor.

In the two rooms on the left and right an officer had a girl pinned on a table and on the floor, tearing at her clothes.

In the room directly opposite the stairwell the situation was even worse, with two officers working together to drag a struggling girl toward the bed.

Max turned his head back and quickly issued combat orders using simple clear sign language.

Max pointed to the two rooms on the left and right then nodded to the four soldiers, indicating they should work in pairs and deal with them separately.

Then Max pointed to himself and Corporal Baumann and then to the room in the middle.

Everyone nodded knowingly.

Max took a deep breath, his eyes sharpening. Max held up three fingers then withdrew them one by one.

The moment he retracted the last finger he swung his hand downward.

Six figures silently pounced on their respective targets.

In the middle room the two Royal Army officers were immersed in the excitement of their impending success, completely unaware that death was about to descend.

When one of them sensed something amiss behind him and instinctively turned around it was already too late.

Max appeared behind him, his left hand flashing out to cover his mouth and nose from behind, silencing him completely.

At the same time the bayonet gripped tightly in Max's right hand was already placed against his neck with its cold blade.

This one needed to be spared for now. Max and the others would have questions to put to him later.

Corporal Baumann's actions were more direct.

Baumann pounced on the other officer and pinned him to the ground.

Before the other officer could react the bayonet in Baumann's hand pierced cleanly through his neck.

The officer did not even have time to scream before his body convulsed a few times and fell completely silent.

Just like slaughtering an animal.

The battle in the other two rooms ended just as quickly.

With an absolute advantage of two against one the other two officers acting alone had no chance to resist and were eliminated almost simultaneously.

The whole process took less than ten seconds.

Rather than immediately killing the prisoner in his hands Max tapped him on the neck with the back of his bayonet, signaling him to behave.

The three rescued girls, dressed in rags, huddled in a corner trembling with fear, their eyes filled with terror and bewilderment.

Max had two soldiers bring blankets from the room and cover the girls, then took them to an empty room next door.

Max and Corporal Baumann then began interrogating the officer who had been spared.

*Oh how the tables have turned.*

Two days ago Max had been the prisoner, tied to a chair.

Now Max held the power of life and death over someone else.

The officer looked at the miserable state of his comrade on the ground and felt the cold blade at his neck. His body trembled violently and his crotch quickly became wet.

*"I'm going to ask you a few questions. And I would like you to answer them."*

Max spoke coldly in fluent Spanish.

*"If you dare to play any tricks, or if you displease me, I've been needing to practice slicing chorizo. Do you understand?"*


Upon hearing Max's words the officer nodded frantically like a chicken pecking at rice, his eyes filled with the relief of surviving a close call.

*"Name, rank, unit number."*

Max's voice was neutral and his face wore a slight smile which served only to scare the officer more.

*"Juan de Rivera,"* the officer said, his voice hoarse and trembling, tinged with a heavy sob. *"Lieutenant Colonel, Battalion Commander, 1st Battalion, 126th Regiment, 48th Brigade, 24th Infantry Division of the State Council."*

Both Max and Corporal Baumann were somewhat surprised by the answer.

They had thought they had only taken down a few ordinary officers but they never expected to catch a big fish, a genuine lieutenant colonel battalion commander.

However looking at the man in front of him who was so scared he had wet himself and had no military bearing whatsoever Max began to have doubts.

*"Lieutenant Colonel Battalion Commander? You?"*

Max lightly tapped the other man's cheek with the flat of his bayonet.

*"It doesn't look like it to me."*

Upon hearing his words Juan's body trembled violently and he almost blurted out the answer.

*"It was bought, sir! This military rank was bought with money!"*

*"I was originally just a nobleman from the north. When I heard that the allies of the British Empire were coming to help I thought the war would end soon. So I thought I'd spend money to join the army and get some seniority, to prepare for the future."*

These words made Max feel relieved about his previous behavior. It was a pipe dream to expect a speculator who climbed the ladder through scheming and money to have backbone.

*"Very good. You are very honest."*

Max nodded and continued.

*"Has the entire 24th Infantry Division been deployed to Seville?"*

*"Yes! The entire division has come and is stationed in the city and surrounding positions!"*

*"What is your mission in Seville?"*

Juan dared not conceal anything and told everything he knew.

*"Our mission is to rely on the city and cooperate with the friendly forces of the British Empire to stop your attack."*

*"Where are the British? Where are they?"*

*"They're here too! Their people are in the city! There seem to be two or three thousand of them, supposedly the elite troops of the Northumberland Fusiliers!"*

Juan seemed to remember something important and in order to save his life hurriedly added.

*"Sir! The British have also sent more than a dozen mages in robes this time!"*

*"Mages? Hmm."*

Max fell into thought.

The lieutenant colonel forced an awkward smile but before he could even breathe a sigh of relief he suddenly felt a chill in his chest.

Juan looked down and saw a thin long bayonet plunged into his heart.

Max slowly pulled out his bayonet. The lieutenant colonel battalion commander who had just been thankful for his survival collapsed to the ground, his face frozen in disbelief.

Max used Juan's relatively clean military uniform to wipe the sticky blood off the bayonet then sheathed it.

Perhaps because his target was such a scumbag Max felt no psychological burden whatsoever, only a sense of peace after cleaning up the trash.

Max turned to look at Corporal Baumann, his voice very calm.

*"We obtained some very valuable items on this trip. We gained a lot."*

*"But now we need to think about how to get back."*

The two turned and went back to the empty room next door.

The three rescued girls were still huddled together. When they saw Max and Baumann come in they shrank back involuntarily, their eyes filled with lingering fear.

Max could completely understand their fear. They did not look very old and it would be strange if they were not afraid in this situation. Fortunately Max and his men had acted quickly and the officers had not had time to cause any more serious harm. The girls were only greatly frightened.

Max slowed his pace and crouched down a few steps away from them, trying to make his tone sound as friendly as possible.

*"Don't be afraid. We've dealt with all the bad guys and no one will hurt you anymore."*

The three girls were silent for a moment. One of them, a little bolder than the others, raised her head and asked timidly.

*"Are you… part of the National Army?"*

Max shook his head.

The girl seemed to pause for a moment then mustered her courage and asked again.

*"So you're the International Brigades that the adults were talking about, the ones that came from other places to help?"*

Max shook his head again.

The girl hesitated. She looked at the unfamiliar military uniform on Max and then at Corporal Baumann, who stood behind him silent and still.

Finally in an almost inaudible voice she asked.

*"So, are you a good person?"*

This question surprised Max.

Max laughed and asked in return.

*"What do you think?"*

The three girls looked at each other and nodded slightly.

*"You look fierce,"* the lead girl whispered, *"but you're probably good people."*

Max smiled but shook his head.

*"Good and bad aren't things that I can decide. You might think I'm good but the man behind me probably thinks I'm bad for stopping him."*

This answer completely stunned the girls. They could not understand why this person who had saved them would not admit to being a good person.

Corporal Baumann and the others were also stunned, because they did not understand what was being said at all. Only Baumann caught the words good and bad.

*"Then why did you save us?"*

*"Because I can't stand seeing your tears, and I'm not letting that jagoff get his rocks off."*

The unfamiliar words Max uttered left the three girls even more confused.

But for some reason his strange deliberately serious answer actually eased their fear and their tense nerves gradually relaxed.

*"Alright, listen carefully to what I'm about to say."*

Max's joking expression vanished.

*"What you need to do now is to quietly return to your families immediately. Then try to leave this city with your families or find a safe place to hide."*

Max paused then emphasized.

*"Although those bastards are dead I reckon their accomplices will be coming to cause trouble soon. Staying here will only put you in more danger. Understand?"*

The girls nodded, seemingly understanding but not quite.

Max stood up, walked to the window and carefully lifted a corner of the curtain to observe outside.

On the streets the citizens who had been driven back by gunfire had long since dispersed and the soldiers of the Royal Army had returned to their posts.

They gathered in small groups to smoke, chat, play cards and help themselves to food and drink without paying. No one paid any attention to the two-story building anymore.

After confirming it was safe Max turned around and extended his hand to the four soldiers standing apart from Baumann.

*"Give me all your grenades."*

Although the soldiers were somewhat puzzled they immediately took the grenades from their belts and put them in Max's hands.

Under the curious gaze of everyone Max began to get busy.

Max pulled some thread from the uniforms and several thin wires from the damaged furniture. Max positioned one body at the bottom of the stairwell and ran thread from the grenade's pull ring around the banister posts, tight enough to catch anyone coming up. The rest of the grenades Max hid beneath the colonel, wires connecting the pull rings to the bed frame and around the body.

*"Sir, what are you doing?"* Corporal Baumann approached, his face full of confusion.

Without looking up Max said.

*"Leaving a little surprise for those who come after."*

After arranging everything Max and his group, along with the three still-shaken girls, quietly left the two-story building through the back door.

Using the system map they easily bypassed the sight of all the nearby Royal Army soldiers and after winding through several alleys delivered the girls to a relatively safe neighborhood.

*"Alright, there are no more soldiers around here. Do you all know how to get back?"*

The three girls nodded.

*"Remember what I said. Hurry home and go somewhere safe with your families."*

The girls bowed gratefully then helped each other walk to the other side of the alley.

The bolder girl turned around and looked at Max. A smile appeared on her face.

*"You are a good person. Thank you!"*

Silently watching their retreating figures Max waved his hand and led his squad stealthily toward the outskirts of the city.

As they were about to leave the city Corporal Baumann finally could not hold back any longer.

*"Sir, what did she say to you back there? You looked strange after."*

*"She said I was a good person,"* Max said.

Baumann blinked.

*"Then why do you look like that?"*

*"Because to her people we are still foreign soldiers on their soil,"* Max said. *"She called me good because I helped her. That's not the same thing."*

Baumann chewed on that for a few steps then asked hesitantly.

*"But didn't we come here to break Britain's energy monopoly and allow all countries to share the progress of industrial technology?"*

*"Yes. Maybe that's true."*

Max nodded. Seeing Corporal Baumann's somewhat conflicted expression Max smiled and patted him on the shoulder.

*"Why are you thinking so much? As a soldier your duty is to obey orders and complete tasks. Don't overthink things."*

After answering Baumann's question the group quickened their pace. A complex expression settled in Max's eyes.

Max did not dwell on it long. There would always be some form of conflict in every world he visited and the only place where there was truly peace was by God's side, and he already knew that well enough. All Max could do now was try to survive this war with his people.
