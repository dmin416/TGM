# Chapter 20

Arc: Mackensen Again

Date: Early February 1914

---

Expedition headquarters under General August von Mackensen issued new orders at once.

Units shattered in the Seville defense would rotate to the rear for rest.

Two full German infantry divisions in good order and high spirits, plus part of the National Army, came in by military train and took over the line from the 16th Brigade and allied troops.

The 16th Infantry Brigade, half gone in men, finally gained a real breather.

Seville sat firmly in friendly hands and the rails ran clear, so Max and his men were spared a long march on foot.

They boarded a troop train from the station inside the city walls.

The Intel tab lit with a fresh notice at the same time.

```
Zeppelin class armored airship L-30 has left Seville for the Atlantic.
Resupply at sea, then rendezvous with the Mediterranean Fleet through the Gibraltar approach.
```

Max read the lines and raised a brow.

In this world the Gibraltar strait and North Africa both lay under the Gaul Republic.

Holy Britannia had long thrown its weight across the continent with one hand, but the Gaul Republic and the German Empire were not friends under that pressure.

After the Franco German war cost Alsace, Lorraine and a Mediterranean outlet, hatred between Berlin and Paris had never cooled.

Most staff officers expected a great war between them sooner or later. Only the hour was in doubt.

*"Old Europe never runs out of trouble."*

On the platform relief columns and the 16th Brigade crossed paths.

Fresh soldiers looked sharp and stared at the battered city and the men who had held it.

Sixteenth Brigade troops looked spent. Soot and powder stains would not wash out of their tunics.

The ranks still dressed straight but many companies had lost near half their files. The gaps showed plain.

Survivors stood taller all the same. Fire had burned the nervous edge off and left something harder behind.

The train pulled out slow and carried the tired brigade toward a rear rest area.

Max sat by the window and watched Seville slide backward.

He had seen the worst fighting of his life in those streets. He had also woken to power there.

*"Sir, hot water."*

Corporal Baumann knew Max liked hot drinks even in the field.

Baumann had found a way to fill a canteen on the car and held it out.

The corporal had served for years but his smile used to stay shy. Now his face carried a new wear.

*"Thank you."*

Max took the canteen and drank.

The car stayed quiet. Most men had slumped asleep in their seats.

Days of combat and days of corpse work had drained them to the bone.

Now they could set the load down and sleep.

The train stopped at a rear zone near the German border.

Expedition engineers had thrown up a new base around a small town. Rows of huts and warehouses rose while trucks and working parties moved everywhere.

The 16th Brigade drew a large camp plot inside the perimeter.

Max heard from brigade and from Lieutenant Colonel Ludwig von Seeckt when the man slipped out for another forbidden drink that the rest of the **8th Infantry Division** would arrive from home soon.

Then the brigade would rejoin its parent formation and fill gaps from replacement depots after the Seville losses.

That meant no return to the front for a stretch. New men needed drill and trust.

The whole brigade treated the news like a gift from heaven.

International Brigade volunteers did not rest long.

After men and kit were made good they rode the rail forward again.

For them the Royal Army had to die and Holy Britannian troops had to leave Spanish soil before the war could end.

Max settled 3rd Company and went to the station in time to see Andrei and his party boarding.

Andrei spotted Max on the platform and looked surprised, then moved.

They had not fought together for many weeks but Max had never treated the International Brigade like a prop.

That honesty was not the usual German officer polish.

Andrei stepped back down from the car and crushed Max in a bear hug.

International Brigade men and German sentries on the platform all stopped and watched.

Many remembered fighting shoulder to shoulder with men who wore other uniforms.

For a moment the war felt like something simpler than maps and ministries.

*"No telling when we meet again, Lieutenant Max. You are the first German officer I actually liked."*

*"If fate allows we will."*

Max smiled and looked up at the chemistry professor leaning from a window with several workers behind him.

*"Same for you. Next round is on me. German beer."*

*"I am a poor drinker but I will hold you to it."*

The professor laughed and the workers nodded along.

White steam rolled from the locomotive and the whistle cried once across the stone.

Andrei waved from the moving step and climbed aboard.

Andrei thought as the train gathered speed.

*"Lieutenant Max, I hope we never face each other across a battlefield."*

---

The day after the brigade settled in camp Max walked the 3rd Company lines with Sergeant Klaus and planned training once replacements arrived.

A courier from expedition headquarters ran up and saluted.

*"Second Lieutenant Maximilian Friedrich?"*

*"Yes."*

Max nodded, puzzled.

*"General Mackensen requests you at once. Follow me to headquarters."*

The courier handed over a written order.

*"General Mackensen?"*

Max took the paper and felt his stomach tighten.

Max had pinned down who this man was in both worlds.

In the old world's books he was the German field marshal who tore through the Balkans, the **Forward General** whose attacks never waited for permission.

The body's old ties to Mackensen and the grandfather story only made the meeting heavier.

*"Why now of all times."*

Max wondered but did not delay.

Max told Klaus to hold the company and rode in the waiting staff car toward expedition command.

German expedition headquarters occupied a requisitioned manor inside Spain.

Wire ringed the walls. Checkpoints stood every few paces. The mood was tight.

The courier led Max through repeated checks of identity disc and order until the gates opened.

Max crossed the courtyard to a separate wing that served as Mackensen's quarters and office.

Two tall men of the general's guard stood at the door in heavy plate. They rested greatswords on the stones and watched every movement.

The courier left Max at the threshold.

Max drew breath, straightened his tunic and knocked.

The door opened on Mackensen's aide.

*"Second Lieutenant Friedrich. Come in. The General has waited some time."*

The aide smiled and stepped aside.

Wind moved the leaves in the inner court. The aide opened the main study door, gestured Max through and stayed outside.

Max walked in.

The room held little beyond a large oak desk, a few chairs and a wall map of Spain.

General August von Mackensen sat at the desk with a file in his hands.

His field gray was pressed clean. White hair and trimmed beard sat exact on a frame that still stood military straight past sixty.

*"Second Lieutenant Maximilian Friedrich reporting as ordered!"*

Max snapped to attention and reported loud.

Mackensen glanced up from the paper and nodded once without a word.

Max sat on the far side of the desk on the front edge of the chair.

Mackensen kept reading.

The room held only the clock tick and the scratch of his pen.

Minutes crawled. Max did not dare shift or speak.

The silence pressed harder than a shouting match.

Sweat gathered on Max's brow.

The body's old fear of Mackensen still lived in muscle memory.

The old general felt like quiet ground that might erupt without warning.

At last Mackensen signed the file, set it aside and looked Max full in the face.

*"Why do you not speak?"*

Mackensen's voice stayed level. Max could not tell if he was pleased or angry.
