# Chapter 19

Arc: Am I a Spell Genius

Date: Early February 1914

---

While Europe shifted and the two empires faced each other across the map, Seville at the center of the storm had a rare quiet spell.

Armored airship patrols over the last days reported the same picture. After a heavy defeat the Royal Army and the Holy Britannian expedition had no stomach for another assault.

The Royal Army 24th Infantry Division, gutted in the street fighting, and the Holy Britannian columns had pulled back more than forty kilometers in one move.

Days passed without gunfire and Seville families who had fled before the battle began to trickle home.

They came back to streets they knew and found wreckage on every side.

Whole blocks stood broken. Fallen walls and blackened frames lay where they had fallen.

German soldiers and allied troops had raised sandbag posts and barricades everywhere so the city that had been calm now looked like a garrison.

Max and the other surviving officers received a new order.

None of them wanted it. Every one of them had to obey it just the same.

They would lead their men and any civilians who volunteered to clear the dead of both sides from the city.

A soldier could die in seconds in combat.

Carrying the body out and burying it could take dozens of times longer, sometimes hundreds.

Staff requisitioned wagons and ox carts across the district.

Stiff cold corpses came up from rubble, from fighting positions and from water filled shell holes.

Loaders stacked them on carts and moved them street by street to mass graves outside the walls.

February in Seville never warmed past twelve degrees but the dead still turned foul as the days went on.

Max and the survivors under him wore layered cloth over their faces and went through the same lift, stack and haul routine each day.

Relief had left their faces. Only numb fatigue remained.

The priest at the central church in Seville gathered townspeople on his own and held several large joint funerals outside the walls for the dead of every uniform.

The priest spoke in a solemn tired voice and prayed for the souls that had passed.

On that ground soaked in blood there were no longer enemies and friends.

There were only the living and the poor dead.

For many who had died in pain and fear, death itself was release.

The living would carry the weight and walk on.

The burials took place in open country beyond the walls.

Fresh trenches ran so long the eye lost the end. Bodies from the city lay in heaps beside the cuts and the sight was hard to bear.

German gray, Holy Britannian red and the mixed dress of Royal Army troops and International Brigade fighters lay side by side and could hurt each other no more.

A priest appointed from Rome stood at the lip of the trench with a heavy Bible and read the service in several tongues by turns.

His voice was worn and old but it steadied the crowd.

*"Dust to dust. Earth to earth."*

*"May God receive their souls whatever nation they served and whatever creed they held."*

*"On this soil they were only lost sheep. May they rest in peace above."*

Max stood at the back of the crowd and watched.

Max did not pray with the others. He only watched volunteers push each body into the pit and saw shovels cover them.

Max felt more than one thing at once and none of it was simple.

War was more naked than any academy map had ever shown him.

Before the crossing and after it, both schools had treated a man as a counter on a board.

Here every dead man had once breathed.

Each one had kin, comrades, wants and small hopes.

Now all of that was gone.

Sergeant Klaus stood beside Max.

Klaus who had been fearless in the alleys wiped his eyes with the back of his hand like a boy.

A friend from his home village had died in the street fight. Men had found the body only that morning.

When the service ended the soldiers went back to the city. Corpses still filled the ruins and the work would run for days more.

Max forced the heavy questions down and put his whole attention on the task.

Max led parties into half collapsed buildings and shifted fallen masonry to reach men buried underneath.

Every German body Max's party recovered passed through his hands for the identity disc and the clerk wrote the name with care.

That was all Max could give the men who had stood in the line with him for a short season and then stopped.

Night came. Men washed down after the disinfectant rinse and returned to the temporary camps.

Fires burned again but the mood was lower than before.

Nobody boasted. Nobody argued over who had killed whom.

They ate in silence and stared at the flames with empty eyes.

Max had little hunger. He drank hot broth and went alone to the room assigned to him.

The burial ground replayed in his head all evening.

Max wondered if he had been too simple about this world.

It was not only a stage where he could win rank and fame.

It was real and it was cruel.

*Men die when they are killed* was not a joke line anymore.

*Survive.*

Max whispered the word to himself. Only survival left room to think about anything else.

Only real strength could guard him or let him change what he could not stomach.

The fog in his chest thinned and something harder took its place.

Max could not stay in grief.

He had to grow strong enough to keep himself alive at minimum.

Deep night settled over the camp. Only patrol boots moved in the quiet streets while the rest slept.

Max opened the Spell tab again.

This time his mind did not wander.

Study was no longer a chore he resented. It was the road to staying alive.

Moonlight lay over the battered city outside the window.

Under that light a soul from another world gathered power in silence.

By day Max cleared wreckage, moved corpses and steadied his platoon.

By night he drilled spell models and memorized glyph chains until his eyes burned.

Any normal man would have broken under the twin load.

Maybe the crossed soul held longer. Maybe the war had woken something in the flesh.

Max held the pace anyway.

Max began to think he might have some gift for the work.

*Shield* had tortured him on the first night worse than any calculus exam.

On the second night he needed only a few hours to grasp the core of the pattern.

When the tab confirmed the spell was recorded Max felt the same drained triumph as finishing a hard exam.

```
Shield recorded on Spell tab.
```

*Mage Armor* went faster.

Spells in the same school echoed each other.

Max used what *Shield* had taught him about abjuration structure and applied it at once.

One more night and *Mage Armor* sat ready beside *Shield* on the tab.

Max stared at the two bright entries and almost laughed aloud.

*"Am I actually a genius at this?"*

Max split his open first ring and second ring preparations between the two defensive spells without hesitation.

Three *Shield*. Three *Mage Armor*.

When Max confirmed the choice the tab began to load each pattern for the morning reset.

A pale blue bar appeared under each icon and the estimate read ready before dawn.

Max exhaled long. A steadiness he had not felt before settled in his chest.

He still had no attack spells worth naming in a firefight.

Two abjuration guards would still matter the next time bayonets and short range shooting closed around him.

The relief brought a new problem with it.

Max could not hide that he cast forever.

In the next crisis he would reach for a spell by reflex.

Then a plain infantry lieutenant who had never been listed among mages would throw up a ward in combat.

That would look wrong to anyone who knew the trade.

Max would have to find someone who understood the rules and ask what to do.

---

Max was still weighing who might know when a light knock came at his door.

*"Max. Max. Are you asleep?"*

Lieutenant Colonel Ludwig von Seeckt kept his voice low outside the panel.

Max got up and opened the door.

Lieutenant Colonel Ludwig wore plain hospital whites and peered in with a guilty look.

*"Colonel? How did you get out? Those ribs are not healed."*

Max stared at him.

Ludwig pressed a finger to his lips.

Ludwig edged inside, shut the door and limped to the table. He scanned the room and looked at Max with open hope.

*"Good brother. Do you have any spirits?"*

Max understood at once. The man was not here for brotherhood. He was here for drink.

Wounded officers in the field hospital were under strict ban on alcohol.

The medical orderlies had likely seized every drop from Ludwig's ration and left him dry for days.

So Ludwig had come to the one officer he knew who might still have a bottle.

German army logistics gave each officer a small weekly spirits ration. Max did not drink in the field so his share should still be intact.

*"Colonel, the doctors said no spirits until the ribs knit."*

Max kept his face stern.

*"Damn the doctors. I know my own body."*

Ludwig slapped the table and winced when the shock hit his chest.

Ludwig drew breath and pasted on a pleading smile. He hooked Max by the shoulder.

*"Max, one sip. One sip only. Get us home alive and I swear I will arrange a meeting with my sister Patricia. I will set it up myself."*

For one drink the Teutonic colonel had offered his own sister.

Max almost laughed at the sight.

*"You make it sound as if your sister would be a gift to me."*

*"Of course she would."*

Ludwig straightened with pride.

*"You would be lucky. The von Seeckt name still carries weight in Dresden. If you married in you would ride a smooth career track."*

Ludwig looked Max up and down and clicked his tongue.

*"Truth though, you surprised me this campaign. The boy I knew chasing partners at balls is not the man standing here."*

Ludwig turned serious.

*"Before the war I would not have wanted Patricia near your sort. Now I have changed my mind."*

*"How kind of you, Colonel."*

Max pulled a bottle of gin from his field pack and a glass from the table.

Max poured Ludwig a short measure and a smaller one for himself.

*"One cup only. If the orderlies catch you I am not covering for you."*

*"Good man."*

Ludwig lit up at the sight of the glass and sipped with care. Satisfaction spread across his face.

*"That is the taste of home shipment. Nothing else matches it."*

Ludwig sighed as if he had drunk something sacred.

Max shook his head and took his own sip.

The burn down his throat cleared the fog in his head.

Both men drank in silence for a while and the room went quiet under the yellow lamp.

Two survivors in the war shared a brief peace.

Ludwig set the glass down and broke the stillness.

*"We owe you for living through that mess."*

*"If you had not built that shaped charge armor piercing round we would all be dead. The Order of the Garter knights never dreamed a round like that could exist."*

*"Captain Hauser and the dead bought that with their lives."*

Max's voice dropped.

*"Yes."* Ludwig exhaled. *"War is war. Their deaths will not be wasted. Brigade staff and I will put every medal and report where it belongs."*

Ludwig drank again and looked at Max as if a thought had just landed.

*"Speaking of the shaped charge round. When you get home file at the Imperial Patent Office at once."*

*"File a patent?"*

Max blinked.

*"Of course."*

Ludwig stared at him as if Max had missed the obvious.

*"You invented it. It is your property. A patent pays you a share on every round they produce. That is real money."*

Max's eyes sharpened.

The work had been an emergency trick to stay alive. He had not thought about profit.

*"So I register, retire and count coin while armored knights still walk the earth? This round will never lack buyers."*

Max grinned at Ludwig.

Ludwig stared back without words and finally said only one thing.

*"You have not changed at all."*

*"Do not celebrate yet."*

Ludwig's face turned odd.

*"This round is not a normal weapon patent. It may change how armored knights fight for good. The office will likely stamp it as a classified imperial patent."*

*"Classified patent? What does that mean?"*

Max asked.

*"The army alone may use it. No sales abroad and no private trade. You cannot license it for cash."*

*"Then no royalty?"*

Max sagged.

*"No royalty stream. The Patent Office pays a single lump settlement instead."*

Ludwig searched his memory.

*"How large that lump is depends on how hard you push."*

*"Push how?"*

*"Sue them."*

Ludwig said the word calmly.

*"Sue? Who?"*

Max wondered if his ears had failed.

*"The Imperial Patent Office and the army."*

Ludwig spoke as if that were ordinary business.

Max went fully blank.

He had never imagined fighting the Imperial Patent Office over money.

*"Colonel, you are joking. Me against the Patent Office and the army?"*

Max could not believe it.

*"Who said you go alone?"*

Ludwig rolled his eyes and took another sip.

*"You hire a lawyer. The office never opens with a fair number. They squeeze the first offer as low as they can. A proper attorney argues your case and pushes for the highest award the patent deserves."*

*"If you never dealt with this before it still happens every day in the empire. Inventors swear their work is priceless. The state tries to pay as little as possible."*

*"Lawyers fight it out in a closed court and a judge sets the final sum from real value and impact. The inventor gets paid and the treasury does not bleed. Fair enough."*

Max dimly recalled that the German Empire in his old world had paid secrecy settlements for the Enigma machine and certain poison gases.

*"Fine. I follow you."*

Max nodded and felt his head spin a little.

*"Relax. I know the game."*

Ludwig clapped his shoulder and took the whole task on himself.

*"Back in Dresden I will find you a sharp lawyer. We will pry real coin from those iron misers."*

*"Then thank you, Colonel."*

Max had no better answer. He did not know why Ludwig had turned so helpful but he raised his glass in a short toast.

Max had allowed one cup. Once the bottle was open the pace often slipped.

Ludwig had poured himself a second without Max noticing.

Max opened his mouth to object and Ludwig cut in with sudden seriousness.

*"You really have changed, Max. I always thought General Mackensen forced you into the academy and the army."*

*"Wait. How do you know that?"*

Ludwig shrugged as if the answer were obvious.

*"Dresden society gossip is not exactly secret. My father knows General Mackensen well enough."*

Max stared at him.

Ludwig ignored the look and went on.

*"Whatever the reason, I see a real soldier in you now. Brave, quick and sharp."*

Max felt something shift.

In talk with Ludwig he saw how wide the world was for a noble born Teutonic knight.

After fighting beside each other they had trust beyond normal rank.

Asking Ludwig about mages looked like the best move open to him.

*"Colonel, I need your advice on something."*

Max set the glass down and tried to sound casual.

*"Oh? Say it."*

Ludwig leaned in.

Max chose his words with care and opened with the old dodge.

*"I have a friend who ran into trouble."*

*"You have a friend?"*

Ludwig cut him off with a grin.

*"A woman?"*

The opening had failed.

*"Ahem. I really do have a friend with a problem."*

Ludwig watched him with eyes that seemed to read through skin.

*"Does your friend look exactly like you, answer to Max and wear second lieutenant bars?"*

*"All right, Colonel. I surrender."*

Max raised both hands.

*"It is me. I think I have become a caster."*

Max admitted it flat.

Ludwig's smile froze.

*"What did you say?"*

*"I became a caster. It started after the last fight."*

*"Either my praise went to your head or one cup broke your brain."*

*"Colonel, I mean it."*

*"Fine. Show me."*

Ludwig set the glass down with interest.

*"Cast one of those tricks. Let me see if you are lying."*

Max stopped hiding.

Max dragged a chair with a split back between them and cast *Mending*.

Light flickered along the crack and the back came whole again.

*"God. Max, you are serious."*

Ludwig shot up and stared at him from boots to collar.

A moment later Ludwig sat back down, poured another drink and swallowed it in one pull as if he needed steel in his nerves.

*"Colonel, I think you are using shock to steal more gin."*

Ludwig held up a hand and cut Max off.

*"Never mind that. Tell me how you turned mage overnight."*

Max told him straight about the two apprentice manuals taken in the fight and the advanced notebook from Senior Instructor Eldridge's body.

*"I read them out of curiosity. After a few passes I felt I could do what the pages described."*

The story sounded thin but it was the only tale that fit.

Max could hardly cite a system menu.

Ludwig listened and clicked his tongue in wonder.

*"Your luck is obscene."*

Ludwig shook his head with envy in his voice.

*"Highland mages are top tier. Their study books are treasure. You picked up three and one is a senior instructor's notebook."*

*"You skimmed them and taught yourself the door."*

Ludwig did not look afraid. He looked like a man who had found new country.

Ludwig paused and added a practical note.

*"Turn the manuals in to brigade tomorrow. They count as major loot. You will probably keep one apprentice copy."*

Max did not flinch.

The system had already consumed all three personal training manuals and burned the knowledge into him. Handing over paper changed nothing.

*"I will submit all three. Colonel, is my situation dangerous?"*

Max asked what mattered most.

*"Dangerous? Why would it be?"*

Ludwig stared at him.

*"You think you are the first adult to wake to magic late? You only ignored the world while you chased parties."*

Ludwig saw Max still puzzled and explained.

*"German mages are fewer and weaker than Holy Britannia or France on paper. This land still throws up talent on its own."*

*"Many adults only learn they can cast when some accident wakes them."*

*"It is rare but there is precedent. Imperial offices register them and guide them. Strong cases enter army or civil service."*

Ludwig's answer lifted the last weight from Max's chest.

*"But."*

Ludwig's look turned complicated.

*"I never heard of a man with no sign of gift who read stolen notebooks and taught himself without a master."*

He rubbed his chin as if pricing a rare piece.

*"Max, your gift in magic may run deeper than you think."*

Ludwig slapped his thigh and lit up.

*"That means you and my sister Patricia were made for each other."*

Max could not follow the jump.

What did his magic have to do with Patricia von Seeckt?

*"Hear me out."*

Ludwig counted on his fingers.

*"You are a combat hero now. When the 16th Brigade rotates home the staff will file every report. One step up is certain."*

*"Only one step?"*

Max frowned.

Ludwig raised a brow.

*"Brother, you have been with the company less than a month. Lieutenant at that speed would break the army record. Normal time is three years minimum and captain is the ceiling for most German officers."*

*"I nearly died for this and I stay a lieutenant?"*

*"Armored knights and infantry are not the same ladder."*

Max swore under his breath.

Ludwig saw the sulk and pressed on.

*"Easy. The brigade lost officers in bulk. Empty posts fill from survivors first. You may hold acting command before the rank catches up. That is only time."*

*"Add the shaped charge round that may change the whole field and a caster with room to grow. What are you worried about?"*

*"Patricia is a magitech prodigy in Dresden, sharp and well favored in our house. If you pair with her the match speaks for itself."*

Ludwig grew more excited as he spoke and seemed to see the wedding already.

*"With our family behind you, maybe General Mackensen nudges the path and your own work carries you. You become a rising name in the German Army."*

Max felt less like talk with a colonel and more like a sales pitch from a matchmaker.

*"Colonel, are we far from the point?"*

Max tried to pull the thread back.

*"We may not live to see Dresden."*

*"Do not say that."*

Ludwig spat the bad luck away.

Ludwig hummed a broken German tune and poured another half cup. The gin bottle sank fast.

*"Enough, Colonel. The orderlies will come for me tomorrow if you keep drinking."*

Max took the bottle and screwed the cap on.

The spirit came up in Flanders and spread through Holy Britannia but northern Germany drank it too under the name gin.

Army ration lists offered gin beside brandy and korn for officers.

Max could not stand the taste.

*"Stingy."*

Ludwig clicked his tongue and drained what was left in his glass.

Ludwig stood and stretched. His ribs pulled and he grimaced.

Ludwig walked to the door and looked back at Max with a wink.

*"Max, you opened my eyes tonight."*

*"You talk like my future is already written, Colonel."*

Max waved the praise off.

*"It will be. Mark my words."*

Ludwig threw the line over his shoulder, cracked the door and peered into the corridor.

The passage was clear. Ludwig limped off into the dark.

Max watched him go and shook his head.

Ludwig's talk had steadied him all the same.

Waking as a caster did not look like danger. It looked like a useful card in a hard game.

Early next morning Max carried the three training manuals whose knowledge the system had already taken and headed for brigade headquarters to turn them in.

Max passed the field hospital tents and had to bite his lip.

Several medical orderlies had Lieutenant Colonel Ludwig boxed in a ring. Every face was stern and every voice was sharp.

German army medical orderlies were often built like line fighters by design or by habit.

Ludwig could not move.

*"Colonel, we are trying to keep you alive."*

*"You drank again last night. We can smell it on you."*

*"One more stunt and we strap you to the cot."*

Ludwig stood like a fresh recruit with his chin down and said nothing.

When Ludwig saw Max trying not to laugh he shot Max a glare and the orderlies turned.

Max walked faster and left before anyone could drag him into the lecture.

Those men were the last NCOs an officer wanted angry.

The 16th Brigade command post had stayed in the municipal hall since the street fighting began.

Max signed in at the door and a staff officer led him to a desk.

Max set the three manuals on the wood. Faint magic still clung to the covers.

The officer had looked bored until the books landed. Then his eyes went wide.

*"These are Highland Mage Corps notebooks?"*

The man knew good loot. He lifted one volume with care and turned a few pages with shaking hands.

*"Three of them? We thought those mages left nothing behind."*

*"Sorry. First capture for me. I did not know the rule at first."*

Max scratched his head and smiled like a harmless boy.

*"Someone told me yesterday and I brought them straight in."*

Word that Max had taken Highland manuals climbed the chain at once.

Soon the whole brigade staff knew he had become a caster in the fight.

Brigadier General Karl Paul asked for a private talk.

When Brigadier General Paul saw Max was not joking his reaction matched Ludwig's. He called Max's road wide open.

The story spread through the 16th Brigade camp in a day and reached National Army units resting next door.

*"The Germans have a caster in the ranks."*

*"A lieutenant. He did well at Seville."*

*"Since when? German mages sit in the rear and the guard."*

*"True. I saw him fly."*

*"He breathes fire too. Lethal."*

Maximilian Friedrich became the name every allied tongue in Seville chased.

Older fights were stitched together and retold in new shapes.

The wildest version had Max dropping grenades from the sky onto three Highland mages at once.

Every day someone found a reason to visit 3rd Company quarters.

Some came from honest curiosity. Volunteers from Germany and the International Brigades rarely stood close to a caster.

Max had refreshed the Intel tab and read the entry again. German casters were fewer and lower in rank than Holy Britannia's pool. Almost no senior mages served in the field.

Others were German officers who had survived and wanted ties to a man with a bright mark on his file.

3rd Company's doorway turned busier than battalion headquarters.

Sergeant Klaus and Corporal Baumann had to act as gate guards and turn visitors away until their throats went dry.

Max did not know whether to laugh or hide.

He felt less like a mage and more like a rare animal in a pen.

While Max weathered the attention the war in Spain finally turned.

For once his worst guesses missed. The front did not worsen.

General August von Mackensen led twelve full German infantry divisions from the border straight at Cuenca and Valencia.

The Royal Army and Holy Britannian columns that had reached the coast suddenly met weight they could not push aside.

The enemy at those two provinces had perhaps five divisions in total and more than half were weak Royal Army formations.

Those numbers could bully the National Army. Against German regulars they were eggs on stone.

Resistance thinned fast and the enemy columns pulled back in mass.

Their commanders knew that to stay was to be ground to dust under Mackensen's march.

The Holy Britannian expedition showed sharp discipline even while scattered. Units broke contact and fell back toward Madrid in good order.

Slower Royal Army blocks took the rear guard losses.

The rail line reopened as the enemy withdrew. The German expedition trapped inland was no longer encircled.

Word reached Seville and the garrison erupted.

Days of dread emptied out and joy rushed in to fill the space.

Soldiers shouted and embraced and threw spike helmets into the air.

No officer stopped them this time.

The officers kept their bearing but flushed faces and bright eyes gave them away.

Max let out a long breath. The stone on his chest for days had finally dropped.

Living still felt good.
