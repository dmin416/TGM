# Chapter 10

**Arc:** Seville Counterattack  
**Date:** Late January 1914

---

Ten minutes earlier.

The 16th Brigade's temporary headquarters was set up in a relatively intact building on the southern edge of Seville. The walls still bore the scars of battle in the form of bullet marks. At this moment in the largest room of the building Brigadier General Paul was vigorously rubbing his temples. His brow was furrowed into a knot.

On the table in front of him lay the compiled casualty reports. The entire brigade had suffered over 37% casualties. Several battalions with particularly heavy losses were nearly shattered as cohesive units.

In addition there were several urgent intelligence reports just delivered by frontline scouts. The chief of staff was reporting this information to him.

*"The Royal Army and the British haven't gone far. They stopped less than 8 kilometers outside the city and are regrouping."*

*"Moreover they've received substantial reinforcements. Initial estimates suggest at least two to three regiments worth of troops."*

This news was already bad enough but worse was yet to come.

*"The critical part is..."*

The staff officer paused as if unsure how to continue.

*"We've confirmed that the enemy's Order of the Garter armored knights have increased from two units to eight."*

*"Eight?!"*

Major General Paul's head snapped up. He felt as if someone had just slapped him hard across the face.

This meant that their side's Teutonic Knights armored knights had lost even the insignificant numerical advantage they had before. In the previous battle Ludwig and his forces had barely managed to destroy two enemy armored knights at the cost of half their own units with a three to one numerical advantage.

Now their armored knights were outnumbered by the enemy. If they clashed again...

Major General Paul didn't dare think further.

*"General should we consider a temporary withdrawal?"*

A young officer suggested quietly.

*"Withdraw?"*

Major General Paul snorted coldly and slammed his palm on the table.

*"Withdraw to where? We're in Seville which we just captured. It's the major city of southern Spain and the Empire's primary objective!"*

*"If we lose it not only will all our previous sacrifices be in vain but the entire southern front will be shaken!"*

He stood up and paced back and forth across the room. His boots made heavy sounds on the floor.

*"We cannot retreat. Not a single step."*

Major General Paul stopped pacing. His eyes became sharp.

*"Send a telegram to the expeditionary force command and the General Staff. Report the situation and tell them that the 16th Brigade will hold Seville until the armored airships arrive!"*

He looked around at his subordinates. His voice was decisive.

*"Order all officers of battalion level and above to immediately report to brigade headquarters for an emergency military conference!"*

The order was swiftly transmitted.

Soon Major Thomas and Lieutenant Colonel Ludwig who had just finished handling the disciplinary issue with Max received the messenger's summons. The two men exchanged glances. Both saw the gravity in the other's eyes. Without a word they immediately set off for brigade headquarters.

At the same time at the 3rd Platoon's temporary encampment Max was holding his second combat review meeting since arriving in this world. Unlike the first meeting when everyone had been reserved the atmosphere this time was noticeably more enthusiastic. Having just survived a bloody battle these soldiers all had pent up thoughts they wanted to share.

*"Platoon leader I think flanking fire is really useful!"*

A corporal was the first to stand up. His expression became somewhat excited when discussing this topic.

*"Especially that heavy machine gun. My God! Set up in that position it was a slaughterhouse! Those Royal Army soldiers fell in rows like wheat!"*

His words immediately resonated with everyone.

*"The rapid redeployment made all the difference! Moving the gun to that flanking position while the enemy was committed turned everything around!"*

*"Yes yes we used to bunch up too close. One shell would take out a whole group. This time we listened to the platoon leader and everyone spread out. It looked messy but casualties were much lower!"*

The soldiers chattered away. Each person shared their own insights more or less.

Max listened with satisfaction. This was exactly the effect he wanted. These soldiers were no longer puppets who only followed orders. They were starting to think actively and summarize lessons learned. This improvement in subjective initiative had far more profound significance than winning a single battle.

However soon someone brought up a question that made everyone fall silent.

*"Platoon leader... how should we deal with the British heavy infantry?"*

A soldier who had survived the hand to hand combat sounded somewhat uncertain.

*"Our bullets hitting them had no effect at all."*

The previously lively atmosphere instantly cooled. Everyone remembered those heavy armored soldiers who had charged through their positions like iron cans. They all felt a chill.

Max looked at Sergeant Klaus.

*"Klaus, how did the army usually handle this kind of situation before?"*

Klaus frowned in thought then shook his head.

*"Platoon leader honestly our regular infantry regiments rarely face these enemies directly. From what I've heard they're usually handled by several German Imperial Guard divisions."*

*"The Imperial Guard divisions also have some soldiers equipped with heavy armor who can engage in equal combat. I've never heard of letting us regular infantry go head to head with them."*

This answer was within Max's expectations. After all the German side couldn't be completely helpless against these heavy infantry. Just like with armored knights there always had to be units that could counter them. It seemed that in this world elite units were used to counter elite units. There was a clear chain of unit counters.

Just as everyone was at a loss a messenger from battalion headquarters came galloping up on horseback and reined in at the 3rd Platoon's assembly area.

*"Is Lieutenant Maximilian here? Major Thomas orders you to immediately report to battalion headquarters for a meeting!"*

Max immediately stood up. Being called to battalion headquarters for a meeting at this time probably meant Major Thomas and the others had learned some news at the brigade meeting that needed to be passed down.

*"Klaus you continue organizing the discussion. Write down all the problems."*

*"Yes, platoon leader!"*

After giving Klaus his instructions Max didn't dare delay. He followed the messenger and walked quickly toward battalion headquarters. He had a feeling that something big was about to happen.

*"I hope it's not bad news..."*

When Max arrived at the 1st Battalion's temporary headquarters he found it already packed with people. All the surviving company and platoon commanders were gathered in this command post hastily set up with a few folding map tables. Each person's face bore a trace of fatigue. Several still had bandages wrapped around them.

Battalion Commander Major Thomas stood in front of the map. His face was quite grim. This expression indicated that the news he'd gotten at brigade headquarters wasn't good.

Seeing Max enter he merely nodded and indicated he should find a place to stand. Once everyone had arrived Major Thomas cleared his throat and got straight to the point.

*"Gentlemen I just received news at the brigade meeting. The enemy has not completely withdrawn but has regrouped and received substantial reinforcements. Brigade headquarters expects that once they finish assembling they will launch a counterattack against us."*

The temporary battalion headquarters erupted in commotion.

*"What? Counterattack?"*

*"Didn't they just get beaten back? They recovered that quickly?"*

*"How many reinforcements?"*

The officers talked among themselves. Anxiety spread rapidly.

*"Quiet!"*

Major Thomas let out a low roar suppressing the commotion. He took a deep breath and continued.

*"Brigade headquarters' orders are clear. Hold Seville! Pin the enemy here until our armored airships arrive to support us."*

*"Hold Seville?"*

The moment this order came out the command post instantly fell silent. Many officers looked at each other not knowing how to hold it.

For these German officers accustomed to fighting in open terrain conducting defensive operations in a city was actually a somewhat unfamiliar proposition.

Or rather for armies of all nations in this era most battles ended outside cities. Offensive street fighting doctrine simply did not exist in any systematic form.

German military training emphasized maneuver warfare in the field and the reduction of fortifications through siege and bombardment. Cities were bypassed, besieged into surrender or bombarded into submission. The idea of deliberately fighting building to building block by block was outside standard practice.

Major Thomas looked at his subordinate officers' reactions. He also felt helpless. Wasn't he the same? In the past whether in the army or military academy what they taught was how to command troops in field combat. No one ever taught how to fight in cities.

At this time due to the German Army's habit of mission type command brigade headquarters had not given any specific tactical instructions to the regiments and battalions. They only said to hold Seville. As for how to hold it? That was up to their own abilities. Either way you had to complete the order.

Of course one person in the battalion headquarters reacted differently from the others. That was Max.

If others felt somewhat confused and at a loss after hearing this order then after hearing it he felt completely terrible.

*This is street fighting!*

Lining up to charge, hand to hand combat and now street fighting right after. So the most brutal forms of warfare and he'd encountered them all in a short time?

In this suffocating silence someone was the first to turn their gaze toward Max standing in the corner. Then a second, a third...

More and more people unconsciously looked at this young second lieutenant who had created miracles on the battlefield. Captain Hauser, several other company commanders and even Battalion Commander Major Thomas all eventually focused their eyes on Max.

Those gazes were full of expectation, reliance and a trace of almost blind trust that they themselves hadn't even noticed.

*"No way..."*

When Max noticed the others' gazes he nearly blacked out.

What was going on? Why was everyone looking at him? Did they think he was some kind of tactical wish granting machine? Shake it and ideas come out?

Max could clearly feel that the 1st Battalion's officers after experiencing the previous series of battles had unconsciously developed a certain path dependence on him. His display of calm and tactical ability surpassing others made him the first person they thought of when encountering difficulties.

*"Lieutenant Maximilian."*

In the end it was still Major Thomas who broke the silence.

*"You just graduated from military academy. What you learned is all the latest military theory. Regarding... regarding this urban combat do you have any thoughts?"*

All eyes stared tightly at Max expecting him to once again say something shocking about tactics.

At this point Max knew he couldn't avoid it. He also understood this wasn't the time to hide his abilities. This concerned the lives of everyone in the 1st Battalion including himself. If he didn't say something German officers would very likely use the most primitive methods to lead the troops into a meat grinder.

He took a deep breath and thought back to what he had experienced, learned and played before. Then he reorganized it in language people of this world could understand.

*"Major Thomas, colleagues... since everyone trusts me I'll share some of my humble opinions."*

Max stepped forward. He took the pencil handed to him by the battalion staff officer and stood in front of the map.

*"What we're about to face is street fighting."*

*"Street fighting?"*

*"That's right. Fighting in the maze like streets and alleys of the city... First we must understand one thing. Traditional combat thinking must be completely abandoned."*

Max's first sentence immediately grabbed everyone's attention.

*"The city is not the field. There are no open battle lines here and no stable rear... Every building, every street could become an independent battlefield."*

Max picked up a pencil and drew a circle on the urban area the 1st Battalion was responsible for on the map.

*"So my first suggestion is to break the whole into parts."*

*"Break the whole into parts?"*

Major Thomas frowned. He could understand these words but didn't understand the specific meaning.

*"Yes. Break the whole into parts."*

Max nodded. He quickly drew several position markers on the map with his pencil.

*"We can no longer rigidly construct a defensive line with companies as units. We must scatter the troops in squad and platoon formations dispersed into every key building."*

He looked at everyone and explained.

*"Our goal is not to hold a line but to control an area."*

*"Every building we occupy is an independent strongpoint. These strongpoints support each other forming a large net with crossfire."*

*"If the enemy charges blindly they'll only suffer heavy losses. To advance they must pull out our nails one by one... This way we can maximize consumption of their forces and delay their attack."*

*"Scatter troops into buildings?"*

Captain Hauser stroked his chin then voiced the doubt others also had.

*"What about command? If the troops are all scattered how do we give orders? What if a position gets surrounded by the enemy and we don't even know?"*

*"This brings me to my second point. Change our method of movement."*

Max drew a line between two adjacent buildings on the map.

*"Don't use the streets if we can avoid it! Streets are fire kill zones for both sides! All our personnel movement and supply transport should be conducted through building interiors."*

*"I've observed. There are many row buildings on Seville's streets all brick and tile construction. Even without explosives we can demolish them at a fairly quick pace! So we punch holes in the walls of adjacent buildings and connect the entire block's interior as much as possible!"*

*"Punch holes in walls?"*

A platoon leader exclaimed.

*"This... would this work?"*

*"Why wouldn't it work?"*

Max countered.

*"We have entrenching tools, pickaxes and the supply train even brought some explosives!"*

The officers looked at each other. The ideas Max was proposing completely overturned their understanding. This wasn't fighting anymore. This was practically demolishing houses.

But thinking carefully they felt Max's words made a lot of sense. The most dangerous thing in cities was the open streets. If they could avoid streets and move freely through building interiors safety and mobility would undoubtedly greatly increase.

However other officers still raised new concerns.

*"Lieutenant Maximilian what if the enemy attacks into a building? Wouldn't they also be able to reach other buildings?"*

Hearing his question Max asked in return.

*"Are our soldiers holding decorations? Would they not fire once the enemy enters a building?"*

*"Wouldn't that mean fighting indoors?"*

*"Is there a problem? The enemy's forces will also be dispersed and bogged down in the buildings..."*

Max paused. He looked very seriously at each officer around him.

*"Gentlemen I hope you can temporarily forget your past experiences. Street fighting is an extremely brutal form of combat. Not a single place in the entire city will be safe!"*

*"If we want to achieve final victory we can only forge it with blood and willpower!"*

The officers looked at each other. The ideas Max was proposing completely overturned their understanding. This was not fighting anymore. This was practically demolishing houses.

For these German officers accustomed to fighting in open terrain conducting defensive operations in a city was actually a somewhat unfamiliar proposition.

Or rather for armies of all nations in this era most battles ended outside cities. Offensive street fighting doctrine simply did not exist in any systematic form.

German military training emphasized maneuver warfare in the field and the reduction of fortifications through siege and bombardment. Cities were bypassed, besieged into surrender or bombarded into submission. The idea of deliberately fighting building to building block by block was outside standard practice.

Major Thomas looked at his subordinate officers' reactions. He also felt helpless. Was he not the same? In the past whether in the army or military academy what they taught was how to command troops in field combat. No one ever taught how to fight in cities.

At this time due to the German Army's habit of mission type command brigade headquarters had not given any specific tactical instructions to the regiments and battalions. They only said to hold Seville. As for how to hold it? That was up to their own abilities. Either way you had to complete the order.

*"Lieutenant Maximilian your proposal is very bold very novel."*

Major Thomas pondered for a moment then spoke slowly.

*"But I must take it to brigade headquarters for General Paul to decide."*

*"Of course Major. This is as it should be."*

Max nodded. He had no objections to this.

Major Thomas then stood up and patted Max on the shoulder.

*"Regardless of whether the general adopts your plan your contribution to the 1st Battalion is already enough. Rest well and prepare for the coming battle."*

After Major Thomas left the other officers also dispersed one after another. Max returned to where his platoon was stationed. His mind was still thinking about a question he had not solved.

Even if his street fighting tactics were adopted there was still one fatal flaw. How to deal with the enemy's armored knights?

Those steel behemoths could ignore most small arms fire. In narrow city streets they were simply mobile fortresses. Once they broke through the defense line the consequences would be unimaginable.

Max thought of the Teutonic Knights. Perhaps he could get some information from them.

He found Sergeant Klaus and instructed him to watch over the platoon then headed alone toward the Teutonic Knights' encampment on the outskirts of the city.

The Teutonic Knights had set up their temporary base in a relatively open area. Several damaged armored knights were parked there. Technicians and squires were busy repairing them. The air was filled with the smell of engine oil and welding sparks.

Max found Ludwig without much difficulty. Ludwig was standing next to his armored knight discussing something with a technician.

Seeing Max approach Ludwig waved away the technician and walked over.

*"Lieutenant Maximilian I was wondering when you would come find me."*

Ludwig's tone carried a hint of knowing amusement.

Max got straight to the point.

*"Lieutenant Colonel I want to ask you a question. If our field artillery fires at the Order of the Garter's armored knights can it penetrate their frontal armor?"*

Ludwig hearing this question first froze then his expression became somewhat complex.

He looked Max up and down as if trying to see something from his face.

*"It seems you have also thought of this problem."*

Ludwig sighed then continued.

*"Actually I just came back from brigade headquarters a few minutes ago. Major General Paul urgently convened another new round of meetings. That set of street fighting tactics you proposed General Paul has already had the military officers of each battalion copy and disseminate it downward! To be honest that stuff really opened my eyes."*

*"Wait, the efficiency is that high?"*

*"When the fire is burning your feet efficiency naturally rises."*

Ludwig nodded then his tone shifted.

*"But just as you thought that set of tactics has one biggest flaw. It has no way to deal with armored knights."*

*"Using field artillery to penetrate frontal armor is basically impossible. The Order of the Garter's Saint George Type 3 has main armor that has undergone high level enchantment with extremely high kinetic resistance."*

*"Our shells are also all high explosive and shrapnel rounds completely unable to cause structural damage."*

*"Moreover an excellent armored knight pilot has reaction speed far exceeding ordinary people. At a distance of several hundred meters he can even make evasive maneuvers the instant he sees the muzzle flash. By the time the shell flies over he has already moved away."*

*"The only weak point is that the rear armor is relatively thin but trying to get behind them in narrow streets is almost impossible."*

Oh well.

This meant that friendly artillery against enemy armored knights was almost useless.

Moreover according to Ludwig's description this world had not yet developed something like armor piercing rounds. Or rather no one had thought of using artillery against armored knights.

Though thinking about it that made sense. If armored knights were all godlike beings who could dodge the moment they saw muzzle flash then artillery really would not be much use.

Of course the objective reason was also that current artillery muzzle velocity was not particularly fast. If later shell velocity gradually increased that would be another story.

Max was not willing to give up. He continued to press.

*"Your Teutonic Knights' weapons like the mace why can they cause damage to them?"*

*"Because our melee weapons have also undergone enchantment specifically designed for armor penetration. This is magic countering magic."*

Magic countering magic.

Max chewed on these words. He became more thoughtful.

So ordinary infantry without magic and without enchanted weapons were destined to be cannon fodder?

Immediately afterward Max who was searching through his brain suddenly had his eyes light up.

*"I have an idea. Suppose there was a weapon that could continuously adhere to armor plating and burn and could follow gaps and seams to burn into the interior of the machine."*

*"You mean firebombs?"*

*"Eh someone has already used them?"*

*"That is correct."*

Max was somewhat surprised. It seemed this world due to the existence of armored knights had also advanced further in offensive and defensive development.

*"The International Brigades people should be very familiar with this thing. Our infantry have also used it briefly. Even the British developed fire scrolls to deal with our armored knights."*

Ludwig paused then continued.

*"But all nations' new armored knights have undergone targeted upgrades. In operational state the cockpit and critical magical power furnace equipment can achieve complete isolation from the outside world."*

*"Moreover they have forced fire suppression and cooling magical units that can rapidly extinguish flames. Now the only ones that can be threatened by firebombs should be old generation armored knights."*

*"Though firebombs are quite good for dealing with infantry especially in narrow terrain like inside cities. I estimate the International Brigades people are probably already collecting materials to make them right now."*

Ludwig rambled on for quite a while. Max listened in a daze. If even firebombs were not effective then infantry really had no way to deal with armored knights.

Max thought for a moment then asked another question.

*"What about pitfalls? Uneven ground? Traps dug into the streets?"*

Ludwig considered this.

*"They can be effective but only in temporarily slowing them down. The units are surprisingly spry and capable. A good pilot can navigate difficult terrain you would not expect. Pitfalls need to be very deep and very well concealed to truly disable one. Otherwise they simply climb out or go around."*

Just as he was feeling at a loss an image suddenly entered his line of sight.

Not far away a technician was holding a huge metal funnel preparing to refill coolant for an armored knight.

Wait. Funnel?!

That conical hollow metal object made Max freeze completely.

A term that in the world before his transmigration had changed the history of armored warfare suddenly jumped out from the depths of his memory.

*"Lieutenant Colonel!"*

Max suddenly grabbed Ludwig's arm. His eyes flashed with a startling light that shocked the latter.

*"Please tell me. The Order of the Garter's main armor if converted to ordinary steel plate would be approximately how thick?!"*

Ludwig was startled by Max's sudden action. He looked at Max's appearance excited to the point of near madness and instinctively answered.

*"Their main armor plating has magic crystal powder added during the smelting process then undergoes enchantment treatment. Although it does not look thick the actual equivalent defense thickness can reach 60 to 70 millimeters of steel armor."*

Hearing this number Max not only was not nervous but instead let out a long breath of relief.

On his face appeared a smile that Ludwig completely could not understand. It was even somewhat eerie.

*"Enough. 60 to 70 millimeters is enough!"*

*"What is enough?"*

Ludwig was completely confused.

*"Max what are you talking about?"*

Max raised his head. He looked at the nobleman lieutenant colonel's face full of confusion and spoke word by word.

*"Lieutenant Colonel I think I may have a way to deal with them."*

*"If you are willing to help me at most two hours and I can show you results."*

*"What did you say?"*

Ludwig frowned suspecting his ears had problems.

*"You have a way? To deal with the Order of the Garter's armored knights?"*

He looked at Max. His eyes were full of doubt. Had this kid gone crazy from fighting?

An infantry second lieutenant had just proposed a set of street fighting theory which was already shocking enough. Now he actually said he had a way to deal with the world's most elite armored knights?

*"Max I admit you are very talented but this is not the time for jokes."*

Ludwig's tone became serious. The expression on his face also became very stern.

*"Do you know what you are saying? That is the Order of the Garter! The pride of the British! A war machine created by countless magical technicians and mages who exhausted their efforts!"*

*"I know."*

Max's expression was exceptionally calm. His confidence was not feigned.

*"Lieutenant Colonel I am not joking. But I need your help. I need materials and I need some professional personnel."*

Ludwig fell silent.

He stared into Max's eyes trying to find the slightest trace of madness or bragging.

But what he saw was only calm and determination.

This young man had commanded troops on the battlefield and forced back a high ring mage. In the conference room he had conceived street fighting theory capable of changing the mode of warfare.

He had broken Ludwig's understanding again and again.

What else was inside his head?

Ludwig's heart was engaged in fierce struggle. From a rational standpoint Max's words sounded like fantasy.

But emotionally everything that had happened before made him unable to resist wanting to believe.

The current situation was already as bad as it could get. The enemy's counterattack was imminent and they were helpless against the enemy's ace units.

Perhaps try his method? A last desperate attempt?

Moreover even if this gamble failed the situation would not be worse than now.

*"Fine!"*

Ludwig gritted his teeth and made his decision.

*"I am betting on you. Tell me what you need. As long as I can get it I will give it to you!"*

*"Excellent!"*

Max's spirits lifted.

*"First I need shells. At least ten 105 millimeter high explosive rounds. They must be intact! In addition I need large amounts of copper. The more the better. Spent shell casings are the best source!"*

*"No problem."*

Ludwig immediately agreed.

*"The artillery has already moved over. They are in the south of the city. I will personally lead people there. In the name of the Teutonic Knights borrowing some things should not be difficult."*

*"I also need several experienced artillerymen. Preferably veterans who understand ammunition structure."*

*"This is also easy. I will borrow them from the artillery battalion along with the shells."*

Ludwig was very decisive in his actions.

*"Finally and most critically I need professional technical personnel."*

*"Technical personnel?"*

*"I need someone who understands chemistry. I also need several highly skilled craftsmen. Preferably those who know casting."*

*"This..."*

Ludwig was stumped.

*"Our knight order only has magical technicians, knight squires and pilots. We do not have this kind of technical personnel."*

Max smiled.

*"Leave this to me."*

Less than ten minutes later Ludwig demonstrated his energy as an Imperial nobleman and Teutonic Knight.

He personally drove a knight order military truck with several squires and charged directly into the artillery camp.

Facing the artillery officers' doubts he simply revealed his Teutonic Knights identity.

Then using a tone that brooked no argument he announced this was for a top secret weapons test related to the outcome of the war under General Paul's secret orders.

The artillery officers were completely bluffed. Although their hearts were full of suspicion none dared truly stop a Teutonic Knights lieutenant colonel.

In the end Ludwig returned with a full load.

The truck was loaded not only with more than ten brand new 105 millimeter high explosive rounds and two large boxes of bright yellow spent casings but he had also borrowed three completely bewildered artillery veterans.

On the other side Max went to find the International Brigades' temporary encampment.

He easily found that bearded commander Andrei. The commander of the Eastern Europa Brigade 2nd Regiment 1st Battalion who had previously fought alongside them in close combat.

The latter just as Ludwig had guessed earlier was leading quite a few people in making firebombs.

*"Andrei my friend are you making firebombs?"*

Max walked over and asked.

*"That is correct Max! Only we have collected too little soap and rubber tires. This means we do not have enough thickening agent. The firebombs' effectiveness will be greatly reduced."*

Andrei put down the work in his hands and sighed. Then he looked at Max with curiosity.

*"Second Lieutenant Max you are coming to find me at this time. Do you have something?"*

*"Well I indeed have something that requires your help!"*

Max did not hide anything and spoke directly.

*"Speak Max. As long as it is something I can do."*

Andrei was a forthright Slav. In all respects very much a bear.

After the previous fighting shoulder to shoulder and personally witnessing Max handle the military discipline problem he already had considerable goodwill toward this young second lieutenant who was not quite like traditional German soldiers.

*"I need several people."*

Max stated his requirements straightforwardly.

*"In your ranks are there any who used to be chemistry teachers? Or blacksmiths and foundry workers are also fine. The more skilled the better."*

*"Chemistry teachers? Craftsmen?"*

Andrei scratched his thick beard. His expression was somewhat strange.

*"My friend this is a volunteer army not a university or factory. But now that you mention it I actually do have several candidates."*

He turned and called several names. Before long several people were brought before Max.

At the front was a middle aged man wearing glasses who looked refined and scholarly. His expression was somewhat weary and melancholy.

The bearded man introduced him as a chemistry professor from a university in Western Russia. He had been wanted for publishing antiwar statements and fled all the way here.

Behind him were several burly men with rough hands full of calluses. They were said to be experienced workers familiar with smelting and casting.

Looking at this temporarily assembled professional team before him Max took a deep breath.

Success or failure rested on this attempt.

Andrei looked at this strange team. His curiosity also rose.

*"So Second Lieutenant Max what exactly are you planning to do?"*

*"I intend to create a way to crack open those British white armored knights."*

Max led this group of people back to the Teutonic Knights' encampment. Ludwig had already had people arrange everything borrowed from the artillery battalion.

He found a table then quickly drew an object with a strange structure on paper. Then he looked at everyone.

*"Gentlemen I need your help to use these things..."*

Max pointed at the shells and casings on the ground.

*"...to manufacture what is shown in the diagram."*

The professor adjusted his glasses and picked up the blueprint to examine it carefully. His brow furrowed tighter and tighter. The workers surrounded those shells. Their eyes were full of curiosity.

Ludwig had been standing to the side like an outsider watching all this.

He watched Max command everything methodically. He watched him gather these completely unrelated people together. German artillerymen, International Brigades professors and workers. The strange feeling in his heart grew stronger and stronger.

How could this famous Dresden playboy have so many odd things in his head?

Perhaps something did change when he was captured.

He watched Max spread out those shells, casings and strange blueprints on the table. Suddenly he felt that at this moment Max looked less like a German officer and more like an alchemist.

*"Alright gentlemen!"*

Max clapped his hands attracting everyone's attention.

*"Time is tight. Let us begin!"*

What followed in this temporary workshop was an unprecedented alchemy session.

Max became the chief director. The chemistry professor from Western Russia became the technical consultant. Those workers from other places became the core executors.

Initially there was still some distance between these people from different countries and different classes.

German soldiers looked down on the International Brigades workers' slovenliness. The workers also maintained vigilance toward these Imperial soldiers.

But as the work progressed under the common goal and Max's effective organization these barriers gradually melted away.

They began to cooperate with each other. An expression or gesture was enough to understand the other's meaning.

Ludwig stood to the side silently watching all this.

He saw that around this young second lieutenant there seemed to be a peculiar cohesive force.

He could make these people with completely different identities, backgrounds and ideologies tightly unite together for a common goal.

This shocked Ludwig even more than the can opener Max was about to create.

The first step was also the most dangerous step. Disassembling intact 105 shells.

The leFH 98/09 type 105 millimeter howitzer shells were separate loading. What Max needed was the TNT bursting charge inside the projectile. This work was handed to the veterans sent by the artillery battalion.

They carefully used tools to unscrew the Dopp Z 98 fuze of the 105 millimeter high explosive round then rotated counterclockwise circle by circle to remove it.

Fine beads of sweat appeared on each person's forehead. After all normally they basically never disassembled shells. Even if they needed to disassemble it was in a professional and safe environment in the rear.

Not like now just finding a shed in the battle zone.

*"Slower. Even slower."*

One veteran murmured to himself as if gently caressing his wife's face.

Ludwig stood in the distance watching this scene. His heart suddenly felt somewhat regretful about staying beside Max to watch the excitement.

He felt he would rather pilot an armored knight to charge. To face ten Order of the Garter armored knights. Rather than stay in this place that could go up to heaven at any moment.

Max appeared much calmer. He watched the artillerymen operate with interest while learning how they separated warhead and shell body.

When the first shell was successfully disassembled and the TNT block was completely extracted everyone breathed a long sigh of relief.

While the artillerymen were disassembling shells on the other side the chemistry professor and workers heated up a huge crucible from the knight order.

Their task was to melt those brass shell casings then cast hollow metal cones according to the pattern on Max's blueprint.

*"Bellows! Increase the air flow!"*

A worker master shouted. His dark skin was reflected red by the furnace fire.

Blazing flames rose up. After careful cleaning the casings had been cut. Now in the high temperature they slowly melted becoming golden liquid.

At the same time another worker was using clay to make conical molds.

His hands flew over the clay. Soon precise conical molds took shape one after another.

The chemistry professor watched the workers' operations while speaking to Max.

*"Second Lieutenant according to my knowledge most countries' shell casings currently use copper zinc alloy. Zinc's boiling point is lower than copper's melting point. Directly melting copper like this will lose that zinc during the process."*

*"Is that not perfect?"*

*"Eh?"*

Max looked at the chemistry professor's somewhat astonished expression and smiled.

*"I actually want pure copper hollow cones. If there are other impurities inside it will instead have an effect."*

*"Is that so? Then I understand."*

The chemistry professor nodded. After confirming these details he calculated explosive quantities and the cone's optimal angle according to Max's description.

At first he was actually full of doubt about Max's theory.

*"Hollow? Using an empty cavity to focus explosion energy?"*

The professor held his glasses shaking his head repeatedly.

*"Second Lieutenant this completely violates traditional explosion theory. Energy should be dispersed. How could it possibly be focused?"*

Max patiently explained from the side.

*"Professor you can think of it like light. We use a concave mirror and can focus sunlight to a point. Explosion energy shock waves expand outward from the surface. To a certain extent it is the same principle."*

Max clapped his hands sharply. The sound cracked through the workshop.

*"My hands moved slower than sound but the compression between them dramatically increased the speed of the pressurized air. That is why it can hurt your ears. An explosion does the same thing but far more powerfully. The cone focuses that compression into a single direction."*

Based on previous indirect inquiries Max had already discovered this world seemed not yet to have discovered the Munroe effect. So now he could only use the most common analogy to describe it.

The professor was half believing but he still picked up paper and pen and began frantically calculating.

He was attracted by this completely new theory. Academic curiosity overcame inherent understanding. For this instant he seemed to return to the university classroom.

This temporary handicraft workshop beside the Teutonic Knights encampment after everyone understood this thing was related to their lives quickly began operating efficiently.

The artillerymen were responsible for disassembly. The workers were responsible for smelting and casting. The professor was responsible for calculations and theoretical support. Max was the brain connecting all links.
