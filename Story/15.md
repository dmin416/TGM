# Chapter 15

Arc: Seville Counterattack

Date: 7 February 1914

---

Max used the system map to thread his party through streets and buildings and gather scattered friendlies as he went.

Some were German soldiers from other companies. Their platoons had broken apart and the chain of command was gone. They wandered the ruins without direction.

A few were International Brigade fighters and National Army men who had somehow found each other and held on behind a broken wall.

When Max met these survivors many were too tense to recognize friendlies.

He had checked the placards first and called out in German and Spanish in turn or they might have fired on each other by mistake.

Max reached the sector where 3rd Platoon held the line with Klaus, Baumann and the patched force he had built on the way.

Rifles rose from a building at the edge of the zone.

*"Password!"*

Max paused, not recalling setting a password.

*"Weißwurst,"* Sergeant Klaus said, his patience worn thin.

*"You must not have heard the church bells at noon. Welcome back!"*

The men in the building lowered their weapons and breathed out.

Before anyone could speak further Klaus waved the squad leader down from the post.

*"Come here and look again."*

The sentry ran out and only then saw the black smeared figure beside the sergeant was his new platoon leader.

The sentry couldn't be blamed. Max had lost his pickelhaube somewhere in the lanes. His hair was caked with plaster dust and smoke had scorched his face, making him hard to recognize.

The messenger snapped straight and saluted.

*"Sorry, sir. I did not recognize you at first."*

*"No salutes in the combat zone. Put your hand down."*

Max pushed the raised arm down and walked on while he spoke to Klaus.

*"Good job maintaining discipline. Just make sure to tell me the password next time."*

*"Yes, sir. That was my mistake."*

*"What happened that you needed to set a password?"*

Max had not expected challenge words in an army of this era. Camps in the field used them after dark; daylight in the lanes had not felt like a place that needed them until now.

*"Other units reported Royal Army men in German dead men's coats trying to pass through our line."*

*"To raid us from inside?"*

*"No."*

Klaus shook his head. His face tightened.

*"Prisoners said officers shoot men who run rearward. Some tried to cross our zone in captured kit and break out toward the south of the city."*

Max sighed but nodded. *"The password was a good idea."* *"Add another one in case you are captured or coerced, to let the others know,"* he added in a low voice.

They talked as they moved and soon brought the party into a sector that was still under control.

Max could finally draw a full breath. He had survived the lanes and knew part of that was luck.

Single shot rifles and the pace of this fight had kept him alive. In a war built on automatic weapons and denser fire he might already be dead.

Max looked at the men around him, black with powder and blood, their faces flat with exhaustion and numb shock. He felt the weight of the day.

Urban fighting had earned its name as a meat grinder.

Before the battle 3rd Platoon with the attached heavy machine gun section had counted more than a hundred riflemen.

Now, even with the stragglers he had collected, fewer than seventy men still drew breath under his hand.

That was with the advantage of being the defenders and the urban combat doctrine they had tried to follow.

Max could not guess how many Royal Army and British bodies the attackers had left in the streets.

He opened the system map and looked over the blue placards that marked friendlies.

The strength figures on each block were grim.

At the start the German expedition, the International Brigades and the National Army had fielded close to twenty thousand men together.

After the earlier assault and the lane fighting the blue placards summed to less than twelve thousand.

Worse still, the survivors sat in several isolated pockets across the city with no clean path to help one another.

Max's sector was the largest and the most important pocket left. The 16th Infantry Brigade headquarters still lay in the city hall in this zone.

Perhaps the brigade staff had moved after warning or Eldridge had smashed the church tower without finding city hall at once.

For now the brigade command post was still alive.

Max shifted his gaze to the red placards on the rim of the map. They crowded most of the city.

He counted at least six battalion sized enemy blocks aimed at this pocket.

The defenders could scrape together only three shattered battalions to meet them.

1st Battalion, 3rd Company was in the worst shape of the lot. The battalion had broken apart and the battalion staff with the other companies seemed trapped in another pocket.

Only 1st Company's elements still fought here. Besides Max's 3rd Platoon the 1st and 2nd Platoons were nearly gone. Together they had fewer than seventy men left.

Max rubbed his temples and forced himself upright. This was not the hour to sigh.

He walked the city hall sector until he found his company commander Captain Hauser.

The veteran captain was sweating over the field kitchen and the supply troops as they hauled crates of ammunition and supplies to the line.

Those men carried rifles as well. They were not combat troops by trade but they were drilled enough to fight when the line needed every rifle.

Captain Hauser's face lit when he saw Max return with his party.

He crossed the space in two strides and crushed Max in a bear hug that drove the air from his lungs.

*"Good lad. I knew you would come back."*

He clapped Max's back hard. Relief and grief mixed in his voice.

*"The 1st and 2nd Platoon leaders are dead. Company headquarters took heavy loss as well."*

Max had expected it and still had no words.

He had barely known those lieutenants. A few hours ago they had sat in the same council and planned the defense.

One of them had backed Max's plan at San Isidro when the company first attacked.

*"Captain Hauser."*

*"Do not try to comfort me, boy. Men die in war."*

Captain Hauser stepped back and wore his hard face again. Max could see the grief pushed down under it.

*"Third Company has you and me now. You think fast. You will help me run what is left. Understood?"*

*"Yes, sir."*

Max came to attention and saluted.

Captain Hauser waved the formality away and took him to the cluster of officers holding this sector.

After a short round of names the group learned the brigade's urban combat doctrine had come from this newly graduated second lieutenant.

*"Young minds turn faster. Some of our old habits are already stale."*

*"Lieutenant Maximilian, give us your read. How do we hold this last line?"*

The German officers in the ring set courtesy aside and listened with plain attention.

The rank badges still showed which regiment each man drew pay from but the heat in the room had broken the old habit of talking past a junior man who had not bled long enough to earn a voice.

Max spoke with the captains and determined the buildings with the best cover and split them between the companies.

Captain Hauser wrote those decisions on the map in short words.

The ring of captains decided that the same tactics that had gotten them through the first wave would be the best to use against the second.

The officers would keep overlapping fields from the windows on both sides of each lane and use crossfire against the enemy troops. 

Captain Hauser marked strongpoints while Max turned ranges and arcs into orders the platoon sergeants could hold without grouping too close together and becoming easy targets.

The work fell to several German companies including Max's 3rd Company.

Third Company looked thin on paper but it was still more intact than most of the units around it.

Max and Captain Hauser settled the plan.

Max led a National Army detachment that had lost more men than Third Company back to defend the rear.

He brought 3rd Platoon and the stragglers forward again and moved with Captain Hauser along the building line toward the front.

Near the line he looked through a window into a side lane and saw a familiar shape.

A Siegfried Type 1 armored knight in black and white livery with the Teutonic cross crouched in the shadow. The machine almost blocked the alley.

The tactical number on the shoulder plate identified that armored knight as Colonel Ludwig von Seeckt's mount.

The Teutonic Knights had tried another strike on an enemy armored knight in the earlier fighting and lost a second Siegfried Type 1 when the rush failed.

Only three armored knights remained in the city and all three were in this core pocket to guard the brigade headquarters.

That also meant any enemy who saw three armored knights in one place would guess where the brigade staff hid and hit the sector harder.

Max was weighing whether to climb up and trade word with Ludwig when movement stirred at the far end of the street.

British and Royal Army soldiers came on in a dense mass. The curtain on the decisive fight rose.

The ambush teams and the main line saw the enemy at the same moment.

*"Enemy in sight! Open fire!"*

A 105 millimeter howitzer crewman who held temporary command of the guns at the square shouted the order.

Two 77 millimeter field guns and the last 105 millimeter howitzer at the square in front of city hall roared.

At a few hundred meters the crews had dropped their tubes nearly level and fired into the thickest block at the street mouth.

Three high explosive shells screamed across the gap and struck the British charge.

**Boom! Boom! Boom!**

Three bursts threw smoke and bodies into the air.

Heat and splinters cleared a wide slice of the column. Blood and burned powder hung in the air.

The first British rush stalled.

*"Good shooting,"* Max said under his breath from the second floor window.

A few more salvos like that and no line officer would be able to drive riflemen down a straight street into direct shellfire, no matter how many men he shot for retreating.

Not every regiment could march through that kind of fire and keep pushing.

The gun crews opened the breeches to reload and the field changed.

Three white steel giants burst out of the smoke and dust.

Order of the Garter armored knights.

Three St. George Type 3 machines shed their pride in fighting alone and closed in a tight triangle.

They raised shields stamped with the Garter badge and shielded the infantry behind them. The wedge drove toward the square in front of city hall.

For once the knight pilots walked in front of the riflemen they had treated as disposable.

*"Armored knights!"*

*"God save us, the knights are covering us!"*

British soldiers behind the machines steadied when the tall backs blocked the shell bursts.

Those pilots and the Highland mages had never done this before. Ordinary infantry was coin to spend.

Now the riflemen found their voices again. They screamed *"Long live the Order of the Garter!"* and pressed close behind the steel.

A simple form of infantry and armor cooperation for this age took shape in the street at once.

*"Faster! Keep firing! Don't let them close in!"*

A German officer on the line screamed the order. He knew that if the armored knights and the British infantry reached the square the defense was finished.

The three gun crews loaded as fast as they could and rammed fresh shells home.

**Boom! Boom! Boom!**

All three tubes fired again.

This salvo did not bite as hard as the first.

The three armored knights held like a closed barrier. Shields and the blue spell shields on the hull doubled the defense.

High explosive shells burst on the shields and threw rings of light and fire across the metal. None punched through.

The machines kept coming faster under the fire. Heavy tread on stone beat through the blood slick street.

Then, when the guns were needed most to hold the infantry, something worse broke.

The precious 105 millimeter howitzer may have failed from heat or from a bad shell.

After the third shot the breech gave a dull roar and the tube tore apart.

**Boom rumble!**

The burst swallowed the whole crew.

Several gunners died before they could cry out.

Hot metal and blast wave knocked over the two 77 millimeter field guns beside it.

One carriage flipped. The other gun looked intact but the crew lay scattered.

The last long range fire in front of the city hall died in seconds.

German soldiers who saw it went grey in the face.

Without shellfire the three St. George Type 3 machines surged forward. Nothing on the street could slow them now.

White feet hammered the paving stones.

British infantry behind them roared and the whole front seemed ready to collapse before their charge.

Ludwig had set the ambush for this moment.

As the three St. George Type 3 machines passed the mouth of the lane where the Teutonic Knights waited Ludwig dropped one arm in a sharp signal.

*"Now! Move!"*

Two Siegfried Type 1 machines that had waited in the shadow with close range shaped charge armor piercing rounds in hand burst from the alley left and right.

They leveled the charges like lances at the side armor of the two outer St. George Type 3 machines.

*"No!"*

The Garter pilots already knew the Germans carried a round that could kill an armored knight in one hit.

Enemy machines rising beside them with those weapons in hand rattled them at once.

*"Watch the flank!"*

*"Damn it!"*

They tried to turn and raise shields. The Teutonic pilots were not about to waste this opportunity.

Fire flared at the entrance to the alley.

Luck did not favor the Order of the Garter a second time.

Both shaped charge rounds detonated properly.

Two hypersonic jets punched through the side armor on the two outer machines that had shrugged off everything else.

Molten copper and steel from each breach ripped through two crew compartments. Overpressure in each hull burst lungs and ruptured organs before secondary fires spread.

**Boom!**

**Boom!**

The two St. George Type 3 machines folded as if their strings had been cut and dropped to their knees.

The street went still for a heartbeat.

Max and Captain Hauser were moving through the buildings on the right and saw the collapse through a window at arm's length.

Flame and blast rattled the glass in the frame.

Even with everything Max had seen before his pulse spiked at armored knights dying that close.

He recovered first. The lane was open.

*"Now! With me!"*

He took part of his party through a hole already broken in the wall and hit the British infantry behind the knights from the flank.

*"Free fire! Grenades! Firebombs! Throw whatever you have!"*

Bullets and blast ripped into the British cluster from the side and broke their step.

German rifles in the buildings across the street answered at once and the lane filled with crackling fire.

Fire from above and from both walls left the British in the open with no clean shelter.

Officers who still stood drove their men toward the buildings on either side.

Most ground floors were sealed. The attackers could only hammer the doors where German rifles waited.

The thresholds became killing ground.

British soldiers fell in the rush. Grenades and firebombs burst at the doors. Slugs crossed the gap and cut down men who advanced in fear.

The Germans knew that if the British broke inside they would die in the rooms.

The British knew that if they stayed in the street the German line would shoot them down from the front.

Neither side had room to pull back. Both fed lives into the same narrow openings.

Farther back in the British column a Highland mage wrapped in soldiers took one step rearward without thinking.

He had followed the senior instructor into the city to support the assault and regretted it almost at once.

He had never imagined combat could look like this.

At first his spells had punched holes through several German blocks and carried the British deep into the city.

Then the work turned into a nightmare.

The enemy was not only ahead. Rifles and grenades came from every side and from streets already marked clear.

The spell slots he had thought ample vanished in fights within minutes.

He wished he had ten times the prepared magic he had brought.

By the time even his third ring fireballs were spent in room clearing he felt helpless for the first time in the field.

That helplessness turned to fear when he joined up with another column and saw a fellow mage who had also burned his prepared spells cut in half by a heavy machine gun without *Mage Armor* left to take the burst.

From that moment he no longer walked in front of the line. He hid behind the infantry.

Only a few cantrips and one more *Mage Armor* remained.

He regretted using up *Fly* on the way in to keep pace with Eldridge.

In the center of the street the loss of two St. George Type 3 machines left three German armored knights against one.

Victory for the Teutonic Knights seemed within reach.

*"All together! Kill the last one!"*

Ludwig broadcast the order through his external speaker. Excitement shook his voice.

He drove his machine forward with Wilhelm and the third Siegfried Type 1 and closed on the lone white knight from three sides.

The Garter pilot's mind went blank.

He had watched two companions die in seconds to a weapon he did not understand.

The shock bent his picture of the world.

He could not grasp how German scrap metal could carry such killing power.

The Order of the Garter had remained strong in the field for a hundred years while the Teutonic Order had dissolved before the Germans revived it to pilot the Siegfried Type 1 armored knights.

While he froze, three black and white machines surrounded him.

Honor and panic forced a last shout from the cockpit.

*"For the glory of the Order of the Garter!"*

He raised his shield and swung his armed sword in a final stand.

Against three Siegfried Type 1 machines the answer was thin.

Ludwig's mace, Wilhelm's war axe and the third knight's axe struck from three angles.

**Clang! Clang! Clang!**

Steel beat on shield and plate until sparks jumped.

The St. George Type 3 hull held for a time then dented and cracked under the rain of blows.

Wilhelm's axe took off one arm. The machine slowed.

In a building on the flank Max and Captain Hauser's men were already in close fight.

The last heavy soldiers of the Northumberland Fusiliers had died earlier in the city but a final wave broke into a room.

German soldiers from the second floor threw them down in a desperate melee and drove bayonets through helmet and plate gaps.

The brief lull let more British riflemen pour through the door.

They shot the Germans on the first floor and tried to climb the narrow stair.

*"Firebombs! Firebombs! Use them now!"*

Max crouched behind the stairwell, fed a stripper clip into his rifle and shouted the order.

The men in the building lit two firebombs without pause and threw them into the packed doorway.

Flame rolled over the British cluster in the tight space. Men screamed and rolled on the boards.

Curtain and table cloth caught. Wooden furniture fed the burn until the whole first floor smoked.

*"Give me one more bottle. Everyone else through the wall to the next building."*

A soldier passed Max a firebomb. The rest fired down the stair under Captain Hauser's lead and slipped through the breach into the next house.

Max struck a match, lit the wick and smashed the bottle into the stairwell.

**Boom!**

Flame drove the British back from the steps. Max crossed into the next building behind his men.

He glanced at the countdown on the system interface. Less than half an hour remained before the armored airship arrived. He still did not know what ordinance it would bring.

The system had marked the arrival in earnest. The support would have to count for something.

*"If we can hold on for thirty more minutes it will get here. How do you lose with an airship overhead?"*

Then every man in the fight felt a cold prickle of magic from above.

Max looked up from below the window frame and his pupils tightened.

A robed figure hung in the air above the street and looked down with ice in his gaze.

Highland Mage Corps senior instructor Eldridge had returned.

Eldridge's face was black with rage and his eyes carried open killing intent.

The heavy losses to the Holy Britannian expedition and the Order of the Garter had pushed the senior instructor past patience.

The last straw was the two apprentice bodies he had just found in the street.

Infantry had driven bayonets into both and left them in the rubble.

He had lost all three apprentices he had raised in a single day.

Even in the Holy Britannian Empire fewer children were born with mage talent each year and fewer still were worth training.

Every apprentice taken into the Highland Mage Corps was chosen from the best of that thin pool.

The corps might admit only a handful in several years. Eldridge had earned three through years of colonial service and two major spell refinements.

With corps resources and his own teaching they should have risen to archmage rank if nothing went wrong.

Each high mage under his name would have raised his voice inside the corps.

Now the lineage was cut and the bodies were cold.

Max felt grim the moment he saw the senior instructor.

He had killed two apprentices in the lanes when they faltered as infantry closed. This man was the real high threat mark on the system, the kind of caster who could change the tide alone.

Every fighter on the street noticed the new shape in the air.

The shooting faltered for a breath.

British soldiers screamed Eldridge's name and their line stiffened.

German soldiers went pale and fear showed plain on their faces.

*"One mage and we already drove him off once! Keep firing!"*

Captain Hauser roared to hold the line.

Max raised his rifle, aimed and fired. The 3rd Platoon men around him aimed at the figure in the air and shot as well.

Eldridge ignored the infantry on the ground. His gaze locked on the three Siegfried Type 1 machines still pressing the last St. George Type 3.

Highland mages were trained to throw area spells from behind walls. Neither Eldridge nor his peers had much practice against armored knights at close range.

That did not stop him from marking the German machines as the first targets to erase.

The rifles in the nearby buildings annoyed him first.

He did not land. He hung over the street and lifted his left hand with its jeweled rings.

He pointed at the building where Max had been firing. The ruby on his index finger flashed and a spell left his hand without a chant.

A fireball larger than a man's head burned dark red before his finger and screamed toward the wall.

*"Move!"*

Max had time for only that warning.

**Boom rumble!**

The ring cast fireball struck the outer wall harder than any apprentice had thrown.

The blast tore a wide hole through brick already pocked by bullets and dropped half the roof.

Stone and tile rained down and Max and Captain Hauser stumbled into the next house and left the post they had held.

That was only the opening move.

With the riflemen driven back Eldridge turned toward the armored fight in the street.

He lifted his hand again and drew a spell scroll from his robes.

A thread of power opened the seal and he tore the precious scroll crafted by a legendary archmage of the Highland corps.

A huge blade of pale purple force hung in the air before him.

*Mordenkainen's Sword.* A seventh ring evocation famous among mages since the old legends.

*"Go."*

Eldridge thrust his hand forward.

The force blade drove at the nearest Siegfried Type 1 with speed that outran a shell.

The pilot had expected the attack and tried to sidestep.

He still misjudged how sharply the spell could turn.

*Shells I can dodge. This is.*

The thought died unfinished.

The purple blade curved in the air, locked onto the machine and accelerated into a straight thrust.

**Zzzt!**

Steel screamed.

One plain thrust punched through the Siegfried Type 1 and left a clean hole through the cockpit.

The pilot never understood what killed him. The machine stopped and fell.

*Damn it.*

Max had his rifle up when he saw it and the curse tore out of him.

High ring combat power ran higher than he had guessed. One spell had disabled an armored knight in a heartbeat.

Max knew the pattern too well.

The figure in the air over the lane had burned a building and driven a killing blow onto a knight.

The sequence read less like a mage duel and more like an attack aircraft with rockets.

Eldridge let out a breath he had been holding. Attacking this close was unfamiliar to him but the first strike had landed.

A cold smile crossed his face. He shifted his gesture and sent the circling force blade at Ludwig's mount.

The blade wheeled once and dropped at a sharp angle.

*"Colonel, watch out!"*

Wilhelm's shout came through the external speaker.

Ludwig saw that evasion would not clear the arc and raised his shield.

The force edge cut deeper than he expected.

**Clang!**

The heavy plate meant to stop shells split down the middle and fell to the street.

The blow still turned the blade. Residual force chopped into both legs of Ludwig's Siegfried Type 1.

**Clang!**

Metal tore. Both mechanical legs sheared off at once.

The hull lost balance and crashed backward beside the ruin Max had just left. Dust rolled up in a cloud.

*"Colonel Ludwig!"*

Max and Captain Hauser cried out together.

The battle turned in a instant.

Three German machines against one British knight had become one crippled hull and one still standing.

One Siegfried Type 1 was dead. Ludwig's cockpit lay on its side and would not walk again.

Only Wilhelm's Siegfried Type 1 could still fight on its feet.

*"Bastard!"*

Wilhelm's eyes burned red in the cockpit.

Grief and rage burned away his fear of the mage above.

Eldridge was about to send the force blade for a finishing cut on Ludwig when Wilhelm made a move no one expected.

He left the crippled St. George Type 3 and drove his machine straight at Eldridge in the air.

*"Die, caster!"*

Eldridge had not thought a German tin can would charge a high mage.

Surprise flickered across his face.

Two companions had died in seconds and this fool still came.

He snorted and turned the force blade toward Wilhelm while he pulled *Fly* to climb.

Wilhelm was faster than he had guessed.

The heavy frame surged forward at a sprint.

The force blade had barely begun its turn when Wilhelm's Siegfried Type 1 reached the hover point.

Eldridge had spent his career behind shields and artillery. He misread how tight the fight would become.

If he had climbed earlier or opened distance instead of standing to cast the end might have been different.

He was still hanging below ten meters while he split his mind on the blade.

A standing Siegfried Type 1 rose four meters toward five.

Wilhelm released the limiters, built speed and jumped until the hull reached nearly its own height in the air.

The fist came down on Eldridge before he could slip aside.

**Bang!**

The blow hurled Eldridge from the air into the British infantry behind him and crushed a swath of men under his fall.

Blood sprayed from his mouth.

His organs felt wrong inside his chest and every bone seemed loose.

Pain in his chest and gut told him ribs had broken.

Years near magic and crystal ore had hardened him beyond an ordinary man.

*Mage Armor* had already frayed under Max's platoon. The steel fist shattered what remained into blue sparks.

He was not dead yet. He fought to rise from the ground.

*"Good work, Adjutant Wilhelm!"*

Max pumped his fist once and shouted to the men beside him. *"Get Colonel Ludwig out of that cockpit!"*

He ran with Captain Hauser toward the fallen Siegfried Type 1 and tried to force the hatch.

In their hurry they forgot the other threat still on the street.

The last St. George Type 3 remained.

Its pilot had watched two German machines die to friendly magic and the senior instructor thrown down by the last German knight.

He hesitated a moment then raised his armed sword.

He did not run to Eldridge. The Garter knights and the Highland mages were allies of convenience at best.

His hate fixed on the Siegfried Type 1 on the ground and the infantry clustered around it.

*"Die, German scum!"*

The pilot laughed and walked his damaged machine forward on one good arm with the great blade ready.

He would finish the downed knight first and then grind the riflemen into the stones.

*"Look out! The enemy is coming!"*

A German soldier screamed the warning.

Riflemen on the distant line saw the same threat and opened fire on the St. George Type 3 without waiting for an order.

**Clang clang clang!**

Bullets beat against the armor and threw sparks in long streams. None of the rounds bit deep.

The gap between infantry and an armored knight showed in full on that street.

Max watched the scarred machine step closer and searched his mind for anything that could stop it.

Rifles and grenades had already failed in this fight. Single shot weapons could not hurt that hull.

Then his eye caught a shape beside Ludwig's fallen mount.

A close range shaped charge armor piercing round had slipped from its rack when the hull went down.

A wild plan formed at once.

The round was built for armored knight mounts but Max had designed it. A grown man could lift the weight if he tried.

Carry it in close to the St. George Type 3 and set it off at short range.

He looked at the knight closing step by step and then at the round on the stones. Doubt crossed his face.

Why should he die for a country he had only served for forty eight hours?

Before he could move Captain Hauser's bloody hand clamped his collar from behind and dragged him into cover.

Max turned and saw a face cut by shrapnel and caked in mud.

Captain Hauser was pale to the lips. Blood loss had drained the color from his mouth.

Max looked lower and saw a sharp splinter of wood driven into the captain's gut.

Blood pumped through the gray tunic in a dark spread.

*"Captain! You are hit! Lie down!"*

Max reached to steady him.

*"Leave me."*

Captain Hauser's voice was thin but his eyes held firm.

He pressed a bundle wrapped in cloth into Max's arms and shoved him deeper into the building.

*"You are young. Live."*

That was the last sentence Captain Hauser spoke to him.

He turned and burst from the doorway with a speed that should not have been possible for a wounded man.

Two other German veterans who could barely stand saw him move and understood without words.

They met each other's eyes and chose the same end.

The St. George Type 3 loomed over them with its armed sword raised. Blue enchantment ran along the edge.

The pilot held the blade in a reverse grip to drive it down through Ludwig's cockpit and finish the kill.

Then he saw three riflemen bend together and heave the thick tube off the ground with everything they had left.

*"No!"*

The image of two companions erased by that weapon flashed through the pilot's mind.

He forgot the downed Siegfried Type 1 and swung the great blade at the three men carrying the round.

His shield was gone with the arm lost earlier in the fight.

The only way to stop them was to cut them apart before they fired.

The blade fell toward their heads. Captain Hauser and the two veterans did not flinch.

They lifted their faces to the steel and roared with the last breath in their lungs.

*"Glory to the German Empire!"*

Captain Hauser found the mechanical firing stud on the tail of the shaped charge and pressed it as the shout left his throat.

**Boom rumble!**

Fire bloomed on the street again.

A jet of metal punched through the front of the St. George Type 3 cockpit and left nothing intact inside.

The Garter pilot and his machine became scrap in one strike.

The price was just as heavy.

The blast blew the veteran closest to the falling blade apart until nothing whole remained of him.

Shrapnel riddled the second man with holes from throat to belt.

The jet hurled Captain Hauser backward across the rubble.

The great sword took his arm off at the shoulder as he fell.

Blast and the falling blade took all three before they could see the knight die.
