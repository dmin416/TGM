# Chapter 18

Arc: Expedition Encircled

Date: Late January 1914

---

At German Great General Staff headquarters the mood over the map of Spain was grim.

Army Chief of Staff Helmuth von Moltke the Younger stood with his officers and stared at red arrows winding around the German expedition and its allies.

Everyone was silent.

None of them had expected a low risk intervention to collapse within days like this.

A staff captain entered with another telegram and passed it to Moltke the Younger.

*"Chief, General Mackensen's telegram."*

Moltke the Younger read it fast.

His brow stayed locked.

Mackensen's plan was the only quick way to save the trapped force.

Moltke the Younger also knew the price.

The empire would drop the mask of limited intervention and step into full commitment, which was almost a declaration of war on the Holy Britannian Empire.

Moltke the Younger thought for a moment and decided.

*"Prepare my car. I will see His Majesty at once."*

Only the Kaiser could make a call of this magnitude.

At a German royal residence outside Dresden the Emperor sat in a gilded audience chamber.

Chief of Staff Moltke the Younger reported while Albert II listened in disbelief.

*"What did you say? The expedition is encircled?"*

Albert II's voice cracked with shock and he wondered if his ears had failed him.

Two days earlier the General Staff had assured him that all was well and that the chief prize Seville sat firmly in German hands.

*"Yes, Your Majesty."*

Moltke the Younger kept his eyes down.

*"Lieutenant General Prittwitz made grave errors in command. Our troops lost touch with the allies and the enemy cut off the path to retreat."*

The Emperor sank into the great oak chair behind his desk and the room seemed to spin.

He did not explode at once because blame would not do any good.

The crisis in Spain had to be answered first.

Albert II forced himself to be calm.

*"What does the General Staff propose?"*

Moltke the Younger offered General Mackensen's request in reply.

*"Your Majesty, General Mackensen asks to lead the twelve divisions on the border into Spain at once to relieve the expedition. The General Staff judges that the fastest effective course available."*

*"General Mackensen."*

The Kaiser repeated the name and drummed his fingers on the wood.

Albert II knew Mackensen's record and knew him as one of the empire's most aggressive field commanders.

Success was likely if anyone could pull the trapped force free.

Albert II voiced the fear that had kept him hesitating.

*"Will that turn the Spanish fight into full war with Holy Britannia?"*

Moltke the Younger paused and looked up at the Emperor.

*"Your Majesty, the risk is real. If we do not relieve the expedition now our position will grow worse still."*

*"Even if we wish to stop, Holy Britannia may not pass up a chance to strike a force already beaten."*

Moltke the Younger's words left Albert II hesitating.

Albert II often preached in public that Germany needed land in the sun and that he would not shrink from war with the Holy Britannian Empire to get it.

He had also pushed army building and above all the expansion of the navy.

Honestly he and the whole German Empire were not ready for all out war with the world's leading power.

The empire's economic turn was not finished. Fleet tonnage still lagged behind Holy Britannia. Colonial returns to the homeland had only just begun to strengthen.

Reason said the moment to fight was not ripe.

Sentiment said to flinch now was cowardice.

*"Helmuth."*

Albert II fixed his army chief of staff with a grave stare.

*"Tell me. Is the army ready for a full war?"*

Moltke the Younger's answer surprised him.

*"Your Majesty, the German Army was born to enter battle when the empire needs it, not when every officer declares himself ready."*

*"So the moment Your Majesty gives the order the General Staff will draft the attack plan at once and every soldier in the empire will fight for Emperor and Reich without counting the cost."*

The words rang hard with the loyalty German staff officers were famous for.

That was the view from the top.

Albert II studied the steadfast chief and thought a long while.

Moltke never said the army was ready. Albert II took that as no.

Albert II had no other option.

Albert II would set himself and the empire back if he weakened now.

It would waste years of work for Germany's second rise.

Albert II rose slowly from the chair and walked to the tall window that looked over Dresden.

*"I approve General Mackensen's request."*

His voice stayed level though his back was to Moltke.

*"Send Mackensen at once with the divisions already assembled. He must reopen the line to the expedition in the shortest time possible."*

*"Yes, Your Majesty."*

Albert II paused and called for the usher outside the door.

*"Summon the naval minister, the air minister and the chiefs of staff of both services. Also the imperial chancellor and the foreign secretary."*

Half an hour later in a large reception room of the Dresden royal palace the mood was leaden.

Germany's military and civilian leaders stood gathered.

Imperial Chancellor Theobald von Bethmann Hollweg, Foreign Secretary Gottlieb von Jagow, the ministers of the three services and the three chiefs had heard part of the news already. Every face was grim.

Moltke the Younger outlined the crisis in Spain and Mackensen's relief plan first.

When the room heard the whole expedition might be destroyed the color drained from every senior face.

*"How how is that possible?"*

Imperial Chancellor Bethmann Hollweg stared at Moltke the Younger.

*"Before the expedition sailed the General Staff swore this was only a limited intervention."*

The chancellor looked at Moltke as if he had not helped persuade the Emperor weeks ago.

Moltke the Younger ignored the charge. He was used to it and knew blame would not help.

He turned calmly to the Emperor on the high seat.

Albert II cleared his throat and pulled every eye to him.

He looked around the circle and stopped on Naval Marshal Alfred von Tirpitz and General Schubert, chief of the air staff.

*"Naval Marshal Tirpitz, General Schubert."*

His tone was calm now without the first shock of the bad news.

*"Are the navy and the air arm ready for all out war with Holy Britannia?"*

Naval Marshal Tirpitz rose. Forked beard, builder of the High Seas Fleet and the Mediterranean squadron, author of risk fleet theory.

He smoothed his beard and spoke in a low voice.

*"When Your Majesty commands the fleets of the empire will sail. That is why the navy exists."*

General Schubert stood next.

*"Your Majesty, the armored airships can leave a screen on the French frontier and the rest will move on your order. Two new hulls with upgraded cannon will finish refit before month's end."*

Both answers matched Moltke the Younger's pattern.

Loyalty and obedience, not a straight yes on readiness.

Albert II knew the fleet and the air arm were no more fully ready than the army. That no longer mattered.

After long thought he gave the final orders.

*"Good. I order all three services to full readiness and every step needed for war with Holy Britannia. In posture at least we must show Britannia we will fight."*

*"For the army, execute Mackensen's plan at once. I give him the twelve infantry divisions on the border. Cross into Spain and break the encirclement as fast as steel allows."*

*"For the navy, the Mediterranean fleet and the High Seas Fleet leave harbor at once. Combat patrols. Show Holy Britannia our strength."*

*"For the air arm, all armored airships to first degree alert. Air General Staff will draft movement plans to support the other services when called."*

Orders fell one after another from the Emperor.

The room kindled though crisis pressed in and the empire was not ready for a head on clash with Holy Britannia. The senior officers' eyes still burned with excitement.

After the war orders Albert II looked to Foreign Secretary Gottlieb von Jagow.

*"Keep contact with Holy Britannia open. Prepare to negotiate. If they offer any channel for talks pursue it and win the best terms you can. War is always the last choice."*

*"Yes, Your Majesty."*

When the council broke encrypted telegrams flew from the General Staff to commands across the empire.

On the border with Spain twelve infantry divisions already on war footing crossed the frontier under Mackensen's personal command by military train.

They would detrain short of the front, deploy and strike toward Cuenca and Valencia against the enemy columns that had cut the expedition off.

More divisions mobilized inland and marched toward the frontier.

Partial call ups of reserves had gone out. Moltke the Younger had drafted a full mobilization decree waiting only the Emperor's signature.
