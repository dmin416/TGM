 # Chapter 18

Arc: Expedition Encircled

Date: Late January 1914

---

At German Great General Staff headquarters the mood over the map of Spain was grim.

Army Chief of Staff Helmuth von Moltke the Younger stood with his officers and stared at red arrows winding around the German expedition and its allies.

Everyone was silent.

None of them had expected a low risk intervention to collapse within days like this.

A staff captain entered with another telegram and passed it to Moltke the Younger.

*"Chief, General Mackensen's telegram."*

Moltke the Younger read it fast.

His brow stayed locked.

Mackensen's plan was the only quick way to save the trapped force.

Moltke the Younger also knew the price.

The empire would drop the mask of limited intervention and step into full commitment, which was almost a declaration of war on the Holy Britannian Empire.

Moltke the Younger thought for a moment and decided.

*"Prepare my car. I will see His Majesty at once."*

Only the Kaiser could make a call of this magnitude.

At a German royal residence outside Dresden the Emperor sat in a gilded audience chamber.

Chief of Staff Moltke the Younger reported while Albert II listened in disbelief.

*"What did you say? The expedition is encircled?"*

Albert II's voice cracked with shock and he wondered if his ears had failed him.

Two days earlier the General Staff had assured him that all was well and that the chief prize Seville sat firmly in German hands.

*"Yes, Your Majesty."*

Moltke the Younger kept his eyes down.

*"Lieutenant General Prittwitz made grave errors in command. Our troops lost touch with the allies and the enemy cut off the path to retreat."*

The Emperor sank into the great oak chair behind his desk and the room seemed to spin.

He did not explode at once because blame would not do any good.

The crisis in Spain had to be answered first.

Albert II forced himself to be calm.

*"What does the General Staff propose?"*

Moltke the Younger offered General Mackensen's request in reply.

*"Your Majesty, General Mackensen asks to lead the twelve divisions on the border into Spain at once to relieve the expedition. The General Staff judges that the fastest effective course available."*

*"General Mackensen."*

The Kaiser repeated the name and drummed his fingers on the wood.

Albert II knew Mackensen's record and knew him as one of the empire's most aggressive field commanders.

Success was likely if anyone could pull the trapped force free.

Albert II voiced the fear that had kept him hesitating.

*"Will that turn the Spanish fight into full war with Holy Britannia?"*

Moltke the Younger paused and looked up at the Emperor.

*"Your Majesty, the risk is real. If we do not relieve the expedition now our position will grow worse still."*

*"Even if we wish to stop, Holy Britannia may not pass up a chance to strike a force already beaten."*

Moltke the Younger's words left Albert II hesitating.

Albert II often preached in public that Germany needed land in the sun and that he would not shrink from war with the Holy Britannian Empire to get it.

He had also pushed army building and above all the expansion of the navy.

Honestly he and the whole German Empire were not ready for all out war with the world's leading power.

The empire's economic turn was not finished. Fleet tonnage still lagged behind Holy Britannia. Colonial returns to the homeland had only just begun to strengthen.

Reason said the moment to fight was not ripe.

Sentiment said to flinch now was cowardice.

*"Helmuth."*

Albert II fixed his army chief of staff with a grave stare.

*"Tell me. Is the army ready for a full war?"*

Moltke the Younger's answer surprised him.

*"Your Majesty, the German Army was born to enter battle when the empire needs it, not when every officer declares himself ready."*

*"So the moment Your Majesty gives the order the General Staff will draft the attack plan at once and every soldier in the empire will fight for Emperor and Reich without counting the cost."*

The words rang hard with the loyalty German staff officers were famous for.

That was the view from the top.

Albert II studied the steadfast chief and thought a long while.

Moltke never said the army was ready. Albert II took that as no.

Albert II had no other option.

Albert II would set himself and the empire back if he weakened now.

It would waste years of work for Germany's second rise.

Albert II rose slowly from the chair and walked to the tall window that looked over Dresden.

*"I approve General Mackensen's request."*

His voice stayed level though his back was to Moltke.

*"Send Mackensen at once with the divisions already assembled. He must reopen the line to the expedition in the shortest time possible."*

*"Yes, Your Majesty."*

Albert II paused and called for the usher outside the door.

*"Summon the naval minister, the air minister and the chiefs of staff of both services. Also the imperial chancellor and the foreign secretary."*

Half an hour later in a large reception room of the Dresden royal palace the mood was leaden.

Germany's military and civilian leaders stood gathered.

Imperial Chancellor Theobald von Bethmann Hollweg, Foreign Secretary Gottlieb von Jagow, the ministers of the three services and the three chiefs had heard part of the news already. Every face was grim.

Moltke the Younger outlined the crisis in Spain and Mackensen's relief plan first.

When the room heard the whole expedition might be destroyed the color drained from every senior face.

*"How how is that possible?"*

Imperial Chancellor Bethmann Hollweg stared at Moltke the Younger.

*"Before the expedition sailed the General Staff swore this was only a limited intervention."*

The chancellor looked at Moltke as if he had not helped persuade the Emperor weeks ago.

Moltke the Younger ignored the charge. He was used to it and knew blame would not help.

He turned calmly to the Emperor on the high seat.

Albert II cleared his throat and pulled every eye to him.

He looked around the circle and stopped on Naval Marshal Alfred von Tirpitz and General Schubert, chief of the air staff.

*"Naval Marshal Tirpitz, General Schubert."*

His tone was calm now without the first shock of the bad news.

*"Are the navy and the air arm ready for all out war with Holy Britannia?"*

Naval Marshal Tirpitz rose. Forked beard, builder of the High Seas Fleet and the Mediterranean squadron, author of risk fleet theory.

He smoothed his beard and spoke in a low voice.

*"When Your Majesty commands the fleets of the empire will sail. That is why the navy exists."*

General Schubert stood next.

*"Your Majesty, the armored airships can leave a screen on the French frontier and the rest will move on your order. Two new hulls with upgraded cannon will finish refit before month's end."*

Both answers matched Moltke the Younger's pattern.

Loyalty and obedience, not a straight yes on readiness.

Albert II knew the fleet and the air arm were no more fully ready than the army. That no longer mattered.

After long thought he gave the final orders.

*"Good. I order all three services to full readiness and every step needed for war with Holy Britannia. In posture at least we must show Britannia we will fight."*

*"For the army, execute Mackensen's plan at once. I give him the twelve infantry divisions on the border. Cross into Spain and break the encirclement as fast as steel allows."*

*"For the navy, the Mediterranean fleet and the High Seas Fleet leave harbor at once. Combat patrols. Show Holy Britannia our strength."*

*"For the air arm, all armored airships to first degree alert. Air General Staff will draft movement plans to support the other services when called."*

Orders fell one after another from the Emperor.

The room kindled though crisis pressed in and the empire was not ready for a head on clash with Holy Britannia. The senior officers' eyes still burned with excitement.

After the war orders Albert II looked to Foreign Secretary Gottlieb von Jagow.

*"Keep contact with Holy Britannia open. Prepare to negotiate. If they offer any channel for talks pursue it and win the best terms you can. War is always the last choice."*

*"Yes, Your Majesty."*

When the council broke encrypted telegrams flew from the General Staff to commands across the empire.

On the border with Spain twelve infantry divisions already on war footing crossed the frontier under Mackensen's personal command by military train.

They would detrain short of the front, deploy and strike toward Cuenca and Valencia against the enemy columns that had cut the expedition off.

More divisions mobilized inland and marched toward the frontier.

Partial call ups of reserves had gone out. Moltke the Younger had drafted a full mobilization decree waiting only the Emperor's signature.

---

Morning at Port Montana came before the sun had fully cleared the horizon.

Heavy sea fog wrapped the harbor and thinned the lighthouse beam to a dull smear.

The quay held row on row of black silhouettes, the sleeping steel hulls of the German Mediterranean Fleet.

The port stayed so quiet that only the cold slap of waves on iron and the lighthouse's steady note carried across the water.

On the bridge of the Mediterranean Fleet flagship, the *Moltke* class battlecruiser SMS *Goeben*, watch officer Captain Müller leaned on one cup of hot coffee after another to fight off the drowsiness of an all night watch.

Captain Müller had lost count of how many cups he had taken.

That hardly mattered. Captain Müller looked through the sidelight at the white fog outside and reckoned he could hand over the watch in a little more than an hour and sleep at last.

The voice pipe behind Captain Müller buzzed sharp and sudden.

Captain Müller came fully awake at once, crossed to the pipe and lifted the handset.

*"Bridge. Go ahead."*

The voice on the line was tight with held back strain.

*"Radio room here. Urgent telegram from Naval General Staff."*

*"Confirm sender authentication code."*

Captain Müller felt his nerves tighten. Naval General Staff traffic normally went to Mediterranean Fleet headquarters first.

A message routed straight to the ship usually meant the fleet commander was aboard and the staff in Dresden needed him at once.

Admiral von Spee was indeed resting on his newly assigned flagship tonight.

That was his habit whenever he shifted flag. He would live aboard the new ship for a stretch until he knew her.

*"Authentication verified. Dresden Naval General Staff operations section, direct."*

The radio room officer answered.

*"I will send a runner. No, wait."*

Captain Müller changed his mind.

*"I am coming down myself."*

Captain Müller set the handset back and turned to a watch sailor on the bridge.

*"Fetch Colonel Richter to the bridge at once. Tell him we have an urgent telegram from Naval General Staff."*

The sailor saluted and left to find the Mediterranean Fleet chief of staff.

Captain Müller went down to the radio room below the bridge.

Tension in the sealed compartment was almost thick enough to touch.

Two radio operators worked with the duty officer while his fingers flew across the code book turning number groups into plain text.

Sweat ran from the translating officer's temple and he never noticed.

Captain Müller asked how long the decode would take.

*"Last group, Captain."*

The duty officer never looked up. His pencil scratched fast across the special message form.

When the final character was written the duty officer lifted the sheet, drew one breath and held it out to Captain Müller with ceremony.

```
TOP SECRET URGENT

To Mediterranean Fleet Commander

The war in Spain has turned. By His Majesty's authority the Mediterranean Fleet will leave harbor at once on combat patrol and await further orders.

Naval Chief of Staff Alfred von Tirpitz
11 February 1914, 0500 hours
```

Captain Müller read the lines and heard his own heartbeat in his ears.

Captain Müller turned from the radio room and ran back to the bridge at full stride.

Colonel Richter had already reached the bridge, sleepy eyed but alert.

Captain Müller stopped in front of Colonel Richter and passed him the sheet without a word.

Colonel Richter's pupils contracted as he read. A moment later he breathed a barely audible *"My God."*

Colonel Richter read the telegram twice and looked up at Captain Müller.

*"Is the Admiral still in his cabin?"*

*"Yes, Colonel. He should still be resting at this hour."*

Colonel Richter nodded and spoke quietly to a sailor, who left at once.

Before long Admiral von Spee's aide, young Captain Hassel, appeared on the bridge in a uniform pressed as if he had never taken it off.

Colonel Richter held out the telegram.

*"Captain Hassel. Urgent Naval General Staff message. It must go to the Admiral immediately."*

Captain Hassel took the sheet, clipped it into his folder and answered with a stiff nod.

*"Yes, Colonel."*

The passage from the bridge to the Admiral's cabin felt several times longer than usual.

Captain Hassel heard his own steps echo in the corridor.

The sentry outside the Admiral's door saw the message folder and stepped aside without a word, then knocked lightly on the hatch.

No answer came.

Captain Hassel exchanged a glance with the sentry and knocked again, harder this time.

A muffled voice asked from inside what was wanted.

*"Admiral, forgive the intrusion. We have an urgent telegram from Naval General Staff."*

Captain Hassel pushed the hatch open and switched on the small lamp by the frame.

Admiral Maximilian von Spee, commander of the German Mediterranean Fleet, was already sitting up in his bunk.

His white hair was disordered but his eyes were fully awake.

Admiral von Spee asked nothing. He took the message and in the dim cabin light read it once and rose from the bed in a single motion.

*"Sound the emergency weigh anchor alarm. Hoist the signal flag on the mainmast and call every captain to a conference on the flagship."*

*"Begin raising steam in every boiler at once."*

Captain Hassel saluted, turned and ran for the bridge to pass the orders.

Soon a piercing whistle tore through the quiet harbor and woke the sleeping fleet.

In a hammock aboard the armored cruiser SMS *Scharnhorst* chief engineer Karl Borg snored like rolling thunder.

When the emergency weigh anchor whistle cut through deck after deck Karl Borg came off the hammock by pure reflex and hit the cold deck hard.

Karl Borg was not fully awake yet but his hands were already hunting for his boots.

*"Damn it. What now?"*

Karl Borg cursed under his breath and kept moving.

*"All hands to stations. Raise steam. Every stoker to the boiler room now."*

Karl Borg roared the orders as he plunged down the narrow ladder into the maze of the hull.

Heat hit Karl Borg when he reached the engine room.

Explosion proof lamps threw a yellow glow through oil mist and mineral dust.

Massive steam pipes wrapped in thick asbestos lagging ran across the overhead and bulkheads.

German warships still burned mixed fuel even with heavy oil boilers installed.

Duty stokers worked in pairs and wrenched open boiler doors one after another.

Red light flared across faces slick with sweat and Luminite ash.

Iron shovels fed processed Luminite ore into embers that had never been allowed to die completely.

Besides crude distillate from Luminite retorts, crushed and washed ore could still be burned directly and drive a boiler to peak pressure in very little time.

Karl Borg bellowed at a stoker on the valve wheel.

*"What pressure do we have?"*

*"Fifty, sir. Just enough to keep ship's power running."*

The stoker shouted back over the machinery and his words blurred in the roar.

*"I want three hundred. I want three hundred inside one hour."*

Karl Borg knew the proper cold start from dead fires to full pressure needed more than four hours by the book.

Imperial Navy regulations forbade any harbor ship except those in maintenance or refit from letting every fire go out, precisely for a moment like this.

Karl Borg trusted his crew and the ship under their feet to meet his demand.

On the weather decks sailors worked under a boatswain whose voice could match the whistle.

They drove the capstan and hauled the heavy chain up from the warm Mediterranean water.

**CLANK CLANK CLANK**

Link after link rose with weed and mud dripping from the iron.

*"Move, you slugs. His Majesty is watching."*

In the flagship SMS *Goeben* combat information center Admiral von Spee stood before a large chart of the sea.

Captains of the Mediterranean Fleet had come over by launch and now stood in a grim ring around him.

Through the sidelights smoke and white steam already poured from every stack as the ships woke and breathed hard.

*"Gentlemen."*

Admiral von Spee did not raise his voice but every man in the room heard him.

*"Naval General Staff and His Majesty have ordered us to sea on emergency sailing."*

Admiral von Spee tapped the decoded telegram.

*"The text names the war in Spain. My read is that His Majesty wants the German Navy to show Holy Britannia our posture through combat patrol."*

Admiral von Spee's gaze hardened.

*"If Holy Britannia loses its head this posture may become a real war."*

The captains looked at one another.

The captain of the lead *Moltke* class battlecruiser SMS *Moltke* spoke first.

*"Admiral, a combat patrol will draw more than Holy Britannia. The Gaul Republic and even the Papal fleet will react."*

Another officer added at once.

*"The Austro Hungarian Navy will sortie under the joint defense treaty as well."*

Admiral von Spee nodded.

*"Correct."*

Admiral von Spee went on.

*"Naval Marshal Tirpitz did not spell it out in the wire but I read his intent. If we and the High Seas Fleet both show a hard face and stir the water, Holy Britannia must weigh the cost of war with care."*

*"The Gaul Republic will be very happy to stab them in the back."*

An armored cruiser captain added that line and several faces in the room almost broke into grins.

During the Morocco crisis the partnership of Holy Britannia and the Gaul Republic had kept German naval officers walking on glass.

In the last two years that friendship had sunk fast over colonial clashes.

Pressure on the German side had eased sharply and the Gaul Republic had done everything it could to make trouble for Holy Britannia.

Gaul warships had paid repeated visits to German ports.

Admiral von Spee settled a few more details and then laid his pointer on a course marked on the chart.

*"Scouting forces will sail first as vanguard and advance along this track to search for the enemy."*

*"The main body will follow in cruising formation three, leave harbor on that sequence and join the scouts at the rendezvous in the marked channel."*

Every captain wrote in his notebook. No one questioned the drill.

Years of hard training and endless exercises had burned the routine into muscle memory.

Admiral von Spee dismissed them.

*"Return to your ships, gentlemen."*

*"In one hour I want the last hull in this fleet clear of Port Montana. God save Germany."*

*"Yes, Admiral."*

The captains saluted together, turned and left for their launches.

An hour later the morning fog began to lift.

The German Mediterranean Fleet showed itself through the thinning mist and sailed out of Port Montana under long blasts of every whistle.

The port garrison did not try to seal the waterfront. The ships made no effort to hide their movement.

The fleet steered into the open Mediterranean in plain sight.

Port Montana locals had watched the sortie from the quays.

In the crowd a man in plain work clothes stood still while the fleet vanished against the horizon. His eyes kept shifting.

The man gasped with the others and traded a few words with men he knew.

After a while the man slipped out of the crowd and turned into a dark alley beside the waterfront.

The alley twisted several times before he stopped at a house that looked long abandoned.

The man checked the street, saw no tail and pushed the creaking door open and went inside.

The man climbed to the second floor, moved a broken wardrobe aside and opened a narrow hidden closet behind it.

A wireless telegraph set sat on the table inside the closet.

---

The next day in London, capital of the Holy Britannian Empire, Buckingham Palace held a grave audience in the great throne room.

Queen Victoria sat on the throne at ninety four years of age.

Gold and Aetherium crystal made the seat and spell light ran across its surface in visible threads that framed the Eternal Queen in hard mystery.

Queen Victoria's face and frame showed none of the ruin of age. She still looked as she had at eighteen when she took the crown.

Black hair lay coiled at the back of her head. Her skin stayed smooth and firm. Her eyes stayed clear and sharp as if time had never touched her.

Below the throne stood the imperial prime minister, the army minister, the navy minister, the colonial secretary and the heads of several military intelligence branches. Every man waited with his hands at his sides.

The army intelligence minister spoke first. His voice carried in the empty hall with strain in it.

*"Your Majesty, the German Army has begun partial mobilization. Our latest reports say twelve infantry divisions have already crossed into Spain. Our expedition at Cuenca and Valencia is digging in for defense but the numbers favor the enemy."*

The navy intelligence minister added at once.

*"On the naval side, Your Majesty, both the German High Seas Fleet and the Mediterranean Fleet have left harbor."*

*"The Mediterranean Fleet is moving toward the Gibraltar approach and the High Seas Fleet has entered the North Sea. The signal is plain. Germany means to stand hard."*

The navy minister stepped forward as the words ended.

*"Your Majesty, the Royal Navy must answer in kind. Home Fleet and Mediterranean Fleet must sail at once and show Germany our resolve."*

The navy minister glanced at the army minister and the army minister spoke with a heavy face.

*"The army needs reinforcement as well. Germany shares a land border with Spain and can feed troops in by rail without end."*

*"Our expedition must move every soldier and every shell by sea. Until relief arrives our force in Spain will fight at a grave disadvantage."*

Both service ministers sounded urgent. The shift in Spain had caught them and the whole Holy Britannian leadership off guard.

In the first staff games Seville was supposed to fall first under heavy reinforcement.

Then the ports southwest of Seville and the Spanish navy in those harbors would follow.

Madrid and the other axes were meant as holding actions.

Nobody had planned for Seville to hold while Madrid fell without warning.

The expedition's drive to the Mediterranean coast had bagged a tactical prize and a strategic trap at once.

*"We cannot watch the expedition die."* The army minister went on. *"We must reinforce Spain and reinforce it on a large scale."*

Tension in the hall kept climbing until Prime Minister Herbert Henry Asquith and the colonial secretary traded a look and the prime minister stepped out.

*"Gentlemen, before we drag the empire into a new war we should count what we have already lost."*

Prime Minister Asquith looked around the room and continued.

*"I believe every man here has read the front reports from Seville. The losses were severe. Three main battalions of the Northumberland Fusiliers, the expedition's elite, were shattered and need full drafts from home before they can fight again."*

*"The Order of the Garter detachment was wiped out."*

A senior instructor from the Highland Mage Corps who had said nothing until now spoke from his place.

*"Five Highland mages died at Seville including Senior Instructor Eldridge. One survivor took severe mental shock and is already on emergency passage home."*

The room had heard all of this before.

Hearing it again still felt like a nightmare.

Five deaths in one day had no match in Highland Mage Corps history.

Even the worst days of the Spell Plague had not cost so much in a single sun.

Prime Minister Asquith summed the point.

*"All of this says our estimate of German military power was badly wrong."*

Prime Minister Asquith paused and swept the room.

*"Worse still, our quarrel with the Gaul Republic has not cooled. If we rush into full war with Germany now, who can swear the French will not strike from behind?"*

Prime Minister Asquith's words left the hall in silence.

The navy minister and the army minister wanted a harder line but had no clean answer.

As hardliners on the Queen's Supreme War Council they also knew that weak reply would bruise imperial prestige and invite German pressure.

*"Prime Minister, do you mean we sit idle and let Germany run Spain?"* The army minister demanded. *"That tells the world we are afraid."*

*"I do not mean that."*

Prime Minister Asquith shook his head and turned on the two service chiefs with a harder tone.

*"I mean do not repeat the same mistake. You soldiers never learn from the last war."*

Prime Minister Asquith drove each word.

*"The Boer War was your push. What did it buy?"*

*"A costly scrape we barely called victory."*

*"Money, stores and lives against the gain we took were out of balance."*

Prime Minister Asquith's voice filled the hall.

*"Tell me."* He stepped toward the army minister. *"Do you want another Boer War and drag this empire into the pit?"*

The question hit direct and the army minister flushed white and red and found no strong reply.

Boer War shadow still sat on many Holy Britannian minds.

A war that looked sure had turned into a mud fight that hurt treasury and name together.

*"Prime Minister, times are not the same."*

The navy minister spoke as a fellow member of the Supreme War Council and tried to shield his colleague.

*"Germany today is a worse threat than the Boers Germany once fed in secret. If we shrink back now the bill will grow later."*

The colonial secretary stepped in and gave the prime minister another weight.

*"Your Majesty. Prime Minister."*

The colonial secretary bowed to Queen Victoria and to Prime Minister Asquith and then faced the rest with a worried face.

*"Spain is grave but we cannot forget the rest of the empire. North America above all."*

The colonial secretary raised a file he had held a long while.

*"In the last months I have taken dozens of urgent wires from the Governor General of the American provinces, His Royal Highness the Prince of Wales. North America is less stable than we thought. Rebel signs have shown in many districts."*

*"They are still small, riots and raids, but the trend is dangerous. The Prince has asked London again and again for troops and stores to hold the ground."*

The colonial secretary pressed harder.

*"Gentlemen, hear this. Spain has paid us nothing yet. North America is the brightest jewel on the Holy Britannian crown. Its weight on our purse and our strategy is direct and larger."*

*"If North America burns while we are tied down against Germany we may fight on two fronts or more."*

The cold facts cooled some of the war hunger in the room.

Imperial strength was not endless.

Long feud with France over African colonies already held large forces.

Spain was heating while North America might flare and global deployment would tear apart.

*"That is nonsense."* The army minister argued on. *"North American rebels are rabble. The Prince has enough men on the ground. We must not throw away Iberia over ghosts."*

*"Rabble?"* The colonial secretary laughed without warmth. *"The men who fought the North American rebellion looked like rabble too until they were not. If Benedict Arnold had not delivered Washington bound into our hands we might still be guessing what North America would look like."*

The army minister choked on his answer.

The cabinet was near open rupture in front of the Queen and Prime Minister Asquith raised a hand to still the room.

*"Enough. Less noise from both sides."*

Prime Minister Asquith waved the colonial secretary down and went on.

*"Every fear here has some truth. This is not the hour for blame. We need a plan that gets the empire through the crisis."*

The fight paused but nobody convinced the other side.

The military bloc still wanted a hard answer to save face and hold Spain.

Prime Minister Asquith's bloc still wanted caution, no new mud war and North America first.

Debate ran a long while. Plans rose and fell.

The throne room stayed tight with worry.

At last every eye turned to the Eternal Queen who had not spoken.

No matter how loud the argument became the final word still sat with the woman who had ruled near a century.

Queen Victoria's face showed nothing. Her deep gaze moved over each minister as if she could read what they hid.

The hall went still.

Every man held his breath for her judgment.

Queen Victoria spoke at last. Her voice was not loud but it reached every ear with born command.

*"Much of what you say is sound. But."*

Queen Victoria's tone sharpened.

*"I stand with the soldiers on Germany. The Holy Britannian Empire must not show one inch of weakness."*

*"My unruly grandson wants to see us flinch."*

*"The more we step back the more he will press. As his grandmother I know that better than any man in this room."*

Queen Victoria rose from the throne and walked to the step above the great world map set in the floor.

*"So the posture must be made."*

Queen Victoria looked up at the ministers. Her eyes were iron.

*"I approve reinforcement. Army ministry will draft a plan to lift troops from home and North Africa and ship them to Spain at once."*

*"Navy ministry, order Home Fleet and Mediterranean Fleet main bodies to sea. I care little whether you call it combat patrol or exercise. I want German crews to see Royal Navy colors every hour of the day."*

*"Yes, Your Majesty."*

Both service ministers answered together, loud with relief and zeal.

Queen Victoria's call matched her grandson Emperor Albert II in Germany almost line for line.

She too believed that whether full war came or not the enemy must never think Holy Britannia would yield.

Queen Victoria turned to the foreign secretary.

*"Sir Edward Grey."*

*"Your Majesty."*

Sir Edward Grey had stood in the corner as if the quarrel did not touch him. He stepped forward now.

Queen Victoria's tone eased a fraction.

*"While the fleets sail keep contact with Germany open and keep negotiation ready. War continues politics but it is not the only tool. I want the best terms for the empire on the field and at the table."*

The sentence matched what Albert II had told Gottlieb von Jagow in Dresden.

*"Yes, Your Majesty."*

Sir Edward Grey bowed.
