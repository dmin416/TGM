# Chapter 1.5

Arc: Emperor side view 1

Date: Late January 1914, night

German Empire. Potsdam. Sanssouci Palace.

The Rococo palace people called the German Versailles sat quiet and grave under night.

Footsteps soon broke the calm.

A coded telegram from the Spain front reached Emperor Albert II's study two hours after it began its relay and translation run.

With the telegram came Chief of the Great General Staff Helmuth von Moltke the Younger and Imperial Chancellor Theobald von Bethmann Hollweg in a hurry.

Helmuth von Moltke the Younger's uncle was the old demigod Moltke who had built the German way of war.

Advisor Helmuth was softer in temper yet he had outworked many sharper men since he served Albert II as aide de camp and had climbed to chief of the army's Great General Staff.

In the study, firelight played in the hearth and painted tired lines on the emperor's face.

Albert II took the sheet from an attendant and read fast.

Halfway down the page the ruler's color went bad. His fingers whitened on the paper.

As he read on, the tight lines eased. A nearby attendant even heard a breath of relief.

*"Thank heaven they pulled him out. Thank heaven they pulled him out."*

Albert II set the paper down, leaned into his chair and exhaled.

A German lieutenant general taken on foreign soil by a hostile power was more than a battlefield loss. It would have been a diplomatic disaster and a storm in the press.

The worst case had not happened.

*"Your Majesty, what the Holy Britannian forces did stomps on the empire's honor."*

Advisor Helmuth broke the brief silence. He was not tall but the gray tunic sat sharp enough to carry weight.

*"They ambushed our military attach√© delegation without a declaration of war. That does not only mock the German Army. It challenges your authority as emperor."*

Advisor Helmuth stepped forward. Anger sat plain in his voice.

*"We must make them pay. Spain is our hinge for influence to the south. It cannot fall to Holy Britannia. If the Royal Army they back wins, Britannian reach stops one march from our border."*

Chancellor Bethmann Hollweg had stayed quiet. Now he spoke in a calmer tone that still carried full weight.

*"Your Majesty, the chief of staff is right. From politics alone, Crown Prince Alfonso studied in the empire and favors our industrial model. He is our natural ally. More than that."*

Hollweg paused and dropped the heavier fact.

*"New survey work says the southern mountains of Spain may hold the largest Aetherium Crystal belt on the continent. So the civil war there is no longer a small quarrel."*

Magic crystal ore.

Albert II's eyes sharpened. He knew the math.

Germany's industry ran on Luminite, but competing with Holy Britannia and France on the magitech path still lived or died on magic crystal ore.

One continental scale strike could shift the whole balance.

He also knew why Holy Britannia had stepped into Spain's war.

If France had not been busy crushing big colonial revolts the neighbor that also touched Spain would have jumped in too.

Why would two magitech powers suddenly hunger for Aetherium Crystals?

The answer was simple.

Mines emptied.

Aetherium Crystals from skyfall meteors were always scarcer than native ores.

After years of unchecked mining by Holy Britannia and France, Aetherium Crystal stocks sat near the red line.

So both magitech giants had wanted to pivot for a long time. The strike in Spain solved timing for both capitals at once.

Albert II frowned, shelved the thought and looked at his chief of staff.

*"Helmuth, if I let you loose how sure are you. One more thing. If we strike first does that count as declaring war on Holy Britannia?"*

Advisor Helmuth considered it for a moment and answered with care.

*"Your Majesty, trust the training and nerve and discipline of imperial soldiers. As for declarations, Holy Britannia chose war without a trumpet. Why should we cling to empty form."*

Albert II had little quarrel with that.

For years, colonial fighting in Africa over land and resources against Holy Britannia and France had never really stopped. Bloody skirmishes were routine.

If every clash needed a formal declaration Europe would already be paste.

*"Continue."*

*"Yes, Your Majesty. If we attack in force the troops already inside Spain will fight with the National Army and the International Brigades in the same plan."*

At *International Brigades* Advisor Helmuth's mouth twitched.

*"International Brigades."*

Albert II's eyes held a complicated flash when he named the unit.

*"I recall you told me the eleventh brigade of volunteers finished forming and entered Spain by land."*

*"Yes, Your Majesty. International Brigades strength inside Spain is now seven brigades."*

Advisor Helmuth nodded. His own feelings matched the emperor's mess.

Volunteers who answered the slogan about breaking monopoly and sharing progress were not career soldiers. Their will to fight and their nerve still earned his respect and a thread of fear.

The empire had raised the brigades and paid for arms, food and trains.

In a sense, the sides were tight allies.

He had also asked himself more than once whether one day those workers and farmers and teachers might aim rifles at Germany.

Chancellor Hollweg stepped up.

*"Your Majesty, the papers have made heroes of the brigades and the Social Democrats who steer the story grow bold. They have staged big marches in several cities. I must remind you that the left remains a timed charge under our throne."*

*"I know it. For now we still need those volunteers to hold Spain together."*

Albert II sighed. As heir of House Wettin he knew what the domestic left could do.

*"Let the Social Democrats strut a little longer. First we fix the fighting in Spain."*

He stood, crossed to the big map of Europe and rested his eyes on the border where Spain met the empire.

*"I authorize the forces already in Spain to act with our allies and carry out the military moves we need. Show Holy Britannia some color."*

*"At once, Your Majesty."*

Advisor Helmuth snapped to attention. Excitement lit his eyes.

*"Yet."*

Albert II shifted. He tapped Seville on the map.

*"I regret sending General Mackensen to command the Spain fight. Draft joint orders in my name and the Great General Staff's. Recall him home for rest."*

Advisor Helmuth froze. He could not see why the emperor would pull a proven fighter on the eve of battle.

Changing command in sight of the enemy was a grave taboo.

Before he could ask, Albert II went on.

*"Lieutenant General Mackensen is not only a general. He is a living relic of the empire. He belongs in a full war, not a local scrape."*

The emperor's voice stayed level.

*"We cannot afford to lose him in Spain. A small limited war does not need reckless attack. It needs a steady hand so matters do not spin worse."*

He turned to Advisor Helmuth.

*"Chief of staff, whom do you want as the new commander of the Spain expeditionary corps."*

Advisor Helmuth thought a moment and answered with care.

*"Maximilian von Prittwitz, Your Majesty."*

*"You trust him."*

*"He is not Mackensen's equal but his style is calm and he shines at defense and counterattack. For Spain he should be safe enough."*

*"Prittwitz."*

Albert II rolled the name and nodded.

*"So be it. Issue the appointment at once and send him straight to the front to relieve Mackensen."*
