# Chapter 6

Arc: Seville

Date: Late January 1914, afternoon

Thanks to the Royal Army's virtually non-existent road blockade and vigilance their evacuation was exceptionally smooth. Before long the group returned safely to the hillside where they had hidden their bicycles.

After pushing his bicycle out of the bushes Max did not immediately order them to leave.

*"Baumann, come up with me and take another look."*

The two quietly climbed to the top of the slope again. Max raised his binoculars and scanned the enemy positions in the distance one last time to confirm whether the enemy had made any new moves.

Max's gaze locked once again onto the high ground where the mana-crystal cannons were deployed.

There seemed to be a few more people on the high ground than before.

The six-power binoculars in his hand only allowed him to make out that they were some high-ranking officers of the Royal Army and a few people dressed in simple robes with very different bearing from the soldiers around them.

Then the system map in his field of vision began to flash wildly and a series of highlighted icons and information popped up.

```
Key targets detected
Commander, 24th Infantry Division, Kingdom of Spain: General José Sanjurjo
Highland Mage Order, British Empire — High Instructor: Eldridge
Highland Mage Order, British Empire — Mage Apprentice x12
24th Infantry Division — Staff Officers x4
```

For a split second Max's heart skipped a beat.

This was the whole feast laid out on one table.

The Highland Mage Corps. Max became excited immediately. Max had learned from the system and the original owner's memories that magic existed in this world and that the British Empire was particularly accomplished in it.

Now a high-level mage instructor, the commander of the Royal Army's 24th Division and a cluster of staff officers and mage apprentices had all gathered at one small artillery position. Max did not know why the divisional commander had come to the front lines or why he was standing alongside British highland mages. But Max knew this was an opportunity that came once in a lifetime.

Max put down the binoculars and handed them to Corporal Baumann beside him, his voice low with excitement.

*"Baumann, do you see those officers and the people in robes?"*

Corporal Baumann adjusted the focus, observed carefully for a moment and nodded.

*"I see them, sir. That looks like an important person, the man in the long robe. He also looks like the British mage type we were taught to identify before we set off."*

*"So. Are you confident you can hit them at this distance?"*

Corporal Baumann was stunned. He had never expected his platoon leader to have such an appetite.

Baumann raised the binoculars again, carefully calculated the distance, moistened his fingers with saliva to sense the approximate wind direction and then shook his head with some difficulty.

*"Sir, it's too far. About four hundred meters. Although our rifles are accurate, without a scope we rely entirely on iron sights. At this distance the chance of accurately hitting a specific target is not high. Thirty percent at most."*

Max was still unwilling to give up. At his call several other soldiers came over.

They were all specially selected top-notch platoon shooters but after careful observation they all said the distance exceeded their daily training range.

Hitting someone at that range was lucky. Sniping a specific target was another matter entirely.

The impulse in Max's heart was almost completely extinguished.

Max forced himself to calm down.

Corporal Baumann and his men were right. The distance was too great and the risk of missing was too high.

Moreover in Max's understanding special units like mages likely had some form of unusual protective method. If they did not kill them with the first shot and instead exposed their position all six of them would probably be finished.

Max sighed and reluctantly gave up on the idea.

Max raised his binoculars one final time, intending to take one last look at the big fish.

However just as he trained the lenses on the seemingly leading mage the man in the robe seemed to sense something. He suddenly turned his head and his gaze shot straight toward the hillside where they were hiding.

Although they were four hundred meters apart Max felt as if he were being stared at by a ferocious beast and the hairs on his body stood on end.

*"Withdraw! Quickly!"*

Max bolted down the slope without any concern for posture, grabbed his bicycle and called for the others to move.

Although Baumann and the others were confused, seeing Max's expression they immediately sprang into action.

The group of six quickly mounted their bikes and sped off back the way they had come without looking back.

---

Meanwhile on the high ground outside Seville.

Eldridge, a high-ranking instructor of the British Highland Mage Order, was frowning slightly as he looked at the unremarkable hill in the distance.

Just now he had vaguely sensed someone watching him.

That gaze carried a faint hint of killing intent, fleeting but caught by his keen mental focus.

Were they scouts from the National Army? Or were they the Germans?

Eldridge was pondering whether to cast a third-ring Eagle Eye spell to scout the situation when a messenger from the Royal Army rushed over and whispered something to General José Sanjurjo, commander of the 24th Division.

General Sanjurjo's face darkened visibly, his brow furrowed with anger.

*"Is it that Juan who caused trouble again?"*

A voice tinged with disdain and impatience came from beside him. It was High Instructor Eldridge.

He had not needed to listen carefully. The messenger's whispers reached his ears perfectly clearly.

A hint of embarrassment flashed across General Sanjurjo's face.

Juan had caused him a great deal of trouble over the years but considering the large sums of money Juan gave him annually he had always turned a blind eye.

But what time was it now. With a major battle imminent, the rebels, the International Brigades and the main German force could be at the city gates at any moment and this idiot was still causing a scandal in the city.

*"There's been a small disturbance in the city,"* General Sanjurjo said to Eldridge, barely suppressing his anger. *"I'll send men to bring that good-for-nothing back and give him a dressing down."*

General Sanjurjo immediately ordered the guards around him to lead a squad of soldiers to bring Lieutenant Colonel Juan de Rivera and his companions back.

Not long after several officers leading a squad of Royal Army soldiers arrived at the two-story building.

They kicked open the front door and barged in.

The captain leading the way cursed and headed for the stairs. He just wanted to find Juan as soon as possible and get back to report to the general.

But when he stepped onto the third step he felt something brush against his foot.

A round black sphere rolled down from the gap in the steps and came to rest at his feet, accompanied by a faint hissing sound.

The hallway was dim. The captain could not make out what was under his feet. He even bent down to pick it up.

Death hovered over him for three and a half seconds.

**BOOM!**

A spherical grenade of German manufacture exploded in the narrow stairwell.

The shockwave and flying debris engulfed the stairwell instantly.

The captain and the two soldiers behind him collapsed without uttering a sound.

The soldiers further back were stunned by the blast, their ears ringing.

When they finally got to their feet they saw their companions on the ground and scrambled out of the building.

The explosion on the street caused panic. Residents nearby screamed and ran while the Royal Army soldiers nervously raised their guns and surrounded the small building.

Shortly after a Highland Mage apprentice who had heard the commotion arrived at the scene with several attendants.

*"Step aside!"*

He pushed past the soldiers blocking the doorway and looked through the open door at the horrific scene on the stairs, his brow furrowed.

After hesitating for a moment he used a specific gesture combined with a short incantation and a circle of pale blue light spread out from him.

```
First-Circle Spell — Life Perception
```

When the spell ended he lowered his hand and said to the soldiers around him.

*"There is no sign of life on the second floor. There are a few on the first floor but they are in very bad condition."*

Several soldiers finally mustered their courage and rushed inside, dragging out the still groaning wounded.

The young apprentice mage accompanied by his attendants stepped over the bloodstains and went inside.

Looking at the three Royal Army soldiers who had died in the stairwell a look of disgust flashed across the mage apprentice's face. He directed the soldiers to clear away the bodies then went up to the second floor with the others.

In the second-floor corridor three tightly closed doors waited in silence.

The Royal Army soldiers hesitated. No one knew what lurked behind the doors.

*"There's no one alive inside,"* the mage apprentice said from behind them, his voice tinged with impatience.

The soldiers cautiously stepped forward.

Without a word the mage apprentice quietly cast a spell on himself and a layer of almost invisible magical aura enveloped his entire body.

```
Second-Circle Spell — Mage Armor
```

The three soldiers walked toward the three doors, glanced at each other and then pushed them open simultaneously.

The anticipated explosion did not occur.

The sight in the rooms made everyone gasp.

In the two rooms on the left and right a dead officer lay in a pool of blood, each death gruesome.

In the largest room in the middle two corpses were entangled in an extremely indecent position. One of them was Lieutenant Colonel Juan.

*"Something's happened! Report to the general immediately!"* an officer shouted.

A soldier scrambled downstairs.

After carefully examining the rest of the rooms the mage apprentice and the soldiers entered the central room.

The air was thick with a strange mixture of blood, alcohol and cologne.

*"Separate them,"* the mage apprentice said, pinching his nose and giving the soldiers a disgusted order.

Two soldiers stepped forward and struggled to pull Juan's body away from the other officer.

Just as they were about to fully separate the two stiff corpses they felt several thin threads snap.

A series of dense hissing sounds filled the room. No one could identify what they were.

When the two bodies were finally separated seven round lumpy iron objects slid out from between them and rolled across the floor with a clanging sound.

Everyone in the room stared wide-eyed.

The young mage apprentice's face drained of all color and only one thought remained in his mind.

*Run.*

But it was too late.

**BOOM!!**

The countless fragments generated by the simultaneous explosion of the seven grenades easily breached the defensive threshold of his first-circle spell.

The shockwave at close range blasted the apprentice away, his body riddled with shrapnel as he slammed heavily against the wall, losing consciousness instantly and dying shortly after.

When the second explosion tore through the building Eldridge and General Sanjurjo, who had just arrived outside, watched flames and thick smoke gush from the second-floor windows.

Looking at the expression on the high instructor's face General Sanjurjo felt a chill run down his spine.

The death of his own rogue officers was nothing to him. The vacant positions would simply attract other buyers.

But the death of a Highland Mage Order apprentice, even a junior one, was not a problem that could be easily resolved.

*"Seal off the entire city! Search every house! Arrest anyone suspicious!"*

General Sanjurjo practically roared the order.

He needed to catch the killers as soon as possible and give the British an explanation before Eldridge's mood deteriorated further.

The order mobilized the entire Royal Army in Seville, focusing every man on searching for suspicious individuals within the city.

Which made the evacuation of Max and his group considerably smoother than it had any right to be.

When Max and his reconnaissance squad returned to the temporary camp of 1st Battalion, 33rd Infantry Regiment it was already getting dark.

They did not even have time to drink water before they rushed to the battalion command post.

Major Thomas and the other officers were studying something on a map. They breathed a visible sigh of relief when they saw Max and the others return safely.

Not long after Captain Hauser arrived at the command post after receiving the news. Seeing that Max, Corporal Baumann and the others were all unharmed he went up to them and gave each one a bear hug.

*"So, how's the reconnaissance going? Lieutenant Maximilian, have you spotted any enemy ambushes?"*

Major Thomas quickly cut to what concerned him most.

*"Reporting, sir. Based on our reconnaissance along the way the enemy did not set up an ambush but retreated entirely into the city of Seville."*

Max paused for a moment.

*"So we went to Seville."*

The command post fell silent instantly.

Major Thomas and Captain Hauser both stared. They looked at each other with identical expressions.

*"I asked you to scout the surrounding area and check for ambushes. How the hell did you go straight into the city? Do you think this is a trip to a rural market?"*

Major Thomas felt as if he were listening to a fantastical story.

After the morning's battle Captain Hauser had placed great trust in Max but even he could not help himself.

*"Lieutenant, do you know what you're saying?"*

*"Sir, of course I know what I'm talking about."*

Max did not explain much. Max gave a brief report of what he had seen and heard along the way: the Royal Army's defensive deployment on the high ground outside the city, the existence of mana-crystal cannons, the 24th Division and the British garrisoning situation and their encounters inside the city.

After hearing Max's account Thomas and Hauser's expressions grew even more complicated.

Just as Major Thomas was about to raise his questions Max took out an officer's identification document and a silver officer's badge from his pocket and placed them on the table.

*"This is the identification of Lieutenant Colonel Juan de Rivera, commander of the 1st Battalion, 126th Regiment of the Kingdom of Spain."*

Then Max took a finely crafted revolver from Corporal Baumann and placed it on the table as well.

*"This is his officer's sidearm."*

All doubts inside the command post were silenced by these two items.

Major Thomas picked up the blood-stained officer's identification and flipped through it.

Then Major Thomas picked up the revolver, a British-pattern officer's sidearm supplied to the Spanish army as part of British arms aid.

All remaining doubts vanished at that moment.

Major Thomas looked up and re-examined the young lieutenant in front of him, realizing he had never truly known him at all.

After a moment the air in the command post began to circulate again.

Major Thomas and Captain Hauser exchanged a glance, both seeing the same thing in each other's eyes.

The young lieutenant in front of them was not only quick to react and adaptable but his professional skills were excessive, surpassing veterans who had served for many years. Even setting aside the morning battle entirely, the intelligence and proof of kills they had brought back from deep behind enemy lines were far more valuable than either had anticipated.

At this point Major Thomas's feelings toward Max had shifted from simple admiration to undisguised high regard.

Captain Hauser standing to one side wore an expression that spoke for itself.

*"Cough."*

Major Thomas cleared his throat, interrupting Hauser's burning gaze, and turned to Max.

*"Lieutenant Maximilian, mark the information you've gathered on the map."*

*"Yes, sir."*

Without the slightest hesitation Max took the pencil handed to him by a staff officer.

Rather than immediately working on the large map in the command post he first took out a hard-covered notebook and a folded map from the inside pocket of his uniform.

Even though the system map was still visible in his field of vision Max followed procedure. In accordance with German Army map-based operational standards he had already copied down the key information from the system map during the reconnaissance itself.

This also perfectly explained how he could remember such a vast amount of enemy troop deployment detail without inviting unnecessary suspicion. If he had simply reconstructed it all from thin air Major Thomas and the others would have had good reason to doubt him.

Max spread out his map and notes then began marking the larger battle map at the command post with a pencil.

Red symbols representing enemy firing points and troop concentrations quickly took shape under his pen.

As more and more red markers appeared on the map Major Thomas and the others grew increasingly serious.

Everyone fell silent, especially when Max drew out the mana-crystal cannon positions on the high ground outside the city.

Senior officers knew that British mana-crystal cannons had an effective range of approximately 4,500 meters.

The positions Max had marked covered most of the area where they would be launching their attack and the destructive effect of mana-crystal fire on infantry concentrations was well understood.

After Max finished marking the map all the officers in the battalion gathered around it and said nothing for a long time.

The defenses of the Royal Army and the British expeditionary force in Seville were far more formidable than they had anticipated.

Major Thomas stared at the map, his brow furrowed. After a while he looked up at Max.

Max knew it was his turn.

*"Sir, I think we shouldn't rush into an attack."*

Max's voice was not loud but it was exceptionally clear in the quiet command post.

*"The enemy's situation is very clear. They are tightening their defenses and relying on the city and those mana-crystal cannons, hoping to make us run headlong into them."*

Max's finger traced across the dense red zone on the map.

*"If we attack rashly now, even if we eventually take Seville, our casualties and those of our allies will be considerable. If the enemy launches an effective counterattack it will be uncertain whether we can hold on."*

The argument was clearly organized and logically sound.

Any rational commander looking at that map would have to seriously consider the possibility. Major Thomas's expression showed he had been persuaded.

Max pressed on.

*"Sir, after this morning's battle, I'm sure you don't want the troops to repeat the same mistake."*

That last sentence finished it.

Of course the reason Max went to so much trouble was not entirely for grand tactical considerations.

Max had his own selfish motives.

Having seen Seville's defenses firsthand he knew all too well that the upcoming siege would be a bloodbath. Max had no intention of sacrificing his life for the glory of the German Empire. He had been in this world for less than twenty-four hours. If the fight could be postponed by one day it should be. If it could be avoided entirely that would be better still.

What Max was doing now was everything he could to delay an attack that was almost certain to produce enormous casualties.

*"You're right."*

After a long silence Major Thomas finally spoke, letting out a heavy sigh.

Major Thomas had been persuaded but a hint of helplessness settled on his face.

*"But Lieutenant Maximilian, I am just a battalion commander. Whether or not to attack is not a decision I can make."*

Major Thomas paced back and forth in the command post a few times then made up his mind.

*"Come with me. We're going to regimental headquarters, then brigade."*

Major Thomas lightly punched the map with his fist.

*"Whether we can persuade the regimental commander and the brigade commander is another matter. But at the very least we must let them know what the situation in Seville looks like right now."*

Major Thomas looked at Max, his eyes filled with an unprecedented gravity.

*"Get ready. We're leaving right away."*

Soon Major Thomas selected several skilled horsemen as guards and together with Max the group galloped off toward brigade headquarters.

Hooves flew and dust rose up behind them.

By the time Max and Major Thomas arrived at the headquarters of the 32nd Infantry Regiment it was already dusk.

Without delay Major Thomas found the regimental commander and immediately gave a succinct account of the situation's gravity.

After hearing the report and examining the evidence Max had brought back the commander of the 32nd Infantry Regiment looked extremely grim.

The colonel made a decisive move and personally led both men to the headquarters of the 16th Infantry Brigade, located in the core of the camp.

The atmosphere inside the brigade headquarters command tent was serious.

When Max stood before the enormous battle map once more he noticed that this brigade-level map was considerably more detailed than the battalion map. Some red enemy symbols had already been placed around the outskirts of Seville, indicating that Max's team had not been the only one sent out on reconnaissance.

Before the brigade commander, brigade staff officers and several liaison officers from the National Army and the International Brigades, Max once again demonstrated his map skills and marked up the intelligence he had gathered.

Max then recounted in detail what he had seen and heard in Seville, from the enemy's defensive deployments to the state of the soldiers' discipline.

Finally Max offered his suggestion again, hoping the command would consider carefully and postpone the attack.

Brigadier General Karl Paul, commander of the 16th Infantry Brigade, finally spoke. He was a man who looked to be in his forties with a resolute face.

*"No wonder Lieutenant General Mackensen assigned you to the military observer group. I initially thought it was some kind of shady nepotism, but now it seems you do have some real skills."*

Upon hearing this Max was momentarily at a loss for words.

Max knew better than anyone that his inclusion in the observer group had nothing to do with his abilities. Lieutenant General Mackensen had wanted to develop him purely because he was his close friend's son. In a sense he really had benefited from nepotism.

Max had no time to dwell on that. He stood quietly and waited for the brigade commander's decision.

*"The intelligence you brought back is very valuable, Lieutenant Maximilian."*

Brigadier General Paul looked carefully at the markings on the map, his tone filled with approval.

*"This information will be of great help to our next steps."*

Hearing this Max's ominous feeling grew stronger.

That did not sound like a cancellation.

Brigadier General Paul's next words confirmed it.

*"However, the offensive plan will not change."*

Not only Max but also Major Thomas and the 32nd Regiment commander beside him changed expression.

*"That's right, sir!"*

One of the International Brigades liaison officers stepped forward, emotions running high.

*"We must seize this opportunity while British reinforcements are still establishing themselves and take Seville in one move. This is the key to liberating all of Andalusia. As Lieutenant Maximilian just said the Royal Army is wreaking havoc in Seville. We cannot wait any longer."*

The National Army liaison officer immediately agreed.

*"Yes. The longer we drag this out the stronger the enemy's defenses will become."*

Major Thomas and the 33rd Regiment commander both objected, arguing that launching a direct attack with insufficient preparation was too risky.

However several brigade staff officers argued that the enemy information was now detailed enough, especially with the mana-crystal cannon positions identified. Based on previous encounters with the British in overseas colonies their mana-crystal cannons had a range of under 5 kilometers. The field artillery regiment currently attached to the 16th Infantry Brigade, whether 77mm cannon or 105mm howitzer, had a range that far exceeded that. It was entirely possible to destroy the mana-crystal cannon positions before the infantry advance began. Once that support was gone the remaining Royal Army and British infantry would not be difficult to deal with.

The two sides held their positions but in the end Brigadier General Paul made the decision, announcing in an irrefutable tone that the attack on Seville would proceed.

The argument inside the tent ended immediately.

Major Thomas looked at Max and gave a slight shake of his head.

Max was marginally flustered but mostly unbothered. This was the way that things were. Whether it was pride or general foolishness he knew better than to get upset at things he could not control.

Max was well aware that the pro-war staff officers were not wrong. With detailed intelligence and superior artillery range the offensive was feasible. Casualties were inevitable either way. Nothing could be changed.

This impromptu conference ended in a resolute and oppressive atmosphere.

Leaving the brigade headquarters tent Max made one last effort, asking after Lieutenant General Mackensen.

He was told that Mackensen had already boarded a military train that afternoon and returned to the expeditionary force headquarters in the rear.

The last option was gone.

Max and Major Thomas rode back to camp side by side in silence, neither speaking a word.

When they returned to 1st Battalion's camp they were greeted by faces full of concern and expectation.

Captain Hauser was the first to step forward.

*"How's it going, Major? What did brigade say?"*

Major Thomas shook his head wearily and said nothing.

The officers surrounding battalion headquarters felt a chill when they saw the commander's expression.

*"The offensive plan remains unchanged,"* Major Thomas said, answering everyone at once.

*"The official order from brigade will arrive in an hour or two. We move into our attack positions in the latter half of the night and launch the attack on Seville tomorrow morning."*

Max knew he had done everything he could. Individual effort was insignificant against the weight of a massive war machine and the will of those above it. The only thing left was to lead his platoon and try to keep as many of them alive as possible.

*"Go back and rest."*

Major Thomas's voice was firm and resolute.

*"You are all soldiers of the German Empire. Our duty is to obey orders and fight for the Empire. Get yourselves in good spirits because we have another tough battle tomorrow. Do you understand?"*

*"Yes, sir!"*

The officers snapped to attention and answered loudly.

---

At the headquarters of the 16th Infantry Brigade most of the people had already left the main tent, leaving only the brigade commander and his chief of staff.

*"You actually had reservations about this attack, didn't you?"*

The chief of staff looked at Brigadier General Paul's tired profile and asked quietly.

*"Why did you ultimately decide to go ahead?"*

Brigadier General Paul was silent for a long time. He let out a long sigh and said nothing. Instead he turned and took out a telegram from a nearby file box, one that had arrived before the meeting, and handed it to the chief of staff.

*"See for yourself."*

It was a telegram from the Imperial General Staff.

The telegram was simple and direct, ordering the 16th Infantry Brigade to capture Seville within a specified window, in coordination with friendly forces, regardless of cost, and to ensure it remained firmly under their control.

While the chief of staff was still absorbing the weight of that order his gaze fell on the final paragraph.

That line stopped him cold.

The General Staff had ordered the Zeppelin-class armored airship L30, currently on standby in Mediterranean airspace, to provide fire support after completing its at-sea replenishment.

*"Armored airship."*

The chief of staff's hand holding the telegram trembled slightly.

He had never imagined the General Staff attached such importance to Seville.

They had committed an armored airship, one of the empire's strategic weapons, to this operation.

A true aerial behemoth, capable of hovering above the enemy and unleashing a torrent of firepower.

Brigadier General Paul sat back down in his chair and rubbed his temples.

*"For me this was not a choice. It was a death order."*

The chief of staff gently placed the telegram back on the table. All his doubts resolved into a complex mixture of excitement and awe.

*"I understand, sir. But why Seville? What is it about this city?"*

*"The newly discovered Aetherium vein is located underground near Seville."*

The chief of staff immediately understood and stopped asking questions.

Brigadier General Paul looked up at the top of the tent as if he could see through the canvas and into the night sky where a storm was about to break.

After a moment he glanced at the time on his pocket watch.

*"Two hours from now have the messengers deliver combat orders to each battalion. Brigade headquarters and all directly subordinate units are to prepare to move forward."*

---

Max returned to 3rd Company's position. Captain Hauser followed him.

*"Max, don't overthink it. Sometimes that's just how soldiers are. We follow orders."*

*"I know, Captain."*

*"Good."* Hauser patted him on the back. *"You've done enough today. Go get some sleep."*

Max nodded, bid farewell to Captain Hauser and returned to his tent.

Max sat down on his cot and finally had a moment alone. A stack of system notifications had been queued in his peripheral vision all day, blinking patiently. Max opened them now.

The first stopped him.

```
Level threshold reached
Level 0 → Level 1
Stat increase applied
Cantrips unlocked (not enabled — no spellbook)
1st ring spells unlocked (not enabled — no spellbook)
```

*Huh.*

Thinking back on it, the ride back had felt marginally easier than the ride in, even with the adrenaline of nearly being spotted by Eldridge still working through his system. He had not thought much of it at the time.

```
Kill confirmed — Juan de Rivera
Lt. Colonel, 24th Infantry Division, Kingdom of Spain
Method: bayonet
XP awarded: 125
```

```
Kill confirmed — Royal Army soldiers x6
Method: boobytrap (Kugelhandgranate)
XP awarded: 150
```

```
Kill confirmed — Highland Mage Order apprentice, British Empire
Method: boobytrap (Kugelhandgranate)
XP awarded: 525
```

```
Total XP: 800
Level: 1
Next level at: 900 XP
```

Max looked at that last entry for a moment.

Five hundred and twenty-five XP for one apprentice mage. He filed that away.

The next notification was different in character.

```
Druid Inheritance — passive trait active
Circle of the Land: Natural Recovery
Sustained by the land and the air — faster physical and spiritual recovery.
Does not function in mana-voided zones.
```

Max smiled and appreciated this system before moving on to the next notification.

The final notification was the airship.

```
Friendly strategic unit detected — analyzing
German Imperial Air Force — Zeppelin-class armored airship — L30
Status: Undergoing at-sea resupply
Estimated arrival time: 15 hours 21 minutes
```

Max closed the notifications.

He lay back on his cot. As his body settled something subtle began to happen. A gentle energy rose through the earth beneath him and gathered from the air around him, working quietly into his limbs and easing the deep ache in his shoulders and legs.

Max was asleep before he had time to think about any of it.
