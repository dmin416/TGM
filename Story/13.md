# Chapter 13

Arc: Seville Counterattack

Date: 7 February 1914

---

Northern districts of Seville saw the Royal Army's second assault open with roaring confidence and end in near silence.

General José Sanjurjo waited at the provisional command post for good news from the front. Courier after courier brought him only bad news.

*"Report, General! 124th Battalion is blocked on Sun Avenue! Casualties are heavy! They request permission to withdraw!"*

*"126th Battalion has three companies cut off! Flank fire from an unknown source has scattered them!"*

*"General! We still cannot force a way in! Those Germans are like rats in a field! They appear from nowhere and we cannot hit them!"*

General Sanjurjo listened to the couriers until his head rang.

He could not understand it.

The northern districts had been practically buried under high explosive magic crystal shells. Why could his men still not enter the city?

The buildings had collapsed. Were the Germans made of metal? Could shells not kill them?

General Sanjurjo didn't know that when Royal Army soldiers picked their way over rubble and ruins into deeper streets they found the same scene as the first assault.

Barbed wire still lay in dense tangles. Obstacles of every strange shape still blocked the lanes. Bullets still struck from every direction.

German soldiers seemed endless. They poured fire from every gap in the ruins and every broken window onto the attacking Royal Army.

This time field guns joined the defense.

Veteran Royal Army soldiers and officers who had seen hard fighting had never expected to find field guns aimed towards them at the end of a street.

The flash of light came before they could hear the roar of the shell.

Old shrapnel rounds seemed to want one last hurrah before leaving the stage.

The deadly spray swept the street like a wall. Royal Army soldiers might not keep their bodies whole.

Many soldiers panicked and tried to hide inside buildings. The 77 millimeter field gun fired fixed ammunition at high speed. Sustained fire soon taught them that hiding indoors was a mistake.

After the crews switched to high explosive shells the gunners used direct fire sights on the windows where soldiers had taken cover.

The second shot sent a 77 millimeter high explosive shell through a window to detonate inside.

The second assault ended in another rout.

Royal Army units on every axis of attack left hundreds of corpses and still more wounded who could not move. They fell back in disorder again.

General Sanjurjo paced the command tent like a bull driven to fury. Curses poured from his mouth without pause.

*"Worthless! All of them worthless! So many men and you cannot take one broken city!"*

The other officers in the tent kept their heads down. None of them dared breathe loud.

The tent flap flew open with a violent jerk.

Cold air rushed in at once.

Highland Mage Corps senior instructor Eldridge entered with two other mages and several British officers. Every face was blank.

Eldridge's gaze drove toward General Sanjurjo like a blade.

*"General Sanjurjo, I want you to explain why our last magic crystal battery was struck by enemy cavalry."*

General Sanjurjo's stomach dropped. He had not expected the British to arrive this quickly.

The report of the battery under attack frightened him more because he had not known his last artillery was already gone.

*"Sir, the magic crystal battery was in the rear, was it not?"*

Eldridge answered without expression.

*"It was in the rear. You ordered it to fire like a fool and gave German cavalry the chance to attack."*

Cold sweat ran down General Sanjurjo's brow. He forced his voice steady.

*"Eldridge, this was an accident. I did not expect enemy cavalry to"*

*"Accident?"*

Eldridge cut him off. Mockery finally edged his tone.

*"You sent every soldier forward and left not one man to guard our most important long range fire. You call that an accident?"*

Color surged and drained from General Sanjurjo's face.

He wanted to answer and found no words.

He alone had insisted on the order that destroyed the battery.

*"My companions and I arrived in time and destroyed that damned cavalry regiment. That changes nothing."*

Eldridge's eyes swept the Royal Army officers in the tent. Each man felt ice along his spine.

*"We have lost our last means of long range suppression. The fighting ahead will grow harder."*

General Sanjurjo squirmed under that stare. He spoke through gritted teeth.

*"Sir, rest assured. In the next assault I will"*

*"You?"*

A British colonel beside Eldridge laughed aloud.

He looked at General Sanjurjo with open contempt and spoke to Eldridge in English.

*"Sir, I would not rely on these Spanish hicks. They waste time and send men to die. They accomplish nothing else."*

Several Royal Army officers understood English. Humiliation and anger showed on every face.

General Sanjurjo shook with rage. His fists clenched until the knuckles cracked.

He did not dare answer.

He knew the colonel spoke the truth.

He also could not afford to offend the British.

*"Colonel Henry, mind your tongue."*

Eldridge spoke flatly. Whether rebuke or warning no one could tell.

He turned back to General Sanjurjo. His tone left no room for argument.

*"General, the next assault will be led by us."*

General Sanjurjo's lips moved. At last he bowed his head in defeat.

*"Yes, sir."*

He knew command of the battle had passed from his hands from that moment.

Colonel Henry smiled with satisfaction. He addressed the Royal Army officers behind him in rough Spanish.

*"Watch closely, bumpkins. Next you will see how real war is fought."*

He turned and left the tent.

The new assault began soon after.

The Northumberland Fusiliers marched at the head in neat ranks and full kit.

Eight white steel giants of the Order of the Garter followed. Each armored knight pressed down the street with crushing presence.

Royal Army soldiers came last on the pretext of clearing the battlefield.

*"Platoon leader! Look! Enemy armored knights!"*

Hans shouted the warning into Max's ear.

Max lay on a rooftop beside Hans with binoculars raised. He swung them in the direction Hans pointed.

Several white armored knights advanced toward the German line with a mass of British infantry at an unhurried pace.

Sunlight struck their smooth armor and threw glare into the defenders' eyes.

Many knights still carried sword and shield as before. Others had taken tower shields, war hammers and similar arms.

Max knew expecting no more armored knights was unrealistic but had hoped the enemy had none left to send.

His street fighting plan worked well against infantry. Against these steel monsters it looked feeble.

*"Warn every section! Enemy armor is on the street! Do not stand and fight! Fall back to the reserve line and leave these machines to our armored knights!"*

Max slid to the roof edge and called down to the messengers waiting below. They scattered at once to carry the order to every position.

On the city map in Max's mind the Order of the Garter armored knights pushed along separate streets under cover of squires and infantry.

Deeper in the city the Teutonic Knights markers stirred as other sentries passed the word. The formation split into six smaller icons.

Max's order could not reach every defender. Many soldiers hidden in buildings fired anyway when they saw the armored knights. Fear drove the triggers.

Rifle and machine gun fire poured over the eight armored knights like rain.

The attempts accomplished nothing.

Bullets struck the armor and threw up sparks too small to matter. They left no scratch.

The armored knights did not slow. They kept a steady pace forward.

They moved like the invulnerable beasts of legend. Every mortal weapon looked insignificant before them.

**Boom!**

One impatient 105mm howitzer crew fired a direct shot at an armored knight. The knight raised a heavy tower shield and sheltered the infantry behind him.

The 105 mm shell burst on the shield and deeply gouged the plating. The knight inside kept moving and the machine never lost step.

The knight fired back once from the gun mount on his other arm. The return round wrecked the howitzer barrel and bent the muzzle toward the ground.

British soldiers behind the armored knights broke into laughter when they saw it.

*"See that? This is the German resistance? How pitiful."*

*"Like throwing pebbles at a castle."*

German soldiers in the ruins watched and despair spread over their faces.

Their bullets could not pierce the armor. The enemy could tear down the cover they lived behind with ease.

One armored knight reached the first street barricade. It did not use a weapon. A massive mechanical leg kicked once.

The barricade of timber, doors and brick collapsed at once.

The machine drove through the wreckage and crushed the barbed wire and debris under its treads.

Another armored knight halted before a three story building the International Brigades held under Andrei after he left the rear workshop.

The armored knight raised a huge war hammer and struck the load bearing wall.

**Boom rumble!**

The wall opened a gap wide enough for a cart.

More blows followed.

The building groaned under the strain and shook hard.

*"Pull back! The building is coming down!"*

Andrei roared inside until his voice tore. International Brigade soldiers fled through the rear door before they could throw firebombs.

They had barely cleared the street when the three story structure collapsed in another thunderous crash. Slow runners vanished under the rubble completely.

The steady advance of the armored knights lifted the spirits of the British infantry behind them.

Without a counter to the armored knights the battle seemed decided in their favor.

Victory looked like a matter of time.

Eight armored knights spread along the street to help the infantry sweep the district clear.

But before all hope was lost, armored knights appeared at the far end of the street.

Black and white Teutonic crosses marked the German Empire machines.

*"Oh? Willing to show yourselves at last?"*

Order of the Garter detachment commander Colonel Marcus sat in the cockpit of his St. George Type 3 armored knight. He watched the distant Teutonic machine and curled his mouth in a cold smile.

He felt no worry.

Battle reports had already told him the Teutonic Knights retained only six armored knights after the last fight.

Eight against six removed the enemy's greatest numerical edge.

The narrow city street mattered more.

That ground denied the Teutonic Knights their preferred swarm tactics.

As for single combat?

The Order of the Garter did not fear any rival armored knight in a duel.

Colonel Marcus believed he could overturn those crude German machines with one hand.

The St. George Type 3 was the newest pure magitech armored knight in service. Armor alloy, protective enchantment and magitech power core all outclassed the German Siegfried series.

Even against the French Charlemagne Type 2 it held the advantage.

Technology outran courage on that scale.

*"All units, attack at will. Destroy them."*

Colonel Marcus gave the order through the communication stone built into the St. George Type 3 cockpit. The stones allowed short range communications on a single channel though at enormous cost.

Intelligence claimed the Germans were trying to miniaturize radio sets for armored knights. So far the effort had not succeeded.

*"Yes, sir!"*

*"Let them taste the Order of the Garter!"*

*"I cannot wait to tear them apart!"*

Confident replies filled the channel.

Order of the Garter armored knights fanned into the side streets and closed on the enemy for the victory that would end a tedious battle.

When they drew near something felt wrong.

The Teutonic armored knights came forward to meet them.

Besides the shields their other hands carried a strange weapon.

It was not the usual mace or war axe. It looked like a lance topped with a metal cone.

The whole thing looked handmade in a village forge. Paint lay uneven on the metal.

*"Ha! What is that thing? A poker for the hearth?"*

*"I thought it was a manure fork."*

*"Have the Germans gone mad? They mean to fight us with scrap?"*

Pilots in the Order of the Garter cockpits laughed at the sight.

The weapon looked like an insult.

Colonel Marcus frowned. Doubt nagged at him.

He dismissed the thought. Perhaps the Germans had run out of proper arms.

His machine closed on the armored knight Ludwig drove.

Colonel Marcus sent the challenge through his external speaker with aristocratic arrogance in his voice.

*"Teutonic Knight, state your name. I do not slay the nameless."*

Ludwig answered from his own speaker with the courtesy owed between armored knights.

*"Colonel Ludwig von Seeckt of the Teutonic Knights."*

He ranged the close range shaped charge armor piercing round Max had named along the shield edge. He drove his machine forward at once.

Colonel Marcus lifted the great armed sword on his St. George Type 3. He meant to cut the limbs from the German machine with superior sword work.

Heavy footsteps rumbled along the street. Every eye on the block fixed on the two steel giants.

At the instant before contact Colonel Marcus struck first with the sword.

Ludwig, taut with nerves, used experience to parry the armed sword with his shield.

Then he followed Max's instruction. He thrust the nose of the shaped charge round to within about twenty centimeters of the enemy cockpit and fired.

The mechanical fuze detonated the TNT filling in the warhead. The blast was slower than modern high explosives yet the detonation wave still exceeded seven thousand meters per second and drove the copper liner at the nose.

Heat and pressure in that instant turned the copper into a jet of molten metal.

Limits of charge energy and imperfect initiation kept the jet from the sharpest needle point. Even so the stream broke past five times the speed of sound in that moment.

The armored knight's emergency shield could not hold against that intensity. It shattered like a soap bubble.

The supersonic heated lance struck the main face plate on a near straight line.

Momentum and pressure at the jet head vaporized armor at the contact point like a hot knife through butter and opened a tiny hole.

**Boom!**

Dazzling fire bloomed between the two armored knights.

The blast warped the air around them.

Squires and British soldiers behind Colonel Marcus did not understand what had happened.

They felt a violent release of energy between the two machines. Momentum from the charge drove both armored knights into a collision.

When the British soldiers found their feet again they saw a sight they would never forget.

The white St. George Type 3 of the Order of the Garter stopped all motion at once.

The armed sword still hung ready for a second thrust.

Then, before every stunned witness, the huge machine toppled straight backward.

**Boom rumble!**

The hull struck the pavement and threw dust into the air.

The street fell silent.

Everyone stood frozen. Minds went blank.

No one on the street could say what had happened.

No one on the street could say why Colonel Marcus's machine had fallen.

Before shock could clear the black and white armored knight dropped the strange lance. He took the great mace from his back.

He charged the dumbstruck infantry.

Cold killing intent covered them all at once.

Similar scenes played out on the other main streets of Seville at the same time.

Colonel Marcus's armored knight had fallen with shocking suddenness. Order of the Garter pilots who had split up to hunt single combat on other streets did not know what they would face.

On another road an Order of the Garter pilot drove his armored knight forward with full confidence. He struck his shield with his armed sword as he advanced.

The pilot's fighting spirit ran high.

He joked with the infantry around him through the external speaker.

*"Men, watch closely! A duel between armored knights is a rare sight."*

The white St. George Type 3 picked up speed and closed on the enemy.

Like their detachment commander Colonel Marcus the Order of the Garter pilots carried pride and confidence of their own.

That confidence came from the order's fearsome record and from Britain's lead in armored knight technology.

Every Order of the Garter pilot believed he would not lose a one on one fight with the enemy.

At the instant before the two steel hulls would collide the pilot saw the plain iron rod in the enemy hand erupt with blinding light at the nose.

Then he knew nothing more.

The black and white Siegfried Type 1 sidestepped with a clean turn and left the road open.

The St. George Type 3 bore a new hole in its front armor. Inertia carried it several more steps before the machine sank to its knees. Thick smoke poured from the cockpit.

Another St. George Type 3 pilot nearby had noticed the communication channel grow oddly quiet.

He did not dwell on it. His own fight demanded his full attention.

Superior machine performance and skilled handling let him catch the Siegfried Type 1's charge on his shield. He opened the distance he wanted for an armed sword cut.

As he prepared to strike back he saw the strange weapon driven straight against the outside of his cockpit.

*What in hell is that thing?*

The thought was his last.

In a short span the Order of the Garter lost four armored knights to improvised shaped charge rounds.

The elite of the empire died without grasping how they had been beaten.

Four armored knights fell in thunderous succession. The infantry and squires beside them fell into panic and disorder.

Their greatest shield had been torn away in an instant like paper. The shock to eye and nerve was ruinous.

Once the greatest enemy armored knights were gone the Teutonic Knights drivers showed no mercy. They crossed the wrecks and charged into the infantry formations.

Infantry without armored cover took terrible losses when they met armored knights head on.

The rushed handmade weapons could not all meet the same standard.

Of the six Teutonic armored knights in the attack four destroyed their targets in the first pass.

Two shaped charge rounds failed in the hands of their carriers.

One Teutonic pilot triggered his round and heard only a low crump at the nose with a weak flash instead of a full blast.

The blast fell far short of full power. A frightful dent and melted scarring marked the enemy armor but the plate held.

*"Damn it! Curse my luck."*

The pilot cursed inside the cockpit.

His opponent recoiled two steps in fright at the flash.

The Teutonic pilot seized the breathing space without hesitation.

He dropped the dead round and pulled a spare shaped charge armor piercing round from the temporary rack on the hull's rear. Ludwig had ordered extra rounds built for just such a misfire.

*"Again!"*

He roared and drove his armored knight forward once more.

The Order of the Garter pilot facing him was gripped by doubt and fear.

He did not know what the last strike had been. He knew only that it could kill.

When the enemy charged again with the same iron rod in hand he raised his shield and braced for the blow.

This time the Siegfried Type 1 moved faster and with more cunning.

The heavy hull feinted and fooled the shield parry. The shaped charge round struck the side armor squarely.

**Boom!**

Fire flared bright again. Fortune did not spare the St. George Type 3 a second time.

The machine groaned under the hit. Side armor gave way completely. The molten jet shredded everything inside.

The Teutonic pilot watched the enemy armored knight topple and drew breath to pull back toward his comrades.

Heavy footsteps from the flank rang an alarm in his mind.

Another white St. George Type 3 rounded the street corner and blocked his path.

*"You will not escape, German savage!"*

The pilot did not know what weapon had killed his comrade. He did not turn aside. He raised his weapon and charged.

The Teutonic knight had spent his shaped charge rounds. One on one against a fresh angry enemy he soon lost ground.

After three exchanges the more agile St. George Type 3 circled behind him. An enchanted armed sword pierced the combustion engine at the rear.

In armored knight combat that blow counted as death.

The short brutal exchange left four black and white Siegfried Type 1 machines in the city streets against three white St. George Type 3 machines.

The armored count stood at four against three.

The Teutonic Knights held the numbers again.

Ludwig and his pilots still had several shaped charge rounds between them.

The Order of the Garter had lost five armored knights in a flash. The survivors lost their nerve.

The three remaining pilots stared at the Teutonic Knights like avatars of slaughter and at the strange lances in their hands. All three chose to pull back.

The order had never suffered a bloodier day in its history.

With the two machines Ludwig had destroyed on the heights earlier in the day the Order of the Garter had lost seven of its newest St. George Type 3 armored knights in a single day.

For an order that had known only victory and called itself the spear of the empire the shame was without precedent.

The day was the darkest in the history of the Order of the Garter.

The battered survivors stopped charging alone into the streets.

They kept close to squires and infantry and used the ranged weapons their pilots usually despised to give direct fire support to the foot soldiers.

The pace of the battle slowed at once.

Couriers carried word of the armored losses to the rear command post with speed.

Eldridge and the other British officers could not believe what they heard.

*"What did you say? Five more gone already?!"*

Colonel Henry seized the courier by the collar. His eyes bulged.

*"Yes, Colonel. Colonel Marcus is dead as well."*

The courier answered with a tremor in his voice.

The air in the tent seemed to freeze.

General Sanjurjo heard that the British had taken heavy losses. A flash of bare satisfaction crossed his face.

He caught himself an instant later and wore grief and shock again.

*"My God! How can this be possible! How could the Germans do such a thing!"*

Eldridge turned a cold look on him and said nothing.

Eldridge and Colonel Henry could not regroup for a fresh assault in time even if they had wished to.

Large bodies of troops were already mixed with the garrison in the streets and locked in close fighting.

An order to retreat now would turn their backs to German soldiers in the ruins.

They fell into the same frenzy General Sanjurjo had shown earlier.

*"We cannot fall back! The armies of the Holy Britannian Empire will not retreat before these bumpkins!"*

Colonel Henry waved his arms out of control and ordered the reserve to prepare for commitment.

Eldridge left the tent without a word. A pulse of magic spread from his body.

He meant to go to the front himself.
