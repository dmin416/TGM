# Chapter 4

Arc: San Isidro

Date: Late January 1914, noon

Once the main force secured its footing inside the village the battalion's baggage train cautiously rolled into a concealed area on the outskirts.

Each company's Feldküche soon began to send up cooking smoke.

Max had been gnawing nothing but a few pieces of hard bread since yesterday and was already starving.

After splitting off from Captain Hauser Max followed the first wave of soldiers rotating down to eat and hurried to 3rd Company's kitchen cart like a hungry wolf.

In the Imperial German Army the staple was black bread, usually eaten with thick soup or stew. Every so often you would also get meat in tins.

On the folding tables beside the field kitchen were piles of military-issue black loaves known as *Kommissbrot*. The fresh smell told Max they had been baked recently.

Max was not surprised. He had already learned that the 16th Infantry Brigade had something a division normally had: a field bakery company. These loaves were most likely baked by that unit and delivered to the companies' kitchens.

Over a hundred bakers in a single company, whose main job in normal conditions was supplying black bread for an entire division.

Among them were more than a dozen technical NCOs, men who had been professional bakers for years before joining the army.

So supplying bread for a single infantry brigade was easy work for a field bakery capable of producing more than twenty thousand loaves a day.

Max swallowed as he looked at the bread, then noticed a chubby cook by the cart slicing something sausage-like into a pot.

Max nodded and whispered to himself.

*Nice, sausage.*

Max knew the German Army's menu had several dishes that used different sausages and some of them actually tasted pretty good.

*"Lieutenant, I could've brought you food,"* his orderly said, unable to help himself as he watched Max's starving expression. *"You didn't need to come in person."*

*"No,"* Max said firmly, shaking his head. *"I need to eat immediately."*

Max took his mess tin from the orderly, didn't cut the line and simply joined the queue with eager anticipation.

When he saw the cook ladle from a huge pot the color of the soup caught his eye immediately. Green. Thick and green and steaming.

*Pea soup.*

Max felt a genuine lift in his chest. Pea soup was something he had always liked.

Then Max noticed the sausage wrapper thrown on the ground and the whole picture resolved.

*"Erbswurst,"* he said quietly to himself, satisfied.

Not every sausage was actually sausage. Germans called most foods packaged in long shapes sausage.

The famous *Erbswurst*, pea sausage, was one of them.

*Erbswurst* was a mixture made from bacon, onions, peas and dehydrated vegetables and it had basically nothing to do with what Max thought of as sausage.

The proper way to eat it was to slice it thick and dissolve it in hot water, turning it into a bowl of thick soup.

As an aside, in Max's old world the inventor of *Erbswurst* eventually sold the patent to a company called Knorr, best known for its soup bases.

Before his arrival here Max had actually wanted to try what *Erbswurst* tasted like. Unfortunately in his old world Knorr had discontinued that century-old product in 2018.

So in a way crossing time and space finally let him fill that small regret.

After collecting soup and bread Max and his orderly sat down on the ground with their backs against a tree.

Copying the other soldiers Max broke the bread into chunks, soaked them in the thick soup and once the bread softened shoveled it into his mouth.

German-style bread-in-soup. Not bad.

Max went back for a second helping. The cook raised an eyebrow but said nothing and ladled it out. Max finished that too.

His orderly watched the whole thing with visible surprise.

Max stood and went back a third time. The cook spread his hands. The pot was empty.

Max looked at it for a moment, then looked at the cook.

*"Please make sure we have this again,"* he said.

The cook blinked, then nodded slowly.

With a hot meal in his belly the hunger in Max's gut finally eased.

After lunch Max and his orderly returned to 3rd Platoon's position.

Once the platoon finished rotating through meals Max called over Sergeant Klaus, the four corporals and the eight squad leaders.

As they gathered all of them wore puzzled expressions, assuming a new combat task was coming down.

Instead Max cleared his throat and told them there was no immediate emergency, so they would use the time to go over the morning's engagement together.

The veterans and NCOs stared at one another, stunned.

In their experience fighting meant obeying orders, winning or losing, then waiting for the next order. No one had ever heard of sitting down for a meeting after the battle.

Max was not surprised by their reaction. He did not over-explain. He went straight to the point and asked whether they had any thoughts about the morning's fight or anything they felt could be done better.

That only deepened the confusion. The atmosphere turned stiff.

It was everyone's first time in this kind of setting and Max was a newly appointed commander, so no one dared to speak first.

In the end a young squad leader, one of the men who had charged at the very front with Max during the farmhouse raid, hesitated and then spoke.

*"Lieutenant… I think the physical strain was much worse than I expected,"* he admitted, scratching the back of his head in embarrassment. *"Especially the flanking run through the riverbed. We sprinted a few hundred meters in rough terrain. By the time we reached the farmhouse wall several of my men were already gasping."*

Max nodded in approval.

*"Good observation. Every tactic starts with stamina."*

Max was pleased someone responded and that it was a meaningful point.

*"Given our current training outline and rations, physical conditioning really is a weak point. I'll remember that. When I assign tasks in the future I'll factor in your energy expenditure as well."*

Once someone broke the ice the others relaxed. They began discussing one after another.

*"Lieutenant, the way you had us advance dispersed by squad, yes, it avoids getting wiped out all at once. But once the shooting starts and everyone's spread out it's harder to control. Sometimes you can't even hear orders,"* one corporal said, voicing his concern.

A squad leader agreed. *"Especially when we're detached from the main force and can't see you, there's this feeling of being unmoored. Like we don't know what we're supposed to do next."*

Max chalked it up to a single cause: not enough training in those methods.

*"These are new tactics. It's normal that it feels unfamiliar. Do it a few more times, build mutual understanding and it will get better."*

Max explained patiently, while privately thinking how perfectly *Auftragstaktik* suited him.

Higher command only cared about the objective. As long as you did not violate military law no one cared exactly how a subordinate unit achieved the mission.

The legendary storm troops of his old world had been born from that same environment, developed by frontline officers.

That model gave Max enormous freedom and it was also the core reason Hauser had ultimately allowed him to take an entire platoon and operate independently that morning.

The debrief ran for about half an hour and the mood grew more animated as it went.

At the end Max specifically told the eight squad leaders to also make time to talk with their own men, hear what they thought and what problems they were running into.

After everyone dispersed Sergeant Klaus stayed behind.

The experienced old soldier looked at Max with sincere admiration.

*"Lieutenant, you opened my eyes today,"* Klaus said earnestly. *"From last night until now, following you, I've learned so much I never even had contact with before."*

*"You're flattering me, Sergeant,"* Max replied. *"I'm just sharing what I know, hoping it keeps losses down when we fight."*

Being able to draw closer to the platoon sergeant, the man with the highest prestige in the platoon, was good for Max as well. It meant his grip on the unit had tightened another notch.

As they talked a battalion messenger jogged up at a run.

*"Lieutenant Maximilian! Major Thomas requests you report to him!"*

Hearing the battalion messenger's words Max remained calm but ready. An order from battalion and he had no idea whether it would be a blessing or a curse.

Max had Sergeant Klaus temporarily take charge of the platoon, emphasizing that they had to stay alert and watch for an enemy counterattack. Then he followed the messenger at a quick walk toward the small chapel in the village center.

Soon as Max entered the chapel, temporarily requisitioned as battalion headquarters, he immediately spotted Captain Hauser and Major Thomas, who stood with hands clasped behind his back at a long table covered with maps.

The atmosphere in the chapel was heavy. Major Thomas's face was still ugly and the grim casualty report from the morning had not left him.

They had taken the village but the price had been too high and the reduction in usable manpower severely limited what he could do next.

Still when he saw Max come in Major Thomas forced his expression into something like a smile.

*"Lieutenant Maximilian, you're here,"* he said, gesturing to an open space beside Hauser. *"Come over."*

*"Major, sir. Captain, sir."* Max saluted and took position beside Hauser.

Major Thomas skipped small talk and went straight to the point.

*"We've just received notice from brigade,"* he said. *"The attacks on our flanks by the National Army and the International Brigade have been going relatively smoothly."*

*"Brigade and regiment both agree we can't stay in this village for long. We have to continue advancing toward Seville as soon as possible."*

Max and Hauser exchanged a glance. Each saw the same weight in the other's reaction.

It meant the short rest was ending and more brutal fighting still lay ahead.

*"But before we launch the next assault,"* Major Thomas continued, his eyes settling on Max, *"I need to send a team to conduct a penetration reconnaissance of the area ahead of us."*

Max understood immediately why the battalion commander had called for him.

At the same time Max was also puzzled. Units in this era did not have dedicated reconnaissance formations yet but such tasks were usually handled by experienced officers, so why was this coming to a platoon leader who had only just arrived at the company.

As if reading his doubt Major Thomas sighed, a note of helplessness in his voice.

*"This morning, 1st and 2nd Companies lost a lot of officers. There aren't many left that I can spare."*

Major Thomas paused, then looked at Max with clear hope.

*"And your judgment and reactions in the fight proved you're better suited to this than the others. I need you to find out what's ahead. I don't want to see my soldiers blunder straight into another machine-gun position."*

Max fell silent for a moment.

Forward reconnaissance before an assault was dangerous by definition. But Max did not mind that.

Max had already shown his hand in order to survive the attack.

That had earned attention from above and it meant Major Thomas would assign him more tasks.

In wartime when a commander discovers a capable junior under him he naturally thinks of that person first when assigning difficult work.

Once the first domino fell there was no stopping until all the rest toppled.

Facing a direct order from a superior Max had no excuse he could use to refuse and he could not disobey.

Max kept his expression neutral but the ghost of a smile crossed his face.

*"Yes, Major. I guarantee I will complete the mission,"* Max replied at attention.

*"Good. Very good. The German Army needs men like you,"* Major Thomas said, his expression easing slightly.

Major Thomas pointed to the operations map on the table, signaling Max to look.

*"Here. Our battalion's next main axis is along this highway, advancing to the northwest. Your task is to reconnoiter this route and the area within five hundred meters on both sides."*

*"I need to know enemy movements, especially whether they've established new defensive positions or set an ambush."*

Based on what he had learned in his previous life, reconnaissance of unknown terrain meant sending out at least a full platoon tasked with bringing back reliable information for higher headquarters.

And in that process he would normally split his platoon into elements: command, scouts, security, casualty evacuation, prisoner handling and sustained observation.

But his unit's training did not support that kind of fine-grained division yet.

And 3rd Platoon was still responsible for a defensive sector and could not be fully pulled away.

So this mission would have to be a small quick in-and-out action.

Having thought it through Max leaned in and studied the map carefully.

Max traced a route with his finger, checked the map's scale and estimated the distance quickly.

The recon path would be more than twenty kilometers one way through complex terrain, farms, woods and scattered villages.

On foot would be too slow and too exhausting.

On horseback mobility would improve and fatigue would drop but it was too visible and too easy for the enemy to spot.

*"Major,"* Max said, looking up, *"I need to borrow some battalion bicycles."*

*"Bicycles?"*

*"Yes, Major. They're quiet, fast and they let us cross open ground quickly or disengage if needed. We can ride to a concealed spot and then proceed on foot to observe."*

Major Thomas considered it for a moment, then nodded.

*"Good idea."*

Major Thomas picked up pen and paper at once, wrote a short authorization and handed it to Max.

*"Go to the supply section. Ask them for what you need."*

Max accepted the written order. Beside him Captain Hauser clapped his shoulder lightly, eyes carrying both encouragement and concern.

*"Be careful out there, Lieutenant Maximilian."*

*"Don't worry, sir."*

Leaving battalion headquarters Max returned at a quick pace to 3rd Platoon's position. Max pulled Sergeant Klaus aside first and summarized the reconnaissance task.

*"I need to pick a few men to go with me,"* Max said in a low voice. *"This mission isn't to fight, it's to be eyes and ears. So we can't take many but they have to be good."*

Max planned to take only five men. Any more would be clumsy and easier to spot.

Klaus understood immediately and asked,

*"Sir, do you have requirements?"*

*"Good stamina, accurate. Most importantly minds that don't panic,"* Max said.

Klaus ran through candidates mentally and recommended several almost at once.

Among them were the young squad leader who had spoken first in the debrief and Corporal Baumann.

Klaus also recommended three soldiers who had performed well in the morning's assault, brave and remaining calm amid chaos.

Max was satisfied and called the chosen men together.

Max did not give a long motivational speech. Max simply explained the nature and danger of the mission clearly.

*"Our job is reconnaissance for the main force, not destroying the enemy. At all times concealment comes first. Without my order you do not fire, unless you've been discovered and cannot break contact."*

Max's gaze swept over each young resolute face.

*"Understood?"*

*"Understood, sir!"*

After giving the others a brief rundown of the mission Max looked seriously at Sergeant Klaus again.

With an old veteran like Klaus holding the position Max felt at least somewhat reassured.

*"While I'm away the platoon is yours, Sergeant Klaus,"* Max said firmly. *"Keep your eyes open. Watch for an enemy counterattack."*

*"Don't worry, Lieutenant,"* Klaus replied, nodding hard. After the morning's battle Klaus no longer questioned Max's orders at all.

With everything arranged Max turned and found his orderly Hans sitting nearby.

The weariness had settled in properly now. His legs were heavy, his eyes gritty, and his shoulders carried a dull ache from the hours of movement and tension since before dawn. Present but not incapacitating.

*"Hans."*

*"Lieutenant?"* The young orderly sprang to his feet.

*"Go to the kitchen cart,"* Max said, rubbing his temples. *"Get me a cup of coffee. As strong as you can make it."*

*"Yes, Lieutenant!"* Hans answered and jogged off at once.

After the orderly left Max took Corporal Baumann and the four other handpicked soldiers and headed toward the rear of the village toward the concealed area where the battalion trains were.

The 77mm field guns' preparation fire that morning had done real damage to the village. Many buildings had been battered by the bombardment.

They crossed village lanes pocked with shell craters and strewn with rubble. Soon they found the trains' concealed area, which doubled as a temporary camp.

More than a dozen heavy four-wheeled wagons and several trucks Max had ridden in earlier were hidden along the edge of a small stand of trees. Horses had been unharnessed and were grazing calmly nearby.

The quartermaster of 1st Battalion, 33rd Infantry Regiment, a middle-aged captain with a Prussian-style moustache, was counting supplies with the train troops.

When Max handed over the written authorization the quartermaster froze for a beat, then his moustache tilted upward.

*"Lieutenant Maximilian?"* He sized him up. *"That beautiful flanking raid this morning, was that you?"*

*"Just good luck, sir,"* Max answered modestly.

*"Luck is part of strength,"* the quartermaster said seriously.

The quartermaster seemed to take a liking to Max. He pocketed the authorization and waved to a soldier beside him.

*"Go and pick out the six bicycles in the best condition and issue them to Lieutenant Maximilian and his men."*

The quartermaster turned back to Max and added a reminder.

*"Lieutenant, these country roads are rough, rutted and full of potholes. They'll punish the bikes. Be careful when you ride."*

*"Thank you for the warning, sir."*

As soldiers went to bring the bicycles Max's eyes drifted toward the back of the supply area. A section that had been fenced off drew his attention.

Two enormous silhouettes were parked there.

They looked like heavy plate-armored knights enlarged several times over, more than three meters tall, resting in a kneeling position. Beside them were weapon racks holding arming swords, kite shields and massive rifles, guns so large they were closer to cannons.

The sight pressed down on Max.

Compared to their huge heavy torsos their limbs looked a bit short, an odd proportion.

Their bodies were covered in surcoats printed with a black-and-white cross. Only thick metal lines and pipes showed through gaps and exhaust stacks at the back hinted that some kind of internal combustion engine drove them.

At the moment many people in similar black-and-white surcoats but without weapons were busy around the two steel giants, maintaining and resupplying them.

*"My God… are those armored knights?"* Corporal Baumann could not help whispering in awe.

The other soldiers craned their necks too, curiosity and shock plain in their eyes.

Most German soldiers had only seen blurry newspaper photographs of such steel creations. Seeing the real thing for the first time was something else entirely.

The visual impact of cold metal and massive size was far beyond anything black-and-white print could convey.

Following their gaze the quartermaster smiled as if understanding.

*"First time seeing the Teutonic Order's prized toys, huh?"*

The quartermaster stepped closer to Max and lowered his voice.

*"Brigade heard we took heavy losses this morning. They specifically sent two Siegfried-type armored knights, plus a whole squad of knight attendants, to reinforce us."*

The quartermaster seemed to hold Max in special regard and added a little more.

*"With those two big monsters the next push will be much easier. If we run into another machine-gun nest they'll go clear it, better than having our men fill it with their lives or having you gamble on some desperate flank like today."*

Max's thoughts stirred. Max knew exactly how cruel and foolish it was to throw bodies at a machine-gun position. The morning's slaughter was still vivid.

If these units really could deal with enemy fire points they would be a blessing for infantry.

Max was curious how they worked on the battlefield. Could their armor stop machine-gun bullets? Could they survive direct fire from field guns?

But Max also knew now was not the time to satisfy curiosity. The reconnaissance mission was urgent.

*"Thank you for telling me, sir,"* Max said, pulling his eyes back.

*"Good luck to you, Lieutenant,"* the quartermaster said.

They pushed their new bicycles out of the supply area. They had not gone far when Hans came panting up behind them, holding a steaming tin cup.

*"Lieutenant, your coffee!"*

Max took the cup. A scorched bitter smell hit him immediately, and something in it pulled at a distant memory. It smelled like the barley tea he had drunk in a previous life, roasted grain and warm bitterness, not unpleasant at all.

Max sipped once. The German army did not issue real coffee. What soldiers received was *Muckefuck*, a substitute brewed from roasted chicory, acorns or grain, anything that would darken hot water and call itself a beverage. Bitter and acrid, scraping his tongue raw.

Max frowned and glanced at his orderly.

*"Hans!"*

*"Yes, Lieutenant!"*

*"Your coffee is awful."*

*"…"*

Then Max tilted his head back, drained the cup in one go and handed it back to Hans.

Max put a hand on Hans's shoulder.

*"Hans."*

*"Yes, Lieutenant!"*

*"Your coffee is awful. But I appreciate the tea."*

Hans blinked.

*"If you need a pick-me-up, sir, I can fetch your cigarette ration. Or some Coca-Schnapps."*

*"Do that,"* Max said. *"Bring both."*

Hans nodded and jogged back toward the supply section. Max and the others waited with their bicycles.

Hans returned shortly with a small paper packet of cigarettes and a flat bottle. Max pocketed both.

*"Thank you, Hans."*

*"Move out,"* Max said, and mounted his bicycle.
